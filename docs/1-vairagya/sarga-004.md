# Sarga 4

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · Śrī Rāma's homecoming and happy daily life (12 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 4 at a glance"
    Vālmīki continues his account to Bharadvāja.

    1. Śrī Rāma returns home and is welcomed with joy (1.4.1–1.4.5).
    2. His full and happy daily life as a prince: worship, court, time with Vasiṣṭha, hunting, family (1.4.6–1.4.11).
    3. A closing verse in a longer metre (1.4.12).

    This short sarga paints the happy life that Sarga 5 will see unravel.

## Verse 1.4.1 { #1-4-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
रामः पुष्पाञ्जलिव्रातैर्विकीर्णः पुरवासिभिः ।<br>
प्रविवेश गृहं श्रीमाञ्जयन्तो विष्टपं यथा ॥ १.४.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुरवासिभिः (by the citizens) पुष्प-अञ्जलि-व्रातैः (with handfuls of flowers) विकीर्णः (showered), श्रीमान् रामः (glorious Rāma) गृहं प्रविवेश (entered his home), यथा जयन्तः विष्टपम् (as Jayanta [enters] heaven).
</p>

**Translation:** Vālmīki said: Showered by the citizens with handfuls of flowers, the glorious Rāma entered his home, as Jayanta, son of Indra, enters heaven.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Homecoming.** Sarga 3 ended with Rāma's return; Sarga 4 describes the welcome. Outwardly everything is festive.
- **Jayanta,** Indra's son. Rāma is pictured as a prince of heaven returning home — the same image of heavenly enjoyment that Ariṣṭanemi turned down (1.1.40).

## Verse 1.4.2 { #1-4-2 }

<p class="shloka">
प्रणनामाथ पितरं वसिष्ठं भ्रातृबान्धवान् ।<br>
ब्राह्मणान्कुलवृद्धांश्च राघवः प्रथमागतः ॥ १.४.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) प्रथम-आगतः राघवः (Rāghava, just arrived) पितरं (his father) वसिष्ठं (Vasiṣṭha) भ्रातृ-बान्धवान् (brothers and relatives) ब्राह्मणान् कुल-वृद्धान् च (brāhmaṇas and family elders) प्रणनाम (bowed to).
</p>

**Translation:** Rāghava, who had come in first, then made his obeisance — to his father, to Vasiṣṭha, to his brothers and kin, to the brāhmaṇas and to the elders of the clan.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2:** <span class="parallel">मातृदेवो भव । पितृदेवो भव । आचार्यदेवो भव । अतिथिदेवो भव ।</span> — *treat your mother, father, teacher and guest as God.* Rāma bows first to his father, then to his teacher Vasiṣṭha.
- **Vasiṣṭha is named second,** right after the father. The teacher is already in Rāma's heart, though no teaching has yet been asked for.

## Verse 1.4.3 { #1-4-3 }

<p class="shloka">
सुहृद्भिर्भ्रातृभिश्चैव पित्रा द्विजगणेन च ।<br>
मुहुरालिङ्गिताचारो राघवो न ममौ मुदा ॥ १.४.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सुहृद्भिः भ्रातृभिः च एव (by friends and brothers) पित्रा (by his father) द्विज-गणेन च (and by the brāhmaṇas) मुहुः आलिङ्गित-आचारः (embraced again and again) राघवः मुदा न ममौ (Rāghava could not contain his joy).
</p>

**Translation:** Embraced again and again by friends, brothers, father and brāhmaṇas, Rāma could hardly contain his joy.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **न ममौ मुदा — joy overflowing.** Remember this happiness. Within a few verses of Sarga 5, the same Rāma will grow pale and silent. The contrast is the point.
- **Joy that depends on circumstances.** It is real, but it is borrowed from the moment. The text will soon ask what joy does not depend on anything.

## Verse 1.4.4 { #1-4-4 }

<p class="shloka">
तस्मिन्गृहे दाशरथेः प्रियप्रकथनैर्मिथः ।<br>
जुघूर्णुर्मधुरैराशा मृदुवंशस्तनैरिव ॥ १.४.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तस्मिन् दाशरथेः गृहे (in that house of Daśaratha's son) आशाः (the quarters) मिथः मधुरैः प्रिय-प्रकथनैः (with sweet, loving conversations among them) मृदु-वंश-स्तनैः इव (as if with soft notes of a flute) जुघूर्णुः (resounded).
</p>

**Translation:** In Rāma's house, every corner rang with sweet, loving conversation, as if with the soft notes of a flute.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The household as music.** The image is of harmony. This is the world at its best: love, welcome, family. The coming dispassion is not a rejection of a bad world, but a questioning of even the best one.

## Verse 1.4.5 { #1-4-5 }

<p class="shloka">
बभूवाथ दिनान्यष्टौ रामागमन उत्सवः ।<br>
सुखं मत्तजनोन्मुक्तकलकोलाहलाकुलः ॥ १.४.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) राम-आगमन-उत्सवः (the festival of Rāma's return) मत्त-जन-उन्मुक्त-कल-कोलाहल-आकुलः (filled with the sweet clamour of joyful people) अष्टौ दिनानि सुखं बभूव (went on happily for eight days).
</p>

**Translation:** The festival of Rāma's homecoming went on happily for eight days, filled with the happy clamour of celebrating people.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Eight days of festival.** A human detail. Celebrations have a beginning and an end; the text will soon notice that all pleasures do (compare Gītā 5.22, quoted at 1.10.30).

## Verse 1.4.6 { #1-4-6 }

<p class="shloka">
उवास स सुखं गेहे ततः प्रभृति राघवः ।<br>
वर्णयन्विविधाकारान्देशाचारानितस्ततः ॥ १.४.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः प्रभृति (from then on) सः राघवः (that Rāghava) इतः ततः (here and there) विविध-आकारान् देश-आचारान् (the many customs of various lands) वर्णयन् (describing) गेहे सुखम् उवास (lived happily at home).
</p>

**Translation:** From then on Rāma lived happily at home, describing the many different customs he had seen in various lands.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The returned traveller.** Rāma shares what he saw. Nothing yet shows the inner change; it is still hidden, even from himself perhaps.

## Verse 1.4.7 { #1-4-7 }

<p class="shloka">
प्रातरुत्थाय रामोऽसौ कृत्वा संध्यां यथाविधि ।<br>
सभासंस्थं ददर्शेन्द्रसमं स्वपितरं तथा ॥ १.४.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
असौ रामः (that Rāma) प्रातः उत्थाय (rising at dawn) यथाविधि संध्यां कृत्वा (performing the twilight worship as prescribed) सभा-संस्थम् इन्द्र-समं स्व-पितरं (his father, seated in the assembly like Indra) तथा ददर्श (then went to see).
</p>

**Translation:** Rising at dawn and performing the morning worship as prescribed, Rāma would go to see his father, who sat in the assembly like Indra.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A dutiful daily life.** Rāma does everything right: worship, respect, attendance at court. His later crisis does not come from neglecting duty.
- **Recall 1.1.14:** the lifelong twilight worship (संध्या) that Kāruṇya questioned. Rāma still performs it.

## Verse 1.4.8 { #1-4-8 }

<p class="shloka">
कथाभिः सुविचित्राभिः स वसिष्ठादिभिः सह ।<br>
स्थित्वा दिनचतुर्भागं ज्ञानगर्भाभिरादृतः ॥ १.४.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सः (he) आदृतः (honoured) वसिष्ठ-आदिभिः सह (with Vasiṣṭha and others) ज्ञान-गर्भाभिः सुविचित्राभिः कथाभिः (with varied stories full of wisdom) दिन-चतुः-भागं स्थित्वा (spending a quarter of the day) …
</p>

**Translation:** Honoured by all, he would spend a quarter of the day with Vasiṣṭha and others, in many kinds of conversations full of wisdom.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 10.9:**
    - <span class="parallel">मच्चित्ता मद्गतप्राणा बोधयन्तः परस्परम् । कथयन्तश्च मां नित्यं तुष्यन्ति च रमन्ति च ॥</span>
    - *Their minds on me, their lives given to me, awakening one another and always speaking of me, they are content and joyful.*
    - Rāma already spends time in ज्ञानगर्भा कथा, wisdom-filled talk. The soil is prepared for the teaching.
- **A quarter of the day.** The daily rhythm includes time with the wise. Perhaps a good habit for any reader.

## Verse 1.4.9 { #1-4-9 }

<p class="shloka">
जगाम पित्रानुज्ञातो महत्या सेनयावृतः ।<br>
वराहमहिषाकीर्णं वनमाखेटकेच्छया ॥ १.४.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पित्रा अनुज्ञातः (permitted by his father) महत्या सेनया आवृतः (surrounded by a large army) आखेटक-इच्छया (wishing to hunt) वराह-महिष-आकीर्णं वनं जगाम (he went to the forest full of boars and buffaloes).
</p>

**Translation:** With his father's permission he would go hunting, surrounded by a large troop, to forests full of boars and buffaloes.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A prince's life.** Hunting was part of a kṣatriya's training. The text paints an ordinary royal routine, complete in every way.

## Verse 1.4.10 { #1-4-10 }

<p class="shloka">
तत आगत्य सदने कृत्वा स्नानादिकं क्रमम् ।<br>
समित्रबान्धवो भुक्त्वा निनाय ससुहृन्निशाम् ॥ १.४.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः सदने आगत्य (then returning home) स्नान-आदिकं क्रमं कृत्वा (bathing and so on in due order) स-मित्र-बान्धवः भुक्त्वा (dining with friends and relatives) स-सुहृत् निशां निनाय (he spent the night with his friends).
</p>

**Translation:** Then, returning home, he would bathe and do the rest in due order, dine with friends and family, and spend the evening with his companions.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Every part of the day is full.** Worship, court, wisdom, hunting, family, friends. Hold this picture in mind; Sarga 10 will show the same day emptied of all its savour.

## Verse 1.4.11 { #1-4-11 }

<p class="shloka">
एवंप्रायदिनाचारो भ्रातृभ्यां सह राघवः ।<br>
आगत्य तीर्थयात्रायाः समुवास पितुर्गृहे ॥ १.४.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एवं-प्राय-दिन-आचारः (with such a daily routine) राघवः (Rāghava) तीर्थ-यात्रायाः आगत्य (having returned from pilgrimage) भ्रातृभ्यां सह (with his two brothers) पितुः गृहे समुवास (lived in his father's house).
</p>

**Translation:** Back from his pilgrimage, Rāghava lived in his father's house together with his two brothers, and his days passed mostly in this manner.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **गृहस्थ life in miniature.** Before any renunciation, the text shows a full and good household life. When Śrī Rāma later returns to such a life as a free person (see [wisdom lived, not renounced](../index.md#wisdom-lived)), it will be the same life, seen differently.

## Verse 1.4.12 { #1-4-12 }

<p class="shloka">
नृपतिसंव्यवहारमनोज्ञया सुजनचेतसि चन्द्रिकयानया ।<br>
परिनिनाय दिनानि स चेष्टया स्तुतसुधारसपेशलयाऽनघ ॥ १.४.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनघ (sinless one), सः (he) नृपति-संव्यवहार-मनोज्ञया (pleasing in his dealings with kings) सुजन-चेतसि अनया चन्द्रिकया [इव] (like moonlight in the hearts of good people) स्तुत-सुधा-रस-पेशलया (gentle as praised nectar) चेष्टया (by his conduct) दिनानि परिनिनाय (passed his days).
</p>

**Translation:** So, O blameless one, he let the days go by, his ways pleasing in all his dealings with princes, a moonlight to the minds of good people, soft as the nectar the poets praise.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre,** as at the end of each sarga.
- **Gītā 12.15:** <span class="parallel">यस्मान्नोद्विजते लोको लोकान्नोद्विजते च यः</span> — *one by whom the world is not disturbed, and who is not disturbed by the world.* Rāma's conduct is gentle and pleasing to all — the outer form of the inner freedom he will later reach.
- **Moonlight.** Soft, cooling, giving pleasure to all. The next sarga turns from this moonlight to an autumn in which the lake dries up.
