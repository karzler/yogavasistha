# Sarga 3

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · The essence of the teaching, and Śrī Rāma's pilgrimage (42 verses)*

!!! info "Who is speaking? Sarga 3 at a glance"
    Vālmīki is now answering **Bharadvāja** (still within his account to King Ariṣṭanemi).

    1. Bharadvāja asks about the state of one liberated while living, beginning with Rāma (1.3.1).
    2. Before the story, Vālmīki gives its conclusion: the world is like the blue of the sky; liberation is the complete release of latent tendencies (vāsanās); pure and impure vāsanās; the jīvanmukta (1.3.2–1.3.17).
    3. The story of **Śrī Rāma** begins: his youth, his wish to go on pilgrimage, his first words, and his journey across the land (1.3.18–1.3.41).
    4. A closing verse in a longer metre: Rāma returns home (1.3.42).

## Verse 1.3.1 { #1-3-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">भरद्वाज उवाच ।</span><br>
जीवन्मुक्तस्थितिं ब्रह्मन्कृत्वा राघवमादितः ।<br>
क्रमात्कथय मे नित्यं भविष्यामि सुखी यथा ॥ १.३.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), राघवम् आदितः कृत्वा (beginning with Rāghava, Śrī Rāma) जीवन्मुक्त-स्थितिं (the state of one liberated while living) मे क्रमात् कथय (tell me step by step), यथा (so that) नित्यं सुखी भविष्यामि (I may be happy always).
</p>

**Translation:** Bharadvāja said: "O sage, beginning with Śrī Rāma, tell me step by step the state of one who is liberated while living, so that I may be happy for ever."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **स्थिति — Gītā 2.72:**
    - <span class="parallel">एषा ब्राह्मी स्थितिः पार्थ नैनां प्राप्य विमुह्यति । स्थित्वास्यामन्तकालेऽपि ब्रह्मनिर्वाणमृच्छति ॥</span>
    - *This is the Brahman-state, Pārtha. Reaching it, one is not deluded again. Established in it even at the end, one attains the peace of Brahman.*
    - The Gītā ends its second chapter with the ब्राह्मी स्थिति of the steady-minded; Bharadvāja asks for the जीवन्मुक्तस्थिति. Both ask what the free person's state is like.
- **क्रमात्, step by step.** The student asks for an order, not a summary. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha's length is its answer to this request.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints व्रह्मन्; the correct form ब्रह्मन् is used here.

## Verse 1.3.2 { #1-3-2 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
भ्रमस्य जागतस्यास्य जातस्याकाशवर्णवत् ।<br>
अपुनःस्मरणं मन्ये साधो विस्मरणं वरम् ॥ १.३.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
साधो (good one), आकाश-वर्ण-वत् जातस्य (arisen like the colour of the sky) अस्य जागतस्य भ्रमस्य (of this illusion of the world) अपुनः-स्मरणं (not calling it to mind again), विस्मरणं (forgetting it) वरं मन्ये (I consider best).
</p>

**Translation:** Vālmīki said: "Good one, this illusion of the world has arisen like the blue colour of the sky. Not calling it to mind again — forgetting it altogether — is, I think, the best."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The blue of the sky.** The sky looks blue, but there is no blue thing there. The world, says Vālmīki, is like that: clearly seen, yet not a thing in itself. This image will recur throughout the text.
- **Forgetting, not fighting.** The answer is not to struggle against the world, but to stop re-creating it in the mind. Freedom as a kind of forgetting.
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.3.6:** <span class="parallel">अथात आदेशो नेति नेति</span> — *now the teaching: not this, not this.* Setting aside what is seen, one by one, is the Upaniṣadic way of "forgetting" what is not the Self.
- **A teaching before the story.** Before telling Rāma's story, Vālmīki gives its conclusion. The story that follows is the path to this point.

## Verse 1.3.3 { #1-3-3 }

<p class="shloka">
दृश्यात्यन्ताभावबोधं विना तन्नानुभूयते ।<br>
कदाचित्केनचिन्नाम स्वबोधोऽन्विष्यतामतः ॥ १.३.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दृश्य-अत्यन्त-अभाव-बोधं विना (without understanding the complete non-existence of the seen) तत् (that [freedom]) कदाचित् केनचित् नाम (ever, by anyone at all) न अनुभूयते (is not experienced). अतः (therefore) स्व-बोधः अन्विष्यताम् (let one's own understanding be sought).
</p>

**Translation:** "Without understanding that what is seen does not truly exist, no one ever experiences that freedom. Therefore, seek your own understanding."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 2.16:**
    - <span class="parallel">नासतो विद्यते भावो नाभावो विद्यते सतः । उभयोरपि दृष्टोऽन्तस्त्वनयोस्तत्त्वदर्शिभिः ॥</span>
    - *The unreal has no being; the real never ceases to be. Those who see the truth have seen the boundary between the two.*
    - दृश्यात्यन्ताभाव — "the complete non-existence of the seen" — is the strongest statement of the Gītā's नासतो विद्यते भावः.
- **स्वबोध.** Not borrowed knowledge, but one's own realisation. No one else can understand on our behalf.
- **A strong claim to weigh.** The Yoga Vasiṣṭha does not say the world is merely changing; it says the seen does not exist at all, as such. Notice how this goes further than many readings of the Gītā.

## Verse 1.3.4 { #1-3-4 }

<p class="shloka">
स चेह संभवत्येव तदर्थमिदमाततम् ।<br>
शास्त्रमाकर्णयसि चेत्तत्त्वमाप्स्यसि नान्यथा ॥ १.३.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सः च (and that [understanding]) इह सम्भवति एव (is certainly possible here); तत्-अर्थम् (for that purpose) इदं शास्त्रम् आततम् (this teaching has been set out at length). [इदम्] आकर्णयसि चेत् (if you listen to it) तत्त्वम् आप्स्यसि (you will reach the truth), न अन्यथा (not otherwise).
</p>

**Translation:** "And that understanding is possible here, in this life. For that purpose this teaching has been set out at length. If you listen to it, you will reach the truth; not otherwise."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **इह — Kena Upaniṣad 2.5:**
    - <span class="parallel">इह चेदवेदीदथ सत्यमस्ति न चेदिहावेदीन्महती विनष्टिः ।</span>
    - *If one knows It here, there is truth. If one does not know It here, great is the loss.*
- **Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.3.4:** <span class="parallel">इह चेदशकद्बोद्धुं प्राक्शरीरस्य विस्रसः</span> — *if one is able to know It here, before the body falls away …*
- **आकर्णय — listen.** The text asks only to be heard fully. श्रवण, listening, is the first step of Vedānta, and the reason this commentary exists.

## Verse 1.3.5 { #1-3-5 }

<p class="shloka">
जगद्भ्रमोऽयं दृश्योऽपि नास्त्येवेत्यनुभूयते ।<br>
वर्णो व्योम्न इवाखेदाद्विचारेणामुनाऽनघ ॥ १.३.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनघ (sinless one), अमुना विचारेण (by this inquiry) अखेदात् (without strain), अयं जगत्-भ्रमः (this illusion of the world), दृश्यः अपि (though seen), व्योम्नः वर्णः इव (like the colour of the sky) न अस्ति एव इति अनुभूयते (is experienced as not existing at all).
</p>

**Translation:** "Through this inquiry, sinless one, without strain, this illusion of the world, though it is seen, is experienced as not existing at all — like the colour of the sky."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **विचार — Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.5:**
    - <span class="parallel">आत्मा वा अरे द्रष्टव्यः श्रोतव्यो मन्तव्यो निदिध्यासितव्यः ।</span>
    - *The Self, my dear, is to be seen, heard about, reflected on, and meditated upon.*
    - विचार corresponds to मनन, reflection: the step between hearing and realising.
- **अखेदात् — without strain.** Inquiry is not presented as a hard discipline but as something that works without exhaustion. Understanding, unlike effort, does not tire.
- **दृश्योऽपि — though seen.** The world does not disappear from view. One still sees the blue sky; one simply no longer believes in a blue thing.

## Verse 1.3.6 { #1-3-6 }

<p class="shloka">
दृश्यं नास्तीति बोधेन मनसो दृश्यमार्जनम् ।<br>
संपन्नं चेत्तदुत्पन्ना परा निर्वाणनिर्वृतिः ॥ १.३.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दृश्यं न अस्ति इति बोधेन (by understanding that the seen does not exist) मनसः दृश्य-मार्जनं (the wiping of the seen from the mind) सम्पन्नं चेत् (if it is accomplished), तत् (then) परा निर्वाण-निर्वृतिः (the supreme peace of nirvāṇa) उत्पन्ना (has arisen).
</p>

**Translation:** "When, through understanding that the seen does not exist, the mind is wiped clean of the seen, then the supreme peace of nirvāṇa has arisen."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 6.15:**
    - <span class="parallel">युञ्जन्नेवं सदात्मानं योगी नियतमानसः । शान्तिं निर्वाणपरमां मत्संस्थामधिगच्छति ॥</span>
    - *Always uniting himself thus, the yogī with a disciplined mind reaches peace, the supreme nirvāṇa that rests in me.*
- **मार्जन — wiping clean.** The image is of a mirror being cleaned. Nothing is added to the mind; only what clouds it is removed.
- **निर्वाण and निर्वृति.** "Blowing out" and "deep contentment": both words for the same peace. The text uses nirvāṇa freely, one of many signs of its openness to Buddhist vocabulary (your third reason).

## Verse 1.3.7 { #1-3-7 }

<p class="shloka">
अन्यथा शास्त्रगर्तेषु लुठतां भवतामिह ।<br>
भवत्यकृत्रिमाज्ञानां कल्पैरपि न निर्वृतिः ॥ १.३.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अन्यथा (otherwise), इह (here) शास्त्र-गर्तेषु लुठतां (rolling about in the pits of the scriptures) अकृत्रिम-अज्ञानां भवताम् (for you, whose ignorance is natural, not put on), कल्पैः अपि (even in aeons) निर्वृतिः न भवति (there is no peace).
</p>

**Translation:** "Otherwise, for people like you, whose ignorance is innate, rolling about in the pits of the scriptures, there is no peace even after aeons."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The pits of scripture.** A striking image: scriptures can become pits to roll in rather than ladders to climb. Learning without inquiry is a trap. (Recall Kāruṇya in 1.1.16.)
- **Gītā 2.52–53:**
    - <span class="parallel">श्रुतिविप्रतिपन्ना ते यदा स्थास्यति निश्चला । समाधावचला बुद्धिस्तदा योगमवाप्स्यसि ॥</span>
    - *When your understanding, now bewildered by much scripture, stands firm and unmoving in contemplation, then you will reach yoga.*
- **Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.2.23:**
    - <span class="parallel">नायमात्मा प्रवचनेन लभ्यो न मेधया न बहुना श्रुतेन ।</span>
    - *This Self is not attained by teaching, nor by intellect, nor by much learning.*
- **A warning for any commentator.** Including this one. The aim is not more learning, but the understanding the learning points to.

## Verse 1.3.8 { #1-3-8 }

<p class="shloka">
अशेषेण परित्यागो वासनानां य उत्तमः ।<br>
मोक्ष इत्युच्यते ब्रह्मन्स एव विमलक्रमः ॥ १.३.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), वासनानाम् (of the vāsanās, latent tendencies) यः अशेषेण उत्तमः परित्यागः (the complete and supreme giving-up), [सः] मोक्षः इति उच्यते (is called liberation); सः एव विमल-क्रमः (that alone is the pure path).
</p>

**Translation:** "The complete, supreme giving up of all latent tendencies (vāsanās) is called liberation, O sage. That alone is the pure path."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **वासना — the key word of this sarga.** A वासना is a latent tendency, the impression left by past experience that shapes future desire and perception. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha defines liberation simply as their total release.
- **Gītā 2.55:**
    - <span class="parallel">प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् । आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥</span>
    - *When one gives up all the desires of the mind, Pārtha, content in the Self by the Self, then one is called steady in wisdom.*
    - प्रजहाति सर्वान् कामान् there, परित्यागः अशेषेण वासनानाम् here: the same completeness.
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.7:** <span class="parallel">यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः । अथ मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्यत्र ब्रह्म समश्नुते ॥</span> — *when all desires in the heart are released, the mortal becomes immortal and attains Brahman here.* (The same verse appears in the Kaṭha; see 1.1.52.)
- **Reading note.** The online text prints मोक्षं इत्युच्यते; the grammatical form मोक्ष इत्युच्यते is used here.

## Verse 1.3.9 { #1-3-9 }

<p class="shloka">
क्षीणायां वासनायां तु चेतो गलति सत्वरम् ।<br>
क्षीणायां शीतसंतत्यां ब्रह्मन्हिमकणो यथा ॥ १.३.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), यथा (just as) शीत-सन्तत्यां क्षीणायां (when the continuous cold fades) हिम-कणः (a crystal of ice) [गलति] (melts), [तथा] वासनायां क्षीणायां तु (when the vāsanā fades) चेतः सत्वरं गलति (the mind melts away quickly).
</p>

**Translation:** "When latent tendencies wane, the mind quickly melts away, O sage, just as a crystal of ice melts when the cold no longer lasts."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The mind as ice.** Ice is only water held in shape by continued cold. The mind is only consciousness held in shape by continued vāsanās. Remove the cold and the ice returns to water; nothing is destroyed.
- **Maitrāyaṇī Upaniṣad 6.34:**
    - <span class="parallel">मन एव मनुष्याणां कारणं बन्धमोक्षयोः ।</span>
    - *The mind alone is the cause of people's bondage and liberation.*
- **A question to sit with.** Which of my habits of thought are "cold" I keep renewing, which hold the mind in its old shape?

## Verse 1.3.10 { #1-3-10 }

<p class="shloka">
अयं वासनया देहो ध्रियते भूतपञ्जरः ।<br>
तनुनान्तर्निविष्टेन मुक्तौघस्तन्तुना यथा ॥ १.३.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अयं भूत-पञ्जरः देहः (this body, a cage of elements) अन्तः-निविष्टेन तनुना तन्तुना (by a thin thread running inside) मुक्त-ओघः यथा (as a string of pearls [is held]), [तथा] वासनया ध्रियते (is held together by vāsanā).
</p>

**Translation:** "This body, a cage made of the elements, is held together by vāsanā, as a string of pearls is held by the fine thread running through it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A reversal of Gītā 7.7:**
    - <span class="parallel">मत्तः परतरं नान्यत्किञ्चिदस्ति धनञ्जय । मयि सर्वमिदं प्रोतं सूत्रे मणिगणा इव ॥</span>
    - *There is nothing higher than me, Dhanañjaya. All this is strung on me, like pearls on a thread.*
    - The same image, two threads. In the Gītā, the thread holding all things is the Lord. Here, the thread holding the individual body is vāsanā. Perhaps both are true: the Lord holds the world; our tendencies hold our particular life in it.
- **When the thread goes, the pearls scatter.** When vāsanā ends, the body is no longer bound together as a cause of future births.

## Verse 1.3.11 { #1-3-11 }

<p class="shloka">
वासना द्विविधा प्रोक्ता शुद्धा च मलिना तथा ।<br>
मलिना जन्मनो हेतुः शुद्धा जन्मविनाशिनी ॥ १.३.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वासना द्वि-विधा प्रोक्ता (vāsanā is said to be of two kinds): शुद्धा च तथा मलिना (pure and impure). मलिना जन्मनः हेतुः (the impure is the cause of birth); शुद्धा जन्म-विनाशिनी (the pure ends birth).
</p>

**Translation:** "Vāsanā is said to be of two kinds, pure and impure. The impure is the cause of rebirth; the pure brings rebirth to an end."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 16.5:**
    - <span class="parallel">दैवी सम्पद्विमोक्षाय निबन्धायासुरी मता ।</span>
    - *The divine qualities are held to lead to liberation, the demonic to bondage.*
    - The same two-fold division: one kind of disposition frees, the other binds.
- **Not all tendencies are a problem.** Liberation does not require having no tendencies at all while living; it requires that they be pure. This is how a free person can still act, eat and speak (see 1.2.30).

## Verse 1.3.12 { #1-3-12 }

<p class="shloka">
अज्ञानसुघनाकारा घनाहंकारशालिनी ।<br>
पुनर्जन्मकरी प्रोक्ता मलिना वासना बुधैः ॥ १.३.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अज्ञान-सुघन-आकारा (dense with ignorance), घन-अहंकार-शालिनी (full of thick ego), पुनः-जन्म-करी (producing rebirth) मलिना वासना (the impure vāsanā) बुधैः प्रोक्ता (is described by the wise).
</p>

**Translation:** "The wise describe impure vāsanā as dense with ignorance, heavy with ego, and productive of rebirth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 18.17:**
    - <span class="parallel">यस्य नाहङ्कृतो भावो बुद्धिर्यस्य न लिप्यते । हत्वापि स इमाँल्लोकान्न हन्ति न निबध्यते ॥</span>
    - *One who has no sense of "I am the doer", whose understanding is not tainted — even if he slays these worlds, he does not slay and is not bound.*
    - अहंकार is the mark of the impure vāsanā. Remove it, and even action does not bind.
- **Two marks.** Ignorance (अज्ञान) and ego (अहंकार). They always come together: the sense of a separate "I" is the first misunderstanding.

## Verse 1.3.13 { #1-3-13 }

<p class="shloka">
पुनर्जन्माङ्कुरं त्यक्त्वा स्थिता संभृष्टबीजवत् ।<br>
देहार्थं ध्रियते ज्ञातज्ञेया शुद्धेति चोच्यते ॥ १.३.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुनः-जन्म-अङ्कुरं त्यक्त्वा (having given up the sprout of rebirth), सम्भृष्ट-बीज-वत् स्थिता (remaining like a roasted seed), देह-अर्थं ध्रियते (kept up only for the sake of the body), ज्ञात-ज्ञेया (in one who has known the knowable), शुद्धा इति च उच्यते (is called pure).
</p>

**Translation:** "That vāsanā which has given up the sprout of rebirth, remaining like a roasted seed and kept only for the body's sake, in one who has known what is to be known — that is called pure."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The roasted seed.** A roasted seed still looks like a seed, but it can never sprout. The tendencies of a liberated person still appear — they walk, eat, speak — but they produce no new bondage.
- **Gītā 4.37:**
    - <span class="parallel">यथैधांसि समिद्धोऽग्निर्भस्मसात्कुरुतेऽर्जुन । ज्ञानाग्निः सर्वकर्माणि भस्मसात्कुरुते तथा ॥</span>
    - *As a blazing fire turns fuel to ash, Arjuna, so the fire of knowledge turns all actions to ash.*
    - Knowledge is the fire that roasts the seed.
- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 2.2.8:** <span class="parallel">भिद्यते हृदयग्रन्थिश्छिद्यन्ते सर्वसंशयाः । क्षीयन्ते चास्य कर्माणि तस्मिन्दृष्टे परावरे ॥</span> — *the knot of the heart is cut, all doubts are dispelled, and one's karma wanes, when That, the high and the low, is seen.*
- **ज्ञातज्ञेय again.** The phrase from 1.1.50 returns: the one "who has known the knowable".

## Verse 1.3.14 { #1-3-14 }

<p class="shloka">
अपुनर्जन्मकरणी जीवन्मुक्तेषु देहिषु ।<br>
वासना विद्यते शुद्धा देहे चक्र इव भ्रमः ॥ १.३.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जीवन्मुक्तेषु देहिषु (in embodied ones liberated while living) अपुनः-जन्म-करणी (not causing rebirth) शुद्धा वासना (a pure vāsanā) विद्यते (exists), देहे (in the body), चक्रे भ्रमः इव (like the spinning that continues in a wheel).
</p>

**Translation:** "In embodied beings liberated while living, a pure vāsanā remains that does not cause rebirth, like the spinning that continues in a potter's wheel after the push has stopped."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The potter's wheel.** The potter lets go, but the wheel keeps turning for a while on its own momentum. The free person's body and habits continue for a while on the momentum of the past, but no new push is given.
- **Chāndogya Upaniṣad 6.14.2:**
    - <span class="parallel">तस्य तावदेव चिरं यावन्न विमोक्ष्येऽथ सम्पत्स्य इति ।</span>
    - *For him the delay lasts only as long as he is not released from the body; then he reaches fulfilment.*
    - Vedānta calls this momentum प्रारब्ध: the karma already set in motion, which runs its course without binding the knower.
- **Why the wise still have a body.** This verse answers a natural question: if one is free, why does life go on? Because the wheel is still spinning.

## Verse 1.3.15 { #1-3-15 }

<p class="shloka">
ये शुद्धवासना भूयो न जन्मानर्थभाजनम् ।<br>
ज्ञातज्ञेयास्त उच्यन्ते जीवन्मुक्ता महाधियः ॥ १.३.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये (those who) शुद्ध-वासनाः (have pure vāsanās), भूयः (again) जन्म-अनर्थ-भाजनं न [भवन्ति] (do not become vessels for the misfortune of birth), ते ज्ञात-ज्ञेयाः (they, who have known the knowable) महा-धियः (of great understanding) जीवन्मुक्ताः उच्यन्ते (are called liberated while living).
</p>

**Translation:** "Those whose vāsanās are pure, who are no longer vessels for the misfortune of rebirth, who have known what is to be known — those great-minded ones are called liberated while living."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **ज्ञातज्ञेय — Gītā 7.2:**
    - <span class="parallel">ज्ञानं तेऽहं सविज्ञानमिदं वक्ष्याम्यशेषतः । यज्ज्ञात्वा नेह भूयोऽन्यज्ज्ञातव्यमवशिष्यते ॥</span>
    - *I will teach you this knowledge together with its realisation, fully. Knowing it, nothing else remains here to be known.*
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.6:** <span class="parallel">अकामो निष्काम आप्तकाम आत्मकामो न तस्य प्राणा उत्क्रामन्ति ब्रह्मैव सन्ब्रह्माप्येति ॥</span> — *free from desire, his desires fulfilled, desiring only the Self — his life-breaths do not depart; being Brahman, he goes to Brahman.*
- **A definition to remember.** Verses 11–15 give the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's own definition of the jīvanmukta: one whose remaining tendencies are pure, like roasted seeds on a slowing wheel.

## Verse 1.3.16 { #1-3-16 }

<p class="shloka">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा महामतिः रामः (how the great-minded Rāma) जीवन्मुक्ति-पदं प्राप्तः (reached the state of living liberation), तत् ते अहं वक्ष्यामि (I will tell you that), जरा-मरण-शान्तये (for the ending of old age and death); शृणु (listen).
</p>

**Translation:** "Listen: I will tell you how the great-minded Rāma reached the state of living liberation, so that old age and death may be stilled for you."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 7.29 — the same phrase:**
    - <span class="parallel">जरामरणमोक्षाय मामाश्रित्य यतन्ते ये । ते ब्रह्म तद्विदुः कृत्स्नमध्यात्मं कर्म चाखिलम् ॥</span>
    - *Those who strive for freedom from old age and death, taking refuge in me, know That Brahman fully, the inner Self and all action.*
    - जरामरणमोक्षाय there, जरामरणशान्तये here.
- **From definition to example.** Verses 8–15 define liberation; now Vālmīki promises a living example: Rāma himself.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints वक्ष्याभि; the correct form वक्ष्यामि is used here.

## Verse 1.3.17 { #1-3-17 }

<p class="shloka">
भरद्वाज महाबुद्धे रामक्रममिमं शुभम् ।<br>
शृणु वक्ष्यामि तेनैव सर्वं ज्ञास्यसि सर्वदा ॥ १.३.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
महा-बुद्धे भरद्वाज (Bharadvāja of great intellect), इमं शुभं राम-क्रमम् (this auspicious path of Rāma) वक्ष्यामि (I will tell); शृणु (listen). तेन एव (by that alone) सर्वदा सर्वं ज्ञास्यसि (you will know everything, for ever).
</p>

**Translation:** "Bharadvāja, great of understanding, listen: I will tell you this auspicious course of Rāma's life. Through it alone you will know everything, for ever."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.1.3:**
    - <span class="parallel">कस्मिन्नु भगवो विज्ञाते सर्वमिदं विज्ञातं भवतीति ।</span>
    - *Revered sir, what is that by knowing which all this becomes known?*
    - तेनैव सर्वं ज्ञास्यसि answers this question: there is one knowledge that includes all knowledge.
- **रामक्रम — Rāma's path.** Not a set of rules, but a life told in sequence. The story itself is the teaching.

## Verse 1.3.18 { #1-3-18 }

<p class="shloka">
विद्यागृहाद्विनिष्क्रम्य रामो राजीवलोचनः ।<br>
दिवसान्यनयद्गेहे लीलाभिरकुतोभयः ॥ १.३.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राजीव-लोचनः रामः (lotus-eyed Rāma) विद्या-गृहात् विनिष्क्रम्य (having left the house of learning) अकुतः-भयः (fearing nothing from anywhere) गेहे (at home) लीलाभिः (in play) दिवसान् अनयत् (spent his days).
</p>

**Translation:** "Having finished his studies, lotus-eyed Rāma spent his days at home in play, without a fear in the world."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The story begins.** After seventeen verses of teaching, Vālmīki turns to Rāma's life. Like Kāruṇya (1.1.10), Rāma has just returned from his teacher's house.
- **Fearless — but not yet free.** Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.9 describes the fearlessness of the knower: <span class="parallel">आनन्दं ब्रह्मणो विद्वान् न बिभेति कुतश्चन</span> — *knowing the bliss of Brahman, one fears nothing from anywhere.* Rāma's youthful अकुतोभय is a carefree innocence, not yet that knowledge. The crisis to come will turn the first fearlessness into the second.
- **लीला.** Play. Rāma's life begins in innocent play; the Yoga Vasiṣṭha will later describe the whole world as consciousness at play.

## Verse 1.3.19 { #1-3-19 }

<p class="shloka">
अथ गच्छति काले तु पालयत्यवनिं नृपे ।<br>
प्रजासु वीतशोकासु स्थितासु विगतज्वरम् ॥ १.३.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) काले गच्छति तु (as time passed), नृपे अवनिं पालयति [सति] (while the king ruled the earth), प्रजासु वीत-शोकासु (the people being free of sorrow) विगत-ज्वरं स्थितासु (and living without distress) …
</p>

**Translation:** "As time went on, while the king ruled the earth and his people lived free of sorrow and distress …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A kingdom at peace.** The setting is not one of outward suffering. Rāma's coming crisis will not be caused by hardship; it will arise in the midst of comfort. This makes it all the more a question about life itself.
- **The sentence continues** into the next verse.

## Verse 1.3.20 { #1-3-20 }

<p class="shloka">
तीर्थपुण्याश्रमश्रेणीर्द्रष्टुमुत्कण्ठितं मनः ।<br>
रामस्याभूद्भृशं तत्र कदाचिद्गुणशालिनः ॥ १.३.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र (then) कदाचित् (one day) गुण-शालिनः रामस्य मनः (the mind of virtuous Rāma) तीर्थ-पुण्य-आश्रम-श्रेणीः (the many sacred places and holy hermitages) द्रष्टुं (to see) भृशम् उत्कण्ठितम् अभूत् (became deeply eager).
</p>

**Translation:** "… one day the mind of the virtuous Rāma became deeply eager to see the many sacred fords and holy hermitages."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The first stirring.** The longing to see holy places is the first sign of something beyond play. The seeker in Rāma begins as a pilgrim.
- **उत्कण्ठित.** Longing, yearning. Spiritual life in this text begins not with a doctrine but with a longing.

## Verse 1.3.21 { #1-3-21 }

<p class="shloka">
राघवश्चिन्तयित्वैवमुपेत्य चरणौ पितुः ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राघवः (Rāghava) एवं चिन्तयित्वा (having thought thus) पितुः (his father's) नख-केसरौ (with nails like stamens) नवौ चरणौ (fresh [lotus-like] feet) उपेत्य (approaching), हंसः पद्मौ इव (as a swan [takes] two lotuses), जग्राह (clasped).
</p>

**Translation:** "Having thought this over, Rāma approached his father and clasped his feet — their toenails like stamens — as a swan takes hold of two fresh lotuses."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reverence first.** Before asking, Rāma bows. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2 instructs: <span class="parallel">पितृदेवो भव</span> — *treat your father as God.*
- **The swan.** The हंस is the classic image of discernment and of the liberated soul (परमहंस). The young Rāma is already pictured as a swan.

## Verse 1.3.22 { #1-3-22 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), नाथ (my lord), मम इदं मानसं (this mind of mine) तीर्थानि देव-सद्मानि (sacred fords, temples) वनानि आयतनानि च (forests and shrines) द्रष्टुम् उत्कण्ठितम् (is eager to see).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Dear father, my lord, my heart longs to see the sacred fords, the temples of the gods, the forests and the shrines."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Śrī Rāma's first words in the text.** He speaks of his mind (मानस) and its longing. The whole Yoga Vasiṣṭha will be about the mind; his first words already name it.
- **What he wants to see, and what he will see.** He sets out to see holy places. He will come back having seen something else: the emptiness of worldly life (Sarga 5 onward).

- **Respectful persuasion.** He appeals to his father's generosity, not to his own rights. The tone of the young seeker is humble even when he wants something.

## Verse 1.3.24 { #1-3-24 }

<p class="shloka">
ग्रामीणललनालोलहस्तपद्मापनोदितैः ।<br>
लाजवर्षैर्विकीर्णात्मा हिमैरिव हिमाचलः ॥ १.३.२९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ग्रामीण-ललना-लोल-हस्त-पद्म-अपनोदितैः (thrown by the restless lotus-hands of the village women) लाज-वर्षैः (by showers of parched grain) विकीर्ण-आत्मा (covered all over), हिमैः हिमाचलः इव (like the Himālaya with snow).
</p>

**Translation:** "… covered with showers of parched grain thrown by the lively, lotus-like hands of the village women, like the Himālaya covered with snow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **लाज, parched grain.** Showered for blessing at auspicious moments, as at weddings. Rāma is blessed by city and village alike.
- **Rāma as a mountain.** Compared to the Himālaya: steady, white with blessings. The image hints at the steadiness he will later reach.

## Verse 1.3.30 { #1-3-30 }

<p class="shloka">
आवर्जयन्विप्रगणान्परिशृण्वन्प्रजाशिषः ।<br>
आलोकयन्दिगन्तांश्च परिचक्राम जाङ्गलान् ॥ १.३.३० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विप्र-गणान् आवर्जयन् (honouring the groups of brāhmaṇas), प्रजा-आशिषः परिशृण्वन् (listening to the people's blessings), दिक्-अन्तान् च आलोकयन् (and gazing at the far horizons), जाङ्गलान् परिचक्राम (he travelled through the open country).
</p>

**Translation:** "Honouring the brāhmaṇas, listening to the blessings of the people, and gazing at the far horizons, he travelled through the open country."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Three things Rāma does:** he honours (आवर्जयन्), he listens (परिशृण्वन्), he looks (आलोकयन्). The pilgrim's attitude: respect, receptivity, attention.
- **दिगन्त — the far horizons.** His eyes are already on the distance. The journey widens his view.

## Verse 1.3.31 { #1-3-31 }

<p class="shloka">
अथारभ्य स्वकात्तस्मात्क्रमात्कोशलमण्डलात् ।<br>
स्नानदानतपोध्यानपूर्वकं स ददर्श ह ॥ १.३.३१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) तस्मात् स्वकात् कोशल-मण्डलात् आरभ्य (beginning from his own land of Kosala) क्रमात् (in order) सः स्नान-दान-तपः-ध्यान-पूर्वकं (with ritual bathing, giving, austerity and meditation) ददर्श ह (he visited) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Then, beginning from his own land of Kosala, he visited, one after another, with bathing, giving, austerity and meditation …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 18.5:**
    - <span class="parallel">यज्ञदानतपःकर्म न त्याज्यं कार्यमेव तत् । यज्ञो दानं तपश्चैव पावनानि मनीषिणाम् ॥</span>
    - *Acts of sacrifice, giving and austerity should not be given up; they should be done. Sacrifice, giving and austerity purify even the wise.*
    - Rāma's pilgrimage includes दान and तपस्, exactly the purifying acts the Gītā says should never be abandoned.
- **Not sightseeing.** Each place is visited with practice: bathing, giving, austerity, meditation. The outer journey is shaped as an inner discipline.
- **The sentence continues** into the next verse.

## Verse 1.3.32 { #1-3-32 }

<p class="shloka">

जङ्गलानि जनान्तेषु तटान्यब्धिमहीभृताम् ॥ १.३.३२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुण्यानि नदी-तीराणि (holy river banks), वनानि आयतनानि च (forests and shrines), जन-अन्तेषु जङ्गलानि (wildernesses at the edges of settlement), अब्धि-महीभृतां तटानि (shores of oceans and slopes of mountains).
</p>

**Translation:** "… holy river banks, forests and shrines, wild lands at the edges of human settlement, the shores of the seas and the slopes of mountains."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The whole of the land.** Verses 32–40 name rivers, lakes, cities and hermitages across the Indian subcontinent. The list maps a sacred geography.

## Verse 1.3.33 { #1-3-33 }

<p class="shloka">
मन्दाकिनीमिन्दुनिभां कालिन्दीं चोत्पलामलाम् ।<br>
सरस्वतीं शतद्रूं च चन्द्रभागामिरावतीम् ॥ १.३.३३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इन्दु-निभां मन्दाकिनीम् (the moon-bright Mandākinī), उत्पल-अमलां कालिन्दीं च (and the Kālindī, pure as a lotus), सरस्वतीं शतद्रूं च (the Sarasvatī and the Śatadrū), चन्द्रभागाम् इरावतीम् (the Candrabhāgā and the Irāvatī) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… the Mandākinī, bright as the moon; the Kālindī (Yamunā), clear as a lotus; the Sarasvatī, the Śatadrū (Sutlej), the Candrabhāgā (Chenab) and the Irāvatī (Ravi) …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rivers of the north.** Several of these are the rivers of the Punjab. The text's geography stretches from the Himālaya to the far south.

## Verse 1.3.34 { #1-3-34 }

<p class="shloka">
वेणीं च कृष्णवेणीं च निर्विन्ध्यां सरयूं तथा ।<br>
चर्मण्वतीं वितस्तां च विपाशां बाहुदामपि ॥ १.३.३४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वेणीं च कृष्णवेणीं च (the Veṇī and the Kṛṣṇaveṇī), निर्विन्ध्यां तथा सरयूं (the Nirvindhyā and the Sarayū), चर्मण्वतीं वितस्तां च (the Carmaṇvatī and the Vitastā), विपाशां बाहुदाम् अपि (the Vipāśā and the Bāhudā too) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… the Veṇī and the Kṛṣṇaveṇī, the Nirvindhyā and the Sarayū, the Carmaṇvatī (Chambal), the Vitastā (Jhelum), the Vipāśā (Beas) and the Bāhudā …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The Sarayū.** The river of Rāma's own city, Ayodhyā, is placed among all the others. Home becomes one holy place among many.
- **The Vitastā (Jhelum)** flows through Kashmir, where the earliest version of this text, the Mokṣopāya, was composed (see 1.1.53).

## Verse 1.3.35 { #1-3-35 }

<p class="shloka">
प्रयागं नैमिषं चैव धर्मारण्यं गयां तथा ।<br>
वाराणसीं श्रीगिरिं च केदारं पुष्करं तथा ॥ १.३.३५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
प्रयागं नैमिषं च एव (Prayāga and Naimiṣa), धर्मारण्यं तथा गयां (Dharmāraṇya and Gayā), वाराणसीं श्रीगिरिं च (Vārāṇasī and Śrīgiri), केदारं तथा पुष्करम् (Kedāra and Puṣkara) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… Prayāga and the Naimiṣa forest, Dharmāraṇya and Gayā, Vārāṇasī and Śrīgiri, Kedāra and Puṣkara …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Naimiṣa.** The forest where, by tradition, the sages gathered to hear the Purāṇas. Rāma passes through the very place where sacred stories are told.
- **Gayā.** Sacred to both Hindus and Buddhists — a small echo of the text's synthesis.

## Verse 1.3.36 { #1-3-36 }

<p class="shloka">
मानसं च क्रमसरस्तथैवोत्तरमानसम् ।<br>
वडवावदनं चैव तीर्थवृन्दं स सादरम् ॥ १.३.३६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सः (he) सादरं (with reverence) मानसं च क्रम-सरः (Lake Mānasa and Krama lake), तथा एव उत्तर-मानसम् (and Uttaramānasa), वडवा-वदनं च एव (and Vaḍavāmukha) तीर्थ-वृन्दं (a host of sacred places) [ददर्श] (visited) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… with reverence he visited Lake Mānasa, the Krama lake and Uttaramānasa, Vaḍavāmukha, and a host of other sacred places …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **मानस — lake and mind.** The sacred Lake Mānasa bears the same name as the mind (मानस) Rāma spoke of in 1.3.22. Perhaps a quiet pun: the pilgrim visits the outer मानस before learning to see the inner one.
- **सादरम्.** Every visit is made with reverence. Attitude matters more than the list.

## Verse 1.3.37 { #1-3-37 }

<p class="shloka">
अग्नितीर्थं महातीर्थमिन्द्रद्युम्नसरस्तथा ।<br>
सरांसि सरितश्चैव तथा नदह्रदावलीम् ॥ १.३.३७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अग्नि-तीर्थं महा-तीर्थम् (Agnitīrtha and Mahātīrtha), तथा इन्द्रद्युम्न-सरः (and the lake of Indradyumna), सरांसि सरितः च एव (lakes and rivers), तथा नद-ह्रद-आवलीम् (and rows of streams and pools) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… Agnitīrtha and Mahātīrtha, the lake of Indradyumna, and many other lakes and rivers, streams and pools …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Water everywhere.** Most of the holy places named are waters: rivers, lakes, fords (तीर्थ, literally a crossing place). Recall Sarga 2: the teaching is a ship or bridge across the ocean of existence. The pilgrim visits the outer crossings first.

## Verse 1.3.38 { #1-3-38 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वामिनं कार्तिकेयं च शालग्रामं हरिं तथा ।<br>
स्थानानि च चतुःषष्टिं हरेरथ हरस्य च ॥ १.३.३८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्वामिनं कार्तिकेयं च (the Lord Kārttikeya), शालग्रामं तथा हरिम् (Śālagrāma and Hari), अथ हरेः हरस्य च चतुः-षष्टिं स्थानानि (and the sixty-four sacred sites of Hari and of Hara) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… the shrine of Lord Kārttikeya, Śālagrāma and Hari, and the sixty-four sacred sites of Viṣṇu and of Śiva …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kārttikeya.** The god also called Skanda and Śarajanman — the form Sanatkumāra took because of Viṣṇu's curse (1.1.59). Rāma, who is Viṣṇu, visits the shrine of the one he once cursed.
- **Hari and Hara together.** Viṣṇu's and Śiva's shrines are named side by side. The text honours both, as later it draws on Kashmir Śaivism too (your third reason).

## Verse 1.3.39 { #1-3-39 }

<p class="shloka">
नानाश्चर्यविचित्राणि चतुरब्धितटानि च ।<br>
विन्ध्यमन्दरकुञ्जांश्च कुलशैलस्थलानि च ॥ १.३.३९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नाना-आश्चर्य-विचित्राणि (full of many wonders) चतुः-अब्धि-तटानि च (and the shores of the four seas), विन्ध्य-मन्दर-कुञ्जान् च (the groves of Vindhya and Mandara), कुल-शैल-स्थलानि च (and the regions of the great mountain ranges) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… the wondrous shores of the four seas, the groves of the Vindhya and Mandara mountains, and the lands of the great mountain ranges …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **To the edges of the world.** The four seas mark the limits of the known earth. Rāma sees everything the world has to offer — and will soon conclude that none of it satisfies.

## Verse 1.3.40 { #1-3-40 }

<p class="shloka">
राजर्षीणां च महतां ब्रह्मर्षीणां तथैव च ।<br>
देवानां ब्राह्मणानां च पावनानाश्रमाञ्छुभान् ॥ १.३.४० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
महतां राजर्षीणां च (of the great royal sages) तथा एव ब्रह्मर्षीणां च (and of the brāhmaṇa sages), देवानां ब्राह्मणानां च (of gods and of brāhmaṇas) पावनान् शुभान् आश्रमान् (the purifying, auspicious hermitages) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… and the purifying, auspicious hermitages of great royal sages and brāhmaṇa sages, of gods and of brāhmaṇas."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **राजर्षि before ब्रह्मर्षि.** The royal sages are named first. Rāma, a future king, visits those who combined rule and wisdom — the ideal he will embody (compare Gītā 4.2, quoted at 1.1.23).
- **Reading note.** The online text prints ब्राह्मणानां चे; the reading ब्राह्मणानां च, which fits the metre, is used here.

## Verse 1.3.41 { #1-3-41 }

<p class="shloka">
भूयोभूयः स बभ्राम भ्रातृभ्यां सह मानदः ।<br>
चतुर्ष्वपि दिगन्तेषु सर्वानेव महीतटान् ॥ १.३.४१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मान-दः (he who gives honour to others) सः (he) भ्रातृभ्यां सह (with his two brothers) चतुर्षु अपि दिक्-अन्तेषु (in all four directions) सर्वान् एव मही-तटान् (all the lands of the earth) भूयः भूयः बभ्राम (wandered again and again).
</p>

**Translation:** "Again and again he wandered with his brothers through all the lands of the earth in every direction, giving honour wherever he went."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **भूयोभूयः, babhrāma.** "Again and again he wandered." The verb भ्रम् means to wander, but its noun भ्रम also means illusion (1.3.2: जगद्भ्रम). Rāma wanders through the world-illusion before seeing through it.
- **मानद — giver of honour.** Even as a prince on pilgrimage, Rāma honours others rather than seeking honour.

## Verse 1.3.42 { #1-3-42 }

<p class="shloka">
अमरकिन्नरमानवमानितः समवलोक्य महीमखिलामिमाम् ।<br>
उपययौ स्वगृहं रघुनन्दनो विहृतदिक् शिवलोकमिवेश्वरः ॥ १.३.४२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अमर-किन्नर-मानव-मानितः (honoured by gods, kinnaras and humans), इमाम् अखिलां महीं समवलोक्य (having seen this whole earth), विहृत-दिक् (having roamed all directions), रघुनन्दनः (the delight of the Raghus) स्व-गृहम् उपययौ (returned home), ईश्वरः शिवलोकम् इव (as the Lord to his own world of Śiva).
</p>

**Translation:** Honoured by gods, kinnaras and humans, having seen the whole earth and roamed every direction, the delight of the Raghus returned home, as the Lord returns to his own heaven.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A change of metre.** As in Sarga 2, the sarga closes in a longer metre (here drutavilambita), signalling the end of the chapter.
- **ईश्वरः शिवलोकमिव.** He returns home like the Lord returning to his own realm. Outwardly a happy homecoming. But Sargas 4–5 show that the one who returns is not the one who left.
- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.2.12 again:** <span class="parallel">परीक्ष्य लोकान्कर्मचितान्ब्राह्मणो निर्वेदमायात्</span> — *having examined the worlds won by action, a seeker turns to dispassion.* Rāma has just examined the whole world. Dispassion (वैराग्य) is about to arrive.
- **A question to sit with.** Have I had a journey after which home looked different?
