# Sarga 32

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · The rain of flowers (27 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 32 at a glance"
    Śrī Vālmīki tells how heaven and earth responded to Rāma's speech.

    1. Everyone who heard — sages, ministers, kings, citizens, servants, the queens at their windows, even caged birds and horses — fell still, filled with wonder and, for a moment, with dispassion (1.32.1–1.32.11).
    2. When Rāma fell silent, a rain of flowers fell from a cloudless sky, sent by the hosts of siddhas (1.32.12–1.32.22).
    3. The siddhas declared that in a whole aeon they had never heard words like these, and that Rāma's words had awakened them (1.32.23–1.32.27).

## Verse 1.32.1 { #1-32-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
वदत्येवं मनोमोहविनिवृत्तिकरं वचः ।<br>
रामे राजीवपत्राक्षे तस्मिन्राजकुमारके ॥ १.३२.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राजीव-पत्र-अक्षे तस्मिन् राज-कुमारके रामे (when that young prince Rāma, with eyes like lotus petals) एवं (thus) मनः-मोह-विनिवृत्ति-करं वचः वदति [सति] (spoke words that end the mind's delusion) …
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vālmīki said: "When the lotus-eyed young prince Rāma had spoken these words, which end the delusion of the mind …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The response.** Sarga 32 describes the effect of Rāma's speech on everyone who heard it, from the sages to the caged birds, and ends with a rain of flowers from heaven.
- **Words that end delusion.** Vālmīki says Rāma's very questions are healing. A question rightly asked is half the answer ([questions worth asking](../index.md#questions-worth-asking)).

## Verse 1.32.2 { #1-32-2 }

<p class="shloka">
सर्वे बभूवुस्तत्रस्था विस्मयोत्फुल्ललोचनाः ।<br>
भिन्नाम्बरा देहरुहैर्गिरः श्रोतुमिवोद्धुरैः ॥ १.३२.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र-स्थाः सर्वे (all who were there) विस्मय-उत्फुल्ल-लोचनाः (with eyes wide in wonder) गिरः श्रोतुम् इव उद्धुरैः देह-रुहैः (with their hair standing up as if to hear the words) भिन्न-अम्बराः (their clothes [as if] split) बभूवुः (were).
</p>

**Translation:** "… all who were there sat with eyes wide in wonder, their hair standing on end as if to hear the words, so that it seemed to split their clothes."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hair standing on end** (रोमाञ्च) — a sign of deep spiritual emotion. Arjuna too, seeing the cosmic form, was <span class="parallel">हृष्टरोमा</span> (Gītā 11.14), *his hair standing on end.*

## Verse 1.32.3 { #1-32-3 }

<p class="shloka">
विरागवासनापास्तसमस्तभववासनाः ।<br>
मुहूर्तममृताम्भोधिवीचीविलुलिता इव ॥ १.३२.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विराग-वासना-अपास्त-समस्त-भव-वासनाः (all their worldly tendencies removed by the tendency to dispassion) मुहूर्तम् (for a moment) अमृत-अम्भोधि-वीची-विलुलिताः इव (as if tossed by the waves of an ocean of nectar) [बभूवुः].
</p>

**Translation:** "For a moment, all their worldly tendencies were swept away by the pull towards dispassion, and they were as if tossed on the waves of an ocean of nectar."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The pure vāsanā** (1.3.11). Hearing Rāma, the listeners' own impure tendencies gave way for a moment to the pure tendency of dispassion. Good words change the hearer.
- **For a moment.** Not yet permanent. The teaching is needed to make it lasting.

## Verse 1.32.4 { #1-32-4 }

<p class="shloka">
ता गिरो रामभद्रस्य तस्य चित्रार्पितैरिव ।<br>
संश्रुताः शृणुकैरन्तरानन्दपदपीवरैः ॥ १.३२.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तस्य रामभद्रस्य ताः गिरः (those words of noble Rāma) चित्र-अर्पितैः इव (as if painted in a picture) अन्तर-आनन्द-पद-पीवरैः शृणुकैः (by listeners swelling with inner joy) संश्रुताः (were heard) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Those words of noble Rāma were heard by listeners who sat still as figures in a painting, full of inner joy …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints श्रृणुकैः; corrected here to शृणुकैः.
- **Still as a painting** — the image of 1.31.24 now describes the listeners. Rāma's stillness has spread to the assembly. The list of listeners runs on to 1.32.11.

## Verse 1.32.5 { #1-32-5 }

<p class="shloka">
वसिष्ठविश्वामित्राद्यैर्मुनिभिः संसदि स्थितैः ।<br>
जयन्तधृष्टिप्रमुखैर्मन्त्रिभिर्मन्त्रकोविदैः ॥ १.३२.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
संसदि स्थितैः (present in the assembly) वसिष्ठ-विश्वामित्र-आद्यैः मुनिभिः (by sages led by Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra) मन्त्र-कोविदैः जयन्त-धृष्टि-प्रमुखैः मन्त्रिभिः (by counsellors expert in policy, led by Jayanta and Dhṛṣṭi) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… by the sages in the assembly, led by Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra; by the counsellors skilled in policy, led by Jayanta and Dhṛṣṭi …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Jayanta and Dhṛṣṭi** are among Daśaratha's eight ministers in Vālmīki's Rāmāyaṇa.

## Verse 1.32.6 { #1-32-6 }

<p class="shloka">
नृपैर्दशरथप्रख्यैः पौरैः पारशवादिभिः ।<br>
सामन्तै राजपुत्रैश्च ब्राह्मणैर्ब्रह्मवादिभिः ॥ १.३२.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दशरथ-प्रख्यैः नृपैः (by kings like Daśaratha) पारशव-आदिभिः पौरैः (by citizens, including those of mixed birth) सामन्तैः राज-पुत्रैः च (by vassals and princes) ब्रह्म-वादिभिः ब्राह्मणैः (by brāhmaṇas who speak of Brahman) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… by kings like Daśaratha; by citizens of every class; by vassal lords and princes; by brāhmaṇas who teach of Brahman …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Every class of society** listened. The teaching is for all.

## Verse 1.32.7 { #1-32-7 }

<p class="shloka">
तथा भृत्यैरमात्यैश्च पञ्जरस्थैश्च पक्षिभिः ।<br>
क्रीडामृगैर्गतस्पन्दैस्तुरंगैस्त्यक्तचर्वणैः ॥ १.३२.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तथा (likewise) भृत्यैः अमात्यैः च (by servants and ministers) पञ्जर-स्थैः पक्षिभिः च (by birds in their cages) गत-स्पन्दैः क्रीडा-मृगैः (by pet deer, now motionless) त्यक्त-चर्वणैः तुरङ्गैः (by horses that had stopped chewing) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… by servants and ministers; by birds in their cages; by pet deer, now motionless; by horses that had stopped chewing …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints भृत्यैऽरमात्यैः; corrected here to भृत्यैरमात्यैः.
- **Even the animals.** A lovely detail: the horses stop chewing, the deer stand still. Rāma's words touch all beings — like Kṛṣṇa's flute in the Bhāgavata, which made even animals stand still.

## Verse 1.32.8 { #1-32-8 }

<p class="shloka">
कौसल्याप्रमुखैश्चैव निजवातायनस्थितैः ।<br>
संशान्तभूषणारावैरस्पन्दैर्वनितागणैः ॥ १.३२.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
निज-वातायन-स्थितैः (standing at their own windows) संशान्त-भूषण-आरावैः (the jingling of their ornaments hushed) अस्पन्दैः (motionless) कौसल्या-प्रमुखैः वनिता-गणैः च एव (and by the women, led by Kausalyā) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… and by the women of the palace, led by Kausalyā, standing motionless at their windows, the jingling of their ornaments hushed …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kausalyā** — Rāma's mother. The women's ornaments fall silent; even the smallest sounds stop.

## Verse 1.32.9 { #1-32-9 }

<p class="shloka">
उद्यानवल्लीनिलयैर्विटङ्कनिलयैरपि ।<br>
अक्षुब्धपक्षततिभिर्विहङ्गैर्विरतारवैः ॥ १.३२.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
उद्यान-वल्ली-निलयैः (living in the garden creepers) विटङ्क-निलयैः अपि (and in the eaves) अक्षुब्ध-पक्ष-ततिभिः (their wings unmoving) विरत-आरवैः (their calls stopped) विहङ्गैः (by the birds) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… by the birds that live in the garden vines and under the eaves, their wings still, their songs stopped …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Nature listens.** The whole palace falls silent.

## Verse 1.32.10 { #1-32-10 }

<p class="shloka">
सिद्धैर्नभश्चरैश्चैव तथा गन्धर्वकिन्नरैः ।<br>
नारदव्यासपुलहप्रमुखैर्मुनिपुङ्गवैः ॥ १.३२.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सिद्धैः नभः-चरैः च एव (by the perfected beings and sky-dwellers) तथा गन्धर्व-किन्नरैः (and by gandharvas and kinnaras) नारद-व्यास-पुलह-प्रमुखैः मुनि-पुङ्गवैः (by the great sages, led by Nārada, Vyāsa and Pulaha) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… by the siddhas and other beings of the sky, by gandharvas and kinnaras, and by the great sages led by Nārada, Vyāsa and Pulaha …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Heaven listens too.** Beings of the sky gather to hear Rāma. Vyāsa — the compiler of the Mahābhārata and the Gītā — is among them.

## Verse 1.32.11 { #1-32-11 }

<p class="shloka">
अन्यैश्च देवदेवेशविद्याधरमहोरगैः ।<br>
रामस्य ता विचित्रार्था महोदारा गिरः श्रुताः ॥ १.३२.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अन्यैः च (and by others) देव-देवेश-विद्याधर-महा-उरगैः (gods, lords of gods, vidyādharas and great serpents) रामस्य ताः विचित्र-अर्थाः महा-उदाराः गिरः (those noble words of Rāma, rich in meaning) श्रुताः (were heard).
</p>

**Translation:** "… and by others too — gods and their lords, vidyādharas and great serpents. All of them heard those noble words of Rāma, so rich in meaning."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The three worlds listen.** Earth, heaven and the underworld (serpents) — all hear Rāma's questions.

## Verse 1.32.12 { #1-32-12 }

<p class="shloka">
अथ तूष्णीं स्थितवति रामे राजीवलोचने ।<br>
तस्मिन्रघुकुलाकाशशशाङ्के शशिसुन्दरे ॥ १.३२.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) रघु-कुल-आकाश-शशाङ्के (the moon in the sky of the Raghu line) शशि-सुन्दरे (lovely as the moon) तस्मिन् राजीव-लोचने रामे (when that lotus-eyed Rāma) तूष्णीं स्थितवति [सति] (had fallen silent) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Then, when lotus-eyed Rāma, lovely as the moon, the moon in the sky of the Raghu line, had fallen silent …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Silence before the answer.** The sentence continues: heaven responds.

## Verse 1.32.13 { #1-32-13 }

<p class="shloka">
साधुवादगिरा सार्धं सिद्धसार्थसमीरिता ।<br>
वितानकसमा व्योम्नः पौष्पी वृष्टिः पपात ह ॥ १.३२.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
साधु-वाद-गिरा सार्धं (together with cries of 'well said!') सिद्ध-सार्थ-समीरिता (sent forth by the hosts of siddhas) वितानक-समा (like a canopy) पौष्पी वृष्टिः (a rain of flowers) व्योम्नः पपात ह (fell from the sky).
</p>

**Translation:** "… a rain of flowers, sent by the hosts of siddhas with cries of 'Well said!', fell from the sky like a canopy."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **पुष्पवृष्टि — a rain of flowers.** In Indian epic, heaven's sign of approval at a great moment. Here it falls not for a victory in battle, but for a young man's questions about life.

## Verse 1.32.14 { #1-32-14 }

<p class="shloka">
मन्दारकोशविश्रान्तभ्रमरद्वन्द्वनादिनी ।<br>
मधुरामोदसौन्दर्यमुदितोन्मदमानवा ॥ १.३२.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा वृष्टिः] मन्दार-कोश-विश्रान्त-भ्रमर-द्वन्द्व-नादिनी (humming with pairs of bees resting in mandāra blossoms) मधुर-आमोद-सौन्दर्य-मुदित-उन्मद-मानवा (delighting and intoxicating people with the beauty of its sweet fragrance) …
</p>

**Translation:** "The shower hummed with pairs of bees resting in the mandāra blossoms, and its sweet fragrance and beauty filled people with joy …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Mandāra** — one of the five trees of Indra's heaven. The flowers come from paradise. The description runs on (1.32.14–1.32.22).

## Verse 1.32.15 { #1-32-15 }

<p class="shloka">
व्योमवातविनुन्नेव तारकाणां परम्परा ।<br>
पतितेव धरापीठे स्वर्गस्त्रीहसितच्छटा ॥ १.३२.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] व्योम-वात-विनुन्ना तारकाणां परम्परा इव (like a stream of stars blown by the winds of the sky) धरा-पीठे पतिता स्वर्ग-स्त्री-हसित-च्छटा इव (like the laughter of heavenly women fallen on the earth) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… like a stream of stars blown down by the sky's winds, like the laughter of the women of heaven fallen upon the earth …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Laughter as white flowers.** In Indian poetics, laughter is imagined as white. The white flowers are heaven's laughter of joy.

<p class="shloka">
वृष्यमूककचन्मेघलवावलिरिव च्युता ।<br>

## Verse 1.32.21 { #1-32-21 }

<p class="shloka">
निरभ्रोत्पलसंकाशव्योमवृष्टिरनाकुला ।<br>
अदृष्टपूर्वा सर्वस्य जनस्य जनितस्मया ॥ १.३२.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] निरभ्र-उत्पल-संकाश-व्योम-वृष्टिः (a rain from a sky clear as a blue lotus) अनाकुला (gentle) अदृष्ट-पूर्वा (never seen before) सर्वस्य जनस्य जनित-स्मया (filling all the people with wonder) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… a gentle rain from a cloudless sky, blue as a lotus, never seen before, filling all the people with wonder …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **From a cloudless sky.** A sign that it came not from nature but from heaven.

## Verse 1.32.22 { #1-32-22 }

<p class="shloka">
अदृश्याम्बरसिद्धौघकरोत्करसमीरिता ।<br>
सा मुहूर्तचतुर्भागं पुष्पवृष्टिः पपात ह ॥ १.३२.२२ ॥

<p class="anvaya">
अदृश्य-अम्बर-सिद्ध-ओघ-कर-उत्कर-समीरिता (thrown by the many hands of hosts of siddhas invisible in the sky) सा पुष्प-वृष्टिः (that rain of flowers) मुहूर्त-चतुर्-भागं पपात ह (fell for a quarter of a muhūrta).

**Translation:** "That rain of flowers, thrown by the many hands of siddhas hidden in the sky, fell for a quarter of a muhūrta."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A quarter of a muhūrta** — about twelve minutes. The heavenly response is real and brief.

## Verse 1.32.23 { #1-32-23 }

<p class="shloka">
आपूरितसभालोके शान्ते कुसुमवर्षणे ।<br>
इमं सिद्धगणालापं शुश्रुवुस्ते सभागताः ॥ १.३२.२३ ॥

<p class="anvaya">

## Verse 1.32.24 { #1-32-24 }

<p class="shloka">
आकल्पं सिद्धसेनासु भ्रमद्भिरभितोदिवम् ।<br>
अपूर्वमिदमस्माभिः श्रुतं श्रुतिरसायनम् ॥ १.३२.२४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आकल्पं (for a whole kalpa) सिद्ध-सेनासु (among the armies of siddhas) अभितः दिवं भ्रमद्भिः (wandering all over heaven) अस्माभिः (by us) इदम् अपूर्वं श्रुति-रसायनं (this unprecedented elixir for the ears) श्रुतम् (has been heard).
</p>

**Translation:** "'In a whole aeon of wandering through the heavens with the hosts of siddhas, we have never heard anything like this — an elixir for the ears!'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Unprecedented.** Even beings who have heard everything for an aeon have never heard questions like these. The rarity of the true question.

## Verse 1.32.25 { #1-32-25 }

<p class="shloka">
यदनेन किलोदारमुक्तं रघुकुलेन्दुना ।<br>
वीतरागतया तद्धि वाक्पतेरप्यगोचरम् ॥ १.३२.२५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनेन रघु-कुल-इन्दुना (by this moon of the Raghu line) वीत-रागतया (out of freedom from passion) यत् उदारम् उक्तं किल (the noble words that were spoken), तत् हि (that indeed) वाक्पतेः अपि अगोचरम् (is beyond even the lord of speech).
</p>

**Translation:** "'What this moon of the Raghus has spoken, from his freedom from passion, is beyond the reach even of Bṛhaspati, the lord of speech.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **वीतराग — free from passion.** The siddhas recognise that Rāma's words come not from cleverness but from dispassion. That is why even Bṛhaspati, the teacher of the gods, could not have spoken them.

## Verse 1.32.26 { #1-32-26 }

<p class="shloka">
अहो बत महत्पुण्यमद्यास्माभिरिदं श्रुतम् ।<br>
वचो राममुखोद्भूतं महाह्लादकरं धियः ॥ १.३२.२६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अहो बत (oh!) अद्य (today) अस्माभिः (by us) महत् पुण्यम् (a great merit) इदं राम-मुख-उद्भूतं धियः महा-आह्लाद-करं वचः श्रुतम् (these words from Rāma's mouth, which greatly delight the mind, have been heard).
</p>

**Translation:** "'Oh, what great merit is ours today, to have heard these words from Rāma's own mouth, which so delight the mind!'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hearing as merit.** Hearing true words (श्रवण) is itself the first step of the path — before reflection (मनन) and contemplation (निदिध्यासन), Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.4.5.

## Verse 1.32.27 { #1-32-27 }

<p class="shloka">
उपशमामृतसुन्दरमादरादधिगतोत्तमतापदमेष यत् ।<br>
कथितवानुचितं रघुनन्दनः सपदि तेन वयं प्रतिबोधिताः ॥ १.३२.२७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एषः रघु-नन्दनः (this delight of the Raghus) यत् (what) उपशम-अमृत-सुन्दरम् (lovely with the nectar of peace) आदरात् अधिगत-उत्तमता-पदम् (reached with care, the highest state) उचितं (fittingly) कथितवान् (has spoken), तेन (by that) वयं सपदि प्रतिबोधिताः (we have at once been awakened).
</p>

**Translation:** "'Rāma, delight of the Raghus, has spoken fittingly, with care, words lovely with the nectar of peace, that lead to the highest state — and by them we have been awakened at once.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints आदरा दधिगतो; corrected here to आदरादधिगतो (word division).
- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (drutavilambita).
- **The student awakens the listeners.** A remarkable reversal: Rāma has not yet received the teaching, yet his questions have already awakened the siddhas. The Lord as student is still the Lord ([the Lord as student](../index.md#the-lord-as-student)).
- **उपशम — peace.** The word of the very first verse (1.1.1) returns at the close of Rāma's speech.
