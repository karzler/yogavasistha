# Sarga 17

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · On craving (tṛṣṇā) (52 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 17 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: on craving (tṛṣṇā), the longest of his laments.

    1. Craving darkens the heart, dries it up, and gnaws through every resolve (1.17.1–1.17.15).
    2. A long garland of images: craving as dark night, serpent, demoness, broken lute, old dancer, monkey, bee, rainbow, moonlight on the lily of sorrow (1.17.16–1.17.40).
    3. Craving as a disease, and its cure: "the giving up of longing is the healing mantra" (1.17.41–1.17.46).
    4. The wise cut craving with the sword of discernment; yet craving pervades the world like sweetness in milk (1.17.47–1.17.52).

## Verse 1.17.1 { #1-17-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
हार्दान्धकारशर्वर्या तृष्णयेह दुरन्तया ।<br>
स्फुरन्ति चेतनाकाशे दोषकौशिकपङ्कतयः ॥ १.१७.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह (here) हार्द-अन्धकार-शर्वर्या (by the night of the heart's darkness) दुर्-अन्तया तृष्णया (by craving, hard to end) चेतना-आकाशे (in the sky of consciousness) दोष-कौशिक-पङ्क्तयः (rows of owls, the faults) स्फुरन्ति (flit about).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Here craving, endless, is the dark night of the heart; in it, flocks of owls — our faults — flit about in the sky of consciousness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **तृष्णा — craving, literally "thirst".** The subject of this sarga, the longest so far. Sarga 15 called craving the flower of the ego's cloud (1.15.12); Sarga 16 called it the mind's wife (1.16.15). Now Rāma looks at her directly.
- **Owls in the night.** Owls are active only in darkness. Faults flourish only where craving has darkened the heart.
- **A synthesis with Buddhism** (your third reason). In the Buddha's second noble truth, तण्हा (Pāli for तृष्णा) is the cause of suffering. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha shares the diagnosis, and gives it an Advaitic cure: not only the ending of craving, but the knowing of the Self in whom craving never arose.

## Verse 1.17.2 { #1-17-2 }

<p class="shloka">
अन्तर्दाहप्रदायिन्या समूढरसमार्दवः ।<br>
पङ्क आदित्यदीप्त्येव शोषं नीतोऽस्मि चिन्तया ॥ १.१७.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अन्तः-दाह-प्रदायिन्या चिन्तया (by anxious longing that burns within) [अहं] समूढ-रस-मार्दवः (with my sap and softness gone) आदित्य-दीप्त्या पङ्कः इव (like mud by the sun's heat) शोषं नीतः अस्मि (have been dried up).
</p>

**Translation:** "Anxious longing, burning within, has dried me up — my sap and softness gone — as the sun's heat dries mud."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dried up.** Craving is not only painful but desiccating. It takes away the softness of the heart.

## Verse 1.17.3 { #1-17-3 }

<p class="shloka">
मम चित्तमहारण्ये व्यामोहतिमिराकुले ।<br>
शून्ये ताण्डविनी जाता भृशमाशापिशाचिका ॥ १.१७.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
व्यामोह-तिमिर-आकुले (filled with the darkness of delusion) शून्ये (empty) मम चित्त-महा-अरण्ये (in the great forest of my mind) आशा-पिशाचिका (the she-demon of hope) भृशं ताण्डविनी जाता (has begun to dance wildly).
</p>

**Translation:** "In the great forest of my mind, dark with delusion and empty, the she-demon of hope dances wildly."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints चित्तमहारण्येव्यामोह; corrected here to चित्तमहारण्ये व्यामोह, a space restored.
- **आशा — hope, expectation.** In the Yoga Vasiṣṭha, hope is not a virtue but a form of craving: always reaching for what is not here.
- **Bhāgavata 11.8.44** (Piṅgalā's song): <span class="parallel">आशा हि परमं दुःखं नैराश्यं परमं सुखम्</span> — *hope is the greatest sorrow; freedom from hope is the greatest happiness.*

## Verse 1.17.4 { #1-17-4 }

<p class="shloka">
वचोरचितनीहारा काञ्चनोपवनोज्ज्वला ।<br>
नूनं विकासमायाति चिन्ताचणकमञ्जरी ॥ १.१७.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वचः-रचित-नीहारा (with dew made of words) काञ्चन-उपवन-उज्ज्वला (bright as a golden garden) चिन्ता-चणक-मञ्जरी (the cluster of chick-pea blossoms of anxious longing) नूनं विकासम् आयाति (surely comes into bloom).
</p>

**Translation:** "Anxious longing blossoms like a cluster of chick-pea flowers, bright as a golden garden, with dew made of words."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dew made of words.** Longing is nourished by talk — by the stories we tell ourselves about what we want.

## Verse 1.17.5 { #1-17-5 }

<p class="shloka">
अलमन्तर्भ्रमायैव तृष्णातरलिताशया ।<br>
आयाता विषमोल्लासमूर्मिरम्बुनिधाविव ॥ १.१७.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा-तरलित-आशया (whose intent is made restless by craving) [चिन्तया] (by longing) अन्तः-भ्रमाय एव अलम् (enough only for inner confusion) अम्बुनिधौ ऊर्मिः इव (like a wave on the ocean) विषम-उल्लासम् आयाता (it rises in uneven excitement).
</p>

**Translation:** "Shaken by craving, longing serves only to confuse me within; it rises in uneven excitement, like a wave on the ocean."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Excitement that confuses.** The rise of desire feels like energy, but leaves only inner turmoil.

## Verse 1.17.6 { #1-17-6 }

<p class="shloka">
उद्दामकल्लोलरवा देहाद्रौ वहतीह मे ।<br>
तरङ्गतरलाकारा तरत्तृष्णातरङ्गिणी ॥ १.१७.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह मे देह-अद्रौ (here, on the mountain of my body) उद्दाम-कल्लोल-रवा (with the roar of unbridled waves) तरङ्ग-तरल-आकारा (with shape restless as ripples) तरत् तृष्णा-तरङ्गिणी (the swift river of craving) वहति (flows).
</p>

**Translation:** "On the mountain of my body flows the swift river of craving, roaring with unbridled waves, its form restless as ripples."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Alliteration.** तरङ्गतरलाकारा तरत्तृष्णातरङ्गिणी: the sound of the Sanskrit imitates the rushing river.

## Verse 1.17.7 { #1-17-7 }

<p class="shloka">
वेगं सरोद्धुमुदितो वात्ययेव जरत्तृणम् ।<br>
नीतः कलुषया क्वापि तृष्णया चित्तचातकः ॥ १.१७.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वेगं संरोद्धुम् उदितः (though risen to check its rush) चित्त-चातकः (the cātaka bird of my mind) कलुषया तृष्णया (by turbid craving) वात्यया जरत्-तृणम् इव (like old grass by a gale) क्व अपि नीतः (has been carried off somewhere).
</p>

**Translation:** "The cātaka bird of my mind rose up to check its rush, but turbid craving carried it off somewhere, like old grass in a gale."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The cātaka** is said to drink only raindrops. The mind, thirsting, is swept away by the very thirst it tried to resist.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints सरोद्धुम्; the sense requires संरोद्धुम्, to check. Retained as printed in the verse.

## Verse 1.17.8 { #1-17-8 }

<p class="shloka">
यां यामहमतीवास्थां संश्रयामि गुणश्रियाम् ।<br>
तां तां कृन्तति मे तृष्णा तन्त्रीमिव कुमूषिका ॥ १.१७.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
गुण-श्रियाम् (of the riches of virtue) यां यां आस्थाम् (whatever support) अहम् अतीव संश्रयामि (I hold to firmly), तां तां (that very one) मे तृष्णा (my craving) कु-मूषिका तन्त्रीम् इव (as a wicked mouse a lute-string) कृन्तति (gnaws through).
</p>

**Translation:** "Whatever support of virtue I hold firmly, craving gnaws it through, as a wicked mouse gnaws through the string of a lute."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Craving undermines virtue.** Every good resolve is quietly gnawed away. An honest admission familiar to every practitioner.
- **Gītā 2.60:** <span class="parallel">यततो ह्यपि कौन्तेय पुरुषस्य विपश्चितः । इन्द्रियाणि प्रमाथीनि हरन्ति प्रसभं मनः ॥</span> — *even of a wise person who strives, Kaunteya, the turbulent senses forcibly carry away the mind.*

## Verse 1.17.9 { #1-17-9 }

<p class="shloka">
पयसीव जरत्पर्णं वायाविव जरत्तृणम् ।<br>
नभसीव शरन्मेघश्चिन्ताचक्रे भ्रमाम्यहम् ॥ १.१७.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पयसि जरत्-पर्णम् इव (like an old leaf on water) वायौ जरत्-तृणम् इव (like old grass in the wind) नभसि शरत्-मेघः इव (like an autumn cloud in the sky) अहं चिन्ता-चक्रे भ्रमामि (I whirl on the wheel of longing).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a dry leaf on water, like old grass in the wind, like an autumn cloud in the sky, I whirl on the wheel of longing."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The wheel.** चक्र — the wheel of longing is a small model of the wheel of saṃsāra (Gītā 3.16, एवं प्रवर्तितं चक्रम्).

## Verse 1.17.10 { #1-17-10 }

<p class="shloka">
गन्तुमास्पदमात्मीयमसमर्थधियो वयम् ।<br>
चिन्ताजाले विमुह्यामो जाले शकुनयो यथा ॥ १.१७.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आत्मीयम् आस्पदं गन्तुम् (to reach our own true home) असमर्थ-धियः वयम् (we, whose minds are unable) चिन्ता-जाले (in the net of longing) जाले शकुनयः यथा (like birds in a net) विमुह्यामः (are bewildered).
</p>

**Translation:** "Unable to reach our own true home, we are bewildered in the net of longing, like birds caught in a net."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **आत्मीयम् आस्पदम् — our own home.** A beautiful phrase. The goal is not somewhere new, but our own place, the Self.
- **Gītā 15.6:** <span class="parallel">यद्गत्वा न निवर्तन्ते तद्धाम परमं मम</span> — *going there, they do not return: that is my supreme abode.*

## Verse 1.17.11 { #1-17-11 }

<p class="shloka">
तृष्णाभिधानया तात दग्धोऽस्मि ज्वालया तथा ।<br>
यथा दाहोपशमनमाशंके नामृतैरपि ॥ १.१७.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), तृष्णा-अभिधानया ज्वालया (by the flame named craving) तथा दग्धः अस्मि (I am so burned) यथा (that) अमृतैः अपि (even by nectar) दाह-उपशमनं न आशंके (I do not expect the burning to cool).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear father, I am so burned by the flame called craving that I do not think even nectar could cool it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Beyond ordinary remedies.** No external pleasure (nectar) can cure craving. Only knowledge can.
- **Gītā 3.39:** <span class="parallel">कामरूपेण कौन्तेय दुष्पूरेणानलेन च</span> — *in the form of desire, Kaunteya, an insatiable fire.*

## Verse 1.17.12 { #1-17-12 }

<p class="shloka">
दूरं दूरमितो गत्वा समेत्य च पुनःपुनः ।<br>
भ्रमत्याशु दिगन्तेषु तृष्णोन्मत्ता तुरङ्गमी ॥ १.१७.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा-उन्मत्ता तुरङ्गमी (the mare maddened by craving) इतः दूरं दूरं गत्वा (going far, far away from here) पुनः पुनः समेत्य च (and returning again and again) आशु दिक्-अन्तेषु भ्रमति (quickly roams to the ends of the directions).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving is a maddened mare, galloping far and farther, returning again and again, racing to the ends of the earth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The horse returns.** Craving runs away and always comes back. It cannot be outrun.

## Verse 1.17.13 { #1-17-13 }

<p class="shloka">
जडसंसर्गिणी तृष्णा कृतोर्ध्वाधोगमागमा ।<br>
क्षुब्धा ग्रन्थिमती नित्यमारघट्टाग्ररज्जुवत् ॥ १.१७.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जड-संसर्गिणी (in contact with dull things) कृत-ऊर्ध्व-अधः-गम-आगमा (going up and down) क्षुब्धा (agitated) ग्रन्थिमती (knotted) तृष्णा (craving) नित्यम् आरघट्ट-अग्र-रज्जु-वत् (always like the rope of a water-wheel).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving, clinging to dull things, always going up and down, agitated and knotted, is like the rope of a water-wheel."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The water-wheel.** The image of 1.16.19 returns: the endless up and down of rebirth.

## Verse 1.17.14 { #1-17-14 }

<p class="shloka">
अन्तर्ग्रथितया देहे सर्वदुश्छेदयाऽनया ।<br>
रज्ज्वेवाशु बलीवर्दस्तृष्णया वाह्यते जनः ॥ १.१७.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
देहे अन्तः ग्रथितया (knotted within the body) सर्व-दुः-छेदया (impossible to cut by anyone) अनया तृष्णया (by this craving) जनः (a person) रज्ज्वा बलीवर्दः इव (as an ox by a rope) आशु वाह्यते (is quickly driven).
</p>

**Translation:** "Knotted deep in the body and impossible to cut, craving drives a person as a rope drives an ox."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Led by the nose.** The ox does not choose its way; the rope chooses. We think we choose our desires, but our desires choose for us.
- **Gītā 3.36:** <span class="parallel">अथ केन प्रयुक्तोऽयं पापं चरति पूरुषः । अनिच्छन्नपि वार्ष्णेय बलादिव नियोजितः ॥</span> — *then by what is a person driven to sin, even unwillingly, as if by force?* Kṛṣṇa answers (3.37): <span class="parallel">काम एष क्रोध एष</span> — *it is desire, it is anger.*

## Verse 1.17.15 { #1-17-15 }

<p class="shloka">
पुत्रमित्रकलत्रादितृष्णया नित्यकृष्टया ।<br>
खगेष्विव किरात्येदं जालं लोकेषु रच्यते ॥ १.१७.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नित्य-कृष्टया (constantly pulling) पुत्र-मित्र-कलत्र-आदि-तृष्णया (by craving for sons, friends, wife and so on) लोकेषु (among people) किरात्या खगेषु इव (as by a huntress among birds) इदं जालं रच्यते (this net is spread).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving for sons, friends, wife and the rest, always pulling, spreads this net among people, as a huntress spreads hers among birds."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Compare 1.15.17.** There the ego spread this net; here, craving. Ego and craving work together.

## Verse 1.17.16 { #1-17-16 }

<p class="shloka">
भीषयत्यपि धीरं मामन्धयत्यपि सेक्षणम् ।<br>
खेदयत्यपि सानन्दं तृष्णाकृष्णेव शर्वरी ॥ १.१७.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) कृष्णा शर्वरी इव (like a dark night) धीरम् अपि मां भीषयति (frightens even me, who am steady) स-ईक्षणम् अपि अन्धयति (blinds even one with eyes) स-आनन्दम् अपि खेदयति (wearies even one full of joy).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving, like a dark night, frightens even the steady, blinds even those with eyes, and wearies even those full of joy."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A triple series begins** (1.16–1.18): craving as the dark night, the black serpent, the black demoness. Each ends with कृष्णेव.

## Verse 1.17.17 { #1-17-17 }

<p class="shloka">
कुटिला कोमलस्पर्शा विषवैषम्यशंसिनी ।<br>
दशत्यपि मनाक्स्पृष्टा तृष्णा कृष्णेव भोगिनी ॥ १.१७.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कुटिला (crooked) कोमल-स्पर्शा (soft to the touch) विष-वैषम्य-शंसिनी (announcing the cruelty of poison) मनाक् स्पृष्टा अपि (touched even slightly) तृष्णा (craving) कृष्णा भोगिनी इव (like a black serpent) दशति (bites).
</p>

**Translation:** "Crooked, soft to the touch, full of poison, craving bites at the slightest touch, like a black serpent."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Soft to the touch.** Craving does not feel dangerous. That is its danger.

## Verse 1.17.18 { #1-17-18 }

<p class="shloka">
भिन्दती हृदयं पुंसां मायामयविधायिनी ।<br>
दौर्भाग्यदायिनी दीना तृष्णा कृष्णेव राक्षसी ॥ १.१७.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुंसां हृदयं भिन्दती (breaking people's hearts) माया-मय-विधायिनी (working by illusion) दौर्भाग्य-दायिनी (bringing misfortune) दीना (miserable) तृष्णा (craving) कृष्णा राक्षसी इव (is like a black demoness).
</p>

**Translation:** "Breaking people's hearts, working through illusion, bringing misfortune, miserable — craving is like a black demoness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **मायामय.** Craving works through illusion: it makes the object seem to promise what it cannot give.

## Verse 1.17.19 { #1-17-19 }

<p class="shloka">
तन्द्रीतन्त्रीगणैः कोशं दधाना परिवेष्टितम् ।<br>
नानन्दे राजते ब्रह्मंस्तृष्णा जर्जरवल्लकी ॥ १.१७.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), तन्द्री-तन्त्री-गणैः परिवेष्टितं कोशं दधाना (with a body wrapped in strings of weariness) जर्जर-वल्लकी तृष्णा (craving, a broken lute) आनन्दे न राजते (does not sound in joy).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, craving is a broken lute, its body wound with strings of weariness; it never sounds in joy."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The broken lute.** It looks like an instrument of pleasure, but it cannot play the music of joy.

## Verse 1.17.20 { #1-17-20 }

<p class="shloka">
नित्यमेवातिमलिना कटुकोन्माददायिनी ।<br>
दीर्घतन्त्री घनस्नेहा तृष्णा गह्वरवल्लरी ॥ १.१७.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नित्यम् एव अति-मलिना (always very impure) कटुक-उन्माद-दायिनी (giving a bitter madness) दीर्घ-तन्त्री (with long tendrils) घन-स्नेहा (thick with sap/attachment) तृष्णा (craving) गह्वर-वल्लरी (is a vine in a cave).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving is a vine in a dark cave: always impure, giving a bitter madness, with long tendrils, thick with sap."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **स्नेह — a pun.** It means both sap (oil) and affection. Craving's 'sap' is attachment.

## Verse 1.17.21 { #1-17-21 }

<p class="shloka">
अनानन्दकरी शून्या निष्फला व्यर्थमुन्नता ।<br>
अमङ्गलकरी क्रूरा तृष्णा क्षीणेव मञ्जरी ॥ १.१७.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अन्-आनन्द-करी (giving no joy) शून्या (empty) निष्फला (fruitless) व्यर्थम् उन्नता (raised high in vain) अ-मङ्गल-करी (inauspicious) क्रूरा (cruel) तृष्णा (craving) क्षीणा मञ्जरी इव (is like a withered blossom).
</p>

**Translation:** "Joyless, empty, fruitless, raised high for nothing, inauspicious and cruel — craving is like a withered flower."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Fruitless.** Craving promises fruit but bears none. Compare Gītā 2.47 on fruit.

## Verse 1.17.22 { #1-17-22 }

<p class="shloka">
अनावर्जितचित्तापि सर्वमेवानुधावति ।<br>
न चाप्नोति फलं किंचित्तृष्णा जीर्णेव कामिनी ॥ १.१७.२२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अन्-आवर्जित-चित्ता अपि (though her heart is not won) सर्वम् एव अनुधावति (she runs after everyone) किंचित् फलं च न आप्नोति (and gains no fruit at all) — तृष्णा जीर्णा कामिनी इव (craving is like an old courtesan).
</p>

**Translation:** "Though no one wins her heart, she runs after everyone and gains nothing: craving is like an ageing courtesan."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Runs after everything.** Craving has no real loyalty to any object. It is not love for a thing, but restlessness itself.

## Verse 1.17.23 { #1-17-23 }

<p class="shloka">
संसारवृन्दे महति नानारससमाकुले ।<br>
भुवनाभोगरङ्गेषु तृष्णा जरठनर्तकी ॥ १.१७.२३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नाना-रस-समाकुले (full of many flavours/emotions) महति संसार-वृन्दे (in the great troupe of worldly existence) भुवन-आभोग-रङ्गेषु (on the stages of the wide world) तृष्णा जरठ-नर्तकी (craving is an old dancer).
</p>

**Translation:** "In the great troupe of worldly life, full of every mood, on the stages of the wide world, craving is an old dancing-girl."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **रस — flavour, emotion.** A word from Indian aesthetics. The world is a drama full of rasas; craving dances in every scene.
- **The world as a stage.** The Yoga Vasiṣṭha often uses theatre imagery; compare 1.17.37 (संसारनाटकनटी).

## Verse 1.17.24 { #1-17-24 }

<p class="shloka">
जराकुसुमितारूढा पातोत्पातफलावलिः ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दीर्घे संसार-जङ्गले (in the long jungle of saṃsāra) जरा-कुसुमित-आरूढा (climbing up, blossoming with old age) पात-उत्पात-फल-आवलिः (with rows of fruits of falls and calamities) तृष्णा (craving) तता विष-लता (is a spreading poison-vine).

**Translation:** "In the long jungle of worldly existence, craving is a spreading poison-vine, flowering into old age, fruiting in falls and calamities."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Flowers and fruits.** Old age is its flower; downfall its fruit. Craving's harvest is decay.

## Verse 1.17.25 { #1-17-25 }

<p class="shloka">
यन्न शक्रोति तत्रापि धत्ते ताण्डवितां गतिम् ।<br>
नृत्यत्यानन्दरहितं तृष्णा जीर्णेव नर्तकी ॥ १.१७.२५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत् न शक्नोति (what she is unable to do) तत्र अपि (even there) ताण्डवितां गतिं धत्ते (she takes a dancing step); तृष्णा (craving) जीर्णा नर्तकी इव (like an old dancer) आनन्द-रहितं नृत्यति (dances without joy).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even where she has no strength, she still tries to dance: craving, like an old dancing-girl, dances on without joy."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dancing without joy.** The saddest image of the sarga. Craving outlives its own pleasure; we go on wanting even when wanting no longer gives anything.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints शक्रोति; read शक्नोति.

## Verse 1.17.26 { #1-17-26 }

<p class="shloka">
भृशं स्फुरति नीहारे शाम्यत्यालोक आगते ।<br>
दुर्लङ्घ्येषु पदं धत्ते चिन्ता चपलबर्हिणी ॥ १.१७.२६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चिन्ता (longing) चपल-बर्हिणी (a restless peahen) नीहारे भृशं स्फुरति (dances wildly in the mist) आलोके आगते शाम्यति (grows still when light comes) दुर्-लङ्घ्येषु पदं धत्ते (sets foot on places hard to cross).
</p>

**Translation:** "Longing is a restless peahen: she dances wildly in the mist, falls still when the light comes, and sets her foot on impassable places."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints दुर्लङ्घयेषु; corrected here to दुर्लङ्घ्येषु.
- **Still when the light comes.** A hint of the cure: the peahen dances in fog but is quieted by light. Craving thrives in ignorance and calms in knowledge.

## Verse 1.17.27 { #1-17-27 }

<p class="shloka">
जडकल्लोलबहुला चिरं शून्यान्तरान्तरा ।<br>
क्षणमुल्लासमायाति तृष्णा प्रावृट्तरङ्गिणी ॥ १.१७.२७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) प्रावृट्-तरङ्गिणी (a monsoon river) जड-कल्लोल-बहुला (full of dull waves) चिरं शून्य-अन्तर-अन्तरा (long empty in its depths) क्षणम् उल्लासम् आयाति (swells up for a moment).

**Translation:** "Craving is a monsoon river: full of muddy waves, empty in its depths, swelling only for a moment."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Seasonal fullness.** The monsoon river is full only briefly; then it is dry. Craving's satisfaction is just as short.

## Verse 1.17.28 { #1-17-28 }

<p class="shloka">
नष्टमुत्सृज्य तिष्ठन्तं तृष्णा वृक्षमिवापरम् ।<br>
पुरुषात्पुरुषं याति तृष्णा लोलेव पक्षिणी ॥ १.१७.२८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) लोला पक्षिणी इव (like a fickle bird) नष्टं [वृक्षम्] उत्सृज्य (leaving a ruined tree) अपरं तिष्ठन्तं वृक्षम् इव (as if for another standing tree) पुरुषात् पुरुषं याति (goes from one person to another).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a fickle bird that leaves a ruined tree for another still standing, craving passes from one person to the next."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Craving outlives the person.** It moves on — to the next life, the next person. It is not personal property but an impersonal force.

## Verse 1.17.29 { #1-17-29 }

<p class="shloka">
पदं करोत्यलङ्घ्येऽपि तृप्तापि फलमीहते ।<br>
चिरं तिष्ठति नैकत्र तृष्णा चपलमर्कटी ॥ १.१७.२९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) चपल-मर्कटी (a restless she-monkey) अ-लङ्घ्ये अपि पदं करोति (sets foot even on the impassable) तृप्ता अपि फलम् ईहते (even when full, wants fruit) एकत्र चिरं न तिष्ठति (never stays long in one place).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving is a restless monkey: she leaps even where none can go, wants fruit even when full, and never stays long in one place."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The monkey mind.** A famous Indian image of the restless mind, used in Hindu, Buddhist and Jaina teaching alike.
- **Full, yet wanting.** Craving is not hunger; hunger ends when fed.

## Verse 1.17.30 { #1-17-30 }

<p class="shloka">
इदं कृत्वेदमायाति सर्वमेवासमञ्जसम् ।<br>
अनारतं च यतते तृष्णा चेष्टेव दैविकी ॥ १.१७.३० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) दैविकी चेष्टा इव (like the working of fate) इदं कृत्वा (having done this) इदम् आयाति (moves on to that) सर्वम् एव असमञ्जसम् (all of it incoherent) अनारतं च यतते (and strives without rest).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving, like the workings of fate, does this, then moves on to that — all of it incoherent — and never stops striving."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Endless doing.** Craving keeps one busy without direction. Compare 1.12.1, Rāma seeing the world as constant meaningless activity.

## Verse 1.17.31 { #1-17-31 }

<p class="shloka">
क्षणमायाति पातालं क्षणं याति नभस्थलम् ।<br>
क्षणं भ्रमति दिक्कुञ्जे तृष्णा हृत्पद्मषट्पदी ॥ १.१७.३१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
हृत्-पद्म-षट्पदी तृष्णा (craving, the bee of the heart-lotus) क्षणं पातालम् आयाति (one moment goes to the underworld) क्षणं नभः-स्थलं याति (one moment to the sky) क्षणं दिक्-कुञ्जे भ्रमति (one moment roams the groves of the directions).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving, the bee in the lotus of the heart, one moment dives to the underworld, the next rises to the sky, and the next wanders through every grove."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The bee in the heart-lotus.** The heart is a lotus (Chāndogya 8.1.1, दहरं पुण्डरीकं वेश्म), the seat of the Self. Craving buzzes in the very place where the Self abides.

## Verse 1.17.32 { #1-17-32 }

<p class="shloka">
सर्वसंसारदोषाणां तृष्णैका दीर्घदुःखदा ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्व-संसार-दोषाणां (of all the faults of worldly existence) तृष्णा एका (craving alone) दीर्घ-दुःख-दा (gives lasting sorrow), या (which) अन्तःपुर-स्थम् अपि (even one living in the inner palace) अति-संकटे योजयति (puts into great danger).

**Translation:** "Of all the faults of worldly existence, craving alone gives lasting sorrow. Even one living safe in the inner palace, it throws into great danger."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The inner palace.** Rāma, a prince, speaks from experience: wealth and protection do not shield one from craving.

## Verse 1.17.33 { #1-17-33 }

<p class="shloka">
प्रयच्छति परं जाड्यं परमालोकरोधिनी ।<br>
मोहनीहारगहना तृष्णा जलदमालिका ॥ १.१७.३३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) जलद-मालिका (a row of clouds) मोह-नीहार-गहना (thick with the mist of delusion) परम-आलोक-रोधिनी (blocking the highest light) परं जाड्यं प्रयच्छति (gives great dullness).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving is a bank of clouds, thick with the mist of delusion; it blocks the highest light and leaves only deep dullness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Blocking the highest light.** The Self is the light; craving is what hides it, as clouds hide the sun.
- **Gītā 3.38:** <span class="parallel">धूमेनाव्रियते वह्निर्यथादर्शो मलेन च । यथोल्बेनावृतो गर्भस्तथा तेनेदमावृतम् ॥</span> — *as fire is covered by smoke, a mirror by dust, an embryo by the womb, so is this [knowledge] covered by desire.*

## Verse 1.17.34 { #1-17-34 }

<p class="shloka">

<p class="anvaya">
संसार-व्यवहारिणां सर्वेषां जन्तु-जातानां (of all beings who live in the world) तृष्णा (craving) परिप्रोत-मनः-माला (is the thread on which the garland of minds is strung) बन्धन-रज्जु-वत् (like a binding rope).
</p>

## Verse 1.17.35 { #1-17-35 }

<p class="shloka">
विचित्रवर्णा विगुणा दीर्घा मलिनसंस्थितिः ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) शक्र-कार्मुक-धर्मिणी (has the nature of a rainbow) विचित्र-वर्णा (of many colours) वि-गुणा (without a string / without virtue) दीर्घा (long) मलिन-संस्थितिः (resting on dark clouds) शून्या शून्य-पदा (empty, standing on emptiness).

क्षणमालोकविमला सान्धकारलवा क्षणम् ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) व्योम-वीथी-उपमा (is like the path of the sky): क्षणम् आलोक-विमला (one moment clear with light) क्षणं स-अन्धकार-लवा (one moment with a touch of darkness) क्षणं नीहार-गहना (one moment thick with mist).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving is like the sky: one moment clear and bright, the next touched with darkness, the next thick with mist."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Changing weather.** Craving is not one state but a constant shifting of moods.

## Verse 1.17.41 { #1-17-41 }

<p class="shloka">
गच्छत्युपशमं तृष्णा कायव्यायामशान्तये ।<br>
तमी घनतमःकृष्णा यथा रक्षोनिवृत्तये ॥ १.१७.४१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (just as) घन-तमः-कृष्णा तमी (the night, black with thick darkness) रक्षः-निवृत्तये (for the withdrawal of demons) [उपशमं याति] ([comes to an end]), [तथा] तृष्णा (craving) काय-व्यायाम-शान्तये (for the ceasing of the body's exertion) उपशमं गच्छति (comes to rest).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving comes to rest only when the body's striving stops — just as the black night ends only when the demons withdraw."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **When does craving end?** Rāma's verse is ambiguous: it can mean craving ends only at death, when the body's exertion ends. The teaching will show it can end sooner — in the living, by knowledge (जीवन्मुक्ति).

## Verse 1.17.42 { #1-17-42 }

<p class="shloka">
तावन्मुह्यत्ययं मूको लोको विलुलिताशयः ।<br>
यावदेवानुसंधत्ते तृष्णा विषविषूचिका ॥ १.१७.४२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यावत् एव (as long as) विष-विषूचिका तृष्णा (craving, a poisonous cholera) अनुसन्धत्ते (persists), तावत् (so long) अयं मूकः लोकः (this dumb world) विलुलित-आशयः [सन्] (with its heart disturbed) मुह्यति (is bewildered).
</p>

**Translation:** "As long as craving persists, like a poisonous cholera, this dumb world, its heart in turmoil, remains bewildered."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Craving as a disease** — this time cholera (विषूचिका). The next verse gives the cure.

## Verse 1.17.43 { #1-17-43 }

<p class="shloka">
लोकोऽयमखिलं दुःखं चिन्तयोज्झितयोज्झति ।<br>
तृष्णाविषूचिकामन्त्रश्चिन्तात्यागो हि कथ्यते ॥ १.१७.४३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अयं लोकः (this world/person) उज्झितया चिन्तया (by longing that is given up) अखिलं दुःखम् उज्झति (gives up all sorrow); चिन्ता-त्यागः हि (the giving up of longing) तृष्णा-विषूचिका-मन्त्रः कथ्यते (is said to be the healing mantra for the cholera of craving).
</p>

**Translation:** "When longing is given up, all sorrow is given up. The giving up of longing is said to be the healing mantra for the cholera of craving."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The cure.** The key verse of the sarga: चिन्तात्याग, letting go of longing, is the remedy. Simple to say, the work of a lifetime to do.
- **Gītā 2.55:** <span class="parallel">प्रजहाति यदा कामान्सर्वान्पार्थ मनोगतान् । आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ॥</span> — *when one gives up all desires of the mind, satisfied in the Self by the Self, then one is called steady in wisdom.*
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.7:** <span class="parallel">यदा सर्वे प्रमुच्यन्ते कामा येऽस्य हृदि श्रिताः । अथ मर्त्योऽमृतो भवत्यत्र ब्रह्म समश्नुते ॥</span> — *when all the desires that dwell in the heart are released, then the mortal becomes immortal and attains Brahman here.*

## Verse 1.17.44 { #1-17-44 }

<p class="shloka">
तृणपाषाणकाष्ठादिसर्वमामिषशङ्कया ।<br>
आददाना स्फुरत्यन्ते तृष्णा मत्स्यी ह्रदे यथा ॥ १.१७.४४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा (craving) तृण-पाषाण-काष्ठ-आदि सर्वम् (grass, stone, wood and everything) आमिष-शङ्कया आददाना (seizing, thinking it is food) अन्ते स्फुरति (in the end flounders), ह्रदे मत्स्यी यथा (like a fish in a pond).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving seizes everything — grass, stone, wood — mistaking it for food, and in the end flounders, like a fish in a pond."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Mistaking anything for food.** Craving does not discriminate; it grabs whatever comes. Discernment (विवेक) is its opposite.

## Verse 1.17.45 { #1-17-45 }

<p class="shloka">
रोगार्तिरङ्गनातृष्णा गम्भीरमपि मानवम् ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">

- **उत्तानता — a pun.** It means both 'shallowness' and 'opening upward'. The sun opens the lotus; disease and lust lay a person bare.

## Verse 1.17.46 { #1-17-46 }

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णाः (cravings) वेणु-लताः इव (like bamboo canes) अन्तः-शून्याः (hollow within) ग्रन्थिमत्यः (knotted) दीर्घ-स्व-अङ्कुर-कण्टकाः (with long shoots and thorns) नित्यं मुक्ता-मणि-प्रियाः (always fond of pearls and jewels).

<p class="anvaya">

- **Reading note.** The online text prints दुच्छेदामपि; corrected here to दुश्छेदामपि.
- **The sword of discernment.** Hope in the middle of the lament. Craving can be cut — not by force or suppression, but by विवेक.

## Verse 1.17.52 { #1-17-52 }

<p class="shloka">
एकैव सर्वभुवनान्तरलब्धलक्ष्या दुर्लक्ष्यतामुपगतैव वपुःस्थितैव ।<br>
तृष्णा स्थिता जगति चञ्चलवीचिमाले क्षीरोदकाम्बुतरले मधुरेव शक्तिः ॥ १.१७.५२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चञ्चल-वीचि-माले (in the restless garland of waves) जगति (in the world) एका एव तृष्णा (craving alone) सर्व-भुवन-अन्तर-लब्ध-लक्ष्या (found within all the worlds) वपुः-स्थिता एव (dwelling in the body) दुर्लक्ष्यताम् उपगता एव (yet hard to see) स्थिता (abides), क्षीरोद-काम्बु-तरले (in the rippling water of the milk ocean) मधुरा शक्तिः इव (like its sweet power).
</p>

**Translation:** Craving alone pervades the restless, wave-garlanded world: present in every world, dwelling in the body, and yet hard to see — like the sweetness that pervades the rippling waters of the milk ocean.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (vasantatilakā).
- **Invisible yet everywhere.** Like sweetness in milk — not seen, but in every drop. Craving is not one thing in the world; it is the flavour of worldly experience.
- **A hidden parallel.** The same language — present everywhere, hard to see, in the body — is used of the Self in the Upaniṣads (Kaṭha 1.2.12, दुर्दर्शं गूढमनुप्रविष्टं गुहाहितम्). Craving is a counterfeit of the Self's all-pervasiveness: the infinite longing of a being who is, in truth, infinite.
- **Kaṭha Upaniṣad 2.1.2:** <span class="parallel">पराचः कामाननुयन्ति बालास्ते मृत्योर्यन्ति विततस्य पाशम्</span> — *the childish follow outward desires; they walk into the wide-spread net of death.*
