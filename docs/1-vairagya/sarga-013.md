# Sarga 13

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · On fortune (Lakṣmī) (22 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 13 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: on fortune (Lakṣmī).

    1. Fortune deludes, is fickle, and brings only worry and harm (1.13.1–1.13.10).
    2. Three rare kinds of people: the rich without blame, the brave without boasting, the powerful who are impartial (1.13.11).
    3. A long garland of images: fortune as night, frost, eclipse, rainbow, lightning, mirage, wave, flame, lioness, sword (1.13.12–1.13.19).
    4. Rāma's conclusion: in fortune he sees no happiness, only sorrow (1.13.20–1.13.22).

## Verse 1.13.1 { #1-13-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
इयमस्मिन्स्थितोदारा संसारे परिकल्पिता ।<br>
श्रीर्मुने परिमोहाय सापि नूनं कदर्थदा ॥ १.१३.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), अस्मिन् संसारे (in this world) स्थित-उदारा परिकल्पिता इयं श्रीः (this fortune, imagined to be so grand and lasting) परिमोहाय [भवति] (exists to delude); सा अपि नूनं कदर्थ-दा (it surely brings only misery).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "O sage, this fortune in the world, imagined to be so grand and lasting, exists only to delude. Surely it brings nothing but misery."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **श्री or लक्ष्मी — fortune, wealth, prosperity.** This whole sarga is Rāma's meditation on wealth. In Indian tradition Lakṣmī is a goddess; here she is personified as a fickle, deceiving woman.
- **परिकल्पिता — imagined.** Fortune's grandeur is a construction of the mind (compare कल्पना, 1.10.38).
- **Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.1.27:** <span class="parallel">न वित्तेन तर्पणीयो मनुष्यः</span> — *a person cannot be satisfied with wealth.* Naciketas' answer to Yama's offer of riches.

## Verse 1.13.2 { #1-13-2 }

<p class="shloka">
उल्लासबहुलानन्तकल्लोलानलमाकुलान् ।<br>
जडान्प्रवहति स्फारान्प्रावृषीव तरङ्गिणी ॥ १.१३.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] प्रावृषि तरङ्गिणी इव (like a river in the rains) उल्लास-बहुल-अनन्त-कल्लोल-अनल-माकुलान् (agitated by countless waves of excitement) स्फारान् जडान् (swollen, dull [people]) प्रवहति (sweeps along).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a river in the monsoon, fortune sweeps along dull, puffed-up people, tossing them on endless waves of excitement."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Swept away.** Wealth does not carry its owners; it carries them off. The river image recurs throughout the Vairāgya Prakaraṇa.

## Verse 1.13.3 { #1-13-3 }

<p class="shloka">
चिन्तादुहितरो बह्वयो भूरिदुर्ललितैधिताः ।<br>
चञ्चलाः प्रभवन्त्यस्यास्तरङ्गाः सरितो यथा ॥ १.१३.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्याः (from it) भूरि-दुर्ललित-एधिताः (fed by much indulgence) बह्व्यः चञ्चलाः चिन्ता-दुहितरः (many restless daughters called worries) सरितः तरङ्गाः यथा (like waves of a river) प्रभवन्ति (are born).
</p>

**Translation:** "From it are born many restless daughters called Worry, fed by much indulgence, like waves rising from a river."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Worries as daughters of wealth.** The more one has, the more one worries about keeping it. A psychological truth in poetic form.

## Verse 1.13.4 { #1-13-4 }

<p class="shloka">
एषा हि पदमेकत्र न निबध्नाति दुर्भगा ।<br>
दग्धेवानियताचारमितश्चेतश्च धावति ॥ १.१३.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एषा दुर्भगा हि (this wretched one) एकत्र पदं न निबध्नाति (never sets her foot in one place); दग्धा इव (as if burned) अनियत-आचारम् (with no fixed course) इतः च इतः च धावति (runs here and there).
</p>

**Translation:** "This wretched one never stays in one place. As if scorched, she runs here and there with no fixed course."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Fortune is fickle.** In Indian imagery, Lakṣmī is चञ्चला, restless, never staying with one person. Rāma sees this as her essence.

## Verse 1.13.5 { #1-13-5 }

<p class="shloka">
जनयन्ती परं दाहं परामृष्टाङ्गिका सती ।<br>
विनाशमेव धत्तेऽन्तर्दीपलेखेव कज्जलम् ॥ १.१३.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
परामृष्ट-अङ्गिका सती (when touched) परं दाहं जनयन्ती (producing great burning), [सा] अन्तः-दीप-लेखा कज्जलम् इव (as a lamp's flame [produces] soot) विनाशम् एव धत्ते (holds only destruction).
</p>

**Translation:** "Touched, she produces only burning; like a lamp's flame that produces soot, she holds only destruction within."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The flame and the soot.** Wealth gives light, and also leaves black soot. Its brightness comes with a hidden residue.

## Verse 1.13.6 { #1-13-6 }

<p class="shloka">
गुणागुणविचारेण विनैव किल पार्श्वगम् ।<br>
राजप्रकृतिवन्मूढा दुरारूढाऽवलम्बते ॥ १.१३.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] मूढा (foolish) दुर्-आरूढा (hard to keep hold of) राज-प्रकृति-वत् (like a king's subjects or a king's nature) गुण-अगुण-विचारेण विना एव किल (without regard to merit or fault) पार्श्व-गम् अवलम्बते (clings to whoever is nearby).
</p>

**Translation:** "Foolish and hard to hold, like a king's favour, she clings to whoever is nearby, without regard to merit or fault."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Wealth without justice.** Fortune does not go to the deserving but to whoever happens to be near. Rāma observes the arbitrariness of prosperity.

## Verse 1.13.7 { #1-13-7 }

<p class="shloka">
कर्मणा तेनतेनैषा विस्तारमनुगच्छति ।<br>
दोषाशीविषवेगस्य यत्क्षीरं विस्तरायते ॥ १.१३.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एषा (she) तेन तेन कर्मणा (by each and every action) विस्तारम् अनुगच्छति (grows wider), यत् (like) दोष-आशीविष-वेगस्य क्षीरं (milk for the venomous serpent of faults) विस्तरायते (that increases it).
</p>

**Translation:** "With every action she spreads further — like milk that only feeds the poisonous snake of our faults."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Feeding the snake.** Giving milk to a snake does not make it less poisonous; it makes it stronger. Wealth feeds our faults rather than curing them.

## Verse 1.13.8 { #1-13-8 }

<p class="shloka">
तावच्छीतमृदुस्पर्शाः परे स्वे च जने जनाः ।<br>
वात्ययेव हिमं यावच्छ्रिया न परुषीकृताः ॥ १.१३.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यावत् श्रिया (until by wealth) वात्यया हिमम् इव (as snow by a gale) परुषी-कृताः न [भवन्ति] (they are made harsh), तावत् (so long) जनाः (people) परे स्वे च जने (to strangers and to their own) शीत-मृदु-स्पर्शाः (are cool and gentle to the touch).
</p>

**Translation:** "People are gentle and kind to strangers and their own alike — until wealth hardens them, as a gale hardens snow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Wealth changes character.** A sharp observation: prosperity often hardens the heart.
- **Gītā 16.13–15** describes the wealthy who think <span class="parallel">इदमद्य मया लब्धम्</span> — *this I have gained today* — and grow arrogant.

## Verse 1.13.9 { #1-13-9 }

<p class="shloka">
प्राज्ञाः शूराः कृतज्ञाश्च पेशला मृदवश्च ये ।<br>
पांसुमुष्ट्येव मणयः श्रिया ते मलिनीकृताः ॥ १.१३.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये (those who are) प्राज्ञाः शूराः कृत-ज्ञाः च (wise, brave and grateful) पेशलाः मृदवः च (pleasant and gentle), ते (they) श्रिया (by wealth) पांसु-मुष्ट्या मणयः इव (like gems by a fistful of dust) मलिनी-कृताः (are tarnished).
</p>

**Translation:** "The wise, the brave, the grateful, the pleasant and gentle — all are tarnished by wealth, as gems are dulled by a fistful of dust."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dust on gems.** Good qualities are not destroyed by wealth, but covered. Compare the stain (मल) on the pure Self (1.2.24).

## Verse 1.13.10 { #1-13-10 }

<p class="shloka">
न श्रीः सुखाय भगवन्दुःखायैव हि वर्धते ।<br>
गुप्ता विनाशनं धत्ते मृतिं विषलता यथा ॥ १.१३.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भगवन् (revered one), श्रीः सुखाय न [भवति] (fortune is not for happiness); दुःखाय एव हि वर्धते (it grows only for sorrow); गुप्ता [सती] (guarded) विष-लता यथा मृतिं [धत्ते] (like a poison creeper [that holds] death) विनाशनं धत्ते (it holds destruction).
</p>

**Translation:** "Revered one, fortune does not bring happiness; it grows only for sorrow. Guarded carefully, it holds destruction, as a poison vine holds death."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.2:**
    - <span class="parallel">अमृतत्वस्य तु नाशास्ति वित्तेन</span>
    - *There is no hope of immortality through wealth.*
    - Yājñavalkya's words to Maitreyī, when she asks whether the whole earth full of wealth would make her immortal. She replies: <span class="parallel">येनाहं नामृता स्यां किमहं तेन कुर्याम्</span> — *what shall I do with that which will not make me immortal?* Maitreyī and Rāma reach the same conclusion.

## Verse 1.13.11 { #1-13-11 }

<p class="shloka">
श्रीमानजननिन्द्यश्च शूरश्चाप्यविकत्थनः ।<br>
समदृष्टिः प्रभुश्चैव दुर्लभाः पुरुषास्त्रयः ॥ १.१३.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
श्रीमान् अ-जन-निन्द्यः च (rich yet not criticised by people) शूरः च अपि अविकत्थनः (brave yet not boastful) सम-दृष्टिः प्रभुः च एव (powerful yet impartial) — [इमे] त्रयः पुरुषाः दुर्लभाः (these three kinds of people are rare).
</p>

**Translation:** "Three kinds of people are rare: the rich who are not criticised, the brave who do not boast, and the powerful who treat all alike."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A famous verse.** A sharp observation on power and character, often quoted on its own.
- **समदृष्टि — equal vision in the powerful.** This is what Rāma will become: a king with equal vision (1.11.7, समलोष्टाश्मकाञ्चनः).
- **Gītā 5.18:** <span class="parallel">विद्याविनयसम्पन्ने ब्राह्मणे गवि हस्तिनि । शुनि चैव श्वपाके च पण्डिताः समदर्शिनः ॥</span> — *the wise see equally a learned brāhmaṇa, a cow, an elephant, a dog and an outcaste.*

## Verse 1.13.12 { #1-13-12 }

<p class="shloka">
एषा हि विषमा दुःखभोगिनां गहना गुहा ।<br>
घनमोहगजेन्द्राणां विन्ध्यशैलमहातटी ॥ १.१३.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एषा हि (she indeed) दुःख-भोगिनां (for those who enjoy sorrows [serpents]) विषमा गहना गुहा (a rugged, dense cave), घन-मोह-गज-इन्द्राणां (for great elephants of thick delusion) विन्ध्य-शैल-महा-तटी (a great slope of the Vindhya mountains).
</p>

**Translation:** "She is a rough, dark cave for the serpents of sorrow, and a great Vindhya slope for the mighty elephants of delusion."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A series of metaphors begins** (1.13.12–1.13.19). Each pairs wealth with a destructive image. Read them as a poem, not a list.

## Verse 1.13.13 { #1-13-13 }

<p class="shloka">
सत्कार्यपद्मरजनी दुःखकैरवचन्द्रिका ।<br>
सुदृष्टिदीपिकावात्या कल्लोलौघतरङ्गिणी ॥ १.१३.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] सत्-कार्य-पद्म-रजनी (night to the lotus of good deeds) दुःख-कैरव-चन्द्रिका (moonlight to the night-lily of sorrow) सु-दृष्टि-दीपिका-वात्या (gale to the lamp of right vision) कल्लोल-ओघ-तरङ्गिणी (a river of surging waves).
</p>

**Translation:** "She is night to the lotus of good deeds, moonlight to the night-lily of sorrow, a gale to the lamp of right vision, a river of surging waves."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Day-lotus and night-lily.** The lotus opens by day, the night-lily (कैरव) by moonlight. Wealth closes good deeds and opens sorrows.

## Verse 1.13.14 { #1-13-14 }

<p class="shloka">
संभ्रमाभ्रादिपदवी विषादविषवर्धिनी ।<br>
केदारिका विकल्पानां खेदायभयभोगिनी ॥ १.१३.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] सम्भ्रम-अभ्र-आदि-पदवी (a path for the clouds of confusion) विषाद-विष-वर्धिनी (an increaser of the poison of despondency) विकल्पानां केदारिका (a field for doubts) खेदाय भय-भोगिनी (a serpent of fear, bringing distress).
</p>

**Translation:** "She is a pathway for clouds of confusion, a feeder of the poison of despair, a field where doubts grow, a serpent of fear bringing distress."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **विकल्प — doubt, mental alternatives.** Wealth multiplies choices and worries. Compare the restless mind of Sarga 16.

## Verse 1.13.15 { #1-13-15 }

<p class="shloka">
हिमं वैराग्यवल्लीनां विकारोलूकयामिनी ।<br>
राहुदंष्ट्रा विवेकेन्दोः सौजन्याम्भोजचन्द्रिका ॥ १.१३.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] वैराग्य-वल्लीनां हिमम् (frost to the creepers of dispassion) विकार-उलूक-यामिनी (night for the owls of agitation) विवेक-इन्दोः राहु-दंष्ट्रा (Rāhu's fang to the moon of discernment) सौजन्य-अम्भोज-चन्द्रिका (moonlight to the lotus of goodness).
</p>

**Translation:** "She is frost to the vines of dispassion, night for the owls of agitation, Rāhu's fang that swallows the moon of discernment, moonlight that closes the lotus of goodness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Frost on dispassion, eclipse of discernment.** Wealth attacks the very qualities Rāma has gained: वैराग्य and विवेक. This is why he fears it.
- **Rāhu** is the demon who swallows the moon in an eclipse. Wealth eclipses discernment.

## Verse 1.13.16 { #1-13-16 }

<p class="shloka">
इन्द्रायुधवदालोलनानारागमनोहरा ।<br>
लोला तडिदिवोत्पन्नध्वंसिनी च जडाश्रया ॥ १.१३.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] इन्द्र-आयुध-वत् (like a rainbow) आलोल-नाना-राग-मनोहरा (charming with many shifting colours) लोला (fickle), तडित् इव (like lightning) उत्पन्न-ध्वंसिनी (vanishing as soon as it appears) जड-आश्रया च (and resting with the dull).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a rainbow, she charms with many shifting colours; fickle as lightning, she vanishes as soon as she appears; and she rests with the dull-witted."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rainbow and lightning.** Both beautiful, both momentary. Wealth as an appearance, not a substance — the teaching of 1.3.2 (the blue of the sky).

## Verse 1.13.17 { #1-13-17 }

<p class="shloka">
चापलावजितारण्य नकुली नकुलीनजा ।<br>
विप्रलम्भनतात्पर्यजितोग्रमृगतृष्णिका ॥ १.१३.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] चापल-अव-जित-अरण्य-नकुली (surpassing a forest mongoose in fickleness) न कुलीन-जा (not born of a noble family) विप्रलम्भन-तात्पर्य-जित-उग्र-मृगतृष्णिका (outdoing the fierce mirage in her intent to deceive).
</p>

**Translation:** "She outdoes the forest mongoose in fickleness; she is of no noble birth; and in her power to deceive, she outdoes even the desert mirage."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A pun.** नकुली (mongoose) and न कुलीनजा (not nobly born) play on the same sounds — one of many plays on words in this sarga.
- **Reading note.** The online text reads चापलावजितारण्य नकुली नकुलीनजा; the compound is difficult, and the sense given here follows the online word division.

## Verse 1.13.18 { #1-13-18 }

<p class="shloka">
लहरीवैकरूपेण पदं क्षणमकुर्वती ।<br>
चला दीपशिखेवातिदुर्ज्ञेयगतिगोचरा ॥ १.१३.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] लहरी इव (like a wave) एक-रूपेण क्षणं पदम् अकुर्वती (not keeping one form even for a moment) दीप-शिखा इव चला (unsteady as a lamp's flame) अति-दुर्ज्ञेय-गति-गोचरा (whose course is very hard to know).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a wave, she never keeps one form even for a moment; unsteady as a lamp's flame, her movements are almost impossible to follow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The wave and the flame.** Two images of change that Rāma will soon apply to the mind itself (Sarga 16). Wealth's instability mirrors the mind's.

## Verse 1.13.19 { #1-13-19 }

<p class="shloka">
सिंहीव विग्रहव्यग्रकरीन्द्रकुलपोथिनी ।<br>
खड्गधारेव शिशिरा तीक्ष्णतीक्ष्णाशयाश्रया ॥ १.१३.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] सिंही इव (like a lioness) विग्रह-व्यग्र-करि-इन्द्र-कुल-पोथिनी (crushing whole herds of great elephants intent on battle) खड्ग-धारा इव (like a sword's edge) शिशिरा (cool) तीक्ष्ण-तीक्ष्ण-आशय-आश्रया (resting with the sharp-minded).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a lioness, she crushes herds of mighty war-elephants; like a sword's edge, she is cool to the touch, yet keen, and stays with the sharp and cunning."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Cool yet deadly.** A sword's edge feels cool, but cuts. Wealth seems pleasant, but wounds.

## Verse 1.13.20 { #1-13-20 }

<p class="shloka">
नानयापहृतार्थिन्या दुराधिपरिलीनया ।<br>
पश्याम्यभव्यया लक्ष्म्या किंचिद्दुःखादृते सुखम् ॥ १.१३.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनया (by this) अपहृत-अर्थिन्या (who takes away from those who seek her) दुर्-आधि-परिलीनया (immersed in grievous anxieties) अभव्यया लक्ष्म्या (inauspicious fortune) दुःखात् ऋते (apart from sorrow) किञ्चित् सुखं न पश्यामि (I see no happiness at all).
</p>

**Translation:** "In this inauspicious fortune, which robs those who seek her and is steeped in anxieties, I see no happiness at all — only sorrow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rāma's conclusion.** He sees no happiness in wealth. This is his honest view at this stage, before he learns that freedom lies not in having or not having, but in not being bound.

## Verse 1.13.21 { #1-13-21 }

<p class="shloka">
दूरेणोत्सारिताऽलक्ष्म्या पुनरेव समादरात् ।<br>
अहो बताश्लिष्यतीव निर्लज्जा दुर्जना सदा ॥ १.१३.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अहो बत (alas, how strange) अलक्ष्म्या (by misfortune) दूरेण उत्सारिता (though driven far away) [सा] पुनः एव समादरात् (she returns with eagerness) निर्लज्जा दुर्जना सदा (shameless and wicked always) आश्लिष्यति इव (as if to embrace again).
</p>

**Translation:** "Alas! Though misfortune drives her far away, back she comes eagerly, shameless and wicked as ever, as if to embrace us again."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The cycle.** Fortune leaves and returns. The rise and fall of wealth is endless (compare 1.8.29, vibhūtis rising and dissolving in time).

## Verse 1.13.22 { #1-13-22 }

<p class="shloka">
मनोरमा कर्षति चित्तवृत्तिं कदर्थसाध्या क्षणभङ्गुरा च ।<br>
व्यालावलीगात्रविवृत्तदेहा श्वभ्रोत्थिता पुष्पलतेव लक्ष्मीः ॥ १.१३.२२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
लक्ष्मीः (fortune) मनोरमा (charming) चित्त-वृत्तिं कर्षति (draws away the mind) कदर्थ-साध्या (won only through misery) क्षण-भङ्गुरा च (and breaking in a moment), श्वभ्र-उत्थिता पुष्प-लता इव (like a flowering vine growing from a pit) व्याल-आवली-गात्र-विवृत्त-देहा (its body entwined with the coils of serpents).
</p>

**Translation:** Fortune is charming and draws the mind away; she is won only through misery and breaks in a moment — like a flowering vine growing out of a pit, its stem wound about with snakes.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (upajāti).
- **The flowering vine in a pit.** Beautiful flowers, but growing from a pit and entwined with snakes. To reach the flowers, one must risk the fall and the bite. The perfect image for the whole sarga.
- **A note on Rāma's view.** Rāma will not, in the end, renounce wealth: he will be a king. What the teaching changes is not his possessions but his relationship to them (Gītā 2.48, सङ्गं त्यक्त्वा).
