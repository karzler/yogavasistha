# Sarga 20

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · On youth (yauvana) (43 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 20 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: on youth (yauvana).

    1. A person climbs into youth only to fall; desire overpowers the mind (1.20.1–1.20.7).
    2. A refrain: "youth does not please me" — a flash of lightning, a wave of wine, a dream embrace, a mirage city (1.20.8–1.20.13).
    3. Youth as darkness, fire, flood, forest, lotus, ocean, wind, mirage — the season of attraction and aversion (1.20.14–1.20.37).
    4. A turn: "have compassion for youth, as for a dying son"; those who cross youth with ease are great souls; a good youth — humble, compassionate, virtuous — is as rare as a forest in the sky (1.20.38–1.20.43).

## Verse 1.20.1 { #1-20-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
बाल्यानर्थमथ त्यक्त्वा पुमानभिहताशयः ।<br>
आरोहति निपाताय यौवनं संभ्रमेण तु ॥ १.२०.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) बाल्य-अनर्थं त्यक्त्वा (having left behind the misfortune of childhood) अभिहत-आशयः पुमान् (a person, his mind struck down) निपाताय (only to fall) सम्भ्रमेण तु (with excitement) यौवनम् आरोहति (climbs up into youth).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Leaving behind the misfortunes of childhood, a person, his mind already wounded, climbs eagerly into youth — only to fall."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **यौवन — youth.** The second stage of life. Rāma himself is in it: "not yet sixteen" (1.5.1, 1.8.2). His critique of youth is self-examination.
- **Climbing only to fall.** Youth is a peak, and every peak has a far side.
- **Gītā 2.13** named the three stages (see 1.19.1): <span class="parallel">कौमारं यौवनं जरा</span>. Rāma has examined the first; now the second.

## Verse 1.20.2 { #1-20-2 }

<p class="shloka">
तत्रानन्तविलासस्य लोलस्य स्वस्य चेतसः ।<br>
वृत्तीरनुभवन्याति दुःखाद्दुःखान्तरं जडः ॥ १.२०.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र (there) अनन्त-विलासस्य लोलस्य स्वस्य चेतसः (of his own restless mind with its endless play) वृत्तीः अनुभवन् (experiencing the movements) जडः (the dull one) दुःखात् दुःख-अन्तरं याति (goes from sorrow to sorrow).
</p>

**Translation:** "There, experiencing the movements of his own restless mind with its endless play, the dull one goes from one sorrow to another."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **From sorrow to sorrow.** Youth promises freedom, but delivers the same mind, now with more power to follow its whims.

## Verse 1.20.3 { #1-20-3 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वचित्तबिलसंस्थेन नानासंभ्रमकारिणा ।<br>
बलात्कामपिशाचेन विवशः परिभूयते ॥ १.२०.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्व-चित्त-बिल-संस्थेन (dwelling in the hole of one's own mind) नाना-सम्भ्रम-कारिणा (causing many agitations) काम-पिशाचेन (by the demon of lust) विवशः (helpless) बलात् परिभूयते (he is forcibly overpowered).
</p>

**Translation:** "The demon of lust, living in the cave of his own mind and causing all kinds of agitation, overpowers him by force, and he is helpless."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **काम — desire, lust.** Youth is the season of काम.
- **Gītā 3.37:** <span class="parallel">काम एष क्रोध एष रजोगुणसमुद्भवः । महाशनो महापाप्मा विद्ध्येनमिह वैरिणम् ॥</span> — *it is desire, it is anger, born of rajas, all-devouring, most sinful; know it as the enemy here.*

## Verse 1.20.4 { #1-20-4 }

<p class="shloka">
चिन्तानां लोलवृत्तीनां ललनानामिवाऽवृतीः ।<br>
अर्पयत्यवशं चेतो बालानामञ्जनं यथा ॥ १.२०.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[यौवनम्] (youth) अवशं चेतः (the helpless mind) लोल-वृत्तीनां चिन्तानाम् (to anxious longings of fickle ways) ललनानाम् इव (as if to women) आवृतीः अर्पयति (hands over to be enveloped), बालानाम् अञ्जनं यथा (as kohl [is applied to the eyes] of children).
</p>

**Translation:** "Youth hands over the helpless mind to be wrapped in fickle longings, as if to wanton women — as kohl is put on a child's eyes whether it wants it or not."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The simile of kohl is compressed and its sense uncertain; the translation follows the likeliest reading: something applied to a helpless subject.

## Verse 1.20.5 { #1-20-5 }

<p class="shloka">
ते ते दोषा दुरारम्भास्तत्र तं तादृशाशयम् ।<br>
तद्रूपं प्रतिलुम्पन्ति दुष्टास्तेनैव ये मुने ॥ १.२०.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), ये दुष्टाः (those who are wicked) ते ते दुर्-आरम्भाः दोषाः (those faults of evil undertakings) तत्र (there, in youth) तादृश-आशयं तं (him, of such a mind) तेन एव तत्-रूपं प्रतिलुम्पन्ति (rob of his form by that very [youth]).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, in youth the wicked faults that lead to evil undertakings rob such a person of his true form."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Robbed of one's true form.** The faults of youth do not add to us; they take away who we really are.

## Verse 1.20.6 { #1-20-6 }

<p class="shloka">
महानरकबीजेन संततभ्रमदायिना ।<br>
यौवनेन न ये नष्टा नष्टा नान्येन ते जनाः ॥ १.२०.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
महा-नरक-बीजेन (the seed of great hell) सन्तत-भ्रम-दायिना (giving constant delusion) यौवनेन (by youth) ये न नष्टाः (those who are not ruined), ते जनाः (those people) अन्येन न नष्टाः (are ruined by nothing else).
</p>

**Translation:** "Youth sows the seed of the deepest hells and keeps a person ever spinning. Whoever survives youth unruined will be ruined by nothing else."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The great test.** Youth is the hardest trial. Whoever passes it is safe. This prepares 1.20.41–43, which praises those who cross youth well — like Rāma himself.

## Verse 1.20.7 { #1-20-7 }

<p class="shloka">
नानारसमयी चित्रवृत्तान्तनिचयोम्भिता ।<br>
भीमा यौवनभूर्येन तीर्णा धीरः स उच्यते ॥ १.२०.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
येन (by whom) नाना-रस-मयी (full of many flavours) चित्र-वृत्तान्त-निचय-उम्भिता (filled with many strange events) भीमा यौवन-भूः (the fearsome land of youth) तीर्णा (has been crossed), सः धीरः उच्यते (he is called steadfast).
</p>

**Translation:** "Whoever has crossed the fearsome land of youth, full of every flavour and crowded with strange events, is called steadfast (dhīra)."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **धीर — the steadfast one.** The word the Gītā uses for the wise: <span class="parallel">धीरस्तत्र न मुह्यति</span> (2.13) — *the steadfast one is not deluded by this.*
- **Crossing youth.** The image of crossing (तरण) returns: life is a river to be crossed, and youth its most dangerous stretch.

## Verse 1.20.8 { #1-20-8 }

<p class="shloka">
निमेषभासुराकारमालोलघनगर्जितम् ।<br>
विद्युत्प्रकाशमशिवं यौवनं मे न रोचते ॥ १.२०.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
निमेष-भासुर-आकारम् (shining for a blink) आलोल-घन-गर्जितम् (with restless thunder of clouds) विद्युत्-प्रकाशम् (bright as lightning) अशिवम् (inauspicious) — यौवनं मे न रोचते (youth does not please me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Shining for a moment, with restless thunder, flashing like lightning, inauspicious — youth does not please me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A refrain begins:** यौवनं मे न रोचते, "youth does not please me" (1.20.8–1.20.13). Like the body-house refrain of Sarga 18.

## Verse 1.20.9 { #1-20-9 }

<p class="shloka">
मधुरं स्वादु तिक्तं च दूषणं दोषभूषणम् ।<br>
सुराकल्लोलसदृशं यौवनं मे न रोचते ॥ १.२०.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मधुरं (sweet) स्वादु (tasty) तिक्तं च (and bitter) दूषणं (corrupting) दोष-भूषणम् (adorned with faults) सुरा-कल्लोल-सदृशम् (like a wave of wine) — यौवनं मे न रोचते (youth does not please me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Sweet, tasty and bitter, corrupting, decked with faults, like a wave of wine — youth does not please me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Wine.** Youth intoxicates. Sweet at first, bitter after.

## Verse 1.20.10 { #1-20-10 }

<p class="shloka">
असत्यं सत्यसंकाशमचिराद्विप्रलम्भदम् ।<br>
स्वप्नाङ्गनासङ्गसमं यौवनं मे न रोचते ॥ १.२०.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
असत्यं सत्य-संकाशम् (unreal yet looking real) अचिरात् विप्रलम्भ-दम् (soon disappointing) स्वप्न-अङ्गना-सङ्ग-समम् (like union with a woman in a dream) — यौवनं मे न रोचते (youth does not please me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Unreal yet seeming real, soon disappointing, like an embrace with a woman in a dream — youth does not please me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Unreal but seeming real** (असत्यं सत्यसंकाशम्). The definition of māyā in four syllables.

## Verse 1.20.11 { #1-20-11 }

<p class="shloka">
सर्वस्याग्रे सर्वपुंसः क्षणमात्रमनोहरम् ।<br>
गन्धर्वनगरप्रख्यं यौवनं मे न रोचते ॥ १.२०.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्वस्य अग्रे (first of all) सर्व-पुंसः (to every person) क्षण-मात्र-मनोहरम् (charming for only a moment) गन्धर्व-नगर-प्रख्यम् (like a city of the gandharvas) — यौवनं मे न रोचते (youth does not please me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Charming to every person for only a moment, like a gandharva city in the clouds — youth does not please me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The gandharva city** — the mirage-city of 1.18.61.

## Verse 1.20.12 { #1-20-12 }

<p class="shloka">
इषुप्रपातमात्रं हि सुखदं दुःखभासुरम् ।<br>
दाहपोषप्रदं नित्यं यौवनं मे न रोचते ॥ १.२०.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इषु-प्रपात-मात्रं हि सुख-दम् (giving pleasure only for the flight of an arrow) दुःख-भासुरम् (bright with sorrow) नित्यं दाह-पोष-प्रदम् (always giving burning and drying) — यौवनं मे न रोचते (youth does not please me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Pleasant only for as long as an arrow's flight, shining with sorrow, always burning and withering — youth does not please me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **An arrow's flight.** Brief, and ending in a wound.

## Verse 1.20.13 { #1-20-13 }

<p class="shloka">
आपातमात्ररमणं सद्भावरहितान्तरम् ।<br>
वेश्यास्त्रीसंगमप्रख्यं यौवनं मे न रोचते ॥ १.२०.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आपात-मात्र-रमणम् (pleasant only at first) सत्-भाव-रहित-अन्तरम् (with no goodness within) वेश्या-स्त्री-सङ्गम-प्रख्यम् (like union with a courtesan) — यौवनं मे न रोचते (youth does not please me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Pleasant only at first, with no goodness within, like a courtesan's embrace — youth does not please me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 18.38:** <span class="parallel">विषयेन्द्रियसंयोगाद्यत्तदग्रेऽमृतोपमम् । परिणामे विषमिव तत्सुखं राजसं स्मृतम् ॥</span> — *the pleasure from contact of the senses with their objects, nectar at first and poison in the end, is called rājasic.* Rāma's आपातमात्ररमण is the Gītā's अग्रेऽमृतोपमम्.

## Verse 1.20.14 { #1-20-14 }

<p class="shloka">
ये केचन समारम्भास्ते सर्वे सर्वदुःखदाः ।<br>
तारुण्ये संनिधिं यान्ति महोत्पाता इव क्षये ॥ १.२०.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये केचन समारम्भाः (whatever undertakings) [सन्ति], ते सर्वे सर्व-दुःख-दाः (all of them bringing every sorrow) तारुण्ये सन्निधिं यान्ति (gather in youth), क्षये महा-उत्पाताः इव (as great portents at the world's end).
</p>

**Translation:** "Every undertaking that brings every kind of sorrow gathers in youth, like great portents at the end of the world."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Portents.** Youth is when all the seeds of later suffering are sown.

## Verse 1.20.15 { #1-20-15 }

<p class="shloka">
हार्दान्धकारकारिण्या भैरवाकारवानपि ।<br>
यौवनाज्ञानयामिन्या बिभेति भगवानपि ॥ १.२०.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
हार्द-अन्धकार-कारिण्या (causing darkness in the heart) यौवन-अज्ञान-यामिन्या (by the night of youthful ignorance) भैरव-आकारवान् भगवान् अपि (even the Lord of fearsome form, Śiva) बिभेति (is afraid).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even the Lord of terrible form, Bhairava himself, fears the night of youthful ignorance that darkens the heart."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Even Śiva fears it.** A strong poetic exaggeration. Śiva burned Kāma, the god of desire, with his third eye; even he is said to be wary of youth's darkness.
- **Kashmir Śaivism** (see [the synthesis of traditions](../index.md#a-synthesis)): भैरव is the supreme form of Śiva in that tradition.

## Verse 1.20.16 { #1-20-16 }

<p class="shloka">
सुविस्मृतशुभाचारं बुद्धिवैधुर्यदायिनम् ।<br>
ददात्यतितरामेष भ्रमं यौवनसंभ्रमः ॥ १.२०.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एषः यौवन-सम्भ्रमः (this agitation of youth) सु-विस्मृत-शुभ-आचारम् (in which good conduct is quite forgotten) बुद्धि-वैधुर्य-दायिनम् (bringing confusion of intellect) भ्रमम् (delusion) अतितराम् ददाति (gives in great measure).
</p>

**Translation:** "The excitement of youth gives great delusion, in which good conduct is forgotten and the intellect is thrown into confusion."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 2.62–63:** <span class="parallel">ध्यायतो विषयान्पुंसः सङ्गस्तेषूपजायते… स्मृतिभ्रंशाद्बुद्धिनाशो बुद्धिनाशात्प्रणश्यति</span> — *dwelling on objects, attachment arises… from loss of memory comes the ruin of intellect, and from that one perishes.* Rāma's सुविस्मृत and बुद्धिवैधुर्य are the Gītā's स्मृतिभ्रंश and बुद्धिनाश.

## Verse 1.20.17 { #1-20-17 }

<p class="shloka">
कान्तावियोगजातेन हृदि दुःस्पर्शवह्निना ।<br>
यौवने दह्यते जन्तुस्तरुर्दावाग्निना यथा ॥ १.२०.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यौवने (in youth) जन्तुः (a creature) कान्ता-वियोग-जातेन (born of separation from the beloved) हृदि दुः-स्पर्श-वह्निना (by a fire in the heart, painful to touch) दावाग्निना तरुः यथा (like a tree by a forest fire) दह्यते (is burned).
</p>

**Translation:** "In youth a creature burns as a tree burns in a forest fire, with a fire in the heart — one that cannot bear to be touched — kindled by separation from the one it loves."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Viraha — the pain of separation.** Rāma, in his own life story, will know this pain fully when Sītā is taken. Here he speaks of it before living it.

## Verse 1.20.18 { #1-20-18 }

<p class="shloka">
सुनिर्मलापि विस्तीर्णा पावन्यपि हि यौवने ।<br>
मतिः कलुषतामेति प्रावृषीव तरङ्गिणी ॥ १.२०.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यौवने हि (in youth indeed) सु-निर्मला अपि (though very pure) विस्तीर्णा (broad) पावनी अपि (and purifying) मतिः (the mind) प्रावृषि तरङ्गिणी इव (like a river in the rains) कलुषताम् एति (becomes muddy).
</p>

**Translation:** "In youth even a pure, broad and purifying mind becomes muddy, like a river in the monsoon."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The monsoon river** again (1.13.2, 1.17.27). Even a pure mind is clouded when the floods of youth arrive.

## Verse 1.20.19 { #1-20-19 }

<p class="shloka">
शक्यते घनकल्लोला भीमा लङ्घयितुं नदी ।<br>
न तु तारुण्यतरला तृष्णातरलितान्तरा ॥ १.२०.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
घन-कल्लोला भीमा नदी (a fearsome river with heavy waves) लङ्घयितुं शक्यते (can be crossed); तु (but) तृष्णा-तरलित-अन्तरा तारुण्य-तरला [नदी] (the restless river of youth, its depths stirred by craving) न [शक्यते] (cannot).
</p>

**Translation:** "A fearsome river with heavy waves can be crossed, but not the restless river of youth, its depths stirred by craving."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Harder than any river.** Compare 1.16.24: restraining the mind is harder than drinking the ocean.

## Verse 1.20.20 { #1-20-20 }

<p class="shloka">
सा कान्ता तौ स्तनौ पीनौ ते विलासास्तदाननम् ।<br>
तारुण्य इति चिन्ताभिर्याति जर्जरतां जनः ॥ १.२०.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सा कान्ता (that beloved) तौ पीनौ स्तनौ (those full breasts) ते विलासाः (those charms) तत् आननम् (that face) — इति चिन्ताभिः (by such thoughts) तारुण्ये जनः जर्जरतां याति (in youth a person is worn down).
</p>

**Translation:** "'That beloved, those full breasts, those charms, that face' — with such thoughts, a person is worn down in youth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

**Translation:** "Good sir, those who cross the dangers of youth with ease are worthy of honour; they are great souls; they alone are true persons on earth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Crossing with ease.** Not by struggle but सुखेन, easily. This is the ideal: not a youth of rigid suppression, but one lived lightly and well.
- **This describes Rāma himself.** Vālmīki's Rāma, and the Rāma of 1.11, is exactly such a young man. He does not yet see it.

## Verse 1.20.42 { #1-20-42 }

<p class="shloka">
सुखेन तीर्यतेऽम्भोधिरुत्कृष्टमकराकरः ।<br>
न कल्लोलबलोल्लासि सदोषं हतयौवनम् ॥ १.२०.४२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
उत्कृष्ट-मकर-आकरः अम्भोधिः (the ocean, full of great crocodiles) सुखेन तीर्यते (is crossed with ease); न [तु] कल्लोल-बल-उल्लासि स-दोषं हत-यौवनम् (but not cursed youth, swelling with the force of its waves and full of faults).
</p>

**Translation:** "The ocean, full of great crocodiles, can be crossed with ease — but not cursed youth, swelling with mighty waves and full of faults."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Harder than the ocean.** Compare 1.20.19. The images of crossing gather to prepare the final verse.

## Verse 1.20.43 { #1-20-43 }

<p class="shloka">
विनयभूषितमार्यजनास्पदं करुणयोज्ज्वलमावलितं गुणैः ।<br>
इह हि दुर्लभमङ्ग सुयौवनं जगति काननमम्बरगं यथा ॥ १.२०.४३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अङ्ग (dear one), इह जगति (here in the world) विनय-भूषितम् (adorned with humility) आर्य-जन-आस्पदम् (the dwelling of the noble) करुणया उज्ज्वलम् (shining with compassion) गुणैः आवलितम् (surrounded by virtues) सु-यौवनम् (a good youth) अम्बर-गं काननं यथा (like a forest in the sky) दुर्लभं हि (is rare indeed).
</p>

**Translation:** Dear one, here in the world a good youth — adorned with humility, the home of the noble, shining with compassion, rich in virtues — is as rare as a forest in the sky.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (drutavilambita).
- **The ideal youth.** After condemning youth for a whole sarga, Rāma describes what a good youth would be: humble, noble, compassionate, virtuous. It is rare — 'like a forest in the sky'.
- **Rāma is describing himself.** This is exactly the young Rāma of 1.11 (see 1.11.17) and of Vālmīki. He sees the ideal clearly but does not see that he embodies it. The reader does.
- **Taittirīya Upaniṣad 2.8.1:** <span class="parallel">युवा स्यात्साधुयुवाध्यायकः । आशिष्ठो द्रढिष्ठो बलिष्ठः</span> — *let there be a youth, a good youth, learned, quick, firm and strong…* The Upaniṣad uses the good youth as the measure of human joy, the first step of its ladder of bliss (the आनन्दमीमांसा) that rises to the bliss of Brahman. The good youth is not the end, but the beginning.
