# Sarga 28

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · Everything changes (43 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 28 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: everything turns into its opposite.

    1. All that is seen is unstable, like a meeting in a dream: hollows become mountains, cities become forests, kings become ash (1.28.1–1.28.12).
    2. The world is an actress in a wild drama; "those days, those great ones, those riches — all have passed into memory; we too will go in a moment" (1.28.13–1.28.24).
    3. "One moment … the next": riches and poverty, health and illness, birth and death — "what is not momentary?" (1.28.25–1.28.33).
    4. Time creates and destroys like night and day; neither misfortune nor fortune lasts (1.28.34–1.28.43).

## Verse 1.28.1 { #1-28-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
यच्चेदं दृश्यते किंचिज्जगत्स्थावरजंगमम् ।<br>
तत्सर्वमस्थिरं ब्रह्मन्स्वप्नसंगमसंनिभम् ॥ १.२८.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), इदं (this) यत् च किंचित् स्थावर-जङ्गमं जगत् दृश्यते (whatever moving or unmoving world is seen), तत् सर्वम् (all of it) अस्थिरम् (unstable) स्वप्न-सङ्गम-सन्निभम् (like a meeting in a dream).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "O sage, whatever is seen in this world, moving or unmoving, is all unstable — like a meeting in a dream."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Like a dream.** The theme of this sarga: everything changes into its opposite. The comparison with a dream, here only a simile, will become the central teaching of the later books.
- **Gauḍapāda's Kārikā 2.31:** <span class="parallel">स्वप्नमाये यथा दृष्टे गन्धर्वनगरं यथा । तथा विश्वमिदं दृष्टं वेदान्तेषु विचक्षणैः ॥</span> — *as a dream and a magic show are seen, as a city in the clouds, so the wise in Vedānta see this world.*

## Verse 1.28.2 { #1-28-2 }

<p class="shloka">
शुष्कसागरसंकाशो निखातो योऽद्य दृश्यते ।<br>
स प्रातरभ्रसंवीतो नगः संपद्यते मुने ॥ १.२८.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), यः (what) अद्य (today) शुष्क-सागर-संकाशः निखातः दृश्यते (is seen as a hollow like a dried-up sea), सः (that) प्रातः (by morning) अभ्र-संवीतः नगः सम्पद्यते (becomes a mountain wrapped in clouds).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, what is seen today as a hollow, like a dried-up sea, becomes by morning a mountain wrapped in clouds."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Opposites become one another.** The hollow becomes the peak. Over geological time this is literally true; the verse speeds it up to show the instability of all forms.

## Verse 1.28.3 { #1-28-3 }

<p class="shloka">
यो वनव्यूहविस्तीर्णो विलीढगगनो महान् ।<br>
दिनैरेव स यात्युर्वीसमतां कूपतां च वा ॥ १.२८.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यः (that which) वन-व्यूह-विस्तीर्णः (spread with dense forests) विलीढ-गगनः महान् [अस्ति] (great, licking the sky), सः (that) दिनैः एव (in days) उर्वी-समतां (level with the ground) कूपतां च वा (or even into a pit) याति (goes).
</p>

**Translation:** "What is vast, spread with forests and touching the sky, in mere days is levelled with the ground, or sinks into a pit."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The great brought low.** Mountains and empires alike.

## Verse 1.28.4 { #1-28-4 }

<p class="shloka">
यदङ्गमद्य संवीतं कौशेयस्रग्विलेपनैः ।<br>
दिगम्बरं तदेव श्वो दूरे विशरिताऽवटे ॥ १.२८.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत् अङ्गम् (the body which) अद्य (today) कौशेय-स्रक्-विलेपनैः संवीतम् (is wrapped in silk, garlands and perfumes), तत् एव (that very one) श्वः (tomorrow) दिगम्बरम् (naked) दूरे अवटे विशरिता (lies scattered in a far-off pit).
</p>

**Translation:** "The body dressed today in silk, garlands and perfumes lies tomorrow naked, scattered in a distant pit."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Today and tomorrow.** The sharpest form of the contrast: one day apart.

## Verse 1.28.5 { #1-28-5 }

<p class="shloka">
यत्राद्य नगरं दृष्टं विचित्राचारचञ्चलम् ।<br>
तत्रैवोदेति दिवसैः संशून्यारण्यधर्मता ॥ १.२८.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत्र (where) अद्य (today) विचित्र-आचार-चञ्चलं नगरं दृष्टम् (a city is seen, bustling with varied ways), तत्र एव (right there) दिवसैः (in some days) संशून्य-अरण्य-धर्मता उदेति (the emptiness of a forest arises).
</p>

**Translation:** "Where today a city bustles with all its varied ways, in a few days the emptiness of a forest arises."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Lost cities.** History is full of them: great cities now forest or desert.

## Verse 1.28.6 { #1-28-6 }

<p class="shloka">
यः पुमानद्य तेजस्वी मण्डलान्यधितिष्ठति ।<br>
स भस्मकूटतां राजन्दिवसैरधिगच्छति ॥ १.२८.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राजन् (O king), यः पुमान् (the man who) अद्य (today) तेजस्वी (glorious) मण्डलानि अधितिष्ठति (rules over kingdoms), सः (he) दिवसैः (in some days) भस्म-कूटताम् अधिगच्छति (becomes a heap of ashes).
</p>

**Translation:** "O king, the man who today rules kingdoms in glory becomes, in a few days, a heap of ashes."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **राजन् — O king.** Rāma addresses his father Daśaratha directly. The words fall heavily: Daśaratha himself will soon die of grief.
- **A heap of ashes.** Compare Gītā 2.27, जातस्य हि ध्रुवो मृत्युः.

## Verse 1.28.7 { #1-28-7 }

<p class="shloka">
अरण्यानी महाभीमा या नभोमण्डलोपमा ।<br>
पताकाच्छादिताकाशा सैव संपद्यते पुरी ॥ १.२८.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
या (that which) महा-भीमा (very fearsome) नभः-मण्डल-उपमा अरण्यानी [अस्ति] (a great forest vast as the sky), सा एव (that very one) पताका-आच्छादित-आकाशा पुरी सम्पद्यते (becomes a city whose sky is covered with flags).
</p>

**Translation:** "A vast, fearsome forest, wide as the sky, becomes a city whose sky is filled with flags."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The reverse.** Forest becomes city as well as city becomes forest (1.28.5). Change runs in both directions.

## Verse 1.28.8 { #1-28-8 }

<p class="shloka">
या लतावलिता भीमा भात्यद्य विपिनावली ।<br>
दिवसैरेव सा याति पुनर्मरुमहीपदम् ॥ १.२८.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
या (that which) अद्य (today) लता-वलिता भीमा विपिन-आवली भाति (shines as a fearsome stretch of forest wrapped in creepers), सा (that) दिवसैः एव (in days) पुनः मरु-मही-पदं याति (becomes desert again).
</p>

**Translation:** "A fearsome stretch of forest, today wrapped in creepers, becomes desert again in a few days."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Forest to desert.** A reminder today of what humans themselves can do to the earth.

## Verse 1.28.9 { #1-28-9 }

<p class="shloka">
सलिलं स्थलतां याति स्थलीभवति वारिभूः ।<br>
विपर्यस्यति सर्वं हि सकाष्ठाम्बुतृणं जगत् ॥ १.२८.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सलिलं स्थलतां याति (water becomes land) वारि-भूः स्थली-भवति (watery ground becomes dry); हि (indeed) स-काष्ठ-अम्बु-तृणं सर्वं जगत् (the whole world, with its wood, water and grass) विपर्यस्यति (turns upside down).
</p>

**Translation:** "Water turns to land, and wetland to dry ground: the whole world, with its wood, water and grass, is turned upside down."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **विपर्यय — reversal.** The key word of the sarga.

## Verse 1.28.10 { #1-28-10 }

<p class="shloka">
अनित्यं यौवनं बाल्यं शरीरं द्रव्यसंचयाः ।<br>
भावाद्भावान्तरं यान्ति तरङ्गवदनारतम् ॥ १.२८.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यौवनं बाल्यं शरीरं द्रव्य-सञ्चयाः (youth, childhood, body and stores of wealth) अनित्यम् (are impermanent); [ते] तरङ्ग-वत् (like waves) अनारतं (ceaselessly) भावात् भाव-अन्तरं यान्ति (go from one state to another).
</p>

**Translation:** "Youth, childhood, the body and stores of wealth are impermanent; like waves, they pass ceaselessly from one state to another."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 2.14:** <span class="parallel">आगमापायिनोऽनित्याः</span> — *they come and go; they are impermanent.*

## Verse 1.28.11 { #1-28-11 }

<p class="shloka">
वातान्तर्दीपकशिखालोलं जगति जीवितम् ।<br>
तडित्स्फुरणसंकाशा पदार्थश्रीर्जगत्त्रये ॥ १.२८.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जगति जीवितम् (life in the world) वात-अन्तः-दीपक-शिखा-लोलम् (is unsteady as a lamp-flame in the wind); जगत्-त्रये (in the three worlds) पदार्थ-श्रीः (the splendour of things) तडित्-स्फुरण-संकाशा (is like a flash of lightning).
</p>

**Translation:** "Life in the world flickers like a lamp-flame in the wind; the splendour of things in the three worlds is like a flash of lightning."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints जगत्र्त्रये; corrected here to जगत्त्रये.
- **The lamp in the wind.** The Gītā uses the opposite image for the steady mind (6.19): <span class="parallel">यथा दीपो निवातस्थो नेङ्गते सोपमा स्मृता</span>. *as a lamp in a windless place does not flicker.* Life flickers; the yogī's mind does not.

## Verse 1.28.12 { #1-28-12 }

<p class="shloka">
विपर्यासमियं याति भूरिभूतपरम्परा ।<br>
बीजराशिरिवाजस्रं पूर्यमाणः पुनःपुनः ॥ १.२८.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इयं भूरि-भूत-परम्परा (this vast succession of beings) अजस्रं पुनः पुनः पूर्यमाणः बीज-राशिः इव (like a heap of seed, constantly refilled again and again) विपर्यासं याति (undergoes change).
</p>

**Translation:** "This vast succession of beings keeps changing, like a heap of seed that is refilled again and again."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Seeds.** Each being is a seed of the next. Compare Kaṭha 1.1.6: <span class="parallel">सस्यमिव मर्त्यः पच्यते सस्यमिवाजायते पुनः</span>. *like grain a mortal ripens, like grain he is born again.*

## Verse 1.28.13 { #1-28-13 }

<p class="shloka">
मनःपवनपर्यस्तभूरिभूतरजःपटा ।<br>
पातोत्पातपरावर्तपराभिनयभूषिता ॥ १.२८.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[इयं जागती स्थितिः] (this worldly state) मनः-पवन-पर्यस्त-भूरि-भूत-रजः-पटा (whose veil of the dust of many beings is tossed by the wind of the mind) पात-उत्पात-परावर्त-पर-अभिनय-भूषिता (adorned with the great acting of falls, rises and reversals) …
</p>

**Translation:** "This state of the world — its veil of the dust of countless beings tossed by the wind of the mind, adorned with the high drama of falls, rises and reversals …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The wind of the mind.** The mind stirs the dust of beings. The world's turmoil is the mind's turmoil. The sentence continues.

## Verse 1.28.14 { #1-28-14 }

<p class="shloka">
आलक्ष्यते स्थितिरियं जागती जनितभ्रमा ।<br>
नृत्तावेशविवृत्तेव संसारारभटीनटी ॥ १.२८.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जनित-भ्रमा इयं जागती स्थितिः (this worldly state, which produces delusion) नृत्त-आवेश-विवृत्ता संसार-आरभटी-नटी इव (like an actress in the violent drama of saṃsāra, whirling in the frenzy of her dance) आलक्ष्यते (appears).
</p>

**Translation:** "… this state of the world, which breeds delusion, looks like an actress in the violent drama of saṃsāra, whirling in the frenzy of her dance."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **आरभटी — the violent style of drama** in Indian dramaturgy. The world as a wild stage-show — as in Sarga 25.

## Verse 1.28.15 { #1-28-15 }

<p class="shloka">
गन्धर्वनगराकारविपर्यासविधायिनी ।<br>
अपाङ्गभङ्गुरोदारव्यवहारमनोरमा ॥ १.२८.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] गन्धर्व-नगर-आकार-विपर्यास-विधायिनी (making changes like the shapes of a cloud-city) अपाङ्ग-भङ्गुर-उदार-व्यवहार-मनोरमा (charming with grand dealings as brief as a sidelong glance) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… making changes like the shifting shapes of a city in the clouds, charming with grand affairs as fleeting as a sidelong glance …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The gandharva city** (1.18.61). The world's forms change like cloud-shapes.

## Verse 1.28.16 { #1-28-16 }

<p class="shloka">
तडित्तरलमालोकमातन्वाना पुनःपुनः ।<br>
संसाररचना राजन्नृत्तसक्तेव राजते ॥ १.२८.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राजन् (O king), संसार-रचना (the arrangement of saṃsāra) पुनः पुनः (again and again) तडित्-तरलम् आलोकम् आतन्वाना (spreading a light as fleeting as lightning) नृत्त-सक्ता इव राजते (shines as if absorbed in dance).
</p>

**Translation:** "O king, the arrangement of saṃsāra shines as if absorbed in dance, again and again spreading a light as fleeting as lightning."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Absorbed in its own dance.** The world has no purpose beyond its own movement — लीला.

## Verse 1.28.17 { #1-28-17 }

<p class="shloka">
दिवसास्ते महान्तस्ते संपदस्ताः क्रियाश्च ताः ।<br>
सर्वं स्मृतिपथं यातं यामो वयमपि क्षणात् ॥ १.२८.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ते दिवसाः (those days) ते महान्तः (those great people) ताः सम्पदः (those riches) ताः क्रियाः च (and those deeds) — सर्वं स्मृति-पथं यातम् (all have gone into memory); वयम् अपि (we too) क्षणात् यामः (go in a moment).
</p>

**Translation:** "Those days, those great people, those riches, those deeds — all have passed into memory. We too will go in a moment."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A famous verse of impermanence.** Simple and devastating. Everything that was is now only a memory, and soon we will be one too.

## Verse 1.28.18 { #1-28-18 }

<p class="shloka">
प्रत्यहं क्षयमायाति प्रत्यहं जायते पुनः ।<br>
अद्यापि हतरूपाया नान्तोऽस्या दग्धसंसृतेः ॥ १.२८.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा संसृतिः] प्रत्यहं क्षयम् आयाति (each day it wastes away) प्रत्यहं पुनः जायते (and each day is born again); अद्य अपि (even now) हत-रूपायाः अस्याः दग्ध-संसृतेः (of this wretched, accursed cycle) अन्तः न [अस्ति] (there is no end).
</p>

**Translation:** "Each day it dies and each day it is born again: even now there is no end to this wretched cycle of existence."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Endless.** Saṃsāra does not end by itself; it must be ended by knowledge.

## Verse 1.28.19 { #1-28-19 }

<p class="shloka">
तिर्यक्त्वं पुरुषा यान्ति तिर्यञ्चो नरतामपि ।<br>
देवाश्चादेवतां यान्ति किमिवेह विभो स्थिरम् ॥ १.२८.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विभो (O lord), पुरुषाः तिर्यक्त्वं यान्ति (humans become animals) तिर्यञ्चः नरताम् अपि (animals become humans) देवाः च अदेवतां यान्ति (gods lose their godhood); इह (here) किम् इव स्थिरम् (what is stable)?
</p>

**Translation:** "O lord, humans become animals, animals become humans, and gods lose their divinity. What here is stable?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rebirth.** The wheel of births moves through all forms of life. Even the gods fall (Gītā 9.21).

## Verse 1.28.20 { #1-28-20 }

<p class="shloka">
रचयन्रश्मिजालेन रात्र्यहानि पुनःपुनः ।<br>
अतिवाह्य रविः कालो विनाशावधिमीक्षते ॥ १.२८.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
रविः कालः (the sun, which is Time) रश्मि-जालेन (with its net of rays) पुनः पुनः रात्रि-अहानि रचयन् (making nights and days again and again) अतिवाह्य (passing them by) विनाश-अवधिम् ईक्षते (looks to the limit of destruction).
</p>

**Translation:** "The sun, which is Time itself, makes nights and days again and again with its net of rays, and passing them by, watches for the hour of destruction."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The sun as Time.** Each sunrise counts a day off every life.

## Verse 1.28.21 { #1-28-21 }

<p class="shloka">
ब्रह्मा विष्णुश्च रुद्रश्च सर्वा वा भूतजातयः ।<br>
नाशमेवानुधावन्ति सलिलानीव वाडवम् ॥ १.२८.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मा विष्णुः च रुद्रः च (Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra) सर्वाः वा भूत-जातयः (or all kinds of beings) सलिलानि वाडवम् इव (as waters [rush to] the submarine fire) नाशम् एव अनुधावन्ति (rush only to destruction).
</p>

**Translation:** "Brahmā, Viṣṇu and Rudra, and every kind of being, all rush towards destruction, as waters rush into the fire under the sea."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Even the Trinity** — as at 1.26.29. In their cosmic functions, they too belong to Time.

</p>

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The taste has gone.** This is वैराग्य exactly: not hatred, but loss of taste (रस). Compare Gītā 2.59: <span class="parallel">रसवर्जं रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते</span>. *even the taste falls away, once the Supreme is seen.* Rāma has lost the taste; he has not yet seen the Supreme.

## Verse 1.28.30 { #1-28-30 }

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आगम-अपाय-परया (bent on coming and going) क्षण-संस्थिति-नाशया (whose stability is destroyed in a moment) अनया (by this) संसारे (in the world) कः इव धीरः अपि (which person, even steady) न बिभेति हि (is not afraid)?
</p>

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Fear before freedom.** Rāma's fear is honest. The Upaniṣads promise its end: Taittirīya 2.9, <span class="parallel">आनन्दं ब्रह्मणो विद्वान् न बिभेति कुतश्चन</span>.

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आपदः क्षणम् आयान्ति (calamities come in a moment) क्षणम् आयान्ति सम्पदः (riches come in a moment); क्षणं जन्म क्षणं मृत्युः (birth is a moment, death is a moment); मुने (O sage), किम् इव न क्षणम् (what is not momentary)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Calamities come in a moment, riches come in a moment; birth is a moment, death is a moment. O sage, what is not momentary?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **What is not momentary?** The decisive question of the sarga. The answer is the one who sees the moments come and go — the witness, which is never itself a moment.
- **Buddhist momentariness** (क्षणिकवाद) taken to its limit, and then answered by the Advaita of the unchanging witness — [the synthesis of traditions](../index.md#how-the-traditions-meet).

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
प्रतिदिनम् (every day) सम-विषम-विपाकतः विभिन्नाः (differing by their even and uneven ripening) त्रिभुवन-भूत-परम्परा-फल-ओघाः (floods of fruit, the successions of beings in the three worlds) समय-पवन-पातिताः (felled by the wind of Time) आतत-संसृति-द्रुमेभ्यः (from the wide-spreading trees of saṃsāra) पतन्ति (fall).
</p>

**Translation:** Every day, floods of fruit — the generations of beings in the three worlds, ripening well or badly — are shaken down by the wind of Time from the wide-spreading trees of saṃsāra, and fall.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (puṣpitāgrā).
- **Fruit falling from the tree** — the image that closes the sarga. Each life ripens and falls. The teaching will show the tree itself to be an appearance in consciousness (Gītā 15.1–3, the inverted aśvattha).
