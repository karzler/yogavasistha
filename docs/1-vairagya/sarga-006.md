# Sarga 6

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · The arrival of Viśvāmitra (55 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 6 at a glance"
    Vālmīki continues his account to Bharadvāja.

    1. While the court waits, worried about Rāma, the sage **Viśvāmitra** arrives (1.6.1–1.6.13).
    2. King Daśaratha goes out on foot with Vasiṣṭha to meet him; a long description of the sage's radiance (1.6.14–1.6.26).
    3. The king's welcome, in a series of beautiful comparisons (1.6.27–1.6.51).
    4. The king's unconditional promise: "whatever you came for, consider it done" (1.6.52–1.6.54).
    5. A closing verse in a longer metre (1.6.55).

## Verse 1.6.1 { #1-6-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
इत्युक्ते मुनिनाथेन संदेहवति पार्थिवे ।<br>
खेदवत्यास्थिते मौनं किंचित्कालप्रतीक्षणे ॥ १.६.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुनि-नाथेन इति उक्ते (when the lord of sages had spoken thus), पार्थिवे (the king) सन्देहवति (full of doubt) खेदवति (downcast) मौनम् आस्थिते (remaining silent) किञ्चित्-काल-प्रतीक्षणे [सति] (waiting for a while) …
</p>

**Translation:** Vālmīki said: When the lord of sages had spoken thus, and the king, doubtful and downcast, remained silent, waiting for some time …

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A pause.** Vasiṣṭha has said there is a reason, but not what it is. The king waits. The text lets the silence stand before the next event.
- **The sentence continues** through 1.6.3.

## Verse 1.6.2 { #1-6-2 }

<p class="shloka">
परिखिन्नासु सर्वासु राज्ञीषु नृपसद्मसु ।<br>
स्थितासु सावधानासु रामचेष्टासु सर्वतः ॥ १.६.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्वासु राज्ञीषु (all the queens) नृप-सद्मसु (in the royal apartments) परिखिन्नासु (deeply troubled) सर्वतः राम-चेष्टासु सावधानासु स्थितासु (watching Rāma's every movement carefully) …
</p>

**Translation:** … and all the queens in the palace, deeply troubled, were watching Rāma's every movement …

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Mothers watching.** A tender detail: the household holds its breath around one silent young man.

## Verse 1.6.3 { #1-6-3 }

<p class="shloka">
एतस्मिन्नेव काले तु विश्वामित्र इति श्रुतः ।<br>
महर्षिरभ्यगाद्द्रष्टुं तमयोध्यानराधिपम् ॥ १.६.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एतस्मिन् एव काले तु (at that very time) विश्वामित्रः इति श्रुतः महर्षिः (the great seer known as Viśvāmitra) तम् अयोध्या-नर-अधिपं द्रष्टुम् अभ्यगात् (came to see that king of Ayodhyā).
</p>

**Translation:** … just then the great ṛṣi famed as Viśvāmitra came to Ayodhyā to see its ruler.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Perfect timing.** Just when the household is at a loss, a great sage appears. In the Yoga Vasiṣṭha, the teacher arrives when the student is ready.
- **Viśvāmitra** was born a king and became a brahmarṣi through tremendous austerity (see 1.6.45). He is the one who will later urge Rāma to speak and Vasiṣṭha to teach.

## Verse 1.6.4 { #1-6-4 }

<p class="shloka">
तस्य यज्ञोऽथ रक्षोभिस्तथा विलुलुपे किल ।<br>
मायावीर्यबलोन्मत्तैर्धर्मकार्यस्य धीमतः ॥ १.६.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (now) तस्य धीमतः धर्म-कार्यस्य (of that wise one devoted to dharma) यज्ञः (sacrifice) माया-वीर्य-बल-उन्मत्तैः रक्षोभिः (by rākṣasas, drunk on their magic, valour and strength) तथा विलुलुपे किल (had been disrupted, it is said).
</p>

**Translation:** For the sacrifice of that wise and dutiful sage had been disrupted by rākṣasas, intoxicated with their magic, valour and strength.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The outer story.** This is the familiar Rāmāyaṇa episode: Viśvāmitra needs Rāma to protect his sacrifice. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha will use it as the occasion for something much greater.

## Verse 1.6.5 { #1-6-5 }

<p class="shloka">
रक्षार्थं तस्य यज्ञस्य द्रष्टुमैच्छत्स पार्थिवम् ।<br>
नहि शक्नोत्यविघ्नेन समाप्तुं स मुनिः क्रतुम् ॥ १.६.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सः मुनिः (that sage) तस्य यज्ञस्य रक्षा-अर्थं (to protect that sacrifice) पार्थिवं द्रष्टुम् ऐच्छत् (wished to see the king); न हि सः मुनिः क्रतुम् अविघ्नेन समाप्तुं शक्नोति (for the sage could not complete the rite without obstruction).
</p>

**Translation:** To protect that sacrifice he wished to see the king, for the sage could not complete his rite without interference.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Even a great sage needs help.** Viśvāmitra, for all his power, asks a king for aid. Wisdom and action, sage and king, depend on each other — the two wings of 1.1.7.

## Verse 1.6.6 { #1-6-6 }

<p class="shloka">
ततस्तेषां विनाशार्थमुद्यतस्तपसां निधिः ।<br>
विश्वामित्रो महातेजा अयोध्यामभ्यगात्पुरीम् ॥ १.६.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः (therefore) तेषां विनाश-अर्थम् उद्यतः (set on their destruction) तपसां निधिः (that treasure of austerities) महा-तेजाः विश्वामित्रः (the radiant Viśvāmitra) अयोध्यां पुरीम् अभ्यगात् (came to the city of Ayodhyā).
</p>

**Translation:** So, set on destroying them, Viśvāmitra, that radiant treasury of austerity, came to the city of Ayodhyā.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **तपसां निधिः.** A "treasure-house of austerities". The sage's power comes from tapas, which the text respects, even as it will teach that knowledge goes further.
- **Reading note.** The online page displays this verse with a layout error and no verse number in Devanagari; the text is taken from its transliteration line.

## Verse 1.6.7 { #1-6-7 }

<p class="shloka">
स राज्ञो दर्शनाकाङ्क्षी द्वाराध्यक्षानुवाच ह ।<br>
शीघ्रमाख्यात मां प्राप्तं कौशिकं गाधिनः सुतम् ॥ १.६.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राज्ञः दर्शन-आकाङ्क्षी (wishing to see the king) सः (he) द्वार-अध्यक्षान् उवाच ह (said to the doorkeepers): शीघ्रम् आख्यात (announce quickly) मां गाधिनः सुतं कौशिकं प्राप्तम् (that I, Kauśika, son of Gādhi, have come).
</p>

**Translation:** Eager for an audience with the king, he told the keepers of the gate: "Go at once and say that Kauśika, Gādhi's son, is here."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kauśika, son of Gādhi.** Viśvāmitra names his royal lineage. He was born a kṣatriya, like Rāma.

## Verse 1.6.8 { #1-6-8 }

<p class="shloka">
तस्य तद्वचनं श्रुत्वा द्वास्था राजगृहं ययुः ।<br>
संभ्रान्तमनसः सर्वे तेन वाक्येन चोदिताः ॥ १.६.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तस्य तत् वचनं श्रुत्वा (hearing his words) सर्वे द्वाःस्थाः (all the doorkeepers) सम्भ्रान्त-मनसः (flustered) तेन वाक्येन चोदिताः (urged by his words) राज-गृहं ययुः (went to the palace).
</p>

**Translation:** Hearing his words, the doorkeepers, flustered, hurried to the palace as he had told them.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The sage's name alone causes a stir.** Viśvāmitra's reputation precedes him, for his temper as well as his power (see Sarga 9).

## Verse 1.6.9 { #1-6-9 }

<p class="shloka">
ते गत्वा राजसदनं विश्वामित्रमृषिं ततः ।<br>
प्राप्तमावेदयामासुः प्रतीहाराः पतेस्तदा ॥ १.६.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ते प्रतीहाराः (those doorkeepers) राज-सदनं गत्वा (going to the palace) ततः (then) तदा पत्युः (to their master) विश्वामित्रम् ऋषिं प्राप्तम् आवेदयामासुः (announced that the seer Viśvāmitra had arrived).
</p>

**Translation:** The gatekeepers made their way into the royal hall and informed their lord that the ṛṣi Viśvāmitra had arrived.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Narrative link.** The arrival is announced step by step, building the sense of a great event.

## Verse 1.6.10 { #1-6-10 }

<p class="shloka">
अथास्थानगतं भूपं राजमण्डलमालिनम् ।<br>
समुपेत्य त्वरायुक्तो याष्टीकोऽसौ व्यजिज्ञपत् ॥ १.६.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) असौ याष्टीकः (the staff-bearer) त्वरा-युक्तः (in haste) आस्थान-गतं (seated in the assembly hall) राज-मण्डल-मालिनं भूपं (the king, surrounded by a circle of kings) समुपेत्य व्यजिज्ञपत् (approached and announced):
</p>

**Translation:** Then the staff-bearer hurried to the king, who sat in the assembly hall surrounded by other kings, and announced:

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The assembly.** Daśaratha sits among kings. The coming scene will be public: the whole court will witness Rāma's questions and Vasiṣṭha's teaching.

## Verse 1.6.11 { #1-6-11 }

<p class="shloka">
देव द्वारि महातेजा बालभास्करभासुरः ।<br>
ज्वालारुणजटाजूटः पुमाञ्छ्रीमानवस्थितः ॥ १.६.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
देव (Your Majesty), द्वारि (at the door) महा-तेजाः (a man of great radiance) बाल-भास्कर-भासुरः (shining like the rising sun) ज्वाला-अरुण-जटा-जूटः (his matted hair red as flame) श्रीमान् पुमान् अवस्थितः (a glorious man stands).
</p>

**Translation:** "Your Majesty, at the door stands a glorious man of great radiance, shining like the rising sun, his matted hair red as flame."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Through another's eyes.** The doorkeeper does not know the sage; he describes what he sees. Radiance (तेजस्) is the visible sign of inner power.

## Verse 1.6.12 { #1-6-12 }

<p class="shloka">
सभासुरपताकान्तं साश्वेभपुरुषायुधम् ।<br>
कृतवांस्तं प्रदेशं यस्तेजोभिः कीर्णकाञ्चनम् ॥ १.६.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यः (who) तेजोभिः (by his radiance) तं स-भासुर-पताका-अन्तं स-अश्व-इभ-पुरुष-आयुधं प्रदेशं (that area with its shining banners, horses, elephants, men and weapons) कीर्ण-काञ्चनं कृतवान् (has made as if strewn with gold).
</p>

**Translation:** "His radiance has turned the whole place — the bright banners, horses, elephants, men and weapons — as if to gold."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Transforming what is near.** The sage's presence changes the look of everything around him. The text will later say the same of the wise: the world is seen differently in their light.

## Verse 1.6.13 { #1-6-13 }

<p class="shloka">
वीक्ष्यमाणे तु याष्टीके निवेदयति राजनि ।<br>
विश्वामित्रो मुनिः प्राप्त इत्यनुद्धतया गिरा ॥ १.६.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
याष्टीके वीक्ष्यमाणे तु (as the staff-bearer was still looking on) राजनि निवेदयति [सति] (and was announcing to the king), अनुद्धतया गिरा (in a calm voice) विश्वामित्रः मुनिः प्राप्तः इति ("the sage Viśvāmitra has come") …
</p>

**Translation:** As the staff-bearer gazed and announced in a calm voice, "The sage Viśvāmitra has come" …

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The name is spoken.** The description of 1.6.11–12 is now given its name. The sentence continues into the next verse.

## Verse 1.6.14 { #1-6-14 }

<p class="shloka">
इति याष्टीकवचनमाकर्ण्य नृपसत्तमः ।<br>
स समन्त्री ससामन्तः प्रोत्तस्थौ हेमविष्टरात् ॥ १.६.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इति याष्टीक-वचनम् आकर्ण्य (hearing the staff-bearer's words) सः नृप-सत्तमः (that best of kings) स-मन्त्री स-सामन्तः (with his ministers and vassals) हेम-विष्टरात् प्रोत्तस्थौ (rose from his golden throne).
</p>

**Translation:** … hearing these words, the best of kings rose from his golden throne together with his ministers and vassals.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The king rises.** Power stands up for wisdom. The golden throne is left behind to greet a man with matted hair and a water-pot.

## Verse 1.6.15 { #1-6-15 }

<p class="shloka">
पदातिरेव सहसा राज्ञां वृन्देन मालितः ।<br>
वसिष्ठवामदेवाभ्यां सह सामन्तसंस्तुतः ॥ १.६.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पदातिः एव सहसा (at once, on foot) राज्ञां वृन्देन मालितः (surrounded by the other kings) वसिष्ठ-वामदेवाभ्यां सह (with Vasiṣṭha and Vāmadeva) सामन्त-संस्तुतः (praised by his vassals) …
</p>

**Translation:** At once, on foot, surrounded by the other kings, with Vasiṣṭha and Vāmadeva, and attended by his vassals …

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **पदाति — on foot.** The king does not ride or send for the sage; he walks to him. Humility before a teacher (compare Gītā 4.34, प्रणिपात).

## Verse 1.6.16 { #1-6-16 }

<p class="shloka">
जगाम तत्र यत्रासौ विश्वामित्रो महामुनिः ।<br>
ददर्श मुनिशार्दूलं द्वारभूमाववस्थितम् ॥ १.६.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत्र असौ महा-मुनिः विश्वामित्रः (where that great sage Viśvāmitra was) तत्र जगाम (he went), द्वार-भूमौ अवस्थितं मुनि-शार्दूलं ददर्श (and saw that tiger among sages standing at the gate).
</p>

**Translation:** … he made his way to the spot where Viśvāmitra, the great muni, stood, and found that tiger of sages waiting at the threshold.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Meeting at the threshold.** The sage waits at the door; the king comes out. A threshold is where two worlds meet — the royal and the ascetic, the worldly and the spiritual.

## Verse 1.6.17 { #1-6-17 }

<p class="shloka">
केनापि कारणेनोर्वीतलमर्कमुपागतम् ।<br>
ब्राह्मेण तेजसाक्रान्तं क्षात्रेण च महौजसा ॥ १.६.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
केन अपि कारणेन (for some reason) उर्वी-तलम् उपागतम् अर्कम् [इव] (like the sun come down to earth), ब्राह्मेण तेजसा (with a brāhmaṇa's spiritual radiance) महा-ओजसा क्षात्रेण च (and a kṣatriya's great might) आक्रान्तम् (filled).
</p>

**Translation:** He looked like the sun come down to earth for some purpose, filled with both a brāhmaṇa's spiritual radiance and a warrior's great might.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Both natures in one person — Gītā 18.42–43:**
    - <span class="parallel">शमो दमस्तपः शौचं क्षान्तिरार्जवमेव च । ज्ञानं विज्ञानमास्तिक्यं ब्रह्मकर्म स्वभावजम् ॥</span>
    - <span class="parallel">शौर्यं तेजो धृतिर्दाक्ष्यं युद्धे चाप्यपलायनम् । दानमीश्वरभावश्च क्षात्रं कर्म स्वभावजम् ॥</span>
    - *Calm, self-control, austerity, purity, patience, honesty, knowledge, realisation, faith: these are the brāhmaṇa's natural qualities. Valour, vigour, steadiness, skill, not fleeing in battle, generosity, leadership: these are the kṣatriya's.*
    - Viśvāmitra, a king who became a brahmarṣi, carries both. He is a living example of the two wings (1.1.7).

## Verse 1.6.18 { #1-6-18 }

<p class="shloka">
जराजरठया नित्यं तपःप्रसररूक्षया ।<br>
जटावल्या वृतस्कन्धं ससंध्याभ्रमिवाचलम् ॥ १.६.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जरा-जरठया (hardened by age) तपः-प्रसर-रूक्षया (rough from long austerity) जटा-वल्या (with masses of matted locks) नित्यं वृत-स्कन्धं (his shoulders always covered) स-संध्या-अभ्रम् अचलम् इव (like a mountain with twilight clouds).
</p>

**Translation:** His shoulders were covered with masses of matted hair, hardened by age and rough from long austerity, like a mountain wrapped in twilight clouds.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The mountain image.** Steady, ancient, crowned with colour. The sage is described in terms of nature, not of rank.

## Verse 1.6.19 { #1-6-19 }

<p class="shloka">
उपशान्तं च कान्तं च दीप्तमप्रतिघाति च ।<br>
निभृतं चोर्जिताकारं दधानं भास्वरं वपुः ॥ १.६.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
उपशान्तं च कान्तं च (calm and lovely) दीप्तम् अप्रतिघाति च (blazing and irresistible) निभृतं च ऊर्जित-आकारं च (quiet yet powerful in form) भास्वरं वपुः दधानम् (bearing a radiant body).
</p>

**Translation:** His radiant form was calm yet lovely, blazing yet unopposable, quiet yet powerful.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Opposites held together.** Calm and blazing, quiet and powerful. The free person is not one-sided. Compare the description of the steady-minded in Gītā 2.55–72: at peace, yet fully capable of action.
- **उपशान्त.** Calm, the same root as उपशम (1.1.1), which gives its name to the fifth prakaraṇa.

## Verse 1.6.20 { #1-6-20 }

<p class="shloka">
पेशलेनातिभीमेन प्रसन्नेनाकुलेन च ।<br>
गम्भीरेणातिपूर्णेन तेजसा रञ्जितप्रभम् ॥ १.६.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा अप्रजावतः (as for one without children) इष्ट-दार-सम्पर्कात् पुत्र-जन्म (the birth of a son from a beloved wife) स्वप्न-दृष्ट-अर्थ-लाभः च (and gaining a treasure seen in a dream), तथा भवत्-आगमनम् (so is your coming).
</p>

**Translation:** "It is like a son born to one who had none, like gaining a treasure seen in a dream."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The treasure seen in a dream.** A hint, perhaps unintended by the king, of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's teaching that the world itself is like a dream. Even the joys we gain have a dreamlike quality.

## Verse 1.6.41 { #1-6-41 }

<p class="shloka">
यथेप्सितेन संयोग इष्टस्यागमनं यथा ।<br>
प्रणष्टस्य यथा लाभो भवदागमनं तथा ॥ १.६.४१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा ईप्सितेन संयोगः (as union with one longed for) यथा इष्टस्य आगमनम् (as the arrival of a loved one) यथा प्रणष्टस्य लाभः (as finding what was lost), तथा भवत्-आगमनम् (so is your coming).
</p>

**Translation:** "It is like meeting one we long for, like the arrival of a loved one, like finding what was lost."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Finding what was lost.** The deepest of the images: the Self is often described as something we have never really lost, only forgotten (compare 1.3.2, विस्मरण).

## Verse 1.6.42 { #1-6-42 }

<p class="shloka">
यथा हर्षो नभोगत्या मृतस्य पुनरागमात् ।<br>
तथा त्वदागमाद्ब्रह्मन्स्वागतं ते महामुने ॥ १.६.४२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा नभो-गत्या हर्षः (as the joy of flying through the sky) [यथा] मृतस्य पुनर्-आगमात् [हर्षः] (as the joy at one returned from death), तथा त्वत्-आगमात् (so from your coming). ब्रह्मन् महा-मुने (O seer, great sage), ते स्वागतम् (welcome to you).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like the joy of flying through the sky, like the joy of someone returned from the dead — such is my joy at your coming. Welcome, great sage."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Returning from death.** The last of the images. The king does not know that his son will indeed be brought back to life — not from bodily death, but from the deadness of despair.

## Verse 1.6.43 { #1-6-43 }

<p class="shloka">
ब्रह्मलोकनिवासो हि कस्य न प्रीतिमावहेत् ।<br>
मुने तवागमस्तद्वत्सत्यमेव ब्रवीमि ते ॥ १.६.४३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्म-लोक-निवासः (living in Brahmā's world) कस्य प्रीतिं न आवहेत् (whom would it not delight)? मुने (O sage), तव आगमः तद्वत् (your coming is like that); सत्यम् एव ते ब्रवीमि (I speak the truth to you).
</p>

**Translation:** "Whom would it not delight to live in Brahmā's world? Your coming is like that, O sage. I tell you truly."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The king's highest image is heaven.** Compare Ariṣṭanemi, who turned heaven down (1.1.40). The king still measures joy by heaven; Rāma will soon measure everything differently.

## Verse 1.6.44 { #1-6-44 }

<p class="shloka">
कश्च ते परमः कामः किं च ते करवाण्यहम् ।<br>
पात्रभूतोऽसि मे विप्र प्राप्तः परमधार्मिकः ॥ १.६.४४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कः च ते परमः कामः (what is your highest wish)? किं च ते अहं करवाणि (what can I do for you)? विप्र (O brāhmaṇa), परम-धार्मिकः प्राप्तः (you, supremely righteous, have come) मे पात्र-भूतः असि (you are a worthy recipient for me).
</p>

**Translation:** "What is your greatest wish? What can I do for you? O brāhmaṇa, you, most righteous of all, have come to me; you are the worthiest recipient of any gift."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **An open offer.** The king asks what he can give, before knowing what will be asked. Compare Brahmā's boon (1.2.7). Offers made before knowing the request will test the giver (Sarga 8).
- **पात्र — a worthy vessel.** In Indian tradition, giving to a worthy person is itself a blessing to the giver (compare Gītā 17.20 on giving to the right person, at the right time and place).

## Verse 1.6.45 { #1-6-45 }

<p class="shloka">
पूर्वं राजर्षिशब्देन तपसा द्योतितप्रभः ।<br>
ब्रह्मर्षित्वमनुप्राप्तः पूज्योऽसि भगवन्मया ॥ १.६.४५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भगवन् (revered one), पूर्वं (formerly) राजर्षि-शब्देन (by the title of royal sage) तपसा द्योतित-प्रभः (your splendour shone through austerity); ब्रह्मर्षित्वम् अनुप्राप्तः (now, having attained the status of brahmarṣi) मया पूज्यः असि (you are to be revered by me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Revered one, once you were called a royal sage, and your splendour shone through austerity. Now you have become a brahmarṣi, and I must honour you."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **From king to brahmarṣi.** Viśvāmitra's famous story: born a king, he reached the highest spiritual rank by his own effort. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha will later praise self-effort (पौरुष) above fate; Viśvāmitra is its living example.
- **Gītā 4.2:** <span class="parallel">इमं राजर्षयो विदुः</span> — *the royal sages knew this* (see 1.1.23).

## Verse 1.6.46 { #1-6-46 }

<p class="shloka">
गङ्गाजलाभिषेकेण यथा प्रीतिर्भवेन्मम ।<br>
तथा त्वद्दर्शनात्प्रीतिरन्तः शीतयतीव माम् ॥ १.६.४६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा गङ्गा-जल-अभिषेकेण मम प्रीतिः भवेत् (as I would feel joy bathing in the Gaṅgā), तथा त्वत्-दर्शनात् प्रीतिः (so the joy of seeing you) माम् अन्तः शीतयति इव (seems to cool me within).
</p>

**Translation:** "As bathing in the Gaṅgā gives me joy, so the sight of you seems to cool me within."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Cooling.** In Indian thought, peace is often pictured as coolness, and agitation as heat. The sage's presence cools; Rāma's coming inquiry will seek a lasting coolness (compare गतज्वर, "free of fever", 1.2.31).

## Verse 1.6.47 { #1-6-47 }

<p class="shloka">
विगतेच्छाभयक्रोधो वीतरागो निरामयः ।<br>
इदमत्यद्भुतं ब्रह्मन्यद्भवान्मामुपागतः ॥ १.६.४७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O seer), इदम् अति-अद्भुतं (this is most wonderful), यत् (that) विगत-इच्छा-भय-क्रोधः (free from desire, fear and anger) वीत-रागः निरामयः (without attachment, untroubled) भवान् माम् उपागतः (you have come to me).
</p>

**Translation:** "It is most wonderful, O seer, that you — free from desire, fear and anger, without attachment, untroubled — have come to me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 5.28 — the same words:**
    - <span class="parallel">यतेन्द्रियमनोबुद्धिर्मुनिर्मोक्षपरायणः । विगतेच्छाभयक्रोधो यः सदा मुक्त एव सः ॥</span>
    - *The sage whose senses, mind and understanding are controlled, intent on liberation, free from desire, fear and anger — he is ever free.*
    - विगतेच्छाभयक्रोधः appears in both verses, letter for letter. The king, perhaps without knowing it, describes Viśvāmitra in the Gītā's words for the liberated.
- **Why it is wonderful.** One who wants nothing has come asking for something. The king is puzzled — and the answer (protection of the sacrifice) will show that the free still act for the world's good.

## Verse 1.6.48 { #1-6-48 }

<p class="shloka">

चन्द्रबिम्ब इवोन्मग्नं वेदवेद्यविदांवर ॥ १.६.४८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वेद-वेद्य-विदां वर (best among those who know what the Vedas teach), अहं च (and I) शुभ-क्षेत्र-गतम् (having come to an auspicious place) आत्मानम् अप-कल्मषम् (myself free of stain) उन्मग्नं चन्द्र-बिम्बम् इव (like the moon's disc risen [from clouds]) [पश्यामि] (see).
</p>

**Translation:** "Best of those who know what the Vedas teach, I feel myself as if I had come to a holy place, cleansed of all stain, like the moon's disc emerging from clouds."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The moon emerging.** The image of something pure that was only hidden. Compare 1.2.24, where delusion is a stain (मल) on what is already pure.

## Verse 1.6.49 { #1-6-49 }

<p class="shloka">
साक्षादिव ब्रह्मणो मे तवाभ्यागमनं मतम् ।<br>
पूतोऽस्म्यनुगृहीतश्च तवाभ्यागमनान्मुने ॥ १.६.४९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तव अभ्यागमनं (your coming) मे (to me) साक्षात् ब्रह्मणः इव मतम् (seems like that of Brahmā himself); मुने (O sage), तव अभ्यागमनात् (from your coming) पूतः अस्मि अनुगृहीतः च (I am purified and blessed).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Purified by presence.** The theme of 1.6.27–49: the very arrival of a wise person transforms. The text prepares us to see how much more Vasiṣṭha's teaching will transform Rāma.

## Verse 1.6.50 { #1-6-50 }

<p class="shloka">
त्वदागमनपुण्येन साधो यदनुरञ्जितम् ।<br>
अद्य मे सफलं जन्म जीवितं तत्सुजीवितम् ॥ १.६.५० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
साधो (good one), त्वत्-आगमन-पुण्येन (by the merit of your coming) अद्य (today) यत् अनुरञ्जितं (being gladdened) मे जन्म स-फलम् (my birth is fruitful); तत् जीवितं सु-जीवितम् (that life is well lived).
</p>

**Translation:** "Today, by the blessing of your coming, my birth has borne fruit; my life is a life well lived."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A life made meaningful by one meeting.** The king's words are courtly, but they point to something real: a single encounter can give meaning to a whole life.
- **A question to sit with.** Which meetings have made my life feel well lived?

## Verse 1.6.51 { #1-6-51 }

<p class="shloka">
त्वामिहाभ्यागतं दृष्ट्वा प्रतिपूज्य प्रणम्य च ।<br>
आत्मन्येव नमाम्यन्तर्दृष्ट्वेन्दुं जलधिर्यथा ॥ १.६.५१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह अभ्यागतं त्वां दृष्ट्वा (seeing you come here) प्रतिपूज्य प्रणम्य च (honouring and bowing to you), अन्तः इन्दुं दृष्ट्वा जलधिः यथा (as the ocean on seeing the moon) आत्मनि एव नमामि (I bow within myself).
</p>

**Translation:** "Seeing you here, honouring you and bowing to you, I bow within myself too, as the ocean swells within on seeing the moon."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The tide.** The ocean rises towards the moon without moving from its place. Outer bowing becomes an inner bowing — devotion that moves the heart, not only the body.
- **आत्मनि एव नमामि.** "I bow within the Self." A hint, perhaps, that the true honour is given inwardly, to the Self that sage and king share.

## Verse 1.6.52 { #1-6-52 }

<p class="shloka">
यत्कार्यं येन वार्थेन प्राप्तोऽसि मुनिपुङ्गव ।<br>
कृतमित्येव तद्विद्धि मान्योऽसीति सदा मम ॥ १.६.५२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुनि-पुङ्गव (bull among sages), यत् कार्यं (whatever task) येन वा अर्थेन (or whatever purpose) प्राप्तः असि (you have come for), तत् कृतम् इति एव विद्धि (know it as already done), [यतः] सदा मम मान्यः असि (for you are always honoured by me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Foremost of munis, whatever work or aim has brought you here, count it as accomplished; you have always had my reverence."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The promise.** "Consider it done." Daśaratha commits before knowing what is asked. This promise drives Sargas 7–9.
- **An echo of Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.1.** Naciketas' father also made a grand offering and was held to it, when his son asked "to whom will you give me?" Promises given in enthusiasm can bind in ways one did not foresee.

<p class="shloka">
स्वकार्ये न विमर्शं त्वं कर्तुमर्हसि कौशिक ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कौशिक (Kauśika), त्वं स्व-कार्ये विमर्शं कर्तुं न अर्हसि (you need not hesitate about your purpose). भगवन् (revered one), त्वयि (for you) यत् प्रतिपद्यते (whatever is wanted) मे अदेयं न अस्ति (there is nothing I would not give).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **"Nothing I would not give."** The king will soon discover that there is one thing: his son. The text gently shows how attachment hides in our generosity.

## Verse 1.6.54 { #1-6-54 }

<p class="shloka">
कार्यस्य न विचारं त्वं कर्तुमर्हसि धर्मतः ।<br>
कर्ता चाहमशेषं ते दैवतं परमं भवान् ॥ १.६.५४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
धर्मतः (as a matter of dharma) त्वं कार्यस्य विचारं कर्तुं न अर्हसि (you need not weigh the task). अहं च ते अशेषं कर्ता (I will do all of it for you); भवान् परमं दैवतम् (you are the highest deity).
</p>

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Taittirīya Upaniṣad 1.11.2:** <span class="parallel">अतिथिदेवो भव</span> — *treat the guest as God.* Daśaratha calls Viśvāmitra परमं दैवतम्, the highest deity.
- **Total commitment.** The king's words leave no room for later refusal. This is the setting for the drama of Sargas 7–9.

## Verse 1.6.55 { #1-6-55 }

<p class="shloka">
इदमतिमधुरं निशम्य वाक्यं श्रुतिसुखमात्मविदा विनीतमुक्तम् ।<br>
प्रथितगुणयशागुणैर्विशिष्टं मुनिवृषभः परमं जगाम हर्षम् ॥ १.६.५५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुनि-वृषभः (the bull among sages) आत्म-विदा विनीतम् उक्तम् (spoken humbly by one who knows the Self) श्रुति-सुखं (pleasant to the ear) प्रथित-गुण-यशा-गुणैः विशिष्टम् (distinguished by famed virtues) इदम् अति-मधुरं वाक्यं निशम्य (hearing these very sweet words) परमं हर्षं जगाम (felt the greatest joy).
</p>

**Translation:** Hearing these very sweet words, pleasant to the ear and spoken humbly by one who knew the Self, rich in virtue and renown, the great sage felt the greatest joy.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (puṣpitāgrā).
- **आत्मविद् — "one who knows the Self".** The narrator calls Daśaratha an आत्मविद्. A surprising title for a king about to be overwhelmed by attachment (Sarga 8). Perhaps a reminder that knowing and living what one knows are different things — the two wings again.
