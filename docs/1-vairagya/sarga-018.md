# Sarga 18

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · On the body (deha) (62 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 18 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: on the body (deha).

    1. The body: changing, neither inert nor conscious, the cause of delusion (1.18.1–1.18.4).
    2. The body as a tree: smiles its flowers, arms its branches, the senses its birds, the ego a vulture nesting in it (1.18.5–1.18.17).
    3. The body as a house, with the refrain "this body-house is not dear to me" (1.18.18–1.18.35).
    4. What is wealth, kingdom or body? Time cuts all down. "Before it abandons me, I will abandon it" (1.18.36–1.18.52).
    5. The real teaching: "I do not belong to the body, the body is not mine, I am not this" (1.18.53). Rāma casts the body aside like grass and abides at ease (1.18.54–1.18.62).

## Verse 1.18.1 { #1-18-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
आर्द्रान्त्रतन्त्रीगहनो विकारी परिपातवान् ।<br>
देहः स्फुरति संसारे सोऽपि दुःखाय केवलम् ॥ १.१८.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आर्द्र-अन्त्र-तन्त्री-गहनः (dense with moist strings of entrails) विकारी (changing) परिपातवान् (bound to fall) देहः (the body) संसारे स्फुरति (throbs in the world); सः अपि (it too) केवलं दुःखाय (is only for sorrow).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "The body, dense with moist strands of entrails, always changing and bound to fall, throbs in the world — and it too is only for sorrow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **देह, शरीर, काय — the body.** After wealth, life, ego, mind and craving, Rāma now turns to the body.
- **Misplaced aversion again.** As with 1.10.46, Rāma's revulsion is real and honest, but not yet wisdom. The Gītā does not ask us to hate the body but to know we are not it (2.18, below at 1.18.53). Rāma's disgust is a stage on the way: the body loses its glamour before it is seen rightly.
- **A traditional theme.** Meditation on the body's impurity (अशुचि-भावना in Jainism, असुभ in Buddhism) is a recognised practice for weakening attachment. Here the Yoga Vasiṣṭha draws on that shared Indian tradition (your third reason).

## Verse 1.18.2 { #1-18-2 }

<p class="shloka">
अज्ञोऽपि तज्ज्ञसदृशो वलितात्मचमत्कृतिः ।<br>
युक्त्या भव्योऽप्यभव्योऽपि न जडो नापि चेतनः ॥ १.१८.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[देहः] (the body) अज्ञः अपि (though ignorant) तज्ज्ञ-सदृशः (seems like one who knows) वलित-आत्म-चमत्कृतिः (enveloping the wonder of the Self) युक्त्या भव्यः अपि अभव्यः अपि (by reasoning both real and unreal) न जडः न अपि चेतनः (neither inert nor conscious).
</p>

**Translation:** "The body is ignorant, yet seems to know; it wraps round the wonder of the Self. By reasoning it is both real and unreal; it is neither inert nor conscious."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Neither inert nor conscious.** A precise philosophical point. The body by itself is matter, yet it moves and seems to know, because consciousness shines through it.
- **आत्मचमत्कृति — the wonder of the Self.** The Self is the real wonder; the body only borrows its light.
- **Kena Upaniṣad 1.2:** <span class="parallel">श्रोत्रस्य श्रोत्रं मनसो मनो यत्… चक्षुषश्चक्षुः</span> — *the ear of the ear, the mind of the mind, the eye of the eye.* The body's knowing is borrowed from the Self.

## Verse 1.18.3 { #1-18-3 }

<p class="shloka">
जडाजडदृशोर्मध्ये दोलायितदुराशयः ।<br>
अविवेकी विमूढात्मा मोहमेव प्रयच्छति ॥ १.१८.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जड-अजड-दृशोः मध्ये (between the views of the inert and the conscious) दोलायित-दुर्-आशयः (with a wretched mind swinging) अविवेकी (undiscerning) विमूढ-आत्मा (deluded) [देहः] मोहम् एव प्रयच्छति (gives only delusion).
</p>

**Translation:** "Swinging between being inert and being conscious, undiscerning and deluded, the body gives only delusion."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Swinging.** We do not know whether we are the body or the consciousness in it, and so we swing between the two. Discernment (विवेक) ends the swinging.
- **Gītā 13.1:** <span class="parallel">इदं शरीरं कौन्तेय क्षेत्रमित्यभिधीयते</span> — *this body is called the field, Kaunteya; the one who knows it is the knower of the field.* The Gītā gives the discernment that ends the swing.

## Verse 1.18.4 { #1-18-4 }

<p class="shloka">
स्तोकेनानन्दमायाति स्तोकेनायाति खेदिताम् ।<br>
नास्ति देहसमः शोच्यो नीचो गुणबहिष्कृतः ॥ १.१८.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[देहः] स्तोकेन आनन्दम् आयाति (comes to joy by a little) स्तोकेन खेदिताम् आयाति (and to sorrow by a little); देह-समः (like the body) शोच्यः नीचः गुण-बहिष्कृतः (pitiable, low, devoid of merit) न अस्ति (there is nothing).
</p>

**Translation:** "A little makes the body happy; a little makes it miserable. There is nothing as pitiable, as low, as devoid of merit as the body."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 2.14:** <span class="parallel">मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः । आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत ॥</span> — *contacts of the senses give cold and heat, pleasure and pain; they come and go; endure them, Bhārata.*

## Verse 1.18.5 { #1-18-5 }

<p class="shloka">
आगमापायिना नित्यं दन्तकेसरशालिना ।<br>
विकासस्मितपुष्पेण प्रतिक्षणमलंकृतः ॥ १.१८.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[देहः] नित्यम् आगम-अपायिना (by what comes and goes always) दन्त-केसर-शालिना (with teeth as its filaments) विकास-स्मित-पुष्पेण (with the flower of a broad smile) प्रतिक्षणम् अलंकृतः (is adorned every moment).
</p>

**Translation:** "The body is adorned each moment with the flower of a smile, which comes and goes, its teeth the stamens."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The body as a tree** (1.18.5–1.18.17). Rāma builds an extended metaphor: smiles are its flowers, arms its branches, eyes its bees. The tree image will become the house image (1.18.18–1.18.34).

## Verse 1.18.6 { #1-18-6 }

<p class="shloka">
भुजशाखो घनस्कन्धो द्विजस्तम्भशुभस्थितिः ।<br>
लोचनालिविलाक्रान्तः शिरःपीठबृहत्फलः ॥ १.१८.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[देहः] भुज-शाखः (with arms as branches) घन-स्कन्धः (with a thick trunk) द्विज-स्तम्भ-शुभ-स्थितिः (well set with teeth as pillars) लोचन-अलि-विल-आक्रान्तः (filled with bee-hollows of eyes) शिरः-पीठ-बृहत्-फलः (with the head as its great fruit).
</p>

**Translation:** "Its branches are arms, its trunk is thick, its teeth are pillars, its eye-sockets hollows where bees dwell, and the head its great fruit."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Bee-hollows.** The eyes, dark and darting, are like bees in a hollow.

## Verse 1.18.7 { #1-18-7 }

<p class="shloka">
श्रवदन्तरसग्रस्तो हस्तपादसुपल्लवः ।<br>
गुल्मवान्कार्यसंघातो विहङ्गमकृतास्पदः ॥ १.१८.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[देहः] स्रवत्-अन्तर-रस-ग्रस्तः (full of saps oozing within) हस्त-पाद-सु-पल्लवः (with hands and feet as fine shoots) गुल्मवान् (with ankles / clumps) कार्य-संघातः (a cluster of activities) विहङ्गम-कृत-आस्पदः (a home made for birds).
</p>

**Translation:** "Full of oozing sap within, with hands and feet as tender shoots, with clumps for ankles, a cluster of activities — it is a home where birds have nested."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints श्रवदन्तर; the sense requires स्रवदन्तर, oozing within. Left as printed, read as स्रवत्.
- **Birds** are the senses (named in 1.18.15). The body is a tree on which the senses perch.

## Verse 1.18.8 { #1-18-8 }

<p class="shloka">
सच्छायो देहवृक्षोऽयं जीवपान्थगणास्पदः ।<br>
कस्यात्मीयः कस्य पर आस्थानास्थे किलात्र के ॥ १.१८.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जीव-पान्थ-गण-आस्पदः (a resting place for travellers, the souls) अयं स-च्छायः देह-वृक्षः (this shady body-tree): कस्य आत्मीयः (whose own is it?) कस्य परः (whose stranger?) अत्र आस्था-अनास्थे के किल (what here are trust and distrust?).
</p>

**Translation:** "This shady body-tree is a resting place for travellers, the souls. Whose own is it? Whose is it not? What do trust or distrust mean here?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The traveller resting under a tree.** The soul is a passing traveller; the body is a tree it rests under for a while. The tree does not belong to the traveller.
- **Gītā 2.22:** <span class="parallel">वासांसि जीर्णानि यथा विहाय नवानि गृह्णाति नरोऽपराणि</span> — *as a person discards worn-out clothes and takes new ones, so the embodied self takes new bodies.*

## Verse 1.18.9 { #1-18-9 }

<p class="shloka">
तात संतरणार्थेन गृहीतायां पुनःपुनः ।<br>
नावि देहलतायां च कस्य स्यादात्मभावना ॥ १.१८.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), सन्तरण-अर्थेन (for the sake of crossing) पुनः पुनः गृहीतायां (taken up again and again) देह-लतायां नावि च (in this boat, the body-vine) कस्य आत्म-भावना स्यात् (who would think of it as himself?).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear father, the body is a boat taken up again and again only for crossing over. Who would take it to be himself?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The body as a boat.** A beautiful, positive image. The body is not the enemy but the means of crossing. No one mistakes the boat for the passenger.
- **The human birth as a boat** is a frequent image (Bhāgavata 11.20.17: <span class="parallel">नृदेहमाद्यं सुलभं सुदुर्लभं प्लवं सुकल्पं गुरुकर्णधारम्</span>). *The human body is a well-made boat, with the guru as its helmsman.* Rāma has found his helmsman.

## Verse 1.18.10 { #1-18-10 }

<p class="shloka">
देहनाम्नि वने शून्ये बहुगर्तसमाकुले ।<br>
तनूरुहासंख्यतरौ विश्वासं कोऽधिगच्छति ॥ १.१८.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
देह-नाम्नि (named the body) शून्ये (empty) बहु-गर्त-समाकुले (full of many holes) तनू-रुह-असंख्य-तरौ (with countless trees of hair) वने (in this forest) कः विश्वासम् अधिगच्छति (who would trust)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Who would trust this empty forest called the body, full of many holes, with countless trees of hair?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Holes.** The body's openings (Gītā 5.13, नवद्वारे पुरे, below at 1.18.30).

## Verse 1.18.11 { #1-18-11 }

<p class="shloka">
मांसस्नाय्वस्थिवलिते शरीरपटहेऽदृढे ।<br>
मार्जारवदहं तात तिष्ठाम्यत्र गतध्वनौ ॥ १.१८.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), मांस-स्नायु-अस्थि-वलिते (wrapped in flesh, sinew and bone) अ-दृढे (not firm) गत-ध्वनौ (silent) अत्र शरीर-पटहे (in this drum of the body) अहं (I) मार्जार-वत् तिष्ठामि (sit like a cat).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear father, I sit like a cat inside this flimsy, silent drum of the body, covered in flesh, sinew and bone."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A cat in a drum.** A strange, vivid image: the self curled inside a drum that makes no music. The body has no meaning in itself; it waits for the music of awareness.

## Verse 1.18.12 { #1-18-12 }

<p class="shloka">
संसारारण्यसंरूढो विलसच्चित्तमर्कटः ।<br>
चिन्तामञ्जरिताकारो दीर्घदुःखघुणक्षतः ॥ १.१८.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[कायः] संसार-अरण्य-संरूढः (grown in the forest of saṃsāra) विलसत्-चित्त-मर्कटः (with the mind-monkey playing on it) चिन्ता-मञ्जरित-आकारः (blossoming with worries) दीर्घ-दुःख-घुण-क्षतः (eaten by the woodworms of long sorrow).
</p>

**Translation:** "Grown in the forest of saṃsāra, the body-tree has the mind-monkey playing on it, blossoms with worries, and is eaten by the woodworms of lasting sorrow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The monkey again** (compare 1.17.29). Craving was the she-monkey; the mind, the monkey.

## Verse 1.18.13 { #1-18-13 }

<p class="shloka">
तृष्णाभुजङ्गमीगेहं कोपकाककृतालयः ।<br>
स्मितपुण्योद्गमः श्रीमाञ्छुभाशुभमहाफलः ॥ १.१८.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[कायः] तृष्णा-भुजङ्गमी-गेहं (the home of the she-serpent craving) कोप-काक-कृत-आलयः (where the crow of anger has made its nest) स्मित-पुण्य-उद्गमः (with smiles as its blossoming) श्रीमान् (splendid) शुभ-अशुभ-महा-फलः (bearing the great fruits of good and evil).
</p>

**Translation:** "It is the home of the she-serpent craving, where the crow of anger has built its nest; its flowers are smiles; it is splendid, and bears the great fruits of good and evil."

- **Good and evil both as fruit.** Merit and demerit both bind. Compare Gītā 2.50, <span class="parallel">बुद्धियुक्तो जहातीह उभे सुकृतदुष्कृते</span>. *One united in wisdom gives up both good and evil deeds here.*

## Verse 1.18.14 { #1-18-14 }

<p class="shloka">
सुस्कन्धोघलताजालो हस्तस्तबकसुन्दरः ।<br>
पवनस्पन्दिताशेषस्वाङ्गावयवपल्लवः ॥ १.१८.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[कायः] सु-स्कन्ध-ओघ-लता-जालः (with fine shoulders and a network of veins as creepers) हस्त-स्तबक-सुन्दरः (lovely with hands as clusters) पवन-स्पन्दित-अशेष-स्व-अङ्ग-अवयव-पल्लवः (all its limbs as leaves stirred by the wind / breath).
</p>

**Translation:** "With fine shoulders and a web of veins as its creepers, lovely with hands like flower-clusters, all its limbs are leaves stirred by the wind of breath."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Stirred by breath.** The limbs move only because the breath (प्राण) moves them. Without breath, the leaves are still.

## Verse 1.18.15 { #1-18-15 }

<p class="shloka">
सर्वेन्द्रियखगाधारः सुजानुस्तम्भ उन्नतः ।<br>
सरसच्छायया युक्तः कामपान्थनिषेवितः ॥ १.१८.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[कायः] सर्व-इन्द्रिय-खग-आधारः (the support of the birds, all the senses) सु-जानु-स्तम्भः (with knees as fine pillars) उन्नतः (tall) स-रस-च्छायया युक्तः (with pleasant shade) काम-पान्थ-निषेवितः (frequented by the travellers, the desires).

<p class="shloka">
मूर्धसंजनिताऽऽदीर्घशिरोरुहतृणावलिः ।<br>
अहंकारगृध्रकृतकुलायः सुषिरोदरः ॥ १.१८.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[कायः] मूर्ध-सञ्जनित-आदीर्घ-शिरोरुह-तृण-आवलिः (with rows of long grass, the hair grown on the head) अहंकार-गृध्र-कृत-कुलायः (where the vulture of ego has made its nest) सुषिर-उदरः (with a hollow belly).

## Verse 1.18.17 { #1-18-17 }

<p class="shloka">
विच्छिन्नवासनाजालमूलत्वाद्दुर्लवाकृतिः ।<br>
व्यायामविरसः कायप्लक्षोऽयं न सुखाय मे ॥ १.१८.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विच्छिन्न-वासना-जाल-मूलत्वात् (because its roots, the network of latent desires, are cut [it cannot be cut easily]) दुर्-लव-आकृतिः (hard to fell) व्यायाम-विरसः (wearisome in its exertion) अयं काय-प्लक्षः (this fig-tree of the body) मे सुखाय न [भवति] (is not for my happiness).
</p>

**Translation:** "This fig-tree of the body, rooted in a network of latent desires, is hard to fell and wearisome to tend; it brings me no happiness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rooted in vāsanās.** The body's real roots are not in the earth but in latent desires (वासना, glossary). The body arises because of them; to cut the tree, one cuts its roots.
- **Reading note.** विच्छिन्न is hard to construe; the sense may be 'with its roots spread wide'. The translation follows the general sense.

## Verse 1.18.18 { #1-18-18 }

<p class="shloka">
कलेवरमहंकारगृहस्थस्य महागृहम् ।<br>
लुठत्वभ्येतु वा स्थैर्यं किमनेन मुने मम ॥ १.१८.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), कलेवरम् (the body) अहंकार-गृहस्थस्य महा-गृहम् (is the great house of the householder, ego); [तत्] लुठतु (let it fall) वा स्थैर्यम् अभ्येतु (or let it stand firm); अनेन मम किम् (what is it to me?).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, the body is the great house of the householder called ego. Let it fall or let it stand — what is it to me?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **From tree to house.** Now the body is a house, and ego the householder living in it. The refrain that follows (1.18.19–1.18.34) is one of the most memorable passages of the Vairāgya Prakaraṇa.
- **"What is it to me?"** The beginning of real detachment: not hatred of the body, but indifference to its fate.

मरणावसरे काया जीवं नानुसरन्ति ये ।<br>
तेषु तात कृतघ्नेषु कैवास्था वद धीमताम् ॥ १.१८.३९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), ये कायाः (those bodies which) मरण-अवसरे (at the time of death) जीवं न अनुसरन्ति (do not follow the soul), तेषु कृत-घ्नेषु (in those ungrateful ones) धीमतां का एव आस्था (what trust can the wise have?) वद (tell me).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear father, the body does not follow the soul at death. What trust can the wise have in something so ungrateful? Tell me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Ungrateful.** We serve the body all our lives, and it abandons us at death. A sharp, personal way of putting impermanence.

</p>

<p class="anvaya">

**Translation:** "The body is as unsteady as the flapping ear of a rutting elephant, as fragile as a hanging drop. Before it abandons me, I will abandon it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Abandon before being abandoned.** A decisive turn: Rāma resolves to give up the body before it gives him up. Not suicide — the context makes clear he means inner renunciation of identification.
- **The hanging drop** — the image of 1.14.1 returns.

## Verse 1.18.53 { #1-18-53 }

<p class="shloka">
नाहं देहस्य नो देहो मम नायमहं तथा ।<br>
इति विश्रान्तचित्ता ये ते मुने पुरुषोत्तमाः ॥ १.१८.५३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), न अहं देहस्य (I do not belong to the body) नो देहः मम (nor is the body mine) तथा अयम् अहं न (nor am I this) इति (thus) ये विश्रान्त-चित्ताः (those whose minds rest) ते पुरुष-उत्तमाः (they are the best of persons).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, 'I do not belong to the body, the body is not mine, and I am not this' — those whose minds rest in this are the best of beings."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The heart of the sarga.** After all the revulsion, here is the real teaching, calm and clear: not 'I hate the body', but **'I am not the body.'** Rāma's misplaced aversion has found its true expression.
- **पुरुषोत्तम.** In the Gītā (15.18), Puruṣottama is the Lord himself. Here it is the one who rests in the knowledge of not being the body. The two meet.
- **Gītā 2.18, 2.20:** <span class="parallel">अन्तवन्त इमे देहा नित्यस्योक्ताः शरीरिणः</span> — *these bodies of the eternal embodied one are said to end*; <span class="parallel">न हन्यते हन्यमाने शरीरे</span> — *it is not killed when the body is killed.*
- **Chāndogya Upaniṣad 8.12.1:** <span class="parallel">मघवन्मर्त्यं वा इदं शरीरमात्तं मृत्युना तदस्यामृतस्याशरीरस्यात्मनोऽधिष्ठानम्</span> — *Maghavan, this body is mortal, held by death; it is the dwelling of the immortal, bodiless Self.* The body is mortal; it is also the dwelling of the immortal. Both sides are true.

## Verse 1.18.59 { #1-18-59 }

<p class="shloka">
कायोऽयमचिरापायो बुद्बुदोऽम्बुनिधाविव ।<br>
व्यर्थं कार्यपरावर्ते परिस्फुरति निष्फलः ॥ १.१८.५९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अयं कायः (this body) अम्बुनिधौ बुद्बुदः इव (like a bubble on the ocean) अचिर-अपायः (soon gone) निष्फलः (fruitless) कार्य-परावर्ते (in the whirl of activity) व्यर्थं परिस्फुरति (throbs in vain).
</p>

**Translation:** "This body, soon gone, is like a bubble on the ocean. Fruitless, it throbs in vain in the whirl of activity."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The bubble.** The bubble is made of the ocean, appears for a moment, and returns. The body is consciousness appearing as form.

## Verse 1.18.60 { #1-18-60 }

<p class="shloka">
मिथ्याज्ञानविकारेऽस्मिन्स्वप्नसंभ्रमपत्तने ।<br>
काये स्फुटतरापाये क्षणमास्था न मे द्विज ॥ १.१८.६० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
द्विज (O brāhmaṇa), मिथ्या-ज्ञान-विकारे (a transformation of false knowledge) स्वप्न-सम्भ्रम-पत्तने (a city of dream-confusion) स्फुटतर-अपाये (whose end is very clear) अस्मिन् काये (in this body) मे क्षणम् आस्था न (I have no trust even for a moment).
</p>

**Translation:** "O brāhmaṇa, I have no trust even for a moment in this body, a product of false knowledge, a city of dream-confusion, whose end is so clear."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A city of dreams.** The dream image becomes central in the later prakaraṇas: waking life is like a long dream.

## Verse 1.18.61 { #1-18-61 }

<p class="shloka">
तडित्सु शरदभ्रेषु गन्धर्वनगरेषु च ।<br>
स्थैर्यं येन विनिर्णीतं स विश्वसितु विग्रहे ॥ १.१८.६१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
येन (one who) तडित्सु शरत्-अभ्रेषु गन्धर्व-नगरेषु च (in lightning, in autumn clouds, and in cities of the gandharvas) स्थैर्यं विनिर्णीतम् (has found permanence) सः (he) विग्रहे विश्वसितु (may trust in the body).
</p>

**Translation:** "Let him trust in the body who has found permanence in lightning, in autumn clouds, and in the gandharvas' cities in the sky."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **गन्धर्वनगर — a city of the gandharvas.** A mirage-city seen in the clouds, a standard Advaita image of an appearance without reality (Gauḍapāda Kārikā 2.31: <span class="parallel">स्वप्नमाये यथा दृष्टे गन्धर्वनगरं यथा । तथा विश्वमिदं दृष्टं वेदान्तेषु विचक्षणैः ॥</span>). *As a dream and illusion are seen, as a city of the gandharvas, so this world is seen by the wise in Vedānta.*

## Verse 1.18.62 { #1-18-62 }

<p class="shloka">
सततभङ्गुरकार्यपरम्परा विजयिजातजयं हठवृत्तिषु ।<br>
प्रबलदोषमिदं तु कलेवरं तृणमिवाहमपोह्य सुखं स्थितः ॥ १.१८.६२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अहम् (I) सतत-भङ्गुर-कार्य-परम्परा-विजयि-जात-जयं (which surpasses even the ever-breaking chain of actions in transience) हठ-वृत्तिषु प्रबल-दोषम् (strong in faults among violent ways) इदं कलेवरं तु (this body) तृणम् इव अपोह्य (casting aside like grass) सुखं स्थितः (abide at ease).
</p>

**Translation:** Casting aside, like a blade of grass, this body — more transient even than the ever-breaking chain of actions, and full of the faults of violent ways — I abide at ease.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (drutavilambita).
- **सुखं स्थितः — I abide at ease.** The sarga ends not in disgust but in ease. Rāma's rejection of the body has become, for a moment, rest. This is the direction of the whole teaching.
- **Casting aside like grass.** Not destroying the body, but letting go of it inwardly, as one lets a blade of grass fall from the hand.
