# Sarga 1

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · Invocations, and the frame story that leads to the dialogue of Vasiṣṭha and Śrī Rāma (66 verses)*

!!! info "Who is speaking? The nested frame of Sarga 1"
    The Yoga Vasiṣṭha is a story told inside a story. In this sarga, each teller passes the thread to the next:

    1. **Agastya** answers **Sutīkṣṇa**'s question (1.1.4–1.1.8), then tells a story (1.1.9 onward).
    2. In it, **Agniveśya** answers his son **Kāruṇya**, who has stopped all action (1.1.10–1.1.18), by telling a story.
    3. In that story, the apsaras **Suruci** meets **Indra's messenger** (1.1.19–1.1.26), who tells her what happened.
    4. The messenger's account: **King Ariṣṭanemi** refuses heaven and is sent to **Vālmīki** (1.1.27–1.1.51).
    5. **Vālmīki** begins to tell the king the dialogue of **Vasiṣṭha and Śrī Rāma**, starting with why the Lord took human birth (1.1.52–1.1.66).

    Every layer is a seeker going to a teacher with a question.

## Verse 1.1.1 { #1-1-1 }

<p class="shloka">
यतः सर्वाणि भूतानि प्रतिभान्ति स्थितानि च ।<br>
यत्रैवोपशमं यान्ति तस्मै सत्यात्मने नमः ॥ १.१.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यतः (from which) सर्वाणि भूतानि (all beings) प्रतिभान्ति (shine forth / appear), स्थितानि च (and in which they abide), यत्र एव (in which alone) उपशमं यान्ति (they come to rest), तस्मै (to that) सत्य-आत्मने (Reality that is the Self) नमः (salutation).
</p>

**Translation:** Salutations to that Reality, the Self, from which all beings appear, in which they abide, and in which alone they finally come to rest.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **प्रतिभान्ति, not जायन्ते.** The verb means "appear", not "are created". From its very first line, the text hints at its central teaching: the world is appearance in consciousness.
- **Echo of the Upaniṣad.** The verse closely mirrors Taittirīya Upaniṣad 3.1:
    - <span class="parallel">यतो वा इमानि भूतानि जायन्ते । येन जातानि जीवन्ति । यत्प्रयन्त्यभिसंविशन्ति । तद्विजिज्ञासस्व । तद्ब्रह्म ॥</span>
    - *That from which these beings are born, by which they live once born, and into which they enter at death: seek to know That. That is Brahman.*
    - The three movements are the same, but Vasiṣṭha changes "born" (जायन्ते) to "appear" (प्रतिभान्ति). That single word may be the whole Yoga Vasiṣṭha in miniature. Brahma Sūtra 1.1.2 (जन्माद्यस्य यतः) belongs to the same lineage.
- **Gītā 7.6:**
    - <span class="parallel">एतद्योनीनि भूतानि सर्वाणीत्युपधारय । अहं कृत्स्नस्य जगतः प्रभवः प्रलयस्तथा ॥</span>
    - *Understand that all beings have these two natures as their source. I am the origin of the whole universe, and its dissolution too.*
    - Kṛṣṇa speaks as "I"; the Yoga Vasiṣṭha speaks of an impersonal सत्य-आत्मा — a thread for the theme of the Lord as Guru versus the Lord as student.
- **Gītā 9.4–5:**
    - <span class="parallel">मया ततमिदं सर्वं जगदव्यक्तमूर्तिना । मत्स्थानि सर्वभूतानि न चाहं तेष्ववस्थितः ॥</span>
    - *All this world is pervaded by Me in My unmanifest form. All beings abide in Me, but I do not abide in them.*
    - <span class="parallel">न च मत्स्थानि भूतानि पश्य मे योगमैश्वरम् । भूतभृन्न च भूतस्थो ममात्मा भूतभावनः ॥</span>
    - *And yet beings do not abide in Me. Behold My divine yoga! My Self sustains beings and brings them forth, yet does not dwell in them.*
    - मत्स्थानि parallels this verse's स्थितानि. The paradox of 9.5 — beings both do and do not abide in Him — makes sense only if they are appearances, which is exactly what प्रतिभान्ति suggests.
- **उपशम.** This word names the fifth prakaraṇa (उपशम-प्रकरण). It appears in the very first verse, so the destination is announced at the start.

## Verse 1.1.2 { #1-1-2 }

<p class="shloka">
ज्ञाता ज्ञानं तथा ज्ञेयं द्रष्टादर्शनदृश्यभूः ।<br>
कर्ता हेतुः क्रिया यस्मात्तस्मै ज्ञस्यात्मने नमः ॥ १.१.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यस्मात् (from which) ज्ञाता (the knower) ज्ञानं (knowledge) तथा (and) ज्ञेयं (the known), द्रष्टा (the seer) दर्शन-दृश्य-भूः (the arising of seeing and the seen), कर्ता (the doer) हेतुः (the cause / means) क्रिया (the action) [arise], तस्मै (to that) ज्ञस्य (of the knower) आत्मने (Self) नमः (salutation).
</p>

**Translation:** Salutations to that Self of the Knower, from which arise the knower, knowledge and the known; the seer, seeing and the seen; the doer, the means and the action.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Three triads (त्रिपुटी).** Knowing, seeing and doing — all of human experience in three groups of three. Each triad is a split of one consciousness into subject, process and object. The verse says all three splits come from one source.
- **From सत् to चित्.** Verse 1 saluted the सत्य-आत्मा, the Self as Being. Verse 2 salutes the Self as Knowing (ज्ञ). Verse 3 will salute the Self as Bliss (ब्रह्मानन्द). The three opening verses may trace सच्चिदानन्द in order.
- **Gītā 18.18:**
    - <span class="parallel">ज्ञानं ज्ञेयं परिज्ञाता त्रिविधा कर्मचोदना । करणं कर्म कर्तेति त्रिविधः कर्मसङ्ग्रहः ॥</span>
    - *Knowledge, the known and the knower are the threefold impulse to action. The instrument, the action and the doer are the threefold basis of action.*
    - The same two triads, almost word for word. In the Gītā, Kṛṣṇa uses them to analyse action so that Arjuna can act without attachment. Here they are offered back to their source in salutation. Same words, two directions: Gītā moves outward into right action; Yoga Vasiṣṭha moves inward to the One behind it.
- **Who knows the Knower?** Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 2.4.14:
    - <span class="parallel">विज्ञातारमरे केन विजानीयात् ॥</span>
    - *By what, my dear, could one know the Knower?*
    - The ज्ञाता is itself one of the three that arise. The true ज्ञ is prior to the knower-known split, and so can never become an object.
- **Synthesis thread.** The three Jewels of Jainism are right faith/vision (दर्शन), right knowledge (ज्ञान) and right conduct (चारित्र). This verse's three triads — knowing, seeing, doing — echo that grouping. Worth noting for the theme of synthesis.
- **Within the text.** Later in the Nirvāṇa Prakaraṇa, Vasiṣṭha returns to this: the space of consciousness from which the triad of seer, seeing and seen rises and into which it sets. The first verses plant seeds the whole text grows.
- **Reading note.** The last word is printed ज्ञस्यात्मने in the online editions; check it against the printed edition, as readings can vary.

## Verse 1.1.3 { #1-1-3 }

<p class="shloka">
स्फुरन्ति सीकरा यस्मादानन्दस्याम्बरेऽवनौ ।<br>
सर्वेषां जीवनं तस्मै ब्रह्मानन्दात्मने नमः ॥ १.१.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यस्मात् (from which) आनन्दस्य (of bliss) सीकराः (droplets / spray) अम्बरे (in the sky) अवनौ (on the earth) स्फुरन्ति (sparkle forth), [यत्] सर्वेषां (of all) जीवनं (the life), तस्मै (to that) ब्रह्म-आनन्द-आत्मने (Self that is the bliss of Brahman) नमः (salutation).
</p>

**Translation:** Salutations to that Self which is the bliss of Brahman, the life of all beings, from which droplets of bliss sparkle forth in the heavens and on the earth.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **सत्, चित्, आनन्द — complete.** Verse 1 saluted the Self as Reality (सत्य), verse 2 as Knowing (ज्ञ), and verse 3 as Bliss (आनन्द). The three invocations together salute सच्चिदानन्द before a single story is told.
- **सीकराः — spray from an ocean.** Every joy in the world, high or low, is a droplet thrown up from one ocean of bliss. We chase the droplets; the verse points to the ocean.
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.3.32:**
    - <span class="parallel">एषोऽस्य परम आनन्द एतस्यैवानन्दस्यान्यानि भूतानि मात्रामुपजीवन्ति ॥</span>
    - *This is its supreme bliss. On a mere fraction of this very bliss, other beings live.*
    - मात्रा (a fraction) is the सीकर (droplet); उपजीवन्ति (live on) is सर्वेषां जीवनम् (the life of all).
- **Bhṛgu's journey completed.** Verse 1 echoed Taittirīya 3.1, where Bhṛgu is told to seek "That from which beings are born". Bhṛgu's final realisation comes in Taittirīya 3.6:
    - <span class="parallel">आनन्दो ब्रह्मेति व्यजानात् । आनन्दाद्ध्येव खल्विमानि भूतानि जायन्ते । आनन्देन जातानि जीवन्ति । आनन्दं प्रयन्त्यभिसंविशन्तीति ॥</span>
    - *He realised: Bliss is Brahman. From bliss indeed these beings are born; by bliss, once born, they live; into bliss they go and enter.*
    - So the three invocations trace Bhṛgu's whole path, from the question (3.1) to the answer (3.6).
- **A question to sit with.** Where do I look for joy, and do I mistake the droplet for the ocean?

## Verse 1.1.4 { #1-1-4 }

<p class="shloka">
सुतीक्ष्णो ब्राह्मणः कश्चित्संशयाकृष्टमानसः ।<br>
अगस्तेराश्रमं गत्वा मुनिं पप्रच्छ सादरम् ॥ १.१.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कश्चित् (a certain) सुतीक्ष्णः (Sutīkṣṇa) ब्राह्मणः (brāhmaṇa), संशय-आकृष्ट-मानसः (his mind gripped by doubt), अगस्तेः (of Agastya) आश्रमं (the hermitage) गत्वा (having gone), मुनिं (the sage) सादरम् (respectfully) पप्रच्छ (asked).
</p>

**Translation:** A certain brāhmaṇa named Sutīkṣṇa, his mind gripped by doubt, went to the hermitage of Agastya and respectfully questioned the sage.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The teaching begins with a doubt.** Not with a doctrine, but with a seeker whose mind is pulled (आकृष्ट) by a question. Honest doubt, carried to a teacher, is the first step.
- **The frame opens.** Sutīkṣṇa and Agastya are the outermost layer of a story within a story within a story. The great dialogue of Vasiṣṭha and Rāma is reached only by passing through several teachers and students. Wisdom here is always received, never invented.
- **Gītā 4.34:**
    - <span class="parallel">तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया । उपदेक्ष्यन्ति ते ज्ञानं ज्ञानिनस्तत्त्वदर्शिनः ॥</span>
    - *Know that by humble reverence, by sincere questioning and by service. The wise who have seen the truth will teach you that knowledge.*
    - Sutīkṣṇa does all three: गत्वा (he goes to the teacher), सादरम् (with reverence), पप्रच्छ (he asks). The verse is Gītā 4.34 put into a story.
- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.2.12:** <span class="parallel">तद्विज्ञानार्थं स गुरुमेवाभिगच्छेत्</span> — *to know That, one must go to a teacher.*

## Verse 1.1.5 { #1-1-5 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">सुतीक्ष्ण उवाच ।</span><br>
भगवन्धर्मतत्त्वज्ञ सर्वशास्त्रविनिश्चित ।<br>
संशयोऽस्ति महानेकस्त्वमेतं कृपया वद ॥ १.१.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सुतीक्ष्णः उवाच (Sutīkṣṇa said): भगवन् (O revered one), धर्म-तत्त्व-ज्ञ (knower of the essence of dharma), सर्व-शास्त्र-विनिश्चित (one settled in all the scriptures), एकः (one) महान् (great) संशयः (doubt) अस्ति (there is); त्वम् (you) एतं (this) कृपया (out of compassion) वद (please explain).
</p>

**Translation:** Sutīkṣṇa said: O revered one, knower of the essence of dharma, settled in the truth of all the scriptures, I have one great doubt. Out of your compassion, please resolve it.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Two marks of the teacher.** धर्मतत्त्वज्ञ (one who knows the essence, not only the rules) and सर्वशास्त्रविनिश्चित (one whose learning has become certainty). Compare Muṇḍaka 1.2.12, where the teacher is <span class="parallel">श्रोत्रियं ब्रह्मनिष्ठम्</span> — learned in scripture and established in Brahman.
- **कृपया.** The student asks for compassion, not information. Teaching is understood as grace.
- **"One great doubt."** Arjuna speaks the same way in Gītā 6.39:
    - <span class="parallel">एतन्मे संशयं कृष्ण छेत्तुमर्हस्यशेषतः । त्वदन्यः संशयस्यास्य छेत्ता न ह्युपपद्यते ॥</span>
    - *Kṛṣṇa, you must cut away this doubt of mine completely. No one but you can cut away this doubt.*
- **A question to sit with.** If I could ask only one great question, what would it be?

## Verse 1.1.6 { #1-1-6 }

<p class="shloka">
मोक्षस्य कारणं कर्म ज्ञानं वा मोक्षसाधनम् ।<br>
उभयं वा विनिश्चित्य एकं कथय कारणम् ॥ १.१.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मोक्षस्य (of liberation) कारणं (the cause) कर्म (action) [किम्] (is it), वा (or) ज्ञानं (knowledge) मोक्ष-साधनम् (the means to liberation), वा (or) उभयम् (both)? विनिश्चित्य (having decided) एकं (the one) कारणं (cause) कथय (tell me).
</p>

**Translation:** Is action the cause of liberation, or is knowledge the means to liberation, or both? Decide, and tell me the one true cause.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Arjuna's question, almost word for word.** Gītā 3.1–2:
    - <span class="parallel">ज्यायसी चेत्कर्मणस्ते मता बुद्धिर्जनार्दन । तत्किं कर्मणि घोरे मां नियोजयसि केशव ॥</span>
    - <span class="parallel">व्यामिश्रेणेव वाक्येन बुद्धिं मोहयसीव मे । तदेकं वद निश्चित्य येन श्रेयोऽहमाप्नुयाम् ॥</span>
    - *If you hold that understanding is higher than action, Janārdana, why do you urge me into this terrible action, Keśava? With these seemingly mixed words you confuse my mind. Tell me decisively the one thing by which I may reach the highest good.*
    - Arjuna: तदेकं वद निश्चित्य. Sutīkṣṇa: विनिश्चित्य एकं कथय. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha opens with the Gītā's question.
- **Gītā 5.1** asks it again: <span class="parallel">यच्छ्रेय एतयोरेकं तन्मे ब्रूहि सुनिश्चितम्</span> — *tell me with certainty which one of these two is better.*
- **A debate across the schools.** Mīmāṃsā holds that action leads to the highest; Śaṅkara's Advaita holds that knowledge alone liberates; others hold that the two combine (ज्ञानकर्मसमुच्चय). Sutīkṣṇa's question puts the text in the middle of this debate from its first page.
- **Connection to "Why I love Yoga Vasistha" (point 4).** Whether Rāma renounces or returns to rule depends on the answer to this very question.

## Verse 1.1.7 { #1-1-7 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">अगस्तिरुवाच ।</span><br>
उभाभ्यामेव पक्षाभ्यां यथा खे पक्षिणां गतिः ।<br>
तथैव ज्ञानकर्मभ्यां जायते परमं पदम् ॥ १.१.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अगस्तिः उवाच (Agastya said): यथा (just as) खे (in the sky) पक्षिणां (of birds) गतिः (flight) उभाभ्याम् एव पक्षाभ्याम् (by both wings alone) [भवति], तथा एव (in the same way) ज्ञान-कर्मभ्यां (through knowledge and action) परमं पदम् (the supreme state) जायते (is attained).
</p>

**Translation:** Agastya said: Just as a bird flies in the sky only with both its wings, so the supreme state is attained through both knowledge and action.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Asked for "one", Agastya answers "both".** He does not choose a side; he refuses the either/or. One wing alone does not make a bird fly; it makes it spin.
- **Gītā 5.2:**
    - <span class="parallel">संन्यासः कर्मयोगश्च निःश्रेयसकरावुभौ । तयोस्तु कर्मसंन्यासात्कर्मयोगो विशिष्यते ॥</span>
    - *Renunciation and the yoga of action both lead to the highest good. But of the two, the yoga of action is better than renouncing action.*
    - Kṛṣṇa also says "both" (उभौ), then leans toward action. Agastya says "both" and keeps them equal, like two wings.
- **Īśa Upaniṣad 11:**
    - <span class="parallel">विद्यां चाविद्यां च यस्तद्वेदोभयं सह । अविद्यया मृत्युं तीर्त्वा विद्ययामृतमश्नुते ॥</span>
    - *One who knows knowledge and action (avidyā) both together crosses death through action and attains immortality through knowledge.*
    - उभयं सह — "both together" — is the two-wing image in Upaniṣadic language.
- **A point to weigh.** Śaṅkara, in his Gītā commentary, argues against combining knowledge and action as equal causes of liberation. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha, though Advaita at its core, here takes a different line. You may wish to reflect on how "action" is meant: action done with knowledge, free of attachment, may be the wing that knowledge needs in order to fly in the world.
- **परमं पदम्.** The phrase recalls Kaṭha Upaniṣad 3.9 (<span class="parallel">तद्विष्णोः परमं पदम्</span>) and Gītā 8.21 (<span class="parallel">तद्धाम परमं मम</span>).
- **The thesis of the whole text.** This image foreshadows how Rāma's story ends: awakened, he does not leave the world, but rules — knowledge and action flying together.

## Verse 1.1.8 { #1-1-8 }

<p class="shloka">
केवलात्कर्मणो ज्ञानान्नहि मोक्षोऽभिजायते ।<br>
किंतूभाभ्यां भवेन्मोक्षः साधनं तूभयं विदुः ॥ १.१.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
केवलात् कर्मणः (from action alone) [वा] ज्ञानात् (or from knowledge [alone]) मोक्षः (liberation) न हि अभिजायते (does not arise); किन्तु (but) उभाभ्यां (from both) मोक्षः भवेत् (liberation comes); उभयं तु (both indeed) साधनं (the means) विदुः (the wise know).
</p>

**Translation:** Liberation does not arise from action alone, nor from knowledge alone. It comes from both together; the wise know both to be the means.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The wing image, stated plainly.** 1.1.7 gave the picture of the bird; this verse gives the doctrine: neither alone, both together.
- **केवल.** The key word is "alone". Agastya does not deny that knowledge liberates; he denies that knowledge which never meets action is complete.
- **Gītā 3.4:**
    - <span class="parallel">न कर्मणामनारम्भान्नैष्कर्म्यं पुरुषोऽश्नुते । न च संन्यसनादेव सिद्धिं समधिगच्छति ॥</span>
    - *Not by refraining from action does one reach freedom from action, nor by mere renunciation does one attain perfection.*
- **A question to sit with.** In my own life, which wing is weaker: understanding, or living what I understand?

## Verse 1.1.9 { #1-1-9 }

<p class="shloka">
अस्मिन्नर्थे पुरावृत्तमितिहासं वदामि ते ।<br>
कारुण्याख्यः पुरा कश्चिद्ब्राह्मणोऽधीतवेदकः ॥ १.१.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्मिन् अर्थे (on this matter) पुरा-वृत्तम् (that happened long ago) इतिहासं (an ancient account) ते वदामि (I tell you). पुरा (once) कारुण्य-आख्यः (named Kāruṇya) कश्चित् (a certain) ब्राह्मणः (brāhmaṇa) अधीत-वेदकः (who had studied the Vedas) [आसीत्] (there was).
</p>

**Translation:** On this matter I will tell you an ancient account. Long ago there was a brāhmaṇa named Kāruṇya, who had studied the Vedas.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Teaching through story.** Agastya answers a philosophical question not with an argument but with a story. This is the method of the whole Yoga Vasiṣṭha.
- **कारुण्य means "compassion".** The young man who will fall into inaction carries the name of compassion. You may wish to reflect on what the name suggests.

## Verse 1.1.10 { #1-1-10 }

<p class="shloka">
अग्निवेश्यस्य पुत्रोऽभूद्वेदवेदाङ्गपारगः ।<br>
गुरोरधीतविद्यः सन्नाजगाम गृहं प्रति ॥ १.१.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सः] अग्निवेश्यस्य पुत्रः (the son of Agniveśya) वेद-वेदाङ्ग-पारगः (one who had mastered the Vedas and their limbs) अभूत् (was). गुरोः (from his teacher) अधीत-विद्यः सन् (having completed his learning) गृहं प्रति (towards home) आजगाम (he returned).
</p>

**Translation:** He was the son of Agniveśya, and had mastered the Vedas and the Vedāṅgas. Having completed his studies with his teacher, he returned home.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Learning complete, life not yet begun.** Kāruṇya has all the knowledge of the scriptures. The crisis comes not from ignorance but from too much unlived learning.
- **A parallel to Śrī Rāma.** In the sargas ahead, Śrī Rāma too returns home, from pilgrimage, and falls into a crisis. The frame story quietly prepares for his.

## Verse 1.1.11 { #1-1-11 }

<p class="shloka">
तस्थावकर्मकृत्तूष्णीं संशयानो गृहे तदा ।<br>
अग्निवेश्यो विलोक्याथ पुत्रं कर्मविवर्जितम् ॥ १.१.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तदा (then) [सः] गृहे (at home) संशयानः (full of doubt) अकर्म-कृत् (doing no action) तूष्णीं (silent) तस्थौ (remained). अथ (then) अग्निवेश्यः (Agniveśya) पुत्रं (his son) कर्म-विवर्जितम् (abstaining from all action) विलोक्य (seeing) …
</p>

**Translation:** Then he stayed at home, silent, doing nothing, full of doubt. Seeing his son abstaining from all action, Agniveśya …

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Doubt leads to paralysis.** संशयानः (doubting) and अकर्मकृत् (not acting) stand side by side. Unresolved doubt does not make us wise; it makes us stop.
- **Arjuna, too, falls silent.** Gītā 2.9:
    - <span class="parallel">न योत्स्य इति गोविन्दमुक्त्वा तूष्णीं बभूव ह ॥</span>
    - *Saying to Govinda, "I will not fight," he fell silent.*
    - The same word, तूष्णीं, for the same state. Kāruṇya is Arjuna in a household instead of on a battlefield.
- **The sentence continues** into the next verse.

## Verse 1.1.12 { #1-1-12 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">अग्निवेश्य उवाच ।</span><br>
प्राह एतद्वचो निन्द्यं गुरुः पुत्रं हिताय च ।<br>
किमेतत्पुत्र कुरुषे पालनं न स्वकर्मणः ॥ १.१.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
गुरुः (the father, his guru) पुत्रं (to his son) हिताय च (for his good) एतत् निन्द्यं वचः (these words of reproach) प्राह (spoke): पुत्र (son), किम् एतत् (what is this)? स्व-कर्मणः (of your own duty) पालनं (the keeping) न कुरुषे (you do not do).
</p>

**Translation:** Agniveśya, his father and teacher, spoke these words of reproach for his son's good: "Son, what is this? Why do you not keep to your own duties?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The father as गुरु.** The verse calls the father गुरु. In this tradition the first teacher is the parent, and correction is given हिताय, for the child's good.
- **स्वकर्म.** Compare Gītā 18.47: <span class="parallel">श्रेयान्स्वधर्मो विगुणः परधर्मात्स्वनुष्ठितात्</span> — *one's own duty, even imperfectly done, is better than another's done well.*
- **Reading note.** The online edition places the speaker line अग्निवेश्य उवाच before this verse; the verse itself reads as narration introducing his words. Check against your printed edition.

## Verse 1.1.13 { #1-1-13 }

<p class="shloka">
अकर्मनिरतः सिद्धिं कथं प्राप्स्यसि तद्वद ।<br>
कर्मणोऽस्मान्निवृत्तेः किं कारणं तन्निवेद्यताम् ॥ १.१.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अकर्म-निरतः (devoted to non-action) [त्वं] सिद्धिं (perfection) कथं प्राप्स्यसि (how will you attain)? तत् वद (tell me that). अस्मात् कर्मणः निवृत्तेः (of this withdrawal from action) किं कारणं (what is the reason)? तत् निवेद्यताम् (let it be explained).
</p>

**Translation:** "Given over to inaction, how will you reach perfection? Tell me. What is the reason for this withdrawal from action? Explain it to me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 3.8:**
    - <span class="parallel">नियतं कुरु कर्म त्वं कर्म ज्यायो ह्यकर्मणः । शरीरयात्रापि च ते न प्रसिद्ध्येदकर्मणः ॥</span>
    - *Do your prescribed action, for action is better than inaction. Even the maintenance of your body would not be possible without action.*
- **सिद्धि.** The father uses the same word as Gītā 3.4 (quoted at 1.1.8): perfection is not reached by renunciation alone.
- **A good teacher asks before judging.** Agniveśya reproaches, but then asks for the reason: किं कारणम्.

## Verse 1.1.14 { #1-1-14 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">कारुण्य उवाच ।</span><br>
यावज्जीवमग्निहोत्रं नित्यं संध्यामुपासयेत् ।<br>
प्रवृत्तिरूपो धर्मोऽयं श्रुत्या स्मृत्या च चोदितः ॥ १.१.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यावत्-जीवम् (as long as one lives) अग्निहोत्रं (the agnihotra fire-offering) [कुर्यात्], नित्यं (daily) संध्याम् उपासयेत् (one should perform the twilight worship). अयं (this) प्रवृत्ति-रूपः धर्मः (dharma in the form of active engagement) श्रुत्या स्मृत्या च (by śruti and by smṛti) चोदितः (is enjoined).
</p>

**Translation:** Kāruṇya said: "'As long as one lives, one should perform the agnihotra and daily worship at the twilights.' This dharma of active engagement is enjoined by both śruti and smṛti."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **प्रवृत्ति and निवृत्ति.** The two great paths of dharma: प्रवृत्ति, engagement in the world, and निवृत्ति, withdrawal from it. This verse states the first; the next states the second.
- **A known Vedic injunction.** <span class="parallel">यावज्जीवमग्निहोत्रं जुहुयात्</span> — "one should offer the agnihotra as long as one lives" — is a classic example in Mīmāṃsā of a lifelong duty.
- **The son has studied well.** His crisis is not ignorance; it is that his scriptures seem to say two opposite things.

## Verse 1.1.15 { #1-1-15 }

<p class="shloka">
न धनेन भवेन्मोक्षः कर्मणा प्रजया न वा ।<br>
त्यागमात्रेण किंत्वेके यतयोऽश्नन्ति चामृतम् ॥ १.१.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
धनेन (by wealth) मोक्षः न भवेत् (liberation does not come), कर्मणा (by action) प्रजया (by offspring) वा न (nor); किन्तु (but) एके यतयः (some renunciants) त्याग-मात्रेण (by renunciation alone) अमृतम् च अश्नन्ति (attain immortality).
</p>

**Translation:** "'Liberation does not come by wealth, nor by action, nor by offspring; some renunciants attain immortality by renunciation alone.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The second śruti.** Kāruṇya quotes the famous Upaniṣadic line (Kaivalya Upaniṣad 1.3; also Mahānārāyaṇa Upaniṣad):
    - <span class="parallel">न कर्मणा न प्रजया धनेन त्यागेनैके अमृतत्वमानशुः ।</span>
    - *Not by action, not by offspring, not by wealth; by renunciation alone some attained immortality.*
- **The contradiction.** 1.1.14 says act for life; 1.1.15 says action does not liberate. Both are scripture. This is the knot Agastya untied with the two wings.
- **त्याग.** Compare Gītā 18.2, where Kṛṣṇa redefines त्याग: not giving up action, but giving up the fruit of action (<span class="parallel">सर्वकर्मफलत्यागं प्राहुस्त्यागं विचक्षणाः</span>).

## Verse 1.1.16 { #1-1-16 }

<p class="shloka">
इति श्रुत्योर्द्वयोर्मध्ये किं कर्तव्यं मया गुरो ।<br>
इति संदिग्धतां गत्वा तूष्णींभूतोऽस्मि कर्मणि ॥ १.१.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
गुरो (O teacher), इति द्वयोः श्रुत्योः मध्ये (between these two scriptures) मया किं कर्तव्यम् (what should I do)? इति संदिग्धतां गत्वा (having fallen into doubt in this way) कर्मणि (in action) तूष्णीं-भूतः अस्मि (I have become silent / still).
</p>

**Translation:** "Caught between these two scriptures, O teacher, what should I do? Fallen into this doubt, I have stopped all action."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Arjuna's confession.** Gītā 2.7:
    - <span class="parallel">कार्पण्यदोषोपहतस्वभावः पृच्छामि त्वां धर्मसम्मूढचेताः ।</span>
    - *My nature overcome by weakness, my mind confused about dharma, I ask you.*
    - Both students are confused not by too little dharma but by competing dharmas.
- **गुरो.** He turns to his father as guru. The honest statement of confusion is the beginning of being taught.
- **A question to sit with.** Where in my life do two good teachings seem to pull in opposite directions?

## Verse 1.1.17 { #1-1-17 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">अगस्तिरुवाच ।</span><br>
इत्युक्त्वा तात विप्रोऽसौ कारुण्यो मौनमागतः ।<br>
तथाविधं सुतं दृष्ट्वा पुनः प्राह गुरुः सुतम् ॥ १.१.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear one), इति उक्त्वा (having said this) असौ विप्रः कारुण्यः (that brāhmaṇa Kāruṇya) मौनम् आगतः (fell silent). तथाविधं सुतं दृष्ट्वा (seeing his son in that state) गुरुः (the father) सुतं पुनः प्राह (spoke again to his son).
</p>

**Translation:** Agastya said: Having said this, dear one, the brāhmaṇa Kāruṇya fell silent. Seeing his son in that state, the father spoke to him again.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Agastya steps back in.** The speaker line reminds us that all of this is still Agastya talking to Sutīkṣṇa. तात, "dear one", is addressed to Sutīkṣṇa.
- **मौन.** Two kinds of silence appear in the Yoga Vasiṣṭha: the silence of confusion, as here, and the silence of realisation. The text will later show the second.

## Verse 1.1.18 { #1-1-18 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">अग्निवेश्य उवाच ।</span><br>
शृणु पुत्र कथामेकां तदर्थं हृदयेऽखिलम् ।<br>
मत्तोऽवधार्य पुत्र त्वं यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥ १.१.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुत्र (son), एकां कथां शृणु (listen to one story); मत्तः (from me) तत्-अर्थम् अखिलम् (its whole meaning) हृदये अवधार्य (fixing in your heart), पुत्र, त्वं यथा इच्छसि तथा कुरु (do as you wish).
</p>

**Translation:** Agniveśya said: "Listen to a story, my son. Take its whole meaning into your heart, and then do as you wish."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 18.63:**
    - <span class="parallel">विमृश्यैतदशेषेण यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु ॥</span>
    - *Reflect on this fully, then do as you wish.*
    - Kṛṣṇa ends his teaching with the same words: यथेच्छसि तथा कुरु. Both teachers respect the student's freedom. Teaching is offered, not imposed.
- **हृदये अवधार्य.** Not just hearing, but fixing the meaning in the heart. This is श्रवण becoming मनन.

## Verse 1.1.19 { #1-1-19 }

<p class="shloka">
सुरुचिर्नाम काचित्स्त्री अप्सरोगणउत्तमा ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सुरुचिः नाम (named Suruci) काचित् स्त्री (a certain woman), अप्सरः-गण-उत्तमा (finest among the apsarases), हिमवतः (of the Himālaya) शिखि-संवृते (thronged with peacocks) शिखरे (on a peak) उपविष्टा (was seated).

## Verse 1.1.20 { #1-1-20 }

<p class="shloka">
रमन्ते कामसंतप्ताः किन्नर्यो यत्र किन्नरैः ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत्र (where) काम-संतप्ताः किन्नर्यः (kinnarīs, burning with desire) किन्नरैः (with kinnaras) रमन्ते (revel); [यः शिखरः] महा-अघ-ओघ-विनाशिना (which destroys great floods of sin) स्वर्धुनी-ओघेन (by the current of the heavenly river, Gaṅgā) संसृष्टे (is touched).

- **ओघ, twice.** The verse plays on ओघ (flood): the flood of the Gaṅgā destroys the flood (ओघ) of sins.

## Verse 1.1.21 { #1-1-21 }

दूतमिन्द्रस्य गच्छन्तमन्तरिक्षे ददर्श सा ।<br>

<p class="anvaya">

## Verse 1.1.22 { #1-1-22 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">सुरुचिरुवाच ।</span><br>
देवदूत महाभाग कुत आगम्यते त्वया ।<br>
अधुना कुत्र गन्तासि तत्सर्वं कृपया वद ॥ १.१.२२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
देवदूत (O messenger of the gods) महाभाग (illustrious one), त्वया कुतः आगम्यते (from where have you come)? अधुना कुत्र गन्तासि (where are you going now)? तत् सर्वं कृपया वद (kindly tell me all of it).
</p>

**Translation:** Suruci said: "O illustrious messenger of the gods, where have you come from, and where are you going now? Kindly tell me everything."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Where from, where to?** A simple traveller's question, but also the oldest philosophical one: where have we come from, and where are we going?
- **कृपया again.** As Sutīkṣṇa asked Agastya (1.1.5), Suruci asks with कृपया. In this text, even small questions are asked humbly.

## Verse 1.1.23 { #1-1-23 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">देवदूत उवाच ।</span><br>
साधु पृष्टं त्वया सुभ्रु यथावत्कथयामि ते ।<br>
अरिष्टनेमी राजर्षिर्दत्त्वा राज्यं सुताय वै ॥ १.१.२३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सुभ्रु (O fair-browed one), त्वया साधु पृष्टम् (you have asked well); ते यथावत् कथयामि (I will tell you exactly). राजर्षिः अरिष्टनेमी (the royal sage Ariṣṭanemi), सुताय राज्यं दत्त्वा वै (having given his kingdom to his son) …
</p>

**Translation:** The messenger said: "You have asked well, fair one; I will tell you exactly as it was. The royal sage Ariṣṭanemi, having handed his kingdom to his son …"

- **साधु पृष्टम्.** "Well asked." A good question is praised before it is answered. Your second reason for loving this text — that it teaches which questions are worth asking — is built into its very style.
- **राजर्षि.** A king who is also a sage: the ideal the whole text will hold up in Śrī Rāma.
- **The sentence continues** into the next verse.

## Verse 1.1.24 { #1-1-24 }

<p class="shloka">
वीतरागः स धर्मात्मा निर्ययौ तपसे वनम् ।<br>
तपश्चरत्यसौ राजा पर्वते गन्धमादने ॥ १.१.२४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वीत-रागः (free from attachment) सः धर्म-आत्मा (that righteous one) तपसे (for austerity) वनं निर्ययौ (went out to the forest). असौ राजा (that king) गन्धमादने पर्वते (on Mount Gandhamādana) तपः चरति (is practising austerity).
</p>

**Translation:** "… that righteous king, free from attachment, went to the forest to practise austerity. He is now practising austerity on Mount Gandhamādana."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **वीतराग.** Gītā 2.56 describes the one of steady wisdom as <span class="parallel">वीतरागभयक्रोधः</span>, free from attachment, fear and anger.
- **Not the text's final answer.** Ariṣṭanemi takes the path of withdrawal, which the Yoga Vasiṣṭha respects. Yet the text's own hero, Śrī Rāma, will reach freedom while still ruling. Watch how both paths are honoured.

## Verse 1.1.35 { #1-1-35 }

<p class="shloka">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">

</p>

**Translation:** "'What are the merits and the faults of heaven? Tell me plainly. Only when I know what it is like there will I decide what to do.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Examine before accepting.** The king does not reject heaven blindly, nor accept it eagerly. He asks for both गुण and दोष. This is विवेक, discernment, the first of the qualities a seeker needs.
- **A question to sit with.** What do I accept in life without first asking its merits and its costs?

<p class="anvaya">

- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 1.2.10:**
    - <span class="parallel">इष्टापूर्तं मन्यमाना वरिष्ठं नान्यच्छ्रेयो वेदयन्ते प्रमूढाः ।</span>
    - *Thinking that rituals and good works are the highest, the deluded know no other good.*

<p class="anvaya">

## Verse 1.1.38 { #1-1-38 }

<p class="shloka">
परोत्कर्षासहिष्णुत्वं स्पर्धा चैव समैश्च तैः ।<br>
कनिष्ठेषु च संतोषो यावत्पुण्यक्षयो भवेत् ॥ १.१.३८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पर-उत्कर्ष-असहिष्णुत्वं (inability to bear others' higher rank), तैः समैः स्पर्धा च एव (rivalry with equals), कनिष्ठेषु च संतोषः (and satisfaction over those lower) [भवति], यावत् पुण्य-क्षयः भवेत् (until one's merit runs out).
</p>

**Translation:** "There, one cannot bear those who are higher, competes with equals, and feels smug over those who are lower — until one's merit runs out."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Heaven has envy, rivalry and pride.** The messenger honestly lists the दोष. Heaven reproduces the same three postures of the ego that make earthly life restless: envy upward, rivalry sideways, pride downward.
- **The deepest pointer in this passage.** If heaven only rearranges the ego's comparisons, it cannot be freedom. Liberation must lie beyond all comparison.
- **A question to sit with.** Where do I feel envy, rivalry or smugness? Would more "merit" end these, or only change their setting?

</p>

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 9.21, almost word for word:**

</p>

<p class="anvaya">

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The snake's skin.** Bṛhadāraṇyaka Upaniṣad 4.4.7 uses the same image for the liberated one: <span class="parallel">तद्यथाहिनिर्ल्वयनी वल्मीके मृता प्रत्यस्ता शयीत</span> — *as a snake's slough lies dead and cast off on an anthill.* (वल्मीक, the anthill, will soon return in the name of Vālmīki.)

## Verse 1.1.42 { #1-1-42 }

<p class="shloka">
देवदूत विमानेदं गृहीत्वा त्वं यथागतः ।<br>
तथा गच्छ महेन्द्रस्य संनिधौ त्वं नमोऽस्तु ते ॥ १.१.४२ ॥

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र (there) [विष्णुः] ब्रह्मणा सत्य-लोक-निवासिभिः [च] पूजितः (was honoured by Brahmā and the residents of Satyaloka), कुमारं विना (except Kumāra); तं दृष्ट्वा (seeing him) ईश्वरः प्रभुः हि उवाच (the Lord spoke).
</p>

**Translation:** "There Viṣṇu was honoured by Brahmā and all the residents of Satyaloka — all except Sanatkumāra. Seeing him, the Lord spoke."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The one who did not bow.** Sanatkumāra, absorbed in his desirelessness, did not honour Viṣṇu. Even spiritual attainment can harden into a quiet pride.
- **Reading note.** विना कुमारं is read as "except Kumāra"; the online translation reads it differently. Check your printed edition.

## Verse 1.1.59 { #1-1-59 }

<p class="shloka">
सनत्कुमार स्तब्धोऽसि निष्कामो गर्वचेष्टया ।<br>
अतस्त्वं भव कामार्तः शरजन्मेति नामतः ॥ १.१.५९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सनत्कुमार (Sanatkumāra), [त्वं] निष्कामः (desireless) गर्व-चेष्टया (through a show of pride) स्तब्धः असि (you are rigid). अतः (therefore) त्वं काम-आर्तः भव (become afflicted by desire), नामतः शरजन्मा इति (by the name Śarajanman, "born in the reeds").
</p>

**Translation:** "'Sanatkumāra, in your desirelessness you have grown rigid with pride. Therefore become afflicted by desire, and be known by the name Śarajanman, "born in the reeds".'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Pride in desirelessness.** The deepest pointer in this passage: one can be proud even of being free of desire. Such pride is still a desire — to be the one who has no desire.
- **Gītā 16.4:** <span class="parallel">दम्भो दर्पोऽभिमानश्च</span> — *hypocrisy, arrogance and pride* — are listed among the demonic qualities. Even a sage is not exempt.
- **शरजन्मन् and Skanda.** Śarajanman, "born in a thicket of reeds", is a name of Skanda (Kārttikeya). Chāndogya Upaniṣad 7.26.2 itself says of Sanatkumāra: <span class="parallel">तं स्कन्द इत्याचक्षते</span> — *they call him Skanda.*
- **A question to sit with.** Which of my spiritual achievements has quietly become a source of pride?

## Verse 1.1.60 { #1-1-60 }

<p class="shloka">
तेनापि शापितो विष्णुः सर्वज्ञत्वं तवास्ति यत् ।<br>
किंचित्कालं हि तत्त्यक्त्वा त्वमज्ञानी भविष्यसि ॥ १.१.६० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तेन अपि (by him too) विष्णुः शापितः (Viṣṇu was cursed): यत् तव सर्वज्ञत्वम् अस्ति (the omniscience that is yours), तत् किञ्चित्-कालं त्यक्त्वा हि (giving that up for some time), त्वम् अज्ञानी भविष्यसि (you will become ignorant).
</p>

**Translation:** "Sanatkumāra in turn cursed Viṣṇu: 'The omniscience that is yours, you will give up for a time, and become one who does not know.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **This is why Rāma needs a teacher.** The curse of Sanatkumāra explains 1.1.55: the omniscient Lord becomes, for a time, अज्ञानी. That is how the Lord can become a student.
- **किञ्चित्कालम्, "for a time".** The ignorance is temporary, a role. In the end, Śrī Rāma will remember what he is. Perhaps this is true of every seeker.

## Verse 1.1.61 { #1-1-61 }

<p class="shloka">
भृगुर्भार्यां हतां दृष्ट्वा ह्युवाच क्रोधमूर्च्छितः ।<br>
विष्णो तवापि भार्याया वियोगो हि भविष्यति ॥ १.१.६१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भृगुः (Bhṛgu) भार्यां हतां दृष्ट्वा (seeing his wife slain) क्रोध-मूर्च्छितः (overwhelmed by rage) हि उवाच (said): विष्णो (O Viṣṇu), तव अपि भार्यायाः वियोगः हि भविष्यति (you too will suffer separation from your wife).
</p>

**Translation:** "Bhṛgu, seeing his wife slain, was overwhelmed with rage and said: 'O Viṣṇu, you too will suffer separation from your wife.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The second curse.** In the Purāṇic account, Viṣṇu slew Bhṛgu's wife, who had sheltered the asuras. Bhṛgu's curse foreshadows Rāma's separation from Sītā.
- **Even the Lord is subject to consequence.** The Yoga Vasiṣṭha does not exempt even Viṣṇu from the law of action and its fruit, once he enters the world.
- **Reading note.** The online edition prints दृष्ट्रा; the correct form दृष्ट्वा is used here.

## Verse 1.1.62 { #1-1-62 }

<p class="shloka">
वृन्दया शापितो विष्णुश्छलनं यत्त्वया कृतम् ।<br>
अतस्त्वं स्त्रीवियोगं तु वचनान्मम यास्यसि ॥ १.१.६२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वृन्दया (by Vṛndā) विष्णुः शापितः (Viṣṇu was cursed): यत् त्वया छलनं कृतम् (since you practised deceit), अतः (therefore) मम वचनात् (by my word) त्वं स्त्री-वियोगं यास्यसि (you will suffer separation from your wife).
</p>

**Translation:** "Vṛndā cursed Viṣṇu: 'Because you deceived me, by my word you will suffer separation from your wife.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The third curse.** Vṛndā, wife of the asura Jalandhara, was deceived by Viṣṇu in her husband's form, and so her husband's protection failed. Tradition associates her with the sacred tulasī plant.
- **A second wife-separation curse.** The curses add up, each pointing to the same event in the Rāmāyaṇa: Rāma's loss of Sītā.

## Verse 1.1.63 { #1-1-63 }

<p class="shloka">
भार्या हि देवदत्तस्य पयोष्णीतीरसंस्थिता ।<br>
नृसिंहवेषधृग्विष्णुं दृष्ट्वा पञ्चत्वमागता ॥ १.१.६३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
देवदत्तस्य भार्या हि (the wife of Devadatta) पयोष्णी-तीर-संस्थिता (living on the bank of the Payoṣṇī river), नृसिंह-वेष-धृक् विष्णुं दृष्ट्वा (seeing Viṣṇu in the form of Narasiṃha) पञ्चत्वम् आगता (died, returned to the five elements).
</p>

**Translation:** "The wife of Devadatta, who lived on the bank of the Payoṣṇī river, saw Viṣṇu in his fearsome form as Narasiṃha and died of fright."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **पञ्चत्वम् आगता.** A gentle idiom for death: "she went to the five-ness", that is, her body returned to the five elements.
- **Reading note.** The online edition prints पयोष्णीतीरसँस्थिता; the standard spelling संस्थिता is used here.

## Verse 1.1.64 { #1-1-64 }

<p class="shloka">
तेन शप्तो हि नृहरिर्दुःखार्तः स्त्रीवियोगतः ।<br>
तवापि भार्यया सार्धं वियोगो हि भविष्यति ॥ १.१.६४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्त्री-वियोगतः दुःख-आर्तः (stricken with grief at the loss of his wife) तेन (by him) नृहरिः शप्तः हि (Narahari was cursed): तव अपि भार्यया सार्धं (for you too, from your wife) वियोगः हि भविष्यति (there will be separation).
</p>

**Translation:** "Stricken with grief at losing his wife, he cursed Narahari: 'You too will be separated from your wife.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The fourth curse.** The pattern is complete: the Lord who caused separation must himself taste separation.
- **Grief that curses.** Each curse comes from someone overwhelmed by pain or pride. The Yoga Vasiṣṭha will later teach how such pain can be understood rather than passed on.

## Verse 1.1.65 { #1-1-65 }

<p class="shloka">
भृगुणैवं कुमारेण शापितो देवशर्मणा ।<br>
वृन्दया शापितो विष्णुस्तेन मानुष्यतां गतः ॥ १.१.६५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एवं (thus) विष्णुः (Viṣṇu) भृगुणा कुमारेण देवशर्मणा (by Bhṛgu, by Kumāra, by Devaśarman) [च] वृन्दया शापितः (and by Vṛndā was cursed); तेन (because of that) मानुष्यतां गतः (he took human birth).
</p>

**Translation:** "Thus Viṣṇu was cursed by Bhṛgu, by Sanatkumāra, by Devaśarman and by Vṛndā; and so he took human birth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Four curses, two themes.** One curse (Sanatkumāra's) brings ignorance; three bring separation from the beloved. Together they explain the two features of Rāma's life the Yoga Vasiṣṭha cares about: he needs teaching, and he suffers loss.
- **Reading note.** The husband is called देवदत्त in 1.1.63 and देवशर्मन् here. Both names appear in the online edition; your printed edition may resolve this.

## Verse 1.1.66 { #1-1-66 }

<p class="shloka">
एतत्ते कथितं सर्वे शापव्याजस्य कारणम् ।<br>
इदानीं वच्मि तत्सर्वे सावधानमतिः शृणु ॥ १.१.६६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
शाप-व्याजस्य कारणम् (the reason behind the pretext of the curse) एतत् सर्वं ते कथितम् (all this has been told to you). इदानीं (now) तत् सर्वं वच्मि (I will tell it all); सावधान-मतिः शृणु (listen with an attentive mind).
</p>

**Translation:** "I have now told you the whole reason behind the pretext of the curse. Now I will tell you everything; listen with an attentive mind."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **सावधानमतिः.** "With an attentive mind." The sarga closes with a call to attention: the real teaching is about to begin.
- **The end of the frame.** Sarga 1 has moved from invocation, through four layers of seekers and teachers, to the Lord himself as a student. The next sargas tell Śrī Rāma's story directly.
- **Reading note.** The online edition prints सर्वे twice in this verse, where सर्वं is expected. Check your printed edition.
