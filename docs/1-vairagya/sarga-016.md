# Sarga 16

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · On the mind (citta) (27 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 16 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: on the mind (citta).

    1. The mind is restless, never satisfied, never at peace, like a feather in the wind, a village dog, a lost deer (1.16.1–1.16.12).
    2. A lament: "by the mind I am bound, burned, cast aside, swept away, blocked, entangled, seized" (1.16.13–1.16.20).
    3. Restraining the mind is harder than drinking the ocean or uprooting Meru (1.16.21–1.16.24).
    4. The key insight: the mind is the cause of the world, and must be healed; thinned by discernment, its joys and sorrows melt away. Rāma rises to conquer it (1.16.25–1.16.27).

!!! note "Reflections: tuning the mind"
    **Time given to the mind is well spent.** Whatever time is spent setting the mind right — tuning it, as an instrument is tuned — is better spent than almost anything else. In this sarga Rāma, speaking from the seeker's side, shows exactly how the mind troubles us.

    **चित् + त = चित्त.** The word is explained as consciousness (चित्) conditioned by name and form — by "this" (इदम्) and "I" (अहम्). Pure consciousness, coloured by "this" and "I", is what is called mind.

    **The painter and the canvas.** A painter cannot paint if someone keeps shaking the canvas. In the same way, the Guru forms a new vṛtti in the disciple's mind and clears away the others; but if there is too much agitation (विक्षेप), the Guru's vṛtti cannot be formed. Somewhere another vṛtti keeps pushing it out. So the mind has to be kept steady.

    **Three faults of the intellect (बुद्धि):**

    1. **Lack of discernment (अविवेक).** The intellect does not discern between what brings joy and what brings sorrow. One may reflect: wherever one went in the world for happiness, how much happiness was found? Joy, or sorrow? Did an impermanent world ever give a permanent joy? This first discernment (सुख-दुःख-विवेक) then deepens into the discernment of seer and seen (द्रष्टृ-दृश्य-विवेक), of the permanent and the impermanent (नित्य-अनित्य-विवेक), and of the real and the unreal (सत्-असत्-विवेक).
    2. **Imbalance.** The intellect leans one way, and gives great value to something that is false (मिथ्या).
    3. **Not seeing the cage.** The intellect does not understand that one has to get out of the cage called the body (देहाभिमान).

    To remove these three faults one goes to the Guru; without satsaṅga such an intellect is hard to form. The fruit of discernment is dispassion (वैराग्य), which has only two causes: discernment and the grace of Īśvara. Then comes the longing for freedom (मुमुक्षुत्व).

    **Six faults of the mind, and the remedy for each.** Seen from the seeker's side, Rāma's lament describes six faults. Their remedies are, in effect, the six treasures (षट्-सम्पत्ति) of the Vedānta tradition — śama, dama, uparati, titikṣā, śraddhā and samādhāna.

    1. **Too many vāsanās** (1.16.4). With many desires, the mind runs from one to the next, and every one of them cries out for attention. *Remedy — ananyatā:* grow one vāsanā, for Īśvara or for knowledge of the Self, and the rest fall away of themselves. The whole sādhanā is the growing of this one vāsanā: the more Bhagavān and Self-knowledge are loved, the less everything else attracts. The Gītā asks for exactly this: <span class="parallel">मय्येव मन आधत्स्व मयि बुद्धिं निवेशय । निवसिष्यसि मय्येव अत ऊर्ध्वं न संशयः</span> — *fix your mind on Me alone, place your understanding in Me; you will dwell in Me hereafter, without doubt* (12.8); <span class="parallel">मयि सर्वाणि कर्माणि संन्यस्याध्यात्मचेतसा । निराशीर्निर्ममो भूत्वा युध्यस्व विगतज्वरः</span> — *offering all actions to Me, with mind on the Self, free of hope and of "mine", fight, without fever* (3.30); <span class="parallel">ये तु सर्वाणि कर्माणि मयि संन्यस्य मत्पराः । अनन्येनैव योगेन मां ध्यायन्त उपासते</span> — *those who offer all actions to Me, intent on Me, and worship Me with undivided devotion* (12.6).
    2. **The senses are not controlled** (1.16.8). *Remedy — saṅkalpa (dama):* hold the senses with a firm resolve.
    3. **Too much involvement** (1.16.10). Sorrow came exactly where one became most involved. As the years go by, inner withdrawal should keep growing; do not commit unnecessarily. *Remedy — uparati,* held by tasting the joy within.
    4. **No tolerance** (1.16.14). The mind cannot bear any hardship. Without patience (धैर्य) and faith, forbearance is weak too. Keep up a little practice of bearing things; physical pain need not become emotional pain. Either say, "Īśvara has given this; I accept it" (Īśvara-vṛtti), or say, "This belongs to the body; I have nothing to do with it" (Ātma-jñāna-vṛtti) — and do not let reflection on Vedānta stop. *Remedy — titikṣā.* <span class="parallel">मात्रास्पर्शास्तु कौन्तेय शीतोष्णसुखदुःखदाः । आगमापायिनोऽनित्यास्तांस्तितिक्षस्व भारत</span> — *contacts of the senses give cold and heat, pleasure and pain; they come and go and do not last: bear them, Bhārata* (Gītā 2.14).
    5. **Doubt.** The mind keeps running. Stay calm; trust; do not be afraid — one is Bhagavān's own project. Keep up study (पाठ) and japa; do not let faith dry up. Test it and see: hand the issue over — "Bhagavān, please deal with this." *Remedy — śraddhā.* <span class="parallel">अज्ञश्चाश्रद्दधानश्च संशयात्मा विनश्यति</span> — *the ignorant, the faithless and the doubter perish* (Gītā 4.40).
    6. **No concentration** (1.16.5). *Remedy — samādhāna:* whatever is done, do it with a collected mind. Train it — "right now, this is the work, this is what to think about" — and let the span of concentration grow. <span class="parallel">यतो यतो निश्चरति मनश्चञ्चलमस्थिरम् । ततस्ततो नियम्यैतदात्मन्येव वशं नयेत्</span> (Gītā 6.26).

    **The sādhanā in short:** correct the vāsanās by undivided devotion; hold the senses by resolve; hold withdrawal by the joy within; grow faith by study and by handing every issue to Bhagavān; do not let the body's troubles reach the mind; and do every task with a collected mind.

## Verse 1.16.1 { #1-16-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
दोषैर्जर्जरतां याति सत्कार्यादार्यसेवनात् ।<br>
वातान्तःपिच्छलववच्चेतश्चलति चञ्चलम् ॥ १.१६.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चेतः (the mind) दोषैः (by faults) जर्जरतां याति (becomes worn out); सत्-कार्यात् आर्य-सेवनात् (from good deeds and from serving the noble) वात-अन्तः-पिच्छ-लव-वत् (like a bit of feather in the wind) चञ्चलं चलति (restlessly moves away).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "The mind is worn out by its faults. Like a wisp of feather in the wind, it flutters restlessly away from good deeds and from the company of the noble."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **चित्त, चेतस्, मनस् — the mind.** After wealth, life and ego, Rāma now turns to the mind, the instrument through which all of these act on us.
- **The feather in the wind.** The mind is not evil; it is light and unanchored. It cannot stay with what is good.

## Verse 1.16.2 { #1-16-2 }

<p class="shloka">
इतश्चेतश्च सुव्यग्रं व्यर्थमेवाभिधावति ।<br>
दूराद्दूरतरं दीनं ग्रामे कौलेयको यथा ॥ १.१६.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[तत्] इतः च इतः च सु-व्यग्रं (very agitated, here and there) व्यर्थम् एव (in vain) दूरात् दूरतरं (farther and farther) अभिधावति (runs), ग्रामे दीनः कौलेयकः यथा (like a wretched dog in a village).
</p>

**Translation:** "Agitated, it runs here and there in vain, farther and farther, like a wretched village dog."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The village dog.** Running from house to house for scraps, never satisfied. The mind's hunting for pleasure is pictured without flattery.

## Verse 1.16.3 { #1-16-3 }

<p class="shloka">
न प्राप्नोति क्वचित्किंचित्प्राप्तैरपि महाधनैः ।<br>
नान्तः संपूर्णतामेति करण्डक इवाम्बुभिः ॥ १.१६.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[तत्] क्वचित् किंचित् न प्राप्नोति (gains nothing anywhere); प्राप्तैः महा-धनैः अपि (even by great riches gained) अम्बुभिः करण्डकः इव (like a wicker basket by water) अन्तः सम्पूर्णतां न एति (it does not become full within).
</p>

**Translation:** "It gains nothing anywhere. Even with great riches gained, it is never full within — like a wicker basket that cannot hold water."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The basket that will not fill.** Desire's satisfaction leaks out as fast as it is poured in.
- **Manusmṛti 2.94** (also Bhāgavata 9.19.14): <span class="parallel">न जातु कामः कामानामुपभोगेन शाम्यति । हविषा कृष्णवर्त्मेव भूय एवाभिवर्धते ॥</span> — *desire is never quenched by enjoying desires; like fire fed with ghee, it only grows.*

## Verse 1.16.4 { #1-16-4 }

<p class="shloka">
नित्यमेव मुने शून्यं कदाशावागुरावृतम् ।<br>
न मनो निवृतिं याति मृगो यूथादिव च्युतः ॥ १.१६.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), [इदं] मनः (this mind) नित्यम् एव शून्यं (always empty) कत्-आशा-वागुरा-आवृतम् (caught in the net of wretched hopes) यूथात् च्युतः मृगः इव (like a deer strayed from its herd) निर्वृतिं न याति (finds no peace).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, the mind is always empty, caught in the net of wretched hopes; like a deer strayed from its herd, it finds no peace."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The lost deer.** Separated from its source, the mind wanders anxiously. The teaching will show the mind its 'herd': its source in consciousness.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints निवृतिं; the expected word is निर्वृतिं, peace. The sense is the same.

## Verse 1.16.5 { #1-16-5 }

<p class="shloka">
तरङ्गतरलां वृत्तिं दधदालूनशीर्णताम् ।<br>
परित्यज्य क्षणमपि हृदये याति न स्थितिम् ॥ १.१६.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[मनः] तरङ्ग-तरलां वृत्तिं दधत् (having a nature restless as waves) आलून-शीर्णतां [दधत्] (and broken and scattered) क्षणम् अपि परित्यज्य (abandoning [it] even for a moment) हृदये स्थितिं न याति (does not settle in the heart).
</p>

**Translation:** "Restless as a wave, broken and scattered, the mind does not settle in the heart even for a moment."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gītā 6.34:** <span class="parallel">चञ्चलं हि मनः कृष्ण प्रमाथि बलवद्दृढम् । तस्याहं निग्रहं मन्ये वायोरिव सुदुष्करम् ॥</span> — *the mind is restless, Kṛṣṇa, turbulent, strong and stubborn; I think it as hard to restrain as the wind.* Arjuna's complaint is Rāma's complaint, almost word for word (compare वातान्तःपिच्छलववत्, 1.16.1).

## Verse 1.16.6 { #1-16-6 }

<p class="shloka">
मनो मननविक्षुब्धं दिशो दश विधावति ।<br>
मन्दराहननोद्धूतं क्षीरार्णवपयो यथा ॥ १.१६.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मनन-विक्षुब्धं मनः (the mind, stirred up by thinking) मन्दर-आहनन-उद्धूतं क्षीर-अर्णव-पयः यथा (like the water of the milk ocean churned by Mount Mandara) दश दिशः विधावति (rushes in the ten directions).
</p>

**Translation:** "Stirred by thinking, the mind rushes in all ten directions, like the milk ocean churned by Mount Mandara."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The churning of the ocean.** The gods and demons churned the milk ocean with Mount Mandara. Here the churning rod is thought itself, and the ocean is the mind.
- **मनन — thinking.** Recall 1.14.11: *he lives whose mind does not live by thinking*.

## Verse 1.16.7 { #1-16-7 }

<p class="shloka">
कल्लोलकलितावर्तं मायामकरमालितम् ।<br>
न निरोद्धुं समर्थोऽस्मि मनोमयमहार्णवम् ॥ १.१६.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कल्लोल-कलित-आवर्तं (with whirlpools formed by waves) माया-मकर-मालितम् (garlanded with the crocodiles of illusion) मनः-मय-महा-अर्णवं (the great ocean of mind) निरोद्धुं समर्थः न अस्मि (I am not able to restrain).
</p>

**Translation:** "This vast sea that is the mind — swirling with eddies its own waves have made, wreathed with the crocodiles of māyā — is beyond my power to hold in check."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **निरोध — restraint.** Patañjali defines yoga as चित्तवृत्तिनिरोधः (Yoga Sūtra 1.2), the restraint of the mind's movements. Rāma says he cannot do it.
- **But the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's answer is different.** It will not ask Rāma to dam the ocean by force, but to see that the ocean is only consciousness. Understanding dissolves what force cannot restrain.

## Verse 1.16.8 { #1-16-8 }

<p class="shloka">
भोगदूर्वाङ्कुराकाङ्क्षी श्वभ्रपातमचिन्तयन् ।<br>
मनोहरिणको ब्रह्मन्दूरं विपरिधावति ॥ १.१६.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), भोग-दूर्वा-अङ्कुर-आकाङ्क्षी (longing for the young grass of pleasures) श्वभ्र-पातम् अचिन्तयन् (not thinking of falling into a pit) मनः-हरिणकः (the little deer of the mind) दूरं विपरिधावति (runs far).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, the little deer of the mind, longing for the tender grass of pleasure, runs far without thinking of the pit it may fall into."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Grass at the edge of a pit.** The deer sees only the grass. The mind sees only the pleasure, not the fall.

## Verse 1.16.9 { #1-16-9 }

<p class="shloka">
न कदाचन मे चेतः स्वामालूनविशीर्णताम् ।<br>
त्यजत्याकुलया वृत्त्या चञ्चलत्वमिवार्णवः ॥ १.१६.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मे चेतः (my mind) आकुलया वृत्त्या (by its agitated nature) स्वाम् आलून-विशीर्णतां (its broken, scattered state) कदाचन न त्यजति (never abandons), अर्णवः चञ्चलत्वम् इव (as the ocean [does not abandon] its restlessness).
</p>

**Translation:** "My mind, by its agitated nature, never gives up its broken, scattered state, just as the ocean never gives up its restlessness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Never at rest.** The ocean's waves are its nature; so the mind's restlessness seems to be its nature. The teaching will show that the mind's true nature is still consciousness, and restlessness is only the surface.

## Verse 1.16.10 { #1-16-10 }

<p class="shloka">
चेतश्चञ्चलया वृत्त्या चिन्तानिचयचञ्चुरम् ।<br>
धृतिं बध्नाति नैकत्र पञ्जरे केसरी यथा ॥ १.१६.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चिन्ता-निचय-चञ्चुरं चेतः (the mind, busy with heaps of worries) चञ्चलया वृत्त्या (by its restless nature) एकत्र धृतिं न बध्नाति (does not hold steady in one place), पञ्जरे केसरी यथा (like a lion in a cage).
</p>

**Translation:** "Busy with heaps of worries and restless by nature, the mind cannot hold still in one place, like a lion in a cage."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

## Verse 1.16.11 { #1-16-11 }

<p class="shloka">
मनो मोहरथारूढं शरीरात्समतासुखम् ।<br>
हरत्यपहतोद्वेगं हंसः क्षीरमिवाम्भसः ॥ १.१६.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मोह-रथ-आरूढं मनः (the mind, mounted on the chariot of delusion) शरीरात् (from the body) अपहृत-उद्वेगं समता-सुखम् (the happiness of equanimity, free from agitation) हरति (steals), हंसः अम्भसः क्षीरम् इव (as a swan [takes] milk from water).
</p>

**Translation:** "Riding the chariot of delusion, the mind steals from the body the calm joy of equanimity, as a swan draws milk out of water."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The swan's discernment, reversed.** The swan (हंस) was believed to separate milk from water, an emblem of विवेक. Here the mind does the opposite: it takes away the good and leaves the rest.
- **The chariot — Kaṭha Upaniṣad 1.3.3–4:** <span class="parallel">आत्मानं रथिनं विद्धि शरीरं रथमेव तु । बुद्धिं तु सारथिं विद्धि मनः प्रग्रहमेव च ॥</span> — *know the Self as the rider, the body as the chariot, the intellect as the charioteer, the mind as the reins.* In Rāma's verse, the mind has climbed into the chariot itself, and delusion drives.

## Verse 1.16.12 { #1-16-12 }

<p class="shloka">
अनल्पकल्पनातल्पे विलीनाश्चित्तवृत्तयः ।<br>
मुनीन्द्र न प्रबुध्यन्ते तेन तप्येऽहमाकुलः ॥ १.१६.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुनि-इन्द्र (O lord of sages), अन्-अल्प-कल्पना-तल्पे (on the bed of endless imaginings) विलीनाः चित्त-वृत्तयः (the movements of the mind, sunk [asleep]) न प्रबुध्यन्ते (do not wake); तेन (therefore) अहम् आकुलः तप्ये (I burn in distress).
</p>

**Translation:** "O lord of sages, the movements of my mind lie asleep on a bed of endless imaginings and do not wake. That is why I burn in distress."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Asleep on a bed of fancies.** The mind is busy and yet asleep: it is active in imagination but does not awaken to truth.
- **Gītā 2.69:** <span class="parallel">या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी</span> — *what is night for all beings, in that the self-controlled one is awake.*

## Verse 1.16.13 { #1-16-13 }

<p class="shloka">

## Verse 1.16.14 { #1-16-14 }

<p class="shloka">
संततामर्षधूमेन चिन्ताज्वालाकुलेन च ।<br>
वह्निनेव तृणं शुष्कं मुने दग्धोऽस्मि चेतसा ॥ १.१६.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), सन्तत-अमर्ष-धूमेन (with constant smoke of resentment) चिन्ता-ज्वाला-आकुलेन च (and full of flames of worry) चेतसा (by the mind) वह्निना शुष्कं तृणम् इव (like dry grass by fire) दग्धः अस्मि (I am burned).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, I am burned by the mind, smoky with constant resentment and full of the flames of worry, like dry grass in a fire."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A series begins** (1.16.13–1.16.20): each verse ends with चेतसा, "by the mind". Bound, burned, abandoned, carried away, blocked, entangled, seized. Read them as one lament.

## Verse 1.16.15 { #1-16-15 }

<p class="shloka">
क्रूरेण जडतां यातस्तृष्णाभार्यानुगामिना ।<br>
शवं कौलेयकेनेव ब्रह्मन्मुक्तोऽस्मि चेतसा ॥ १.१६.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), क्रूरेण (cruel) तृष्णा-भार्या-अनुगामिना (following its wife, craving) चेतसा (by the mind) जडतां यातः (having become dull) कौलेयकेन शवम् इव (like a corpse by a dog) मुक्तः अस्मि (I am abandoned).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, I am made dull and then cast aside by the cruel mind, which follows its wife, craving — as a dog leaves a corpse after feeding."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Craving as the mind's wife.** The mind follows craving wherever she leads. Sarga 17 will be about her.

## Verse 1.16.16 { #1-16-16 }

<p class="shloka">
तरङ्गतरलास्फालवृत्तिना जडरूपिणा ।<br>
तटवृक्ष इवौघेन ब्रह्मन्नीतोऽस्मि चेतसा ॥ १.१६.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), तरङ्ग-तरल-आस्फाल-वृत्तिना (with a nature of restless, pounding waves) जड-रूपिणा चेतसा (by the dull mind) ओघेन तट-वृक्षः इव (like a riverbank tree by a flood) नीतः अस्मि (I am carried away).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The riverbank tree.** Rooted, yet uprooted. Compare 1.13.2: fortune too was a flooding river.

## Verse 1.16.17 { #1-16-17 }

<p class="shloka">
अवान्तरनिपाताय शून्ये वा भ्रमणाय च ।<br>
तृणं चण्डानिलेनेव दूरे नीतोऽस्मि चेतसा ॥ १.१६.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अवान्तर-निपाताय (to fall in between) शून्ये भ्रमणाय वा (or to wander in the void) चण्ड-अनिलेन तृणम् इव (like grass by a fierce wind) चेतसा (by the mind) दूरे नीतः अस्मि (I am carried far).
</p>

**Translation:** "The mind carries me far off, like grass in a fierce wind, to fall somewhere along the way or to wander in the void."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Neither here nor there.** The mind does not even carry us somewhere; it drops us anywhere, or nowhere.

## Verse 1.16.18 { #1-16-18 }

<p class="shloka">
संसारजलधेरस्मान्नित्यमुत्तरणोन्मुखः ।<br>
सेतुनेव पयःपूरो रोधितोऽस्मि कुचेतसा ॥ १.१६.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्मात् संसार-जलधेः (from this ocean of worldly existence) नित्यम् उत्तरण-उन्मुखः (always eager to cross) [अहं] कु-चेतसा (by the wicked mind) सेतुना पयः-पूरः इव (like a flood of water by a dam) रोधितः अस्मि (I am blocked).
</p>

**Translation:** "I always long to cross this ocean of worldly existence, but the wicked mind blocks me, as a dam holds back a flood."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The longing is there.** Rāma wants to cross. The obstacle is not the world but his own mind.
- **Gītā 12.7:** <span class="parallel">तेषामहं समुद्धर्ता मृत्युसंसारसागरात्</span> — *I lift them out of the ocean of death and saṃsāra.*

## Verse 1.16.19 { #1-16-19 }

<p class="shloka">
पातालाद्गच्छता पृथ्वीं पृथ्व्याः पातालगामिना ।<br>
कूपकाष्ठं कुदाम्नेव वेष्टितोऽस्मि कुचेतसा ॥ १.१६.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पातालात् पृथ्वीं गच्छता (going from the underworld to the earth) पृथ्व्याः पाताल-गामिना (and from the earth to the underworld) कु-चेतसा (by the wicked mind) कु-दाम्ना कूप-काष्ठम् इव (like the well-pole by a bad rope) वेष्टितः अस्मि (I am entangled).
</p>

**Translation:** "The wicked mind, going down to the underworld and up to the earth again, entangles me as a bad rope coils round the pole of a well."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The well's rope.** Up and down, up and down — rebirth in the higher and lower worlds is pictured as the endless motion of a well-bucket.
- **Gītā 9.21:** <span class="parallel">क्षीणे पुण्ये मर्त्यलोकं विशन्ति</span> — *when their merit is spent, they enter the mortal world again.*

## Verse 1.16.20 { #1-16-20 }

<p class="shloka">
मिथ्यैव स्फाररूपेण विचाराद्विशरारुणा ।<br>
बालो वेतालकेनेव गृहीतोऽस्मि कुचेतसा ॥ १.१६.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मिथ्या एव स्फार-रूपेण (only falsely appearing vast) विचारात् विशरारुणा (falling apart under inquiry) कु-चेतसा (by the wicked mind) बालः वेतालकेन इव (like a child by a ghost) गृहीतः अस्मि (I am seized).
</p>

**Translation:** "I am seized by the wicked mind, which only seems vast, and falls apart under inquiry — as a child is seized by a ghost."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **विचाराद्विशरारु — it falls apart under inquiry.** The most important phrase in the sarga. The mind seems huge and powerful, but it dissolves when examined, as a ghost vanishes when you look closely. **This is the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's method in a word: विचार, inquiry.**
- **Gauḍapāda's Kārikā 3.31:** <span class="parallel">मनसो ह्यमनीभावे द्वैतं नैवोपलभ्यते</span> — *when the mind becomes no-mind, duality is no longer perceived.*

## Verse 1.16.21 { #1-16-21 }

<p class="shloka">
वह्नेरुष्णतरः शैलादपि कष्टतरक्रमः ।<br>
वज्रादपि दृढो ब्रह्मन्दुर्निग्रहमनोग्रहः ॥ १.१६.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), दुर्-निग्रह-मनः-ग्रहः (the demon of the mind, hard to subdue) वह्नेः उष्णतरः (hotter than fire) शैलात् अपि कष्टतर-क्रमः (harder to cross than a mountain) वज्रात् अपि दृढः (firmer even than a thunderbolt).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, the demon of the mind, so hard to subdue, is hotter than fire, harder to cross than a mountain, and firmer than a thunderbolt."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **ग्रह — demon, planet, seizer.** The mind is a seizing spirit. It is hard to subdue, but not impossible (Gītā 6.35, below at 1.16.24).

## Verse 1.16.22 { #1-16-22 }

<p class="shloka">
चेतः पतति कार्येषु विहगः स्वामिषेष्विव ।<br>
क्षणेन विरतिं याति बालः क्रीडनकादिव ॥ १.१६.२२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चेतः (the mind) विहगः स्व-आमिषेषु इव (like a bird on its prey) कार्येषु पतति (swoops on objects of action); बालः क्रीडनकात् इव (like a child from a toy) क्षणेन विरतिं याति (in a moment loses interest).
</p>

**Translation:** "The mind swoops on things like a bird on its prey, and loses interest in a moment, like a child tired of a toy."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Grasp, then boredom.** The mind's cycle: eager seizing, quick disinterest. Neither brings satisfaction.

## Verse 1.16.23 { #1-16-23 }

जडप्रकृतिरालोलो विततावर्तवृत्तिमान् ।<br>
मनोऽब्धिरहितव्यालो दूरं नयति तात माम् ॥ १.१६.२३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), जड-प्रकृतिः (dull by nature) आलोलः (restless) वितत-आवर्त-वृत्तिमान् (full of wide whirlpools) अहित-व्यालः मनः-अब्धिः (the ocean of the mind, with its hostile serpents) मां दूरं नयति (carries me far).

## Verse 1.16.24 { #1-16-24 }

<p class="shloka">
अप्यब्धिपानान्महतः सुमेरून्मूलनादपि ।<br>
अपि वह्न्यशनात्साधो विषमश्चित्तनिग्रहः ॥ १.१६.२४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
साधो (good one), महतः अब्धि-पानात् अपि (than drinking up the great ocean) सुमेरु-उन्मूलनात् अपि (than uprooting Mount Meru) वह्नि-अशनात् अपि (than eating fire) चित्त-निग्रहः विषमः (restraining the mind is harder).
</p>

**Translation:** "Good sir, restraining the mind is harder than drinking up the ocean, harder than uprooting Mount Meru, harder than eating fire."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A famous verse.** Often quoted on the difficulty of mental discipline.
- **Gītā 6.35** — Kṛṣṇa's answer to Arjuna:
    - <span class="parallel">असंशयं महाबाहो मनो दुर्निग्रहं चलम् । अभ्यासेन तु कौन्तेय वैराग्येण च गृह्यते ॥</span>
    - *No doubt, mighty-armed one, the mind is hard to subdue and restless. But it is held through practice and dispassion.*
    - Rāma even uses the Gītā's word दुर्निग्रह (1.16.21). Kṛṣṇa's twofold answer — अभ्यास and वैराग्य — is exactly what Vasiṣṭha will teach. Rāma already has the second; the teaching gives him the first.

## Verse 1.16.25 { #1-16-25 }

<p class="shloka">
चित्तं कारणमर्थानां तस्मिन्सति जगत्त्रयम् ।<br>
तस्मिन्क्षीणे जगत्क्षीणं तच्चिकित्स्यं प्रयत्नतः ॥ १.१६.२५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चित्तं (the mind) अर्थानां कारणम् (is the cause of things); तस्मिन् सति (when it exists) जगत्-त्रयम् [अस्ति] (the three worlds exist); तस्मिन् क्षीणे (when it wanes) जगत् क्षीणम् (the world wanes); [तस्मात्] तत् प्रयत्नतः चिकित्स्यम् (so it must be treated with effort).
</p>

**Translation:** "All objects have the mind as their cause. With mind present, the three worlds are present; with mind dwindled, the world dwindles. It is the mind, then, that must be treated, and treated with care."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The key verse of the sarga.** The mind is the source of the world as experienced. This is the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's central teaching, stated by Rāma himself before the teaching begins: **मन एव जगत्** — the world is the mind.
- **चिकित्स्य — to be treated, healed.** The mind is not to be killed but cured. The medical image returns (compare 1.15.3, अहंकारो ममामयः).
- **Upaniṣad parallels:**
    - **Maitri Upaniṣad 6.34:** <span class="parallel">मन एव मनुष्याणां कारणं बन्धमोक्षयोः</span> — *the mind alone is the cause of bondage and liberation for human beings.*
    - **Gītā 6.5–6:** <span class="parallel">उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं… बन्धुरात्मात्मनस्तस्य येनात्मैवात्मना जितः</span> — *let one lift oneself by oneself… the self is a friend to one who has conquered it.*
- **Tuning the mind.** "It must be treated with care" (प्रयत्नतः) — the whole of sādhanā is this careful treatment; see the reflection on the six faults of the mind at the top of this sarga.

## Verse 1.16.26 { #1-16-26 }

<p class="shloka">
चित्तादिमानि सुखदुःखशतानि नूनमभ्यागतान्यगवरादिव काननानि ।<br>
तस्मिन्विवेकवशतस्तनुतां प्रयाते मन्ये मुने निपुणमेव गलन्ति तानि ॥ १.१६.२६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), इमानि सुख-दुःख-शतानि (these hundreds of joys and sorrows) अग-वरात् काननानि इव (as forests from a great mountain) नूनं चित्तात् अभ्यागतानि (surely arise from the mind); विवेक-वशतः (by discernment) तस्मिन् तनुतां प्रयाते (when it becomes thin) तानि निपुणम् एव गलन्ति (they surely melt away) [इति] मन्ये (I think).
</p>

**Translation:** O sage, these hundreds of joys and sorrows surely arise from the mind, as forests grow on a great mountain. When the mind is thinned by discernment, I think they will all melt away completely.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (vasantatilakā).
- **Thinning the mind (तनुता).** Not destroying it, but making it thin, transparent, by विवेक. Rāma already sees the way out.
- **Muṇḍaka Upaniṣad 2.2.8:** <span class="parallel">भिद्यते हृदयग्रन्थिश्छिद्यन्ते सर्वसंशयाः । क्षीयन्ते चास्य कर्माणि तस्मिन्दृष्टे परावरे ॥</span> — *the knot of the heart is cut, all doubts are destroyed, and one's karmas wane, when That, the highest and the lowest, is seen.*
- **Discernment first.** The mind thins through विवेक — the first remedy for the faults of the intellect (see the reflection at the top of this sarga): discernment of joy and sorrow, of seer and seen, of the lasting and the passing, of the real and the unreal.

## Verse 1.16.27 { #1-16-27 }

<p class="shloka">
सकलगुणजयाशा यत्र बद्धा महद्भिस्तमरिमिह विजेतुं चित्तमभ्युत्थितोऽहम् ।<br>
विगतरतितयान्तर्नाभिनन्दामि लक्ष्मीं जडमलिनविलासां मेघलेखामिवेन्दुः ॥ १.१६.२७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत्र (in which) महद्भिः (by the great) सकल-गुण-जय-आशा बद्धा (the hope of winning every virtue is placed), तम् अरिं चित्तम् (that enemy, the mind) इह विजेतुम् (to conquer here) अहम् अभ्युत्थितः (I have risen up); विगत-रतितया (being without delight) अन्तः (within) जड-मलिन-विलासां लक्ष्मीं (fortune, whose play is dull and impure) न अभिनन्दामि (I do not welcome), मेघ-लेखाम् इन्दुः इव (as the moon [does not welcome] a streak of cloud).
</p>

**Translation:** I have risen up to conquer that enemy, the mind, on which the great rest all hope of winning every virtue. With no delight left in me, I do not welcome fortune with its dull, impure play — as the moon takes no delight in a streak of cloud.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (mālinī).
- **The mind as enemy, and as the key to every virtue.** The same mind that binds is where all virtue is won. To conquer the mind is to win everything.
- **Gītā 6.6:** <span class="parallel">अनात्मनस्तु शत्रुत्वे वर्तेतात्मैव शत्रुवत्</span> — *for one who has not conquered himself, his own self acts like an enemy.*
- **The moon and the cloud.** The moon is not harmed by the cloud, only veiled. Rāma's true nature, like the moon, is untouched.
