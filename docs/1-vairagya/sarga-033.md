# Sarga 33

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · The sages assemble (46 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 33 at a glance"
    The end of the Vairāgya Prakaraṇa.

    1. The siddhas summon the great sages of heaven to hear the answer to Rāma's questions; Nārada, Vyāsa, Pulastya, Aṅgiras and others descend into Daśaratha's assembly (1.33.1–1.33.14).
    2. Ascetics and kings, sky-beings and earth-beings, honour one another and take their seats; Rāma stands bowed before them (1.33.15–1.33.28).
    3. The sages praise Rāma's speech: rich in dispassion, clear, noble; the lamp of wisdom blazes in his heart; such a person is rare as sandalwood (1.33.29–1.33.45).
    4. Their promise: if Rāma's wish is not fulfilled through them, they are fools. The teaching begins in the next book (1.33.46).

## Verse 1.33.1 { #1-33-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">सिद्धा ऊचुः ।</span><br>
पावनस्यास्य वचसः प्रोक्तस्य रघुकेतुना ।<br>
निर्णयं श्रोतुमुचितं वक्ष्यमाणं महर्षिभिः ॥ १.३३.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
रघु-केतुना प्रोक्तस्य (spoken by the banner of the Raghus) अस्य पावनस्य वचसः (of this purifying speech) महा-ऋषिभिः वक्ष्यमाणं (which will be given by the great sages) निर्णयं (the answer, the final judgment) श्रोतुम् उचितम् (it is fitting to hear).
</p>

**Translation:** The siddhas said: "It is fitting that we hear the answer the great sages will give to this purifying speech of Rāma, the banner of the Raghus."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **निर्णय — the answer, the verdict.** The siddhas know that the questions are only half; now the sages must answer. Sarga 33 sets the scene for the teaching that fills the rest of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha.
- **पावन — purifying.** Even Rāma's questions purify. Compare 1.32.1.

## Verse 1.33.2 { #1-33-2 }

<p class="shloka">
नारदव्यासपुलहप्रमुखा मुनिपुङ्गवाः ।<br>
आगच्छताश्वविघ्नेन सर्व एव महर्षयः ॥ १.३३.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नारद-व्यास-पुलह-प्रमुखाः मुनि-पुङ्गवाः (great sages led by Nārada, Vyāsa and Pulaha) सर्वे एव महा-ऋषयः (all the great seers) आशु अविघ्नेन आगच्छत (come quickly, without hindrance).
</p>

**Translation:** "Great sages led by Nārada, Vyāsa and Pulaha — all you great seers — come quickly, without delay."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The siddhas summon the sages.** Heaven calls its wisest to Daśaratha's court to hear the teaching.

## Verse 1.33.3 { #1-33-3 }

<p class="shloka">
पतामः परितः पुण्यामेतां दाशरथीं सभाम् ।<br>
नीरन्ध्रां कनकोद्योतां पद्मिनीमिव षट्पदाः ॥ १.३३.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
षट्पदाः पद्मिनीम् इव (as bees [descend on] a lotus-pond) नीरन्ध्रां कनक-उद्योतां (crowded and golden-bright) पुण्याम् एतां दाशरथीं सभाम् (this holy assembly of Daśaratha) परितः पतामः (let us descend all around).
</p>

**Translation:** "Let us descend all around this holy assembly of Daśaratha, crowded and golden-bright, as bees descend on a lotus-pond."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Bees to a lotus.** The image of sages drawn to wisdom like bees to nectar — as the bee drinks the honey of the lotus, the seeker drinks the honey of the teaching.

## Verse 1.33.4 { #1-33-4 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
इत्युक्ता सा समस्तैव व्योमवासनिवासिनी ।<br>
तां पपात सभां तत्र दिव्या मुनिपरम्परा ॥ १.३३.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इति उक्ता (thus addressed) सा समस्ता एव (that whole) व्योम-वास-निवासिनी (dwelling in the sky) दिव्या मुनि-परम्परा (divine company of sages) तत्र (there) तां सभां पपात (descended into that assembly).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vālmīki said: "So addressed, the whole divine company of sages who dwell in the sky descended into that assembly."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Heaven comes to earth.** The sages of the sky join the sages of the earth. The teaching is so important that both worlds gather for it.

## Verse 1.33.5 { #1-33-5 }

<p class="shloka">
अग्रस्थितमनुत्सृष्टरणद्वीणं मुनीश्वरम् ।<br>
पयः पीनघनश्यामं व्यासमेव किलान्तरा ॥ १.३३.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[ते] अन्तरा (among them) अग्र-स्थितम् (standing in front) अनुत्सृष्ट-रणत्-वीणं मुनि-ईश्वरं (the lord of sages, whose sounding vīṇā was not set down, [Nārada]) पयः-पीन-घन-श्यामं व्यासम् एव किल (and Vyāsa himself, dark as a cloud heavy with rain) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Among them, in front, were the lord of sages Nārada, still holding his sounding vīṇā, and Vyāsa himself, dark as a rain-cloud …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Nārada and Vyāsa.** Nārada, the wandering sage with his vīṇā, and Vyāsa — the 'dark' (कृष्ण) compiler of the Vedas, the Mahābhārata and the Gītā. The two great narrators of Indian scripture come to hear Rāma.
- **Reading note.** The online text places the vīṇā-bearing sage and Vyāsa in one phrase; the vīṇā belongs to Nārada, as 1.33.11 confirms.

## Verse 1.33.6 { #1-33-6 }

<p class="shloka">
भृग्वंगिरःपुलस्त्यादिमुनिनायकमण्डिता ।<br>
च्यवनोद्दालकोशीरशरलोमादिमालिता ॥ १.३३.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा मुनि-परम्परा] भृगु-अङ्गिरः-पुलस्त्य-आदि-मुनि-नायक-मण्डिता (adorned with leading sages Bhṛgu, Aṅgiras, Pulastya and others) च्यवन-उद्दालक-उशीर-शरलोम-आदि-मालिता (garlanded with Cyavana, Uddālaka, Uśīra, Śaraloman and more).
</p>

**Translation:** "The company was adorned with leading sages — Bhṛgu, Aṅgiras, Pulastya and others — and garlanded with Cyavana, Uddālaka, Uśīra, Śaraloman and many more."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Uddālaka** — the teacher of the Chāndogya Upaniṣad, who taught his son Śvetaketu <span class="parallel">तत्त्वमसि</span> — *you are That* (6.8.7). The Upaniṣadic teachers themselves come to hear Rāma.

## Verse 1.33.7 { #1-33-7 }

<p class="shloka">
परस्परपरामर्शदुःसंस्थानमृगाजिना ।<br>
लोलाक्षमालावलया सुकमण्डलुधारिणी ॥ १.३३.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] परस्पर-परामर्श-दुः-संस्थान-मृग-अजिना (their deerskins disarranged by brushing against each other) लोल-अक्ष-माला-वलया (with swaying rosaries) सु-कमण्डलु-धारिणी (carrying fine water-pots).
</p>

**Translation:** "Their deerskins were disarranged as they brushed past one another; their rosaries swayed; they carried fine water-pots."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The marks of the ascetic** — deerskin, rosary (अक्षमाला), water-pot (कमण्डलु). A crowd of hermits, jostling in their eagerness.

## Verse 1.33.8 { #1-33-8 }

<p class="shloka">
तारावलिरिव व्योम्नि तेजःप्रसरपाटला ।<br>
सूर्यावलिरिवान्योन्यं भासिताननमण्डना ॥ १.३३.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] व्योम्नि तारा-आवलिः इव (like a row of stars in the sky) तेजः-प्रसर-पाटला (reddish with spreading radiance) सूर्य-आवलिः इव (like a row of suns) अन्योन्यं भासित-आनन-मण्डना (lighting up each other's faces).
</p>

**Translation:** "They were like a row of stars in the sky, glowing with spreading radiance; like a row of suns, lighting up one another's faces."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Lighting each other.** The image of a company of the wise: each one's light reflects on the others. This is सत्सङ्ग.

## Verse 1.33.9 { #1-33-9 }

<p class="shloka">
रत्नावलिरिवान्योन्यं नानावर्णकृतांगिका ।<br>
मुक्तावलिरिवान्योन्यं कृतशोभातिशायिनी ॥ १.३३.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] अन्योन्यं नाना-वर्ण-कृत-अङ्गिका रत्न-आवलिः इव (like a row of jewels of many colours) अन्योन्यं कृत-शोभा-अतिशायिनी मुक्ता-आवलिः इव (like a string of pearls, each enhancing the beauty of the others).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like a string of many-coloured jewels, like a string of pearls each adding to the beauty of the rest."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Many colours, one string.** The sages differ in tradition and temperament, yet together they shine as one.

## Verse 1.33.10 { #1-33-10 }

<p class="shloka">
कौमुदीवृष्टिरन्येव द्वितीयेवार्कमण्डली ।<br>
संभृतेवातिकालेन पूर्णचन्द्रपरम्परा ॥ १.३३.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] अन्या कौमुदी-वृष्टिः इव (like another rain of moonlight) द्वितीया अर्क-मण्डली इव (like a second disc of the sun) अति-कालेन सम्भृता पूर्ण-चन्द्र-परम्परा इव (like a line of full moons gathered over a long time).
</p>

**Translation:** "They were like a second rain of moonlight, like a second sun, like a line of full moons gathered over ages."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **After the rain of flowers, a rain of moonlight.** The second gift of heaven: not flowers, but sages.

## Verse 1.33.11 { #1-33-11 }

<p class="shloka">
ताराजाल इवाम्भोदो व्यासो यत्र विराजते ।<br>
तारौघ इव शीतांशुर्नारदोऽत्र विराजते ॥ १.३३.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत्र (where) व्यासः (Vyāsa) तारा-जाले अम्भोदः इव (like a cloud among a net of stars) विराजते (shines), अत्र (there) नारदः (Nārada) तारा-ओघे शीतांशुः इव (like the moon among a host of stars) विराजते (shines).
</p>

**Translation:** "There Vyāsa shone like a dark cloud among a net of stars, and Nārada shone like the moon amid a host of stars."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dark cloud and bright moon.** Vyāsa is 'dark' (कृष्णद्वैपायन); Nārada is pictured as white and shining.

## Verse 1.33.12 { #1-33-12 }

<p class="shloka">
देवेष्विव सुराधीशः पुलस्त्योऽत्र विराजते ।<br>
आदित्य इव देवानामंगिरास्तु विराजते ॥ १.३३.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अत्र (here) पुलस्त्यः (Pulastya) देवेषु सुर-अधीशः इव (like the lord of gods among the gods) विराजते (shines); अङ्गिराः तु (and Aṅgiras) देवानाम् आदित्यः इव (like the sun among the gods) विराजते (shines).
</p>

**Translation:** "Pulastya shone like Indra among the gods, and Aṅgiras like the sun among the gods."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Pulastya** is, by tradition, the grandfather of Rāvaṇa, Rāma's future enemy. Even he comes to hear Rāma. A quiet irony.

## Verse 1.33.13 { #1-33-13 }

<p class="shloka">
अथास्यां सिद्धसेनायां पतन्त्यां नभसो रसाम् ।<br>
उत्तस्थौ मुनिसंपूर्णा तदा दाशरथी सभा ॥ १.३३.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) अस्यां सिद्ध-सेनायां नभसः रसां पतन्त्याम् [सत्याम्] (as this army of siddhas descended from the sky to the earth) तदा (then) मुनि-सम्पूर्णा दाशरथी सभा (Daśaratha's assembly, full of sages) उत्तस्थौ (rose).
</p>

**Translation:** "As this host of siddhas came down from the sky to the earth, Daśaratha's assembly, full of sages, rose to its feet."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rising to receive.** The courtesy of hospitality: the assembly stands to greet the arriving sages.

## Verse 1.33.14 { #1-33-14 }

<p class="shloka">
मिश्रीभूता विरेजुस्ते नभश्चरमहीचराः ।<br>
परस्परवृतांगाभा भासयन्तो दिशो दश ॥ १.३३.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ते नभः-चर-मही-चराः (those who move in the sky and those who move on earth) मिश्री-भूताः (mingled together) परस्पर-वृत-अङ्ग-आभाः (their bodily radiance mingling) दश दिशः भासयन्तः (lighting up the ten directions) विरेजुः (shone).
</p>

**Translation:** "Mingling together, the beings of the sky and the beings of the earth shone, their radiance blending and lighting up all ten directions."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Heaven and earth mingle.** A picture of the unity the teaching will reveal: no real division between higher and lower worlds.

## Verse 1.33.15 { #1-33-15 }

<p class="shloka">
वेणुदण्डावृतकरा लीलाकमलधारिणः ।<br>
दूर्वांकुराक्रान्तशिखाः सचूडामणिमूर्धजाः ॥ १.३३.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वेणु-दण्ड-आवृत-कराः (with bamboo staffs in their hands) लीला-कमल-धारिणः (holding play-lotuses) दूर्वा-अङ्कुर-आक्रान्त-शिखाः (with shoots of dūrvā grass in their top-knots) स-चूडामणि-मूर्धजाः (with crest-jewels in their hair) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Some held bamboo staffs, some held lotuses; some had shoots of sacred grass in their top-knots, some had jewels in their hair …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Ascetics and kings together.** The staffs and grass belong to the sages, the jewels to the kings — two ways of life in one assembly (1.33.15–1.33.17).

## Verse 1.33.16 { #1-33-16 }

<p class="shloka">
जटाजूटैश्च कपिला मौलिमालितमस्तकाः ।<br>
प्रकोष्ठगाक्षवलया मल्लिकावलयान्विताः ॥ १.३३.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जटा-जूटैः च कपिलाः (tawny with masses of matted hair) मौलि-मालित-मस्तकाः (with crowned heads) प्रकोष्ठग-अक्ष-वलयाः (with rosaries on their forearms) मल्लिका-वलय-अन्विताः (with bracelets of jasmine) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… some tawny with matted locks, some with crowned heads, some with rosaries on their forearms, some with bracelets of jasmine …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Matted locks and crowns.** The ascetic and the king side by side.

## Verse 1.33.17 { #1-33-17 }

<p class="shloka">
चीरवल्कलसंवीताः स्रक्कौशेयावगुण्ठिताः ।<br>
विलोलमेखलापाशाश्चलन्मुक्ताकलापिनः ॥ १.३३.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चीर-वल्कल-संवीताः (clad in rags and bark) स्रक्-कौशेय-अवगुण्ठिताः (covered with garlands and silk) विलोल-मेखला-पाशाः (with swaying girdles) चलत्-मुक्ता-कलापिनः (with moving strands of pearls).
</p>

**Translation:** "… some clad in rags and bark, some in silk and garlands, with swaying girdles and moving strands of pearls."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Bark and silk.** Renunciants and householders together hear the teaching. Both are the audience of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha ([wisdom lived, not renounced](../index.md#wisdom-lived)).

## Verse 1.33.18 { #1-33-18 }

<p class="shloka">
वसिष्ठविश्वामित्रौ तान्पूजयामासतुः क्रमात् ।<br>
अर्घ्यैः पाद्यौर्वचोभिश्च सर्वानेव नभश्चरान् ॥ १.३३.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वसिष्ठ-विश्वामित्रौ (Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra) तान् सर्वान् एव नभः-चरान् (all those sky-dwellers) अर्घ्यैः पाद्यैः वचोभिः च (with water for the hands, water for the feet, and words) क्रमात् पूजयामासतुः (honoured in due order).
</p>

**Translation:** "Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra honoured all those beings of the sky in due order, with water for their hands and feet and words of welcome."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **अर्घ्य and पाद्य.** The traditional welcome of a guest. Compare Taittirīya 1.11.2, अतिथिदेवो भव — *let the guest be a god to you.*

## Verse 1.33.19 { #1-33-19 }

<p class="shloka">
वसिष्ठविश्वामित्रौ ते पूजयामासुरादरात् ।<br>
अर्घ्यैः पाद्यैर्वचोभिश्च नभश्चरमहागणाः ॥ १.३३.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नभः-चर-महा-गणाः ते च (and those great hosts of sky-beings) वसिष्ठ-विश्वामित्रौ (Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra) आदरात् (with respect) अर्घ्यैः पाद्यैः वचोभिः च (with water for hands and feet and with words) पूजयामासुः (honoured in turn).
</p>

**Translation:** "And the great hosts of the sky honoured Vasiṣṭha and Viśvāmitra in turn, with the same respect."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Mutual honour.** Each honours the other. The wise do not stand on rank.

## Verse 1.33.20 { #1-33-20 }

<p class="shloka">
सर्वादरेण सिद्धौघं पूजयामास भूपतिः ।<br>
सिद्धौघो भूपतिं चैव कुशलप्रश्नवार्तया ॥ १.३३.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
रामात् (from Rāma) दृष्टा चमत्कृतिः (the wonder seen) शशिनः शीता ज्योत्स्ना इव (like the cool light of the moon) सु-तरोः मञ्जरी इव (like a blossom from a fine tree) पुष्पात् आमोद-लेखा इव (like a trail of fragrance from a flower) [अस्ति].
</p>

**Translation:** "'The wonder that has come from Rāma is like cool moonlight from the moon, like a blossom from a noble tree, like a trail of fragrance from a flower.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Moonlight from the moon.** The wonder flows naturally from Rāma, as light from the moon. It is his nature, not an effort.

अस्मिन्नुद्दामदौरात्म्यदैवनिर्माणनिर्मिते ।<br>
द्विजेन्द्रा दग्धसंसारे सारो ह्यत्यन्तदुर्लभः ॥ १.३३.४३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
द्विज-इन्द्राः (O best of the twice-born) उद्दाम-दौरात्म्य-दैव-निर्माण-निर्मिते (made by fate's fashioning, full of unchecked wickedness) अस्मिन् दग्ध-संसारे (in this wretched world) सारः (the essence) हि अत्यन्त-दुर्लभः (is very hard to find).
</p>

**Translation:** "'O best of brāhmaṇas, in this wretched world, shaped by fate and full of unchecked wickedness, the essence is very hard to find.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **सार — the essence.** What is truly worth having. The sages call Rāma such an essence.

## Verse 1.33.44 { #1-33-44 }

<p class="shloka">
यतन्ते सारसंप्राप्तौ ये यशोनिधयो धियः ।<br>
धन्या धुरि सतां गण्यास्त एव पुरुषोत्तमाः ॥ १.३३.४४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये (those) यशः-निधयः धियः (minds that are treasuries of fame) सार-सम्प्राप्तौ यतन्ते (strive to reach the essence), ते एव (they alone) धन्याः (are blessed) सतां धुरि गण्याः (counted first among the good) पुरुष-उत्तमाः (the best of persons).
</p>

**Translation:** "'Those whose minds strive to reach the essence are blessed; they are counted first among the good; they are the best of people.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **पुरुषोत्तम** — 'best of persons' (compare 1.18.53). The sages use a name that the Gītā gives to the Lord himself (15.18) — without knowing, or perhaps knowing, who they are describing.

## Verse 1.33.45 { #1-33-45 }

<p class="shloka">
न रामेण समोऽस्तीह दृष्टो लोकेषु कश्चन ।<br>
विवेकवानुदारात्मा न भावी चेति नो मतिः ॥ १.३३.४५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह लोकेषु (here in the worlds) रामेण समः कश्चन (anyone equal to Rāma) विवेकवान् उदार-आत्मा (so discerning and great-souled) न दृष्टः अस्ति (has not been seen) न भावी च (nor will there be) इति नः मतिः (this is our conviction).
</p>

**Translation:** "'No one as discerning and great-souled as Rāma has been seen in the worlds, nor will there ever be — this is our conviction.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Unequalled.** The sages' praise reaches its height. Compare Vālmīki's opening question in the Rāmāyaṇa (1.1.2): <span class="parallel">कोन्वस्मिन्साम्प्रतं लोके गुणवान्कश्च वीर्यवान्</span> — *who in this world today is virtuous and mighty?* The answer was Rāma.

## Verse 1.33.46 { #1-33-46 }

<p class="shloka">
सकललोकचमत्कृतिकारिणोऽप्यभिमतं यदि राघवचेतसः ।<br>
फलति नो तदिमे वयमेव हि स्फुटतरं मुनयो हतबुद्धयः ॥ १.३३.४६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यदि (if) सकल-लोक-चमत्कृति-कारिणः राघव-चेतसः अपि (even of Rāghava's mind, which fills all the worlds with wonder) अभिमतं (wish) नः [द्वारा] न फलति (is not fulfilled through us), तत् (then) इमे वयं मुनयः एव (we sages ourselves) स्फुटतरं हत-बुद्धयः (are clearly fools).
</p>

**Translation:** "'If the wish of Rāghava's mind, which fills all the worlds with wonder, is not fulfilled through us, then we sages are plainly fools.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (drutavilambita) — and the last verse of the Vairāgya Prakaraṇa.
- **The sages' promise.** Rāma's wish — to be free while living — will be fulfilled. The sages stake their own wisdom on it. The Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa, 'the book of the seeker of liberation', begins the answer.
- **Reading note.** The online text reads फलति नो; the translation takes नः as 'through us', which fits the sages' resolve in the next book. It could also be read 'is not fulfilled for us'.
- **The end of the first book.** Vairāgya, dispassion, is complete. Rāma has asked every question worth asking. The answer begins.
