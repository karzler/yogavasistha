# Sarga 27

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · Nothing brings rest (41 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 27 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: nothing in the world brings rest.

    1. Childhood, household and old age pass; craving's river erodes the tree of contentment (1.27.1–1.27.7).
    2. Great souls are rare — steady in hardship and prosperity; the true heroes are those who cross the ocean of the mind (1.27.8–1.27.12).
    3. Family, wealth and the three aims of pleasure, wealth and duty bring no rest without the fourth (1.27.13–1.27.25).
    4. Even "short" and "long" are unreal; the world is pervaded by consciousness — yet Rāma confesses: "I do not know how this life is to be lived" (1.27.26–1.27.41).

## Verse 1.27.1 { #1-27-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
अन्यच्च ताताऽतितरामरम्ये मनोरमे चेह जगत्स्वरूपे ।<br>
न किंचिदायाति तदर्थजातं येनातिविश्रान्तिमुपैति चेतः ॥ १.२७.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), अन्यत् च (and further): इह (here) अतितराम् अरम्ये मनोरमे च जगत्-स्वरूपे (in the world's nature, so unlovely and yet charming) तत् किंचित् अर्थ-जातं (any such thing) न आयाति (does not come) येन (by which) चेतः अतिविश्रान्तिम् उपैति (the mind would find complete rest).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "And more, dear father: in this world, so unlovely and yet so charming, nothing at all comes our way that could bring the mind to complete rest."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **विश्रान्ति — rest.** The word Rāma keeps returning to (glossary). What he seeks is not pleasure but rest — and nothing in the world gives it.
- **Unlovely yet charming.** The world's double face: it attracts and it disappoints.

## Verse 1.27.2 { #1-27-2 }

<p class="shloka">
बाल्ये गते कल्पितकेलिलोले मनोमृगे दारदरीषु जीर्णे ।<br>
शरीरके जर्जरतां प्रयाते विदूयते केवलमेव लोकः ॥ १.२७.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कल्पित-केलि-लोले बाल्ये गते (when childhood, restless with make-believe games, has passed) मनः-मृगे दार-दरीषु जीर्णे (when the deer of the mind has grown old in the caves of married life) शरीरके जर्जरतां प्रयाते (when the poor body has grown decrepit) लोकः केवलम् एव विदूयते (people simply suffer).
</p>

**Translation:** "When childhood with its make-believe games is gone, when the deer of the mind has grown old in the caves of domestic life, and the poor body has worn out — people are left only to suffer."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A whole life in one verse:** childhood, household, old age. Compare the stages in Sargas 19–22.
- **दारदरी — the caves of married life.** A pun on दार (wife) and दरी (cave). Rāma speaks as one who has not yet married, seeing domestic life only as a trap. The teaching will show another way to live it.

## Verse 1.27.3 { #1-27-3 }

<p class="shloka">
जरातुषाराभिहतां शरीरसरोजिनीं दूरतरे विमुच्य ।<br>
क्षणाद्गते जीवितचञ्चरीके जनस्य संसारसरोऽवशुष्कम् ॥ १.२७.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जीवित-चञ्चरीके (when the bee of life) जरा-तुषार-अभिहतां शरीर-सरोजिनीं (the lotus-pond of the body, struck by the frost of old age) दूरतरे विमुच्य (leaving far behind) क्षणात् गते [सति] (has flown in a moment), जनस्य संसार-सरः अवशुष्कम् (a person's lake of worldly life is dried up).
</p>

**Translation:** "When the bee of life leaves the lotus-pond of the body, struck by the frost of old age, and flies away in a moment, that person's lake of worldly life is dried up."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The bee leaves the lotus.** Life departs from the body as a bee leaves a frozen flower.

## Verse 1.27.4 { #1-27-4 }

<p class="shloka">
यदा यदा पाकमुपैति नूनं तदा तदेयं रतिमातनोति ।<br>
जराभराऽनल्पनवप्रसूना विजर्जरा कायलता नराणाम् ॥ १.२७.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नराणां (of people) जरा-भर-अनल्प-नव-प्रसूना (with many new blossoms under the burden of age) विजर्जरा इयं काय-लता (this decrepit vine of the body) यदा यदा नूनं पाकम् उपैति (whenever it ripens) तदा तदा (then) रतिम् आतनोति (only extends its clinging).
</p>

**Translation:** "The worn-out vine of the body, covered in the new white blossoms of age, only clings the more to life as it ripens."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Clinging grows with age** — the paradox of 1.22.7–8 again. Compare Yoga Sūtra 2.9: <span class="parallel">स्वरसवाही विदुषोऽपि तथारूढोऽभिनिवेशः</span>. *Clinging to life flows by its own momentum, and is rooted even in the wise.*

## Verse 1.27.5 { #1-27-5 }

<p class="shloka">
तृष्णानदी सारतरप्रवाहग्रस्ताखिलानन्तपदार्थजाता ।<br>
तटस्थसंतोषसुवृक्षमूलनिकाषदक्षा वहतीह लोके ॥ १.२७.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह लोके (here in the world) तृष्णा-नदी (the river of craving) सारतर-प्रवाह-ग्रस्त-अखिल-अनन्त-पदार्थ-जाता (whose strong current has swallowed all the endless things) तट-स्थ-सन्तोष-सु-वृक्ष-मूल-निकाष-दक्षा (skilled at eroding the roots of the good tree of contentment on its bank) वहति (flows).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving runs through the world like a river. Its powerful current has carried off things beyond counting, and it knows just how to wear away the roots of contentment, the good tree standing on its bank."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **सन्तोष — contentment.** The tree on the bank of craving's river. Contentment will be one of the four 'gatekeepers' of liberation in the Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa.
- **Yoga Sūtra 2.42:** <span class="parallel">सन्तोषादनुत्तमः सुखलाभः</span> — *from contentment comes unsurpassed happiness.*

## Verse 1.27.6 { #1-27-6 }

<p class="shloka">
शारीरनौश्चर्मनिबन्धबद्धा भवाम्बुधावालुलिता भ्रमन्ती ।<br>
प्रलोड्यते पञ्चभिरिन्द्रियाख्यैरधोभवन्ती मकरैरधीरा ॥ १.२७.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चर्म-निबन्ध-बद्धा (bound with ties of skin) शारीर-नौः (the boat of the body) भव-अम्बुधौ (in the ocean of existence) आलुलिता भ्रमन्ती (tossed and wandering) अधीरा (unsteady) इन्द्रिय-आख्यैः पञ्चभिः मकरैः (by five sea-monsters called the senses) अधः भवन्ती (going under) प्रलोड्यते (is churned).
</p>

**Translation:** "The boat of the body, bound with ties of skin, is tossed about in the ocean of existence, and the five sea-monsters called the senses churn it and drag it down."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The boat of the body** — a positive image at 1.18.9; here, in the hands of the senses, it is sinking. The same boat; a different pilot.

## Verse 1.27.7 { #1-27-7 }

<p class="shloka">
तृष्णालताकाननचारिणोऽमी शाखाशतं काममहीरुहेषु ।<br>
परिभ्रमन्तः क्षपयन्ति कालं मनोमृगा नो फलमाप्नुवन्ति ॥ १.२७.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तृष्णा-लता-कानन-चारिणः (roaming in the forest of craving-vines) अमी मनः-मृगाः (these deer of the mind) काम-महीरुहेषु (in the trees of desire) शाखा-शतं परिभ्रमन्तः (wandering over a hundred branches) कालं क्षपयन्ति (waste time) फलं नो आप्नुवन्ति (and find no fruit).
</p>

**Translation:** "Grazing in a jungle of craving's creepers, these deer of the mind leap from branch to branch of desire's trees, a hundred of them, and spend their time without ever tasting fruit."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **No fruit.** All the busy wandering of desire leaves one empty-handed.

## Verse 1.27.8 { #1-27-8 }

<p class="shloka">
कृच्छ्रेषु दूरास्तविषादमोहाः स्वास्थ्येषु नोत्सिक्तमनोभिरामाः ।<br>
सुदुर्लभाः संप्रति सुन्दरीभिरनाहतान्तःकरणा महान्तः ॥ १.२७.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सम्प्रति (now) महान्तः (great souls) कृच्छ्रेषु दूर-अस्त-विषाद-मोहाः (who in hardships keep dejection and delusion far away) स्वास्थ्येषु न उत्सिक्त-मनः-अभिरामाः (who in good times are not puffed up) सुन्दरीभिः अनाहत-अन्तःकरणाः (whose hearts are not struck by beautiful women) सु-दुर्लभाः (are very rare).
</p>

**Translation:** "Great souls are very rare now: those who in hardship keep despair and confusion far away, who in good times are not puffed up, and whose hearts are not overcome by beauty."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints सुन्दरीभिः रनाहतान्तःकरणा; corrected here to सुन्दरीभिरनाहतान्तःकरणा.
- **Steady in both hardship and prosperity.** The theme of the reflection at [1.14.3](sarga-014.md#1-14-3): hard situations need not become hard experience.
- **Gītā 2.56:** <span class="parallel">दुःखेष्वनुद्विग्नमनाः सुखेषु विगतस्पृहः । वीतरागभयक्रोधः स्थितधीर्मुनिरुच्यते ॥</span> — *undisturbed in sorrow, without longing in pleasure, free from passion, fear and anger — such a one is called a sage of steady wisdom.*

## Verse 1.27.9 { #1-27-9 }

<p class="shloka">
तरन्ति मातङ्गघटातरङ्गं रणाम्बुधिं ये मयि ते न शूराः ।<br>
शूरास्त एवेह मनस्तरङ्गं देहेन्द्रियाम्भोधिमिमं तरन्ति ॥ १.२७.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये (those who) मातङ्ग-घटा-तरङ्गं रण-अम्बुधिं (the ocean of battle, whose waves are herds of elephants) तरन्ति (cross), ते (they) मयि (in my view) न शूराः (are not heroes); ते एव इह शूराः (they alone are heroes here) [ये] (who) इमं मनः-तरङ्गं देह-इन्द्रिय-अम्भोधिम् (this ocean of body and senses, whose waves are the mind) तरन्ति (cross).
</p>

**Translation:** "I do not call them heroes who cross the sea of battle, where the waves are ranks of elephants. The real heroes are those who cross this sea of body and senses, whose waves are the mind."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A famous verse.** Spoken by Rāma, the greatest warrior of his age, who will defeat Rāvaṇa — yet he says the real victory is over one's own mind.
- **Dhammapada 103** (Buddhist parallel): *though one conquer a thousand times a thousand men in battle, he who conquers himself is the greatest conqueror.* See [how the traditions meet](../index.md#how-the-traditions-meet).
- **Gītā 6.6:** <span class="parallel">बन्धुरात्मात्मनस्तस्य येनात्मैवात्मना जितः</span> — *the self is a friend to one who has conquered himself by himself.*

## Verse 1.27.10 { #1-27-10 }

<p class="shloka">
अक्लिष्टपर्यन्तफलाभिरामा न दृश्यते कस्यचिदेव काचित् ।<br>
क्रियादुराशाहतचित्तवृत्तिर्यामेत्य विश्रान्तिमुपैति लोकः ॥ १.२७.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अक्लिष्ट-पर्यन्त-फल-अभिरामा (delightful, with a final fruit free of pain) काचित् [क्रिया] (any [action]) कस्यचित् एव न दृश्यते (is seen for no one) यां एत्य (reaching which) क्रिया-दुराशा-हत-चित्त-वृत्तिः लोकः (people, their minds wounded by wrong hopes in action) विश्रान्तिम् उपैति (find rest).
</p>

**Translation:** "No one ever seems to find an action whose final fruit is delightful and free of pain — an action that would let people, their minds wounded by the false hopes of activity, come to rest."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rest is not reached by action.** No action, however good, brings final rest, because every action has a result that passes. Rest comes from knowledge (Gītā 4.33, <span class="parallel">सर्वं कर्माखिलं पार्थ ज्ञाने परिसमाप्यते</span>). *all action, Pārtha, culminates in knowledge.*

## Verse 1.27.11 { #1-27-11 }

<p class="shloka">
कीर्त्या जगद्दिक्कुहरं प्रतापैः श्रिया गृहं सत्त्वबलेन लक्ष्मीम् ।<br>
ये पूरयन्त्यक्षतधैर्यबन्धा न ते जगत्यां सुलभा महान्तः ॥ १.२७.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये (those who) अक्षत-धैर्य-बन्धाः (whose bond of courage is unbroken) कीर्त्या जगत्-दिक्-कुहरं (with fame the hollows of the world's directions) प्रतापैः श्रिया गृहं (with valour and prosperity their homes) सत्त्व-बलेन लक्ष्मीं (with inner strength their fortune) पूरयन्ति (fill), ते महान्तः (those great ones) जगत्यां न सुलभाः (are not easy to find in the world).
</p>

**Translation:** "Great people whose courage never breaks — who fill the world with their fame, their homes with valour and prosperity, and their fortune with inner strength — are not easily found in the world."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **सत्त्व — inner strength.** The worldly ideal of the great king or householder. Rāma does not despise it; he notes how rare it is.

## Verse 1.27.12 { #1-27-12 }

<p class="shloka">
अप्यन्तरस्थं गिरिशैलभित्तेर्वज्रालयाभ्यन्तरसंस्थितं वा ।<br>
सर्वं समायान्ति ससिद्धिवेगाः सर्वाः श्रियः सन्ततमापदश्च ॥ १.२७.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
गिरि-शैल-भित्तेः अन्तः-स्थम् अपि (even what lies within a mountain wall) वज्र-आलय-अभ्यन्तर-संस्थितं वा (or inside a diamond palace) सर्वं (all of it) स-सिद्धि-वेगाः सर्वाः श्रियः (all fortunes, with the speed of success) आपदः च (and calamities) सन्ततं समायान्ति (constantly reach).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even what lies inside a mountain wall or within a palace of diamond is reached, again and again, by every kind of fortune and every kind of calamity, swift as success itself."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **No hiding place.** Neither good fortune nor bad can be kept out. The only safety is a mind that is not shaken by either.

## Verse 1.27.13 { #1-27-13 }

<p class="shloka">
पुत्राश्च दाराश्च धनं च बुद्ध्या प्रकल्प्यते तात रसायनाभम् ।<br>
सर्वं तु तन्नोपकरोत्यथान्ते यत्रातिरम्या विषमूर्च्छनैव ॥ १.२७.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तात (dear father), पुत्राः च दाराः च धनं च (sons, wife and wealth) बुद्ध्या (by the mind) रसायन-आभं प्रकल्प्यते (are imagined as if they were an elixir); तु (but) अथ अन्ते (in the end) तत् सर्वं न उपकरोति (none of it helps) यत्र (when) अति-रम्या विष-मूर्च्छना एव [भवति] (there is only the 'delightful' swoon of poison).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear father, sons, wife and wealth are imagined by the mind to be an elixir of life. But in the end none of it helps, when there comes only the swoon of a poison that once seemed delightful."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Imagined as elixir.** Again कल्पना. Family and wealth are good; the mistake is imagining they can save us from death.
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.4.5** (Yājñavalkya to Maitreyī): <span class="parallel">न वा अरे पत्युः कामाय पतिः प्रियो भवत्यात्मनस्तु कामाय पतिः प्रियो भवति</span> — *a husband is not dear for the husband's sake, but for the sake of the Self.* All love points beyond its object to the Self.

## Verse 1.27.14 { #1-27-14 }

<p class="shloka">
विषादयुक्तो विषमामवस्था मुपागतः कायवयोवसाने ।<br>
भावान्स्मरन्स्वानिह धर्मरिक्तान् जन्तुर्जरावानिह दह्यतेऽन्तः ॥ १.२७.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
काय-वयः-अवसाने (at the end of the body's life) विषाद-युक्तः (filled with dejection) विषमाम् अवस्थाम् उपागतः (fallen into a hard state) जरावान् जन्तुः (the aged creature) इह (here) धर्म-रिक्तान् स्वान् भावान् स्मरन् (remembering his own deeds, empty of dharma) अन्तः दह्यते (burns within).
</p>

**Translation:** "At the end of life, the old creature, sunk in dejection and fallen into a hard state, remembers his own deeds, empty of dharma, and burns within."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Regret at the end.** The pain of having lived without dharma. Compare 1.20.40, पश्चात्ताप.
- **The reason to begin now.** Rāma asks his questions at sixteen so as not to burn with regret at the end.

## Verse 1.27.15 { #1-27-15 }

<p class="shloka">
कामार्थधर्माप्तिकृतान्तराभिः क्रियाभिरादौ दिवसानि नीत्वा ।<br>
चेतश्चलद्बर्हिणपिच्छलोलं विश्रान्तिमागच्छतु केन पुंसः ॥ १.२७.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
आदौ (at first) काम-अर्थ-धर्म-आप्ति-कृत-अन्तराभिः क्रियाभिः (with actions that leave gaps, aimed at pleasure, wealth and duty) दिवसानि नीत्वा (having spent one's days), पुंसः (a person's) चलत्-बर्हिण-पिच्छ-लोलं चेतः (mind, restless as a fluttering peacock feather) केन विश्रान्तिम् आगच्छतु (by what could it come to rest)?
</p>

**Translation:** "After spending one's days in actions aimed at pleasure, wealth and duty, always leaving gaps — by what can a person's mind, restless as a fluttering peacock feather, ever come to rest?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The four aims of life** (पुरुषार्थ): काम (pleasure), अर्थ (wealth), धर्म (duty) and मोक्ष (liberation). Rāma lists only three. The missing fourth is exactly what he is asking for. Without मोक्ष, the other three never bring rest.
- **कृतान्तर — leaving gaps.** Every pursuit leaves a gap, an incompleteness, because its object is incomplete.

## Verse 1.27.16 { #1-27-16 }

<p class="shloka">
पुरोगतैरप्यनवाप्तरूपैस्तरङ्गिणीतुङ्गतरङ्गकल्पैः ।<br>

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्वत्र (everywhere) महीध्राः पाषाण-मयाः (mountains are made of stone) मही मृदा (earth of clay) वृक्षाः दारुभिः एव (trees of wood only) जनाः मांसैः (people of flesh) पौरुष-बद्ध-भावाः (with natures bound by their own efforts); इह (here) विकार-हीनम् अपूर्वं न अस्ति (there is nothing new that is free of change).
</p>

**Translation:** "Hills are stone wherever you go; the ground is clay, trees are timber, people are flesh, each formed by their own striving. Nothing here is new, and nothing escapes change."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Nothing new under the sun.** Every thing is only its material in another form. Compare Chāndogya 6.1.4: <span class="parallel">वाचारम्भणं विकारो नामधेयं मृत्तिकेत्येव सत्यम्</span>. *the modification is only a name arising from speech; the truth is only clay.*

## Verse 1.27.34 { #1-27-34 }

<p class="shloka">
आलोक्यते चेतनयाऽनुविद्धा पयोनुबद्धोऽस्तनयो नभः स्थाः ।<br>
पृथग्विभागेन पदार्थलक्ष्म्या एतज्जगन्नेतरदस्ति किंचित् ॥ १.२७.३४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एतत् जगत् (this world) पृथक्-विभागेन पदार्थ-लक्ष्म्या (in the splendour of objects with their separate divisions) चेतनया अनुविद्धा आलोक्यते (is seen to be pervaded by consciousness); इतरत् किंचित् न अस्ति (there is nothing else).
</p>

**Translation:** "This world, for all the splendour of its separate objects, is seen to be pervaded by consciousness; there is nothing else."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A glimpse of the truth.** Amid his lament, Rāma states the central teaching himself: the world is pervaded by consciousness, and there is nothing else. He sees it, but does not yet rest in it.
- **Reading note.** The first line is corrupt in the online text (पयोनुबद्धोऽस्तनयो नभः स्थाः); the sense of the second line is clear, and the translation follows it.
- **Īśa Upaniṣad 1:** <span class="parallel">ईशा वास्यमिदं सर्वं यत्किञ्च जगत्यां जगत्</span> — *all this, whatever moves in the world, is pervaded by the Lord.*

## Verse 1.27.35 { #1-27-35 }

<p class="shloka">
चमत्कृतिश्चेह मनस्विलोकचेतश्चमत्कारकरी नराणाम् ।<br>
स्वप्नेऽपि साधो विषयं कदाचित्केषांचिदभ्येति न चित्ररूपा ॥ १.२७.३५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
साधो (good sir), इह च (and here) मनस्वि-लोक-चेतः-चमत्कार-करी (that astonishes the minds of thoughtful people) नराणां चमत्कृतिः (a wonder for people) चित्र-रूपा (of marvellous form) केषांचित् (for some) स्वप्ने अपि कदाचित् विषयं न अभ्येति (never comes within reach even in a dream).
</p>

**Translation:** "And here, good sir, is a marvel that astonishes thoughtful minds — a wonder of extraordinary form that, for some people, never comes within reach even in a dream."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The marvel some never dream of.** Rāma seems to refer to the wonder of awakening, which most people never even imagine.

## Verse 1.27.36 { #1-27-36 }

<p class="shloka">
अद्यापि यातेऽपि च कल्पनाया आकाशवल्लीफलवन्महत्त्वे ।<br>
उदेति नो लोभलवाहतानामुदारवृत्तान्तमयी कथैव ॥ १.२७.३६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अद्य अपि (even today) कल्पनायाः महत्त्वे (when the greatness of imagination) आकाश-वल्ली-फल-वत् यातः अपि (has become like the fruit of a vine in the sky) लोभ-लव-आहतानाम् (for those struck by even a little greed) उदार-वृत्तान्त-मयी कथा एव नो उदेति (not even a story of noble deeds arises).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even now, when the grand things we imagine prove to be like fruit on a vine growing in the sky, those struck by even a little greed never hear so much as a story of noble deeds."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The verse is difficult; the sense seems to be that greed shuts the mind even to inspiring stories of the great.

## Verse 1.27.37 { #1-27-37 }

<p class="shloka">
आदातुमिच्छन्पदमुत्तमानां स्वचेतसैवापहतोऽद्य लोकः ।<br>
पतत्यशङ्कं पशुरद्रिकूटादानीलवल्लीफलवाञ्छयैव ॥ १.२७.३७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अद्य (today) उत्तमानां पदम् आदातुम् इच्छन् (wanting to reach the rank of the best) लोकः (people) स्व-चेतसा एव अपहतः (carried away by their own mind) पशुः आ-नील-वल्ली-फल-वाञ्छया एव (as an animal, just from longing for the fruit of a dark vine) अद्रि-कूटात् (from a mountain peak) अशङ्कं पतति (falls without hesitation).
</p>

**Translation:** "Today people want to reach the rank of the best, but are carried away by their own minds, and fall without a thought — like an animal that tumbles off a mountain peak reaching for the fruit of a dark vine."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Ambition without wisdom.** The wish for greatness, driven by the mind's craving, leads to a fall. Compare the deer at the edge of the pit (1.16.8).

## Verse 1.27.38 { #1-27-38 }

<p class="shloka">
अवान्तरन्यस्तनिरर्थकांशच्छायालतापत्रफलप्रसूनाः ।<br>
शरीर एव क्षतसंपदश्च श्वभ्रद्रुमा अद्यतना नराश्च ॥ १.२७.३८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अद्यतनाः नराः च (and people of today) श्वभ्र-द्रुमाः [इव] (like trees growing in a pit) अवान्तर-न्यस्त-निरर्थक-अंश-छाया-लता-पत्र-फल-प्रसूनाः (whose shade, vines, leaves, fruit and flowers are useless parts in between) शरीरे एव क्षत-सम्पदः (whose wealth is wasted on the body alone).
</p>

**Translation:** "People today are like trees growing in a pit: their shade, vines, leaves, fruit and flowers are wasted down there, and all their wealth is spent on the body."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Trees in a pit.** Their shade and fruit help no one. A life spent only on the body gives nothing to the world.

## Verse 1.27.39 { #1-27-39 }

<p class="shloka">
क्वचिज्जना मार्दवसुन्दरेषु क्वचित्कठोरेषु च संचरन्ति ।<br>
देशान्तरालेषु निरन्तरेषु वनान्तखण्डेष्विव कृष्णसाराः ॥ १.२७.३९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जनाः (people) क्वचित् मार्दव-सुन्दरेषु (sometimes in gentle and lovely places) क्वचित् कठोरेषु च (and sometimes in harsh ones) निरन्तरेषु देश-अन्तरालेषु (in continuous stretches of land) वन-अन्त-खण्डेषु इव कृष्णसाराः (like blackbuck in patches at the forest's edge) सञ्चरन्ति (wander).
</p>

**Translation:** "People wander sometimes through gentle, lovely places and sometimes through harsh ones, like blackbuck roaming the patches of land at the forest's edge."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Gentle and harsh places.** The pairs of opposites again. The deer wanders through both without choosing; so do we, through fortune and misfortune.

## Verse 1.27.40 { #1-27-40 }

<p class="shloka">
धातुर्नवानि दिवसं प्रति भीषणानि रम्याणि वा विलुलितान्ततमाकुलानि ।<br>
कार्याणि कष्टफलपाकहतोदयानि विस्मापयन्ति न शवस्य मनांसि केषाम् ॥ १.२७.४० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
धातुः (of the Creator) दिवसं प्रति (every day) नवानि (new) भीषणानि रम्याणि वा (terrible or lovely) विलुलित-अन्त-तम-आकुलानि (disordered and confused in their outcomes) कष्ट-फल-पाक-हत-उदयानि (whose promise is ruined by the ripening of painful fruit) कार्याणि (works) केषां मनांसि न विस्मापयन्ति (whose minds do they not astonish) शवस्य [विना] (except a corpse's)?
</p>

**Translation:** "The Creator's new works, every day — terrible or lovely, confused in their outcomes, their promise ruined by painful results — whose mind do they not astonish, unless it be a corpse's?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (vasantatilakā).
- **Astonishment.** Wonder (विस्मय) is the beginning of philosophy. Only a corpse is not amazed by the world.

## Verse 1.27.41 { #1-27-41 }

<p class="shloka">
जनः कामासक्तो विविधकुकलाचेष्टनपरः स तु स्वप्नेऽप्यस्मिञ्जगति सुलभो नाद्य सुजनः ।<br>
क्रिया दुःखासङ्गाऽविधुरविधुरा नूनमखिला न जाने नेतव्या कथमिव दशा जीवितमयी ॥ १.२७.४१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कामासक्तः (attached to desire) विविध-कु-कला-चेष्टन-परः (given to many kinds of wrong arts) सः जनः [अस्ति] (that is the person [we see]); तु (but) अस्मिन् जगति (in this world) अद्य (today) सु-जनः (a good person) स्वप्ने अपि न सुलभः (is not easy to find even in a dream); अखिला क्रिया (all action) नूनं दुःख-आसङ्ग-अविधुर-विधुरा (is surely full of distress, never free from sorrow); जीवित-मयी दशा (the state of being alive) कथम् इव नेतव्या (how is it to be lived) [इति] न जाने (I do not know).
</p>

**Translation:** People are attached to desire and busy with every kind of wrong; a good person is hard to find in this world today, even in a dream. All action is shot through with sorrow. I do not know how this life is to be lived.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (śikhariṇī).
- **'I do not know how this life is to be lived.'** The sarga ends with Rāma's most honest confession. It is the exact point Arjuna reaches in Gītā 2.7: <span class="parallel">धर्मसंमूढचेताः</span> — *my mind is confused about my duty.* Both confessions make the student ready for the teacher.
