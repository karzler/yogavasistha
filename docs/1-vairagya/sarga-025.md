# Sarga 25

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · The dance of Fate and Death (32 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 25 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: Fate, Death and Destiny as cosmic dancers.

    1. Fate and Time "make and devour"; their form is pure activity, and the world is their dance-hall (1.25.1–1.25.4).
    2. Kṛtānta, Death, dances like a skull-bearing ascetic, in love with his bride Niyati, Destiny (1.25.5–1.25.10).
    3. Destiny's dance: the heavens are her ornaments, the hells her anklet; at the end of the aeons she takes the face of Time (1.25.11–1.25.21).
    4. Death adorned with mountains, sun and moon, oceans; after the dance of destruction, he begins again the gentle dance of creation, like a child playing with mud (1.25.22–1.25.32).

## Verse 1.25.1 { #1-25-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
अत्रैव दुर्विलासानां चूडामणिरिहापरः ।<br>
करोत्यत्तीति लोकेऽस्मिन्दैवं कालश्च कथ्यते ॥ १.२५.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अत्र एव (right here) इह (in this world) दुर्विलासानां चूडामणिः (the crest-jewel of wicked sports) अपरः (another one) [अस्ति]; अस्मिन् लोके (in this world) 'करोति अत्ति' इति ('it makes and it eats') दैवं कालः च कथ्यते (is called Fate and Time).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Here in this world there is another crest-jewel of wicked sport: that which 'makes and devours', called Fate and Time."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints चूडमणि; corrected here to चूडामणि.
- **दैव — Fate.** Time is now joined by Fate (दैव), Death (कृतान्त) and Destiny (नियति). Sarga 25 is a great cosmic dance of these powers, drawn in the imagery of Śiva's तांडव.
- **Is Fate real?** In the Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa Vasiṣṭha will answer at length: 'fate' is only the momentum of one's own past effort, and present effort (पौरुष) can overcome it.

## Verse 1.25.2 { #1-25-2 }

<p class="shloka">
क्रियामात्रादृते यस्य स्वपरिस्पन्दरूपिणः ।<br>
नान्यदालक्ष्यते रूपं न कर्म न समीहितम् ॥ १.२५.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्व-परिस्पन्द-रूपिणः यस्य (of which, whose form is its own vibration) क्रिया-मात्रात् ऋते (apart from mere activity) अन्यत् रूपं न आलक्ष्यते (no other form is seen) न कर्म न समीहितम् (no deed, no intention).
</p>

**Translation:** "Its very form is its own movement. Apart from sheer activity, it has no other form to be seen — no particular deed, no intention."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **स्पन्द — vibration.** The key word of Kashmir Śaivism: consciousness as pure pulsation. Here Fate is pure activity without a doer or purpose.

## Verse 1.25.3 { #1-25-3 }

<p class="shloka">
तेनेयमखिला भूतसंततिः परिपेलवा ।<br>
तापेन हिममालेव नीता विधुरतां भृशम् ॥ १.२५.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तेन (by it) इयम् अखिला परिपेलवा भूत-सन्ततिः (this whole, very fragile line of beings) तापेन हिम-माला इव (as a wreath of snow by heat) भृशं विधुरतां नीता (is brought to great distress).
</p>

**Translation:** "By it this whole fragile line of beings is brought to ruin, as a wreath of snow melts in the heat."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Snow in heat.** Compare 1.3.9, the mind melting like ice. Here it is the beings themselves that melt.

## Verse 1.25.4 { #1-25-4 }

<p class="shloka">
यदिदं दृश्यते किंचिज्जगदाभोगि मण्डलम् ।<br>
तत्तस्य नर्तनागारमिहासावतिनृत्यति ॥ १.२५.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत् इदं किंचित् आभोगि जगत्-मण्डलं दृश्यते (whatever of this vast world-sphere is seen), तत् तस्य नर्तन-आगारम् (that is its dance-hall); इह असौ अतिनृत्यति (here it dances wildly).
</p>

**Translation:** "This whole vast sphere of the world that we see is its dance-hall; here it dances wildly."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The world as a dance-hall.** The image that runs through the whole sarga: the cosmos as the stage of a divine dance.

## Verse 1.25.5 { #1-25-5 }

<p class="shloka">
तृतीयं च कृतान्तेति नाम बिभ्रत्सुदारुणम् ।<br>
कापालिकवपुर्मत्तं दैवं जगति नृत्यति ॥ १.२५.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
च तृतीयं (and a third) सु-दारुणं कृतान्त इति नाम बिभ्रत् (bearing the dreadful name Kṛtānta, 'the ender') कापालिक-वपुः (in the form of a skull-bearing ascetic) मत्तं दैवं (drunken Fate) जगति नृत्यति (dances in the world).
</p>

**Translation:** "And a third, bearing the dreadful name Kṛtānta, 'the one who makes an end', dances drunken in the world in the form of a skull-carrying ascetic."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kṛtānta — Death, 'the ender'.** Pictured as a Kāpālika, an ascetic of a Śaiva sect who carried a skull-bowl. The image is of Śiva as Bhairava.

## Verse 1.25.6 { #1-25-6 }

<p class="shloka">
नृत्यतो हि कृतान्तस्य नितान्तमिव रागिणः ।<br>
नित्यं नियतिकान्तायां मुने परमकामिता ॥ १.२५.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), हि (indeed) नृत्यतः (as he dances) नितान्तम् इव रागिणः कृतान्तस्य (of Kṛtānta, who is utterly passionate) नियति-कान्तायां (for his beloved, Destiny) नित्यं परम-कामिता [अस्ति] (there is always the greatest love).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, as Kṛtānta dances, full of passion, he is always deeply in love with his beloved, Niyati, Destiny."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Niyati — Destiny, the fixed order.** Kṛtānta and Niyati are a divine couple, like Śiva and Śakti. In Kashmir Śaivism, नियति is one of the five 'coverings' (कञ्चुक) that limit pure consciousness into an individual: the sense of a fixed causal order.

## Verse 1.25.7 { #1-25-7 }

<p class="shloka">
शेषः शशिकलाशुभ्रो गङ्गावाहश्च तौ त्रिधा ।<br>
उपवीते अवीते च उभौ संसारवक्षसि ॥ १.२५.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
शशि-कला-शुभ्रः शेषः (Śeṣa, white as the crescent moon) गङ्गा-वाहः च (and the stream of the Gaṅgā) तौ उभौ (both of them) संसार-वक्षसि (on the chest of the world) त्रिधा (in three strands) उपवीते अवीते च (as his sacred thread, worn and unworn) [स्तः] ([are]).
</p>

**Translation:** "On the chest of the world, Śeṣa the serpent, white as the crescent moon, and the stream of the Gaṅgā hang in three strands as his sacred thread."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The sacred thread.** Kṛtānta is dressed like a cosmic brāhmaṇa; his sacred thread is made of Śeṣa and the Gaṅgā — again the imagery of Śiva, who wears the serpent and the Gaṅgā.

## Verse 1.25.8 { #1-25-8 }

<p class="shloka">
चन्द्रार्कमण्डले हेमकटकौ करमूलयोः ।<br>
लीलासरसिजं हस्ते ब्रह्मन्ब्रह्माण्डकर्णिका ॥ १.२५.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O sage), चन्द्र-अर्क-मण्डले (the discs of moon and sun) कर-मूलयोः (at his wrists) हेम-कटकौ (are golden bracelets); हस्ते (in his hand) ब्रह्माण्ड-कर्णिका (with the cosmic egg as its centre) लीला-सरसिजम् (is a play-lotus).
</p>

**Translation:** "O sage, the discs of the sun and moon are golden bracelets on his wrists; in his hand is a play-lotus whose seed-pod is the cosmic egg."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The universe as a toy lotus.** The cosmic egg (ब्रह्माण्ड) is only the centre of a flower he twirls in his hand.

## Verse 1.25.9 { #1-25-9 }

<p class="shloka">
ताराबिन्दुचितं लोलपुष्करावर्तपल्लवम् ।<br>
एकार्णवपयोधौ तमेकमम्बरमम्बरम् ॥ १.२५.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एक-अर्णव-पयोधौ (in the single ocean of the deluge) तारा-बिन्दु-चितम् (dotted with stars) लोल-पुष्कर-आवर्त-पल्लवम् (with fringes of swirling clouds) तम् एकम् अम्बरम् (that one sky) [तस्य] अम्बरम् (is his garment).
</p>

**Translation:** "In the single ocean of the deluge, the one sky, dotted with stars and fringed with swirling clouds, is his garment."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **अम्बरम् अम्बरम् — a pun.** अम्बर means both sky and garment. The sky is his robe — as Śiva is दिगम्बर, 'clothed in the directions'.

## Verse 1.25.10 { #1-25-10 }

<p class="shloka">
एवंरूपस्य तस्याग्रे नियतिर्नित्यकामिनी ।<br>
अनस्तमितसंरम्भमारम्भैः परिनृत्यति ॥ १.२५.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एवं-रूपस्य तस्य अग्रे (before him of such a form) नित्य-कामिनी नियतिः (Destiny, ever in love) अनस्तमित-संरम्भम् (with unceasing zeal) आरम्भैः (with her undertakings) परिनृत्यति (dances all around).
</p>

**Translation:** "Before him, so adorned, Destiny, his ever-loving bride, dances all around with unceasing zeal, through all her undertakings."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Niyati dances.** The fixed order of causes and effects is her dance. Everything that happens 'by fate' is a step of her dance.

## Verse 1.25.11 { #1-25-11 }

<p class="shloka">
तस्या नर्तनलोलाया जगन्मण्डपकोटरे ।<br>
अरुद्धस्पन्दरूपाया आगमापायचञ्चुरे ॥ १.२५.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नर्तन-लोलायाः (eager to dance) अरुद्ध-स्पन्द-रूपायाः (whose nature is unchecked movement) आगम-अपाय-चञ्चुरे (skilled in coming and going) तस्याः (of her) जगत्-मण्डप-कोटरे (in the hollow of the world-pavilion) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Of her, eager in her dance, whose very nature is unchecked movement, skilled in bringing and taking away, dancing in the hall of the world …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **आगमापाय — coming and going.** The Gītā's word for the transient (2.14). Destiny's whole art is making things come and go. The sentence runs on.

## Verse 1.25.12 { #1-25-12 }

<p class="shloka">
चारुभूषणमङ्गेषु देवलोकान्तरावली ।<br>
आपातालं नभोलम्बं कबरीमण्डलं बृहत् ॥ १.२५.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[तस्याः] अङ्गेषु (on her limbs) देव-लोक-अन्तर-आवली (the series of heavenly worlds) चारु-भूषणम् (is a lovely ornament); आपातालं नभः-लम्बं (from the underworld to the sky) बृहत् कबरी-मण्डलम् (is her great mass of braided hair).
</p>

**Translation:** "… the heavenly worlds are the lovely ornaments on her limbs, and her great braid of hair stretches from the underworld to the sky."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The cosmos as her body.** As in the Gītā's universal form (11.7, <span class="parallel">इहैकस्थं जगत्कृत्स्नं पश्याद्य सचराचरम्</span>). *See here the whole world in one place, in my body.*

## Verse 1.25.13 { #1-25-13 }

<p class="shloka">
नरकाली च मञ्जीरमाला कलकलोज्ज्वला ।<br>
प्रोता दुष्कृतसूत्रेण पातालचरणे स्थिता ॥ १.२५.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नरक-आली च (and the row of hells) दुष्कृत-सूत्रेण प्रोता (strung on the thread of evil deeds) कल-कल-उज्ज्वला (bright and jingling) मञ्जीर-माला (an anklet) पाताल-चरणे स्थिता (rests on her foot, the underworld).
</p>

**Translation:** "The row of hells, strung on the thread of evil deeds, jingles brightly as an anklet on her foot, the underworld."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hells strung on the thread of deeds.** Even hell is only the jingling of Destiny's anklet — its thread is our own misdeeds.

## Verse 1.25.14 { #1-25-14 }

<p class="shloka">
कस्तूरिकातिलककं क्रियासंख्योपकल्पितम् ।<br>
चित्रितं चित्रगुप्तेन यमे वदनपट्टके ॥ १.२५.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यमे वदन-पट्टके (on Yama's forehead) चित्रगुप्तेन (by Citragupta) क्रिया-संख्या-उपकल्पितं (made from the count of deeds) कस्तूरिका-तिलककं चित्रितम् (a musk mark is painted).
</p>

**Translation:** "On the forehead of Yama, Citragupta paints a musk mark made from the tally of all deeds."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Citragupta** is Yama's scribe, who records every person's deeds. The ledger of karma is a cosmetic mark on Death's face.

## Verse 1.25.15 { #1-25-15 }

<p class="shloka">
कालास्यं समुपादाय कल्पान्तेषु किलाकुला ।<br>
नृत्यत्येषा पुनर्देवी स्फुटच्छैलघनारवम् ॥ १.२५.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कल्प-अन्तेषु (at the ends of the aeons) किल आकुला (frenzied) एषा देवी (this goddess) काल-आस्यं समुपादाय (taking on the face of Time) स्फुटत्-शैल-घन-आरवं (with the roar of splitting mountains and clouds) पुनः नृत्यति (dances again).
</p>

**Translation:** "At the ends of the aeons this goddess, frenzied, takes on the face of Time and dances again, to the roar of splitting mountains and thundering clouds."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kālī.** The goddess who takes the face of Time (काल) is Kālī — Destiny becomes Time, and Time becomes the dancer of dissolution.

## Verse 1.25.16 { #1-25-16 }

<p class="shloka">
पश्चात्प्रालम्बविभ्रान्तकौमारभृतबर्हिभिः ।<br>
नेत्रत्रयबृहद्रन्ध्रभूरिभाङ्कारभीषणैः ॥ १.२५.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पश्चात् (behind) प्रालम्ब-विभ्रान्त-कौमार-भृत-बर्हिभिः (with the peacocks of Kumāra, dangling and whirling) नेत्र-त्रय-बृहत्-रन्ध्र-भूरि-भाङ्कार-भीषणैः (fearsome with booming from the great openings of three eyes) …
</p>

**Translation:** "Behind her whirl the peacocks of Kumāra, and terrible booming comes from the great openings of three eyes …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints वृहद्; corrected here to बृहद्.
- **A catalogue of ornaments** (1.25.16–1.25.19). Her ornaments are the gods themselves: Kumāra's peacock, Śiva's heads, Indra's skull. The sentence runs on to verse 19.
- **Reading note.** These verses are densely compressed; the translation gives the general picture rather than every detail.

## Verse 1.25.17 { #1-25-17 }

<p class="shloka">
लम्बलोलजटाचन्द्रविकीर्णहरमूर्धभिः ।<br>
उच्चरच्चारुमन्दारगौरीकबरचामरैः ॥ १.२५.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
लम्ब-लोल-जटा-चन्द्र-विकीर्ण-हर-मूर्धभिः (with Hara's heads, from whose long swaying locks the moon is scattered) उच्चरत्-चारु-मन्दार-गौरी-कबर-चामरैः (with whisks made of Gaurī's braids, from which mandāra flowers fall) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… with Śiva's heads, the moon tumbling from their long swaying locks, and with whisks made of Gaurī's braids, shedding mandāra flowers …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Śiva and Gaurī as ornaments.** At the dissolution even Śiva and Pārvatī are part of the dancer's adornment.

## Verse 1.25.18 { #1-25-18 }

<p class="shloka">
उत्ताण्डवाचलाकारभैरवोदरतुम्बकैः ।<br>
रणत्सशतरन्ध्रेन्द्रदेहभिक्षाकपालकैः ॥ १.२५.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
उत्ताण्डव-अचल-आकार-भैरव-उदर-तुम्बकैः (with gourds made from Bhairava's mountain-like belly, dancing wildly) रणत्-स-शत-रन्ध्र-इन्द्र-देह-भिक्षा-कपालकैः (with begging skull-bowls made from Indra's body, ringing through its hundred holes) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… with gourds made from the mountainous belly of Bhairava, dancing wildly, and begging-bowls made from Indra's body, ringing through its hundred holes …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Indra's hundred holes.** Indra is called 'thousand-eyed'; here his body becomes a skull-bowl that rings through its many openings. Grotesque, but the point is clear: no god is beyond dissolution.

## Verse 1.25.19 { #1-25-19 }

<p class="shloka">
शुष्कशारीरखट्वाङ्गभरैरापूरिताम्बरम् ।<br>
भीषयत्यात्मनात्मानं सर्वसंहारकारिणी ॥ १.२५.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्व-संहार-कारिणी (the destroyer of all) शुष्क-शारीर-खट्वाङ्ग-भरैः (with heaps of skull-staffs made of dry bodies) आपूरित-अम्बरं [यथा भवति तथा] (filling the sky) आत्मना आत्मानं भीषयति (frightens herself by herself).
</p>

**Translation:** "… she who destroys all, filling the sky with heaps of skull-staffs made of dried bodies, frightens even herself."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **आत्मनात्मानं — she frightens herself.** There is no one else. At the dissolution only the One remains, and its terror is only its own reflection.
- **Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.2:** <span class="parallel">सोऽबिभेत् … यन्मदन्यन्नास्ति कस्मान्नु बिभेमीति</span> — *He was afraid … then he thought: since there is nothing other than me, of what am I afraid?*

## Verse 1.25.20 { #1-25-20 }

<p class="shloka">
विश्वरूपशिरश्चक्रचारुपुष्करमालया ।<br>
ताण्डवेषु विवल्गन्त्या महाकल्पेषु राजते ॥ १.२५.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सा] महा-कल्पेषु ताण्डवेषु (in the wild dances of the great aeons) विवल्गन्त्या (leaping) विश्वरूप-शिरः-चक्र-चारु-पुष्कर-मालया (with a lovely lotus-garland of the heads of Viśvarūpa) राजते (shines).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **ताण्डव — the cosmic dance.** Śiva's dance of destruction. Here performed by Destiny, Time's bride.

## Verse 1.25.32 { #1-25-32 }

<p class="shloka">
भूयः करोति भुवनानि वनान्तराणि लोकान्तराणि जनजालककल्पनां च ।<br>
आचारचारुकलनामचलां चलां च पङ्काद्यथार्भकजनो रचनामखिन्नः ॥ १.२५.३२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सः] भूयः (again) भुवनानि वन-अन्तराणि लोक-अन्तराणि (worlds, forests and other realms) जन-जालक-कल्पनां च (and the fashioning of crowds of people) आचार-चारु-कलनाम् अचलां चलां च रचनां (a creation of fair customs, both fixed and moving) अखिन्नः (untiring) पङ्कात् अर्भक-जनः यथा (as a child [makes things] from mud) करोति (makes).
</p>

**Translation:** Again and again, untiring, he makes worlds, forests, other realms and crowds of people — a creation of fixed and moving things with fine customs — the way a child makes things out of mud.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (vasantatilakā).
- **Like a child playing with mud.** The final image of the sarga: all creation is a child's game with mud. It is made and unmade without weariness or purpose. This is लीला, the play that the teaching will reveal as the nature of the world.
- **Brahma Sūtra 2.1.33:** <span class="parallel">लोकवत्तु लीलाकैवल्यम्</span> — *[creation is] mere play, as in the world.* The Lord creates not from need, but as play.
