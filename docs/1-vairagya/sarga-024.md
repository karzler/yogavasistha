# Sarga 24

*Vairāgya Prakaraṇa · Time as a prince at play (10 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 24 at a glance"
    Śrī Rāma's speech continues: Time as a prince at play.

    1. Time is a young king hunting in the jungle of the world, with all creatures as his game (1.24.1–1.24.2).
    2. His pleasure-pond is the ocean of dissolution, his breakfast the worlds, his tigress the fierce Goddess, his pets the gods themselves (1.24.3–1.24.8).
    3. His bow is Non-existence; he plays everywhere like a restless monkey (1.24.9–1.24.10).

## Verse 1.24.1 { #1-24-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
अस्योड्डामरलीलस्य दूरास्तसकलापदः ।<br>
संसारे राजपुत्रस्य कालस्याकलितौजसः ॥ १.२४.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
संसारे (in the world) अस्य (of this) उड्डामर-लीलस्य (whose play is tremendous) दूर-अस्त-सकल-आपदः (who has cast all misfortunes far from himself) अकलित-ओजसः (of immeasurable power) राज-पुत्रस्य कालस्य (of Time, the prince) …
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "In this world, Time is like a prince at play — his sport tremendous, his power beyond measure, himself untouched by any misfortune …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Time as a prince on a hunt.** This short sarga continues the meditation on Time with a single extended image: Time as a young king out hunting, with his queen (fate, 1.24.3–1.24.9).
- **Untouched by misfortune.** Time causes every misfortune and suffers none. The sentence runs on into the next verse.

## Verse 1.24.2 { #1-24-2 }

<p class="shloka">
अस्यैवाचरतो दीनैर्मुग्धैर्भूतमृगब्रजैः ।<br>
आखेटकं जर्जरिते जगज्जङ्गलजालके ॥ १.२४.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्य एव (of this very one) जर्जरिते जगत्-जङ्गल-जालके (in the tattered net-like jungle of the world) दीनैः मुग्धैः भूत-मृग-व्रजैः (with the wretched, bewildered herds of creatures as game) आखेटकम् आचरतः (who goes hunting) …
</p>

**Translation:** "… who goes hunting in the tattered jungle of the world, with the wretched, bewildered herds of creatures for his game …"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The hunt.** Beings are the deer; the world is the forest; Time is the hunter. Compare 1.17.15, desire as a hunter.

## Verse 1.24.3 { #1-24-3 }

<p class="shloka">
एकदेशोल्लसच्चारुवडवानलपङ्कजा ।<br>
क्रीडापुष्करिणी रम्या कल्पकालमहार्णवः ॥ १.२४.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कल्प-काल-महा-अर्णवः (the great ocean at the end of an aeon) एक-देश-उल्लसत्-चारु-वडवा-अनल-पङ्कजा (with the lovely submarine fire blooming in one corner like a lotus) [तस्य] रम्या क्रीडा-पुष्करिणी [भवति] (is his lovely pleasure-pond).
</p>

**Translation:** "The great ocean of the world's dissolution, with the submarine fire blooming in one corner like a lotus, is his pleasure-pond."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Cosmic scale.** The ocean that swallows the world at the end of an aeon is only a garden pool for Time.

## Verse 1.24.4 { #1-24-4 }

<p class="shloka">
कटुतिक्ताम्लभूताद्यैः सदधिक्षीरसागरैः ।<br>
तैरेव तैः पर्युषितैर्जगद्भिः कल्ववर्तनम् ॥ १.२४.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कटु-तिक्त-अम्ल-भूत-आद्यैः (with bitter, pungent and sour beings and the like) स-दधि-क्षीर-सागरैः (with the oceans of curd and milk) तैः एव तैः पर्युषितैः जगद्भिः (with those same stale worlds) [तस्य] कल्य-वर्तनम् [भवति] (is his morning meal).
</p>

**Translation:** "His breakfast is the worlds themselves, stale and leftover, with their bitter, pungent and sour creatures and their oceans of curd and milk."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text prints कल्ववर्तनम्; the sense suggests कल्यवर्तनम्, a morning meal. The oceans of curd and milk are from Purāṇic cosmology, which pictures seven concentric oceans.

## Verse 1.24.5 { #1-24-5 }

<p class="shloka">
चण्डी चतुरसंचारा सर्वमातृगणान्विता ।<br>
संसारवनविन्यस्ता व्याघ्री भूतौघघातिनी ॥ १.२४.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चण्डी (fierce Caṇḍī) चतुर-सञ्चारा (skilful in movement) सर्व-मातृ-गण-अन्विता (with all the hosts of mother-goddesses) संसार-वन-विन्यस्ता (placed in the forest of the world) भूत-ओघ-घातिनी (killing hosts of beings) व्याघ्री [तस्य भवति] (is his tigress).
</p>

**Translation:** "Fierce Caṇḍī, swift in her movements, attended by all the mother-goddesses, is the tigress he has set loose in the forest of the world to kill the hosts of beings."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Caṇḍī — the fierce Goddess.** Time's hunting companion is the Goddess in her destructive form, with the Mātṛkās, the 'mothers'. Śākta and Śaiva imagery woven into the text ([the synthesis of traditions](../index.md#a-synthesis)).

## Verse 1.24.6 { #1-24-6 }

<p class="shloka">
ज्ञध्वी करतले ज्ञथ्वी पानपात्री रसान्विता ।<br>
कमलोत्पलकह्लारलोलजालकमालिता ॥ १.२४.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कर-तले (in his palm) रस-अन्विता (full of the juice of life) कमल-उत्पल-कह्लार-लोल-जालक-मालिता (garlanded with swaying lotuses, blue lilies and white water-lilies) पान-पात्री [अस्ति] (is a drinking cup).
</p>

**Translation:** "In his palm he holds a drinking cup, full of the juice of life, garlanded with swaying lotuses and lilies."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The words ज्ञध्वी and ज्ञथ्वी in the first line are not Sanskrit words and appear to be a corruption in the online text. The translation follows the clear sense of the rest: the earth itself is Time's wine-cup.

## Verse 1.24.7 { #1-24-7 }

<p class="shloka">
विरावी विकटास्फोटो नृसिंहो भुजपञ्जरे ।<br>
सटाविकटपीनांसः कृतः क्रीडाशकुन्तकः ॥ १.२४.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विरावी (roaring) विकट-आस्फोटः (with terrible bursts) सटा-विकट-पीन-अंसः (with broad shoulders made fearsome by his mane) नृसिंहः (Narasiṃha) भुज-पञ्जरे (in the cage of his arms) क्रीडा-शकुन्तकः कृतः (has been made a pet bird).
</p>

**Translation:** "Narasiṃha himself, roaring and terrible, his broad shoulders bristling with mane, has been made a pet bird in the cage of his arms."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Even the avatāras.** Narasiṃha, Viṣṇu's man-lion incarnation, is only a pet in Time's hand. The divine forms too belong to the world of Time.

## Verse 1.24.8 { #1-24-8 }

<p class="shloka">
अलाबुवीणामधुरः शरद्व्योमलसच्छविः ।<br>
देवः किल महाकालो लीलाकोकिलबालकः ॥ १.२४.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अलाबु-वीणा-मधुरः (sweet as a gourd-lute) शरत्-व्योम-लसत्-छविः (with the shining colour of the autumn sky) महाकालः देवः किल (the god Mahākāla himself) लीला-कोकिल-बालकः [तस्य] (is his playful young cuckoo).
</p>

**Translation:** "The god Mahākāla himself, sweet-voiced as a gourd-lute and coloured like the autumn sky, is his playful young cuckoo."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Mahākāla — Śiva as 'great Time'.** Even he is a songbird for this Time. The poet suggests a Time beyond even the god of Time — that is, the Absolute.

## Verse 1.24.9 { #1-24-9 }

<p class="shloka">
अजस्रस्फूर्जिताकारो वान्तदुःखशरावलिः ।<br>
अभावनामकोदण्डः परिस्फुरति सर्वतः ॥ १.२४.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अजस्र-स्फूर्जित-आकारः (ever-thundering in form) वान्त-दुःख-शर-आवलिः (shooting out rows of arrows of sorrow) अभाव-नामक-कोदण्डः (his bow called Non-existence) सर्वतः परिस्फुरति (flashes everywhere).
</p>

**Translation:** "His bow, called Non-existence, thunders without pause and shoots out showers of arrows of sorrow everywhere."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The bow of Non-existence.** Every arrow it shoots is a death. Compare Kṛṣṇa's words in Gītā 11.33, <span class="parallel">निमित्तमात्रं भव सव्यसाचिन्</span>. *be only the instrument, Arjuna* — Time has already slain them.

## Verse 1.24.10 { #1-24-10 }

<p class="shloka">
अनुत्तमस्त्वधिकविलासपण्डितो भ्रमच्चलन्परिविलसन्विदारयन् ।<br>
जरज्जगज्जनितविलोलमर्कटः परिस्फुरद्वपुरिह काल ईहते ॥ १.२४.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह (here) अन्-उत्तमः (unsurpassed) तु अधिक-विलास-पण्डितः (expert in lavish sport) जरत्-जगत्-जनित-विलोल-मर्कटः (a restless monkey born of the old world) परिस्फुरत्-वपुः (with flashing body) कालः (Time) भ्रमन् चलन् परिविलसन् विदारयन् (wandering, moving, sporting, tearing) ईहते (acts).
</p>

**Translation:** Here Time, unsurpassed and expert in lavish sport, acts like a restless monkey born of the ageing world — its body flashing, wandering, moving, playing, tearing apart.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A closing verse in a longer metre** (rucirā).
- **Time as a monkey.** The image of the restless mind (1.17.29) is now given to Time. The two are closer than they seem: the teaching will show that Time is a measure the mind makes.
