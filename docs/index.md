# योगवासिष्ठ — Yoga Vasiṣṭha

*A verse-by-verse study: the Sanskrit text, word-by-word meanings, an English translation, and pointers for reflection.*

## What is the Yoga Vasiṣṭha?

The Yoga Vasiṣṭha is a dialogue between the sage Vasiṣṭha and the young prince Śrī Rāma. Troubled by the suffering and impermanence of life, Śrī Rāma asks his teacher what is real and how one should live. Vasiṣṭha answers through philosophy and through many stories.

Traditionally attributed to Vālmīki, the full text has about 29,000 verses in six books, called prakaraṇas. Its central teaching is that the world is an appearance in consciousness, and that liberation is possible while living fully in the world.

Four features set it apart. The commentary on each verse returns to them often.

1. <a id="the-lord-as-student"></a>**The Lord as student.** In the Bhagavad Gītā the Lord is the Guru, teaching Arjuna. In the Yoga Vasiṣṭha the Lord is the student: Śrī Rāma sits before Vasiṣṭha to learn. Together the two texts show the teaching from both sides.

2. <a id="questions-worth-asking"></a>**Questions worth asking.** Often we do not even know what to ask. Here Śrī Rāma, still a teenager, asks the questions that matter most — about suffering, time, the mind and the meaning of life. The text teaches how to ask before it teaches the answers.

3. <a id="a-synthesis"></a>**A synthesis.** The text is Advaita Vedānta at its core, yet it weaves in insights from other traditions, such as Jainism, Buddhism and Kashmir Śaivism, into one whole.

4. <a id="wisdom-lived"></a>**Wisdom lived, not renounced.** Śrī Rāma does not take up renunciation. Having awakened, he goes on to live a full householder life, showing that liberation is possible in the midst of duty and the world.

## How to use this site

**Read in order.** The text builds on itself, so start at [Sarga 1](1-vairagya/sarga-001.md) and go on from there. Use the menu to move between prakaraṇas and sargas, and the links at the foot of each page to go to the next or previous sarga. Each prakaraṇa's page lists its sargas with a one-line summary, and each sarga opens with a short box giving its outline.

**Each verse has four parts:**

1. **The Sanskrit verse** in Devanagari, with its reference: Prakaraṇa.Sarga.Verse, so 1.2.5 is the fifth verse of the second sarga of the first prakaraṇa.
2. **Word by word.** The verse's words rearranged into prose order (anvaya), each followed by its meaning in brackets. Compounds are split with hyphens, and words added to complete the sense are shown in square brackets.
3. **Translation.** A plain English rendering of the whole verse, made directly from the Sanskrit.
4. **Pointers for Reflection.** Key words, connections with other verses, and parallels in the Bhagavad Gītā and the Upaniṣads, quoted in Sanskrit with an English rendering. They are starting points for your own reflection, not a final word.

**Search and find.** Use the search box at the top to find any English or Sanskrit word. Key Sanskrit terms are rendered the same way throughout; the [Glossary](glossary.md) lists them with a link to the verse where each first appears.

**Link to a verse.** Every verse has its own address. Hover over a verse heading and click the ¶ sign to get it; for example, verse 1.1.2 is at `…/1-vairagya/sarga-001/#1-1-2`.

**About the text.** The Sanskrit follows the commonly printed text. Where it has an obvious misprint, the verse is corrected and a **Reading note** says what was changed; where a reading is uncertain, it is left as printed and flagged.
