# Sarga 4

*Utpatti Prakaraṇa · What is the mind? (80 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 4 at a glance"
    The first day of the Utpatti teaching ends, and the second begins with the question "What is mind?"

    1. Evening falls on the hushed assembly; Vasiṣṭha stops for the evening rites; the court disperses; night falls and dawn returns, described in a long poem (3.4.1–3.4.35).
    2. Śrī Rāma asks what the mind is. Vasiṣṭha: it has no form but a name; it is the appearing of objects, which is imagining (3.4.36–3.4.48).
    3. The seen lies in the seer as a plant in its seed. When the seen is absent, the seer is "alone" (kevalībhāva), and liking and disliking die down (3.4.49–3.4.58).
    4. Śrī Rāma objects with Gītā 2.16: if the world is real, it can never end. Vasiṣṭha answers that if it were real no one could be free, but the free exist; so the world is Brahman, "the full in the full" (3.4.59–3.4.70).
    5. Śrī Rāma protests that this sounds absurd; Vasiṣṭha promises to show how world and mind were never born, and how the restless mind spins its display (3.4.71–3.4.80).

## Verse 3.4.1 { #3-4-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
कथयत्येवमुद्दामवचने मुनिनायके ।<br>
श्रोतुमेकरसे जाते जने मौनमुपस्थिते ॥ ३.४.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुनि-नायके (the chief of sages) एवम् उद्दाम-वचने कथयति [सति] (speaking such lofty words), जने (the people) श्रोतुम् एक-रसे जाते (becoming wholly intent on listening) मौनम् उपस्थिते (and having fallen silent) —
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vālmīki said: "As the chief of sages spoke these lofty words, the whole gathering became wholly absorbed in listening, and fell silent —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The frame returns.** Vālmīki, telling the story to Bharadvāja, describes the assembly at the end of the first day of the Utpatti teaching. Verses 1–35 are a poem of evening, night and dawn.
- **एकरस** — "of one taste": the listeners' minds have become one with what they hear.

## Verse 3.4.2 { #3-4-2 }

<p class="shloka">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">

**Pointers for Reflection:**

## Verse 3.4.4 { #3-4-4 }

<p class="shloka">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">

- **मुहूर्त** — a period of 48 minutes. Evening approaches.

## Verse 3.4.6 { #3-4-6 }

<p class="shloka">
श्रुतं चिन्तयितुं भानुरिवाहोरचनाभ्रमम् ।<br>
तत्याजैकान्तमगमच्छून्यमस्तगिरेस्तटम् ॥ ३.४.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भानुः (the sun) श्रुतं चिन्तयितुम् इव (as if to reflect on what it had heard) अहः-रचना-भ्रमं तत्याज (left the whirl of making the day) [च] अस्त-गिरेः शून्यम् एकान्तं तटम् अगमत् (and went to the empty, solitary slope of the western mountain).
</p>

**Translation:** "As if wanting quiet to ponder what it had heard, the sun laid down the day's busy work and withdrew behind the mountain of sunset, to a deserted, solitary ridge."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hearing, then reflection.** श्रवण is followed by मनन. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka: <span class="parallel">आत्मा वा अरे द्रष्टव्यः श्रोतव्यो मन्तव्यो निदिध्यासितव्यः</span> — *the Self is to be seen, heard, reflected on and meditated upon* (2.4.5). Solitude helps reflection: <span class="parallel">विविक्तदेशसेवित्वमरतिर्जनसंसदि</span> (Gītā 13.10).

## Verse 3.4.7 { #3-4-7 }

<p class="shloka">
उत्तस्थुर्मिहिकारम्भसमता वनभूमिषु ।<br>
विज्ञानश्रवणादन्तःशीतलाः शान्तता इव ॥ ३.४.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वन-भूमिषु (in the forest lands) मिहिका-आरम्भ-समताः (the evenness of rising mist) उत्तस्थुः (arose), विज्ञान-श्रवणात् (from hearing wisdom) अन्तः-शीतलाः शान्तताः इव (like the inner coolness of peace).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Coolness** (शीतलता) is the text's constant image for peace — the opposite of the burning of desire.

<p class="shloka">

</p>

<p class="anvaya">
