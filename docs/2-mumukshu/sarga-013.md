# Sarga 13

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · The knower, and the first gatekeeper: calm (84 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 13 at a glance"
    The knower in the world, and the first gatekeeper: calm (शम).

    1. Those who have seen the Self act and do not act; they neither crave nor reject, and shine as they always were (2.13.1–2.13.11).
    2. Inner foolishness is the worst disease; better to beg than to live in folly. This teaching is a light that does not go out (2.13.12–2.13.22). (Verse 15 is missing in the source.)
    3. Sorrows are endless, pleasures a blade of grass; do not sleep in a burning house. The supreme state exists — inquire; you have nothing to lose (2.13.23–2.13.36).
    4. It is reached not by travel or austerity but by mastering the mind, sitting comfortably and inquiring (2.13.37–2.13.49).
    5. Calm cools the desert of saṃsāra, turns enemies to friends, is armour against sorrow. "Such a one is called calm" (2.13.50–2.13.84).

## Verse 2.13.1 { #2-13-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
एतां दृष्टिमवष्टभ्य दृष्टात्मानः सुबुद्धयः ।<br>
विचरन्तीह संसारे महान्तोऽभ्युदिता इव ॥ २.१३.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एतां दृष्टिम् अवष्टभ्य (holding to this view) दृष्ट-आत्मानः सु-बुद्धयः (the wise who have seen the Self) इह संसारे (here in saṃsāra) महान्तः अभ्युदिताः इव (like great ones risen high) विचरन्ति (move about).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "Holding firmly to this vision, the wise who have seen the Self move through saṃsāra like great ones risen above it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **In the world, above it.** The knower does not leave the world; he moves in it from a higher vantage, like the sun crossing the sky.
- **Sarga 13** describes the knower, then the first gatekeeper: शम, calm (from v. 50).

## Verse 2.13.2 { #2-13-2 }

<p class="shloka">
न शोचन्ति न वाञ्छन्ति न याचन्ते शुभाशुभम् ।<br>
सर्वमेव च कुर्वन्ति न कुर्वन्तीह किंचन ॥ २.१३.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[ते] शुभ-अशुभं न शोचन्ति (do not grieve over good or bad) न वाञ्छन्ति (do not crave) न याचन्ते (do not beg); सर्वम् एव च कुर्वन्ति (they do everything) इह किंचन न कुर्वन्ति (and do nothing at all here).
</p>

**Translation:** "They do not grieve over good or bad fortune, crave it, or beg for it. They do everything, and yet here they do nothing at all."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The Gītā:** <span class="parallel">यो न हृष्यति न द्वेष्टि न शोचति न काङ्क्षति । शुभाशुभपरित्यागी</span> — *who neither rejoices nor hates, neither grieves nor craves, who has left behind good and bad* (12.17); and <span class="parallel">कर्मण्यभिप्रवृत्तोऽपि नैव किंचित्करोति सः</span> — *though fully engaged in action, he does nothing at all* (4.20).

## Verse 2.13.3 { #2-13-3 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वच्छमेवावतिष्ठन्ते स्वच्छं कुर्वन्ति यान्ति हि ।<br>
हेयोपादेयतापक्षरहिताः स्वात्मनि स्थिताः ॥ २.१३.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्व-आत्मनि स्थिताः (established in their own Self) हेय-उपादेयता-पक्ष-रहिताः (without taking sides on what to reject or accept) स्वच्छम् एव अवतिष्ठन्ते (they remain clear) स्वच्छं कुर्वन्ति (act clearly) यान्ति हि (and go their way).
</p>

**Translation:** "Established in their own Self, taking no side over what to reject or accept, they remain clear, act clearly, and go their way."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **No grasping, no rejecting.** हेय-उपादेय — to be shunned, to be seized. The knower's clarity is free of both pulls.

## Verse 2.13.4 { #2-13-4 }

<p class="shloka">
आयान्ति च न चायान्ति प्रयान्ति च न यान्ति च ।<br>
कुर्वन्त्यपि न कुर्वन्ति न वदन्ति वदन्ति च ॥ २.१३.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[ते] आयान्ति च न च आयान्ति (they come and do not come) प्रयान्ति च न यान्ति च (go and do not go) कुर्वन्ति अपि न कुर्वन्ति (act and do not act) न वदन्ति वदन्ति च (do not speak and speak).
</p>

**Translation:** "They arrive, and do not arrive; they set out, and do not leave; they act, and do not act; they are silent, and they speak."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Paradox of the free.** The Īśa: <span class="parallel">तदेजति तन्नैजति तद्दूरे तद्वन्तिके</span> — *it moves and it moves not; it is far and it is near* (5). The knower shares this nature: movement without a mover.

## Verse 2.13.5 { #2-13-5 }

<p class="shloka">
ये केचन समारम्भा याश्च काश्चन दृष्टयः ।<br>
हेयोपादेयतस्तास्ताः क्षीयन्तेऽधिगते पदे ॥ २.१३.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये केचन समारम्भाः (whatever undertakings) याः च काः-चन दृष्टयः (and whatever views) हेय-उपादेयतः (from [the notion of] rejecting and accepting) — ताः ताः (all of them) पदे अधिगते (when the state is reached) क्षीयन्ते (fade away).
</p>

**Translation:** "Whatever undertakings and views there are, born of accepting and rejecting, all fade away when that state is reached."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Repeated from 2.12.7.** The refrain marks the teaching's key points.

## Verse 2.13.6 { #2-13-6 }

<p class="shloka">
परित्यक्तसमस्तेहं मनोमधुरवृत्तिमत् ।<br>
सर्वतः सुखमभ्येति चन्द्रबिम्ब इव स्थितम् ॥ २.१३.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
परित्यक्त-समस्त-ईहं (having given up all striving) मधुर-वृत्तिमत् मनः (the mind, sweet in its ways) सर्वतः सुखम् अभ्येति (finds happiness everywhere), चन्द्र-बिम्बः इव स्थितम् (steady like the moon's disc).
</p>

**Translation:** "Having given up all restless striving, the mind becomes gentle in its ways and finds happiness everywhere, steady as the moon's disc."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Striving ends in sweetness.** The effort of Sargas 4–9 was to reach this effortless state. Effort is the road; rest is the destination.

## Verse 2.13.7 { #2-13-7 }

<p class="shloka">
अपि निर्मननारम्भमव्यस्ताखिलकौतुकम् ।<br>
आत्मन्येव न मात्यन्तरिन्दाविव रसायनम् ॥ २.१३.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
निर्मनन-आरम्भम् अपि (though free of any start of thought) अव्यस्त-अखिल-कौतुकम् (all its eagerness gone) [तत् सुखम्] आत्मनि एव अन्तः न माति (that joy cannot be contained within the Self), इन्दौ इव रसायनम् (like the nectar in the moon).
</p>

**Translation:** "Though thought no longer stirs and all eagerness is gone, that joy overflows the Self, as nectar overflows the moon."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Overflowing.** Inner peace is not empty but full to overflowing. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka: <span class="parallel">पूर्णमदः पूर्णमिदम्</span> — *that is full, this is full*.

## Verse 2.13.8 { #2-13-8 }

<p class="shloka">
न करोतीन्द्रजालानि नानुधावति वासनाम् ।<br>
बालचापलमुत्सृज्य पूर्वमेव विराजते ॥ २.१३.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[मनः] इन्द्रजालानि न करोति (does not perform conjuring tricks) वासनां न अनुधावति (does not chase tendencies); बाल-चापलम् उत्सृज्य (dropping childish restlessness) पूर्वम् एव विराजते (it shines as it was before).
</p>

**Translation:** "The mind no longer conjures up illusions or chases its tendencies. Giving up its childish restlessness, it shines as it always was."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **As before.** पूर्वम् एव — the original state. Nothing new is gained; the old restlessness is dropped.

## Verse 2.13.9 { #2-13-9 }

<p class="shloka">
एवंविधा हि वृत्तय<br>
आत्मतत्त्वावलोकनाल्लभ्यन्ते नान्यथा ॥ २.१३.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एवंविधाः वृत्तयः हि (such states of mind indeed) आत्म-तत्त्व-अवलोकनात् लभ्यन्ते (are gained by seeing the truth of the Self), न अन्यथा (not otherwise).
</p>

**Translation:** "Such states of mind come only from seeing the truth of the Self, and in no other way."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Only by seeing.** Not by willpower or technique alone. Calm is a fruit of understanding.

## Verse 2.13.10 { #2-13-10 }

<p class="shloka">
तस्माद्विचारेणात्मैवान्वेष्टव्य उपासनीयो<br>
ज्ञातव्यो यावज्जीवं पुरुषेण नेतरदिति ॥ २.१३.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तस्मात् (therefore) पुरुषेण (by a person) यावत्-जीवं (as long as life lasts) विचारेण (through inquiry) आत्मा एव अन्वेष्टव्यः (the Self alone should be sought) उपासनीयः (contemplated) ज्ञातव्यः (and known), न इतरत् इति (and nothing else).
</p>

**Translation:** "So as long as one lives, the Self alone should be sought through inquiry, contemplated and known — nothing else."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A lifelong task.** The Bṛhadāraṇyaka: <span class="parallel">आत्मा वा अरे द्रष्टव्यः श्रोतव्यो मन्तव्यो निदिध्यासितव्यः</span> (2.4.5). The Muṇḍaka: <span class="parallel">तमेवैकं जानथ आत्मानमन्या वाचो विमुञ्चथ</span> — *know that one Self alone; give up other talk* (2.2.5).

## Verse 2.13.11 { #2-13-11 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वानुभूतेश्च शास्त्रस्य गुरोश्चैवैकवाक्यता ।<br>
यस्याभ्यासेन तेनात्मा सन्ततेनावलोक्यते ॥ २.१३.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्व-अनुभूतेः (of one's own experience) शास्त्रस्य (of scripture) गुरोः च एव (and of the teacher) एक-वाक्यता (agreement) — यस्य सन्ततेन अभ्यासेन (by the continued practice of which) तेन आत्मा अवलोक्यते (the Self is seen by him).
</p>

**Translation:** "When one's own experience, scripture and the teacher speak with one voice — by steady practice of that agreement, the Self is seen."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Three witnesses agree.** The method of Śuka's story (2.1.31–35) is now made explicit. Experience alone may err; scripture alone stays abstract; a teacher alone may be followed blindly. Together they are reliable.

## Verse 2.13.12 { #2-13-12 }

<p class="shloka">
अवहेलितशास्त्रार्थैरवज्ञातमहाजनैः ।<br>
कष्टामप्यापदं प्राप्तो न मूढैः समतामियात् ॥ २.१३.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[पुरुषः] कष्टाम् आपदं प्राप्तः अपि (even having fallen into dire trouble) अवहेलित-शास्त्र-अर्थैः (with those who scorn the meaning of scripture) अवज्ञात-महा-जनैः (and disregard great people) मूढैः (with fools) समतां न इयात् (should not become like).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even in dire trouble, do not become like the fools who scorn the meaning of scripture and disregard the great."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hold your standard in hard times.** Calamity tempts one to abandon principles. That is when they matter most.

## Verse 2.13.13 { #2-13-13 }

<p class="shloka">
न व्याधिर्न विषं नापत्तथा नाधिश्च भूतले ।<br>
खेदाय स्वशरीरस्थं मौर्ख्यमेकं यथा नृणाम् ॥ २.१३.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भूतले (on earth) न व्याधिः (no illness) न विषं (no poison) न आपत् (no calamity) तथा न आधिः च (nor mental anguish) नृणां खेदाय [तथा] (so distresses people) यथा स्व-शरीर-स्थम् एकं मौर्ख्यम् (as their own foolishness, dwelling within).
</p>

**Translation:** "No illness, poison, calamity or anguish on earth troubles people as much as the one foolishness that dwells within them."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The enemy within.** Outer troubles pass; inner confusion multiplies them. Śāntideva: <span class="parallel">बाह्या भावा मया तद्वच्छक्या वारयितुं न हि । स्वचित्तं वारयिष्यामि किं ममान्यैर्निवारितैः</span> — *I cannot control outer things; I will control my own mind — what need to control the rest?* (Bodhicaryāvatāra 5.14).

## Verse 2.13.14 { #2-13-14 }

<p class="shloka">
किंचित्संस्कृतबुद्धीनां श्रुतं शास्त्रमिदं यथा ।<br>
मौर्ख्यापहं तथा शास्त्रमन्यदस्ति न किंचन ॥ २.१३.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (as) किंचित्-संस्कृत-बुद्धीनां श्रुतम् इदं शास्त्रम् (this scripture, heard by those of somewhat refined mind) मौर्ख्य-अपहम् (removes foolishness), तथा अन्यत् किंचन शास्त्रं न अस्ति (there is no other scripture like it).
</p>

**Translation:** "For those whose minds are somewhat refined, no other scripture removes foolishness as this one does when it is heard."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A bold claim for itself.** The Yoga Vasiṣṭha holds that its stories and arguments reach the ordinary thoughtful person directly. It is written for those "somewhat refined", not only for scholars.

## Verse 2.13.16 { #2-13-16 }

<p class="shloka">
आपदो या दुरुत्तारा याश्च तुच्छाः कुयोनयः ।<br>
तास्ता मौर्ख्यात्प्रसूयन्ते खदिरादिव कण्टकाः ॥ २.१३.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
याः दुरुत्ताराः आपदः (the calamities hard to escape) याः च तुच्छाः कु-योनयः (and the low, wretched births) — ताः ताः मौर्ख्यात् प्रसूयन्ते (all arise from foolishness), खदिरात् कण्टकाः इव (as thorns from the khadira tree).
</p>

**Translation:** "All the calamities hard to escape and all the low, wretched births spring from foolishness, as thorns from the khadira tree."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Thorns of the khadira.** The khadira (acacia) is all thorns. Foolishness produces suffering as naturally.

## Verse 2.13.17 { #2-13-17 }

<p class="shloka">
वरं शरावहस्तस्य चाण्डालागारवीथिषु ।<br>
भिक्षार्थमटनं राम न मौर्ख्यहतजीवितम् ॥ २.१३.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राम (O Rāma), चाण्डाल-आगार-वीथिषु (in the streets of outcastes' dwellings) शराव-हस्तस्य (with a begging-bowl in hand) भिक्षा-अर्थम् अटनं वरम् (wandering for alms is better), न मौर्ख्य-हत-जीवितम् (than a life ruined by foolishness).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāma, better to go from door to door with a begging bowl through the lanes where outcastes live than to drag out a life wrecked by foolishness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Poverty versus folly.** Outer poverty is no disgrace; inner poverty is. The Buddha and his monks went for alms; their wisdom made them the richest of men.

## Verse 2.13.18 { #2-13-18 }

<p class="shloka">
वरं घोरान्धकूपेषु कोटरेष्वेव भूरुहाम् ।<br>
अन्धकीटत्वमेकान्ते न मौर्ख्यमतिदुःखदम् ॥ २.१३.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
घोर-अन्ध-कूपेषु (in dreadful dark wells) भू-रुहां कोटरेषु एव (or in the hollows of trees) एकान्ते अन्ध-कीटत्वं वरम् (to be a blind worm alone is better), न अति-दुःख-दं मौर्ख्यम् (than the extreme sorrow of foolishness).
</p>

**Translation:** "It is better to be a blind worm alone in a dreadful dark well or the hollow of a tree than to suffer the extreme misery of foolishness."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hyperbole with a purpose.** Vasiṣṭha's strong language wakes the listener. Folly is not a small fault; it is the root of all others.

## Verse 2.13.19 { #2-13-19 }

<p class="shloka">
इममालोकमासाद्य मोक्षोपायमयं जनः ।<br>
अन्धतामेति न पुनः कश्चिन्मोहतमस्यपि ॥ २.१३.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अयं जनः (a person) इमं मोक्ष-उपाय-मयम् आलोकम् आसाद्य (having gained this light, the means to liberation) पुनः अन्धतां न एति (does not become blind again) — कश्चित् (anyone) मोह-तमसि अपि (even in the darkness of delusion).
</p>

**Translation:** "Once someone has received this light, this means to liberation, he does not become blind again, even in the darkness of delusion."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Light that stays.** True insight cannot be fully lost. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">यज्ज्ञात्वा न पुनर्मोहमेवं यास्यसि पाण्डव</span> — *knowing which, you will not fall into delusion again* (4.35).

## Verse 2.13.20 { #2-13-20 }

<p class="shloka">
तावन्नयति संकोचं तृष्णा वै मानवाम्बुजम् ।<br>
यावद्विवेकसूर्यस्य नोदिता विमला प्रभा ॥ २.१३.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तावत् (so long) तृष्णा वै मानव-अम्बुजं संकोचं नयति (craving keeps the lotus of the human heart closed) यावत् विवेक-सूर्यस्य विमला प्रभा न उदिता (as long as the pure light of the sun of discernment has not risen).
</p>

**Translation:** "Craving keeps the lotus of the human heart closed only until the pure light of the sun of discernment rises."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Sun and lotus.** The Gītā: <span class="parallel">तेषामादित्यवज्ज्ञानं प्रकाशयति तत्परम्</span> — *for them knowledge, like the sun, reveals the supreme* (5.16).

## Verse 2.13.21 { #2-13-21 }

<p class="shloka">
संसारदुःखमोक्षार्थे मादृशैः सह बन्धुभिः ।<br>

**Translation:** "Calm is the summit of all virtues and the true ornament of effort; in crises and in danger it shines in its glory."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Calm crowns effort.** Effort without calm is frantic; calm without effort is idle. Together they are complete.

## Verse 2.13.84 { #2-13-84 }

<p class="shloka">
शमममृतमहार्यमार्यगुप्तं परमवलम्ब्य परं पदं प्रयाताः ।<br>
रघुतनय यथा महानुभावाः क्रममनुपालय सिद्धये तमेव ॥ २.१३.८४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
रघु-तनय (O son of Raghu), यथा (as) महा-अनुभावाः (the great souls) अमृतम् अहार्यम् आर्य-गुप्तं परं शमम् अवलम्ब्य (relying on supreme calm — nectar, unshakeable, guarded by the noble) परं पदं प्रयाताः (reached the supreme state), [तथा] सिद्धये तम् एव क्रमम् अनुपालय (so follow that same path for your fulfilment).
</p>

**Translation:** "Son of Raghu, as the great souls reached the supreme state by relying on supreme calm — that nectar, unshakeable, guarded by the noble — so follow the same path to fulfilment."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The first gatekeeper.** Calm is not one virtue among many; it is a complete path for one who masters it (2.11.61).
