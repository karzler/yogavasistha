# Sarga 1

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · The story of Śuka (45 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 1 at a glance"
    Viśvāmitra tells the story of Śuka, which mirrors Rāma's own.

    1. Rāma already knows; his mind needs only a little polishing and rest (2.1.1–2.1.5).
    2. Śuka finds the truth by his own reflection, yet cannot rest in it; he dismisses his father's teaching as familiar (2.1.6–2.1.17).
    3. Sent to King Janaka, he is tested by neglect and by pleasure, and is moved by neither (2.1.18–2.1.28).
    4. Janaka confirms what Śuka knew: saṃsāra rises from one's own imagining and ends with it. "You are free" (2.1.29–2.1.41).
    5. Śuka rests in silence, then in samādhi, and merges like a drop in the ocean (2.1.42–2.1.45).

## Verse 2.1.1 { #2-1-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">वाल्मीकिरुवाच ।</span><br>
इति नादेन महता वचस्युक्ते सभागतैः ।<br>
राममग्रगतं प्रीत्या विश्वामित्रोऽभ्यभाषत ॥ २.१.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सभागतैः (by those gathered in the assembly) महता नादेन (with a loud voice) इति वचसि उक्ते (when these words had been spoken), विश्वामित्रः (Viśvāmitra) अग्रगतं रामं (Rāma, who stood before him) प्रीत्या अभ्यभाषत (addressed with affection).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vālmīki said: When the assembly had spoken these words aloud, Viśvāmitra turned with affection to Rāma, who stood before him, and spoke.

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The second book begins.** Book 1 closed with Śrī Rāma's questions and the sages gathered to answer them (1.33). Now the answer starts — not yet with Vasiṣṭha, but with Viśvāmitra, the sage who came to take Rāma away to protect his sacrifice (1.7).
- **प्रीत्या — with affection.** The teaching opens in love, not in judgment. The Taittirīya Upaniṣad's teacher blesses his students before teaching: <span class="parallel">सह नाववतु । सह नौ भुनक्तु</span> — *may it protect us both; may it nourish us both*.

## Verse 2.1.2 { #2-1-2 }

<p class="shloka">
न राघव तवास्त्यन्यज्ज्ञेयं ज्ञानवतां वर ।<br>
स्वयैव सूक्ष्मया बुद्ध्या सर्वं विज्ञातवानसि ॥ २.१.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राघव (O Rāghava) ज्ञानवतां वर (best of the wise), तव (for you) अन्यत् ज्ञेयं न अस्ति (there is nothing else to be known); स्वया एव सूक्ष्मया बुद्ध्या (by your own subtle intellect) सर्वं विज्ञातवान् असि (you have understood everything).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāghava, best of the wise, nothing more remains for you to know. With your own subtle intellect you have already grasped it all."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A surprising verdict.** Rāma has just asked for teaching, and the sage tells him he already knows. His long inquiry in Book 1 was itself the knowing; what he lacks is not information.
- **स्वया बुद्ध्या — by your own intellect.** Truth is not delivered from outside like a parcel. The Kena Upaniṣad says it is known <span class="parallel">प्रतिबोधविदितम्</span> — *in every act of awareness* (2.4).

## Verse 2.1.3 { #2-1-3 }

<p class="shloka">
केवलं मार्जनामात्रं मनागेवोपयुज्यते ।<br>
स्वभावविमले नित्यं स्वबुद्धिमुकुरे तव ॥ २.१.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तव (in your) स्वभाव-विमले (pure by its very nature) नित्यं (always) स्व-बुद्धि-मुकुरे (in the mirror of your own intellect) केवलं (only) मनाक् एव (just a little) मार्जना-मात्रम् (mere polishing) उपयुज्यते (is needed).
</p>

**Translation:** "The mirror of your intellect is always clear by nature; all it needs is a little polishing."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The mirror.** The Gītā uses the same picture: <span class="parallel">यथादर्शो मलेन च</span> — *as a mirror is covered by dust*, so knowledge is covered by desire (3.38). The face is already in the mirror; one only wipes away what hides it.
- **Polishing, not making.** Teaching does not create the Self; it removes what obscures it. This is the whole method of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha.

## Verse 2.1.4 { #2-1-4 }

<p class="shloka">
भगवद्व्यासपुत्रस्य शुकस्येव मतिस्तव ।<br>
विश्रान्तिमात्रमेवान्तर्ज्ञातज्ञेयाप्यपेक्षते ॥ २.१.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तव मतिः (your mind) भगवत्-व्यास-पुत्रस्य शुकस्य इव (like that of Śuka, son of the blessed Vyāsa) ज्ञात-ज्ञेया अपि (though it has known what is to be known) अन्तः (within) विश्रान्ति-मात्रम् एव अपेक्षते (needs only to come to rest).
</p>

**Translation:** "Like the mind of Śuka, son of the blessed Vyāsa, your mind has known all there is to know; it needs only to come to rest within."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Knowing and resting.** Two different things. One can understand the truth and still not rest in it — doubt and restlessness linger. The rest of this sarga is the story of how Śuka moved from the first to the second.
- **विश्रान्ति — repose.** A key word of the whole text (glossary). The goal is not more thought but the end of the need for it.

## Verse 2.1.5 { #2-1-5 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
भगवद्व्यासपुत्रस्य शुकस्य भगवन्कथम् ।<br>
ज्ञेयेऽप्यादौ न विश्रान्तं विश्रान्तं च धिया पुनः ॥ २.१.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भगवन् (O blessed one), भगवत्-व्यास-पुत्रस्य शुकस्य (for Śuka, son of the blessed Vyāsa) आदौ (at first) ज्ञेये अपि न विश्रान्तं (there was no rest even in what is to be known), पुनः च धिया विश्रान्तं (and later rest through understanding) — कथम् (how [was this])?
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Blessed one, how was it that Śuka, son of the blessed Vyāsa, at first found no rest even in the truth he knew, and later did find rest in his understanding?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The right question.** Śrī Rāma does not ask "what is the truth?" but "how does knowledge become rest?" — the question of one who already has the understanding but not yet the peace.
- **Arjuna too** heard the teaching and still asked again and again; at the end Kṛṣṇa asks him <span class="parallel">कच्चिदज्ञानसंमोहः प्रनष्टस्ते धनंजय</span> — *has your delusion born of ignorance been destroyed?* (Gītā 18.72). Knowing must be confirmed until it settles.

## Verse 2.1.6 { #2-1-6 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">विश्वामित्र उवाच ।</span><br>
आत्मोदन्तसमं राम कथ्यमानमिदं मया ।<br>
श्रृणु व्यासात्मजोदन्तं जन्मनामन्तकारणम् ॥ २.१.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राम (O Rāma), मया कथ्यमानम् (being told by me) आत्म-उदन्त-समम् (like your own story) जन्मनाम् अन्त-कारणम् (the cause of the end of births) इदं व्यास-आत्मज-उदन्तं (this story of Vyāsa's son) शृणु (hear).
</p>

**Translation:** Viśvāmitra said: "Rāma, listen to the story of Vyāsa's son, which I shall tell you. It is like your own story, and it brings an end to rebirth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Like your own story.** Śuka's path mirrors Rāma's: both reached dispassion by their own reflection. Scripture tells us stories so that we recognise ourselves in them.
- **जन्मनामन्तकारणम्.** Even hearing such a story is called liberating. The stories of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha are not decoration; each is a teaching in narrative form.

## Verse 2.1.7 { #2-1-7 }

<p class="shloka">
योऽयमञ्जनशैलाभो निविष्टो हेमविष्टरे ।<br>
पार्श्वे तव पितुर्व्यासो भगवान्भास्करद्युतिः ॥ २.१.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यः अयम् (he who is here) अञ्जन-शैल-आभः (resembling a mountain of collyrium, i.e. dark) तव पितुः पार्श्वे (beside your father) हेम-विष्टरे निविष्टः (seated on a golden seat) भगवान् भास्कर-द्युतिः व्यासः (the blessed Vyāsa, radiant as the sun) —
</p>

**Translation:** "You see the blessed Vyāsa sitting beside your father on a golden seat — dark as a mountain of collyrium, yet radiant as the sun."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dark and radiant.** Vyāsa is called Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, "the dark one born on an island". Dark in body, sun-like in wisdom — outer appearance is no measure of inner light.
- **Vyāsa is present.** The sage of the story is in the hall. This is how the Yoga Vasiṣṭha teaches: the past is brought into the room.

## Verse 2.1.8 { #2-1-8 }

<p class="shloka">
अस्याभूदिन्दुवदनस्तनयो नयकोविदः ।<br>
शुको नाम महाप्राज्ञो यज्ञो मूर्त्येव सुस्थितः ॥ २.१.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्य (to him) इन्दु-वदनः (moon-faced) नय-कोविदः (skilled in right conduct) महा-प्राज्ञः (greatly wise) मूर्त्या सुस्थितः यज्ञः इव (like sacrifice itself standing in bodily form) शुकः नाम तनयः अभूत् (a son named Śuka was born).
</p>

**Translation:** "To him was born a son named Śuka — moon-faced, skilled in right conduct, deeply wise, like the sacrifice itself standing in bodily form."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Śuka.** In the Mahābhārata (Śānti Parva) and the Bhāgavata, Śuka is born already detached and later recites the Bhāgavata to King Parīkṣit. Here he is the model of the mature seeker.
- **Sacrifice in bodily form.** A life wholly offered. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">यज्ञार्थात्कर्मणोऽन्यत्र लोकोऽयं कर्मबन्धनः</span> — *action not done as sacrifice binds the world* (3.9).

## Verse 2.1.9 { #2-1-9 }

<p class="shloka">
प्रविचारयतो लोकयात्रामलमिमां हृदि ।<br>
तवेव किल तस्यापि विवेक उदभूदयम् ॥ २.१.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इमां लोक-यात्राम् (this course of the world) हृदि अलं प्रविचारयतः (as he reflected deeply on it in his heart) तव इव (as with you) तस्य अपि (in him too) अयं विवेकः किल उदभूत् (this discernment arose).
</p>

**Translation:** "As he reflected deeply in his heart on the way the world goes, the same discernment arose in him as in you."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Discernment born of reflection.** Neither Śuka nor Rāma was taught dispassion; it rose from looking honestly at life. Compare Rāma's own reflection at 1.12.
- **The Buddha's way.** Prince Siddhārtha too saw old age, sickness and death and began to ask. Reflection on the course of the world (लोकयात्रा) is the common door of the seekers of every tradition.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints उद्भूदयम्; corrected here to उदभूदयम् (उदभूत् + अयम्).

## Verse 2.1.10 { #2-1-10 }

<p class="shloka">
तेनासौ स्वविवेकेन स्वयमेव महामनाः ।<br>
प्रविचार्य चिरं चारु यत्सत्यं तदवाप्तवान् ॥ २.१.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तेन स्व-विवेकेन (by that discernment of his own) असौ महा-मनाः (this great-minded one) स्वयम् एव (by himself) चिरं चारु प्रविचार्य (reflecting long and well) यत् सत्यं तत् अवाप्तवान् (attained what is true).
</p>

**Translation:** "Through his own discernment that great soul, reflecting long and carefully by himself, reached the truth."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Long and well.** चिरं चारु — deep inquiry is neither hurried nor careless. The Upaniṣad's word is <span class="parallel">मन्तव्यः</span> — *to be reflected upon* (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.4.5): hearing must be followed by reflection.
- **By himself.** Śuka had found the truth without a teacher. The rest of the story shows why, even so, a teacher was still needed.

## Verse 2.1.11 { #2-1-11 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वयं प्राप्ते परे वस्तुन्यविश्रान्तमनाः स्थितः ।<br>
इदं वस्त्विति विश्वासं नासावात्मन्युपाययौ ॥ २.१.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
परे वस्तुनि स्वयं प्राप्ते (though the supreme reality had been reached by himself) [सः] अविश्रान्त-मनाः स्थितः (he remained with a mind not at rest); इदं वस्तु इति ("this is the reality") विश्वासम् आत्मनि न उपययौ (he did not come to conviction within himself).
</p>

**Translation:** "Though he had reached the supreme reality on his own, his mind did not settle. He could not come to the inner conviction: 'This is it.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Truth without conviction.** One of the most honest verses in scripture. Self-found insight may still lack certainty (विश्वास). The mind asks: "Am I imagining this?"
- **Why a teacher.** The Chāndogya says <span class="parallel">आचार्यवान्पुरुषो वेद</span> — *one who has a teacher knows* (6.14.2). The teacher does not add new truth; he removes the last doubt.
- **Doubt as the last obstacle.** The Gītā: <span class="parallel">संशयात्मा विनश्यति</span> — *the doubting self is lost* (4.40).

## Verse 2.1.12 { #2-1-12 }

<p class="shloka">
केवलं विररामास्य चेतो विगतचापलम् ।<br>
भोगेभ्यो भूरिभङ्गेभ्यो धाराभ्य इव चातकः ॥ २.१.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
केवलम् (only) अस्य चेतः (his mind) विगत-चापलम् (its restlessness gone) भूरि-भङ्गेभ्यः भोगेभ्यः (from pleasures that are full of breakage) विरराम (turned away), चातकः धाराभ्यः इव (as the cātaka bird from [ordinary] streams of water).
</p>

**Translation:** "His mind had only grown still and turned away from pleasures, so full of ruin — as the cātaka bird turns from every stream and will drink only rain."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The cātaka.** In Indian poetry this bird drinks only raindrops falling from the cloud, refusing all other water. Śuka had lost his taste for worldly streams, but had not yet drunk the rain he longed for.
- **Dispassion is not yet fulfilment.** Turning away from pleasure is a negative state; it needs the positive rest of knowing. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">रसोऽप्यस्य परं दृष्ट्वा निवर्तते</span> — *even the taste goes, once the Supreme is seen* (2.59).

## Verse 2.1.13 { #2-1-13 }

<p class="shloka">
एकदा सोऽमलप्रज्ञो मेरावेकान्तसुस्थितम् ।<br>
पप्रच्छ पितरं भक्त्या कृष्णद्वैपायनं मुनिम् ॥ २.१.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एकदा (once) सः अमल-प्रज्ञः (he of pure wisdom) मेरौ एकान्त-सुस्थितं (sitting at ease in solitude on Meru) पितरं कृष्ण-द्वैपायनं मुनिं (his father, the sage Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana) भक्त्या पप्रच्छ (asked with devotion).
</p>

**Translation:** "Once, that pure-minded one went with devotion to his father, the sage Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, who sat at ease in solitude on Mount Meru, and asked him:"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **भक्त्या — with devotion.** Even a son who already knows approaches his teacher with reverence. The Gītā's method: <span class="parallel">तद्विद्धि प्रणिपातेन परिप्रश्नेन सेवया</span> — *know it by humble approach, by questioning and by service* (4.34).

## Verse 2.1.14 { #2-1-14 }

<p class="shloka">
संसाराडम्बरमिदं कथमभ्युत्थितं मुने ।<br>
कथं च प्रशमं याति कियत्कस्य कदेति वा ॥ २.१.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), इदं संसार-आडम्बरम् (this pageant of worldly existence) कथम् अभ्युत्थितम् (how did it arise)? कथं च प्रशमं याति (and how does it come to rest)? कियत् (how great is it), कस्य (whose is it), कदा इति वा (and when)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Sage, how did this whole pageant of worldly existence arise? How does it come to an end? How vast is it, whose is it, and when did it begin?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The great questions.** Origin, ending, extent, owner, time — the questions of every seeker. The Śvetāśvatara Upaniṣad opens the same way: <span class="parallel">किं कारणं ब्रह्म कुतः स्म जाताः</span> — *what is the cause? whence are we born?* (1.1).
- **कस्य — whose?** The most searching of them. To whom does saṃsāra belong? The answer the text will give: to the one who imagines it.

## Verse 2.1.15 { #2-1-15 }

<p class="shloka">
इति पृष्टेन मुनिना व्यासेनाखिलमात्मजे ।<br>
यथावदमलं प्रोक्तं वक्तव्यं विदितात्मना ॥ २.१.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इति पृष्टेन (thus asked) विदित-आत्मना मुनिना व्यासेन (by the sage Vyāsa, who knew the Self) आत्मजे (to his son) अखिलं वक्तव्यं (all that was to be said) यथावत् अमलं प्रोक्तम् (was told rightly and purely).
</p>

**Translation:** "Asked this, the sage Vyāsa, who knew the Self, told his son everything that needed telling, accurately and purely."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The teaching was correct.** Nothing was lacking in Vyāsa's answer. Whether teaching bears fruit depends also on how it is received — the next verse shows the obstacle.

## Verse 2.1.16 { #2-1-16 }

<p class="shloka">
अज्ञासिषं पूर्वमेतदहमित्यथ तत्पितुः ।<br>
स शुकः शुभया बुद्ध्या न वाक्यं बह्वमन्यत ॥ २.१.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
"अहं पूर्वम् एतत् अज्ञासिषम्" इति ("I knew this before") — अथ सः शुकः (then that Śuka) शुभया बुद्ध्या (with his good intellect) तत्-पितुः वाक्यं (his father's words) बहु न अमन्यत (did not value highly).
</p>

**Translation:** "'I knew this already,' Śuka thought; and so, for all his good understanding, he did not give much weight to his father's words."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **"I knew this already."** The subtlest obstacle: the teaching sounds familiar, so it is set aside. Familiarity is not realisation. The Kena Upaniṣad warns: <span class="parallel">यदि मन्यसे सुवेदेति दभ्रमेवापि नूनं त्वं वेत्थ</span> — *if you think "I know it well", you know very little* (2.1).
- **The closeness of father and son.** It is hard to receive as a teacher one who is also one's own father. Familiarity of person, as well as of words, can block the teaching.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints आऽज्ञासिषं; corrected here to अज्ञासिषं ("I knew").

## Verse 2.1.17 { #2-1-17 }

<p class="shloka">
व्यासोऽपि भगवान्बुद्ध्वा पुत्राभिप्रायमीदृशम् ।<br>
प्रत्युवाच पुनः पुत्रं नाहं जानामि तत्त्वतः ॥ २.१.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भगवान् व्यासः अपि (the blessed Vyāsa too) पुत्र-अभिप्रायम् ईदृशं बुद्ध्वा (understanding his son's attitude to be thus) पुनः पुत्रं प्रत्युवाच (replied again to his son): "अहं तत्त्वतः न जानामि" ("I do not truly know").
</p>

**Translation:** "The blessed Vyāsa, seeing what was in his son's mind, answered him again: 'I do not know this truly.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The teacher's humility.** Vyāsa, who knew, says "I do not know" — to remove the barrier of familiarity and send Śuka to a teacher he will accept. Skilful means: the teacher serves the student's need, not his own standing.
- **Not-knowing as wisdom.** The Kena: <span class="parallel">यस्यामतं तस्य मतं मतं यस्य न वेद सः</span> — *it is known by one who does not claim to know it; one who claims to know it does not know* (2.3).

## Verse 2.1.18 { #2-1-18 }

<p class="shloka">
जनको नाम भूपालो विद्यते वसुधातले ।<br>
यथावद्वेत्त्यसौ वेद्यं तस्मात्सर्वमवाप्स्यसि ॥ २.१.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वसुधा-तले (on the earth) जनकः नाम भूपालः विद्यते (there is a king named Janaka); असौ वेद्यं यथावत् वेत्ति (he knows what is to be known as it is); तस्मात् सर्वम् अवाप्स्यसि (from him you will obtain everything).
</p>

**Translation:** "'On earth there is a king called Janaka. He knows the truth as it really is. From him you will receive everything.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Janaka.** The royal sage of the Upaniṣads (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 4) and of the Gītā: <span class="parallel">कर्मणैव हि संसिद्धिमास्थिता जनकादयः</span> — *by action itself Janaka and others reached perfection* (3.20). A king who lives in a palace teaches the ascetic's son: freedom does not depend on outer renunciation.
- **The lesson for Rāma.** Rāma too is a prince who must rule. Janaka is the model of what Rāma will become: a knower who governs.

## Verse 2.1.19 { #2-1-19 }

<p class="shloka">
पित्रेत्युक्ते शुकः प्रायात्सुमेरोर्वसुधातले ।<br>
विदेहनगरीं प्राप जनकेनाभिपालिताम् ॥ २.१.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पित्रा इति उक्ते (when his father had said this) शुकः सुमेरोः वसुधा-तले प्रायात् (Śuka went from Sumeru down to the earth) [च] जनकेन अभिपालितां विदेह-नगरीं प्राप (and reached the city of Videha, ruled by Janaka).
</p>

**Translation:** "At his father's word, Śuka went down from Mount Sumeru to the earth and came to Videha, the city ruled by Janaka."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Coming down from the mountain.** From the solitary heights of Meru to a busy royal city. The seeker must sometimes leave the place of his own meditation to find the truth in the world.
- **Obedience.** Śuka, though he "already knew", obeys at once. Willingness to be taught is itself the beginning of rest.

## Verse 2.1.20 { #2-1-20 }

<p class="shloka">
आवेदितोऽसौ याष्टीकैर्जनकाय महात्मने ।<br>
द्वारि व्याससुतो राजञ्शुकोऽत्र स्थितवानिति ॥ २.१.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
असौ (he) याष्टीकैः (by the staff-bearing door-keepers) महात्मने जनकाय आवेदितः (was announced to the great-souled Janaka): "राजन्, व्यास-सुतः शुकः अत्र द्वारि स्थितवान्" इति ("O king, Śuka, son of Vyāsa, stands here at the gate").
</p>

**Translation:** "The door-keepers with their staffs announced him to the great-souled Janaka: 'King, Śuka, the son of Vyāsa, is standing at the gate.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **At the gate.** The seeker arrives at the threshold. Every true teaching has a gate, and a waiting there.
- **A famous name.** He is announced as Vyāsa's son — a great lineage. The test that follows will show whether he clings to that identity.

## Verse 2.1.21 { #2-1-21 }

<p class="shloka">
जिज्ञासार्थं शुकस्यासावास्तामेवेत्यवज्ञया ।<br>
उक्त्वा बभूव जनकस्तूष्णीं सप्त दिनान्यथ ॥ २.१.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ जनकः (then Janaka) शुकस्य जिज्ञासा-अर्थम् (to test Śuka) "असौ आस्ताम् एव" इति ("let him just stay there") अवज्ञया उक्त्वा (saying with apparent disregard) सप्त दिनानि तूष्णीं बभूव (remained silent for seven days).
</p>

**Translation:** "Janaka, to test Śuka, said with seeming indifference, 'Let him wait there,' and said nothing more for seven days."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The test of waiting.** For the son of the great Vyāsa to be left at the gate for a week — a test of pride. Janaka is not cruel; he is examining whether any sense of importance remains.
- **जिज्ञासार्थम्.** The word means "to examine", but it is the same word as the Brahma Sūtra's <span class="parallel">अथातो ब्रह्मजिज्ञासा</span> — *now, therefore, the inquiry into Brahman* (1.1.1). The teacher's test and the student's inquiry are one process.

## Verse 2.1.22 { #2-1-22 }

<p class="shloka">
ततः प्रवेशयामास जनकः शुकमङ्गणम् ।<br>
तत्राहानि स सप्तैव तथैवावसदुन्मनाः ॥ २.१.२२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः (then) जनकः शुकम् अङ्गणं प्रवेशयामास (Janaka had Śuka brought into the courtyard); तत्र सः (there he) सप्त एव अहानि (for seven more days) तथा एव उन्मनाः अवसत् (stayed just as before, his mind turned inward).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then Janaka had Śuka brought into the courtyard, and there he stayed another seven days, his mind as inwardly absorbed as before."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **उन्मनाः.** Not "distraught" here but "with mind lifted up, absorbed" — Śuka is unmoved by the neglect. The second test is passed as quietly as the first.
- **Patience.** Śāntideva: <span class="parallel">न च क्षान्तिसमं तपः</span> — *there is no austerity equal to patience* (Bodhicaryāvatāra 6.2).

## Verse 2.1.23 { #2-1-23 }

<p class="shloka">
अथ प्रवेशयामास जनकोऽन्तःपुरं शुकम् ।<br>
राजा न दृश्यते तावदिति सप्त दिनानि च ॥ २.१.२३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ जनकः शुकम् अन्तःपुरं प्रवेशयामास (then Janaka had Śuka taken into the inner palace); "राजा न दृश्यते" इति ("the king is not to be seen") तावत् सप्त दिनानि च (so it was for another seven days).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then Janaka had him taken into the inner palace; and for seven more days the king did not show himself."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Closer, but not yet.** Gate, courtyard, inner palace — Śuka is drawn nearer stage by stage, while the teacher stays hidden. Spiritual progress often feels like this: closer, yet without the meeting one expects.

## Verse 2.1.24 { #2-1-24 }

<p class="shloka">
तत्रोन्मदाभिः कान्ताभिर्भोजनैर्भोगसंचयैः ।<br>
जनको लालयामास शुकं शशिसमाननम् ॥ २.१.२४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र (there) जनकः (Janaka) शशि-सम-आननं शुकम् (the moon-faced Śuka) उन्मदाभिः कान्ताभिः (with intoxicating beauties) भोजनैः (with fine food) भोग-संचयैः (with heaps of pleasures) लालयामास (indulged).
</p>

**Translation:** "There Janaka lavished on the moon-faced Śuka every enjoyment — lovely, intoxicating women, rich food, and pleasures of every kind."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The test of pleasure.** After neglect, luxury. Many who bear hardship are undone by comfort. The pair of tests examines the mind on both sides, like the Gītā's <span class="parallel">समदुःखसुखं धीरम्</span> — *the steady one, equal in pain and pleasure* (2.15).
- **Jaina teaching** speaks of the *pariṣaha*, hardships the monk must bear without disturbance — but also of favourable conditions, which are the harder test.

## Verse 2.1.25 { #2-1-25 }

<p class="shloka">
ते भोगास्तानि दुःखानि व्यासपुत्रस्य तन्मनः ।<br>
नाजह्नुर्मन्दपवना बद्धपीठमिवाचलम् ॥ २.१.२५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ते भोगाः (those pleasures) तानि दुःखानि (those hardships) व्यास-पुत्रस्य तत् मनः (that mind of Vyāsa's son) न आजह्रुः (did not carry away), मन्द-पवनाः बद्ध-पीठम् अचलम् इव (as gentle breezes [do not move] a firmly-based mountain).
</p>

**Translation:** "Neither those pleasures nor those hardships could move the mind of Vyāsa's son, any more than light breezes can move a mountain set on its base."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Both together.** "Those pleasures, those hardships" — named in one breath, because for Śuka they were the same. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">यस्मिन्स्थितो न दुःखेन गुरुणापि विचाल्यते</span> — *established there, one is not shaken even by heavy sorrow* (6.22).
- **The mountain.** Not rigid but rooted. The Buddha in the Dhammapada: *selo yathā ekaghano vātena na samīrati* — *as a solid rock is not shaken by the wind, so the wise are unmoved by praise and blame* (81).

## Verse 2.1.26 { #2-1-26 }

<p class="shloka">
केवलं सुसमः स्वस्थो मौनी मुदितमानसः ।<br>
अतिष्ठत्स शुकस्तत्र संपूर्ण इव चन्द्रमाः ॥ २.१.२६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सः शुकः (that Śuka) तत्र (there) केवलं सु-समः (perfectly even) स्वस्थः (abiding in himself) मौनी (silent) मुदित-मानसः (joyful in mind) संपूर्णः चन्द्रमाः इव अतिष्ठत् (remained like the full moon).
</p>

**Translation:** "There Śuka remained — perfectly even, rooted in himself, silent and glad at heart, like the full moon."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Five marks.** Even (सुसम), self-abiding (स्वस्थ), silent (मौनी), glad (मुदित), full (संपूर्ण). These are the marks of a mind at rest; note that joy is among them. Detachment is not grim.
- **स्वस्थ.** Literally "standing in oneself" — also the ordinary Sanskrit word for "healthy". True health is resting in the Self.

## Verse 2.1.27 { #2-1-27 }

<p class="shloka">
परिज्ञातस्वभावं तं शुकं स जनको नृपः ।<br>
आनीतं मुदितात्मानमवलोक्य ननाम ह ॥ २.१.२७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सः नृपः जनकः (King Janaka) परिज्ञात-स्वभावं (whose nature was now fully known) आनीतं (brought before him) मुदित-आत्मानं तं शुकम् (that Śuka, joyful in spirit) अवलोक्य (seeing) ननाम ह (bowed down).
</p>

**Translation:** "When Śuka, whose nature had now been fully proved, was brought before him joyful in spirit, King Janaka saw him and bowed."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The teacher bows.** Janaka bows to his student. Having tested him, he recognises a knower. In the Upaniṣads too the teacher honours the student who is ready.
- **परिज्ञातस्वभाव.** The tests were not to make Śuka something, but to reveal what he already was — as the mirror of 2.1.3 needed only polishing.

## Verse 2.1.28 { #2-1-28 }

<p class="shloka">
निःशेषितजगत्कार्यं प्राप्ताखिलमनोरथ ।<br>
किमीप्सितं तवेत्याशु कृतस्वागतमाह तम् ॥ २.१.२८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[जनकः] कृत-स्वागतं (having welcomed him) निःशेषित-जगत्-कार्यं (him who had finished with all worldly business) प्राप्त-अखिल-मनोरथं (who had attained every aim) तम् आशु आह (quickly said to him): "तव किम् ईप्सितम्" इति ("what do you seek?").
</p>

**Translation:** "After welcoming him — one who had finished with all worldly business and whose every aim was fulfilled — Janaka asked him at once: 'What is it you seek?'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **"What do you seek?"** A real question to one who seemingly wants nothing. The teacher asks the student to name his need, so that the answer can meet it.
- **आप्तकाम.** The Bṛhadāraṇyaka describes the free one as <span class="parallel">अकामो निष्काम आप्तकाम आत्मकामः</span> — *desireless, free of desire, all desires fulfilled, desiring only the Self* (4.4.6). What can such a one seek? Only confirmation.

## Verse 2.1.29 { #2-1-29 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीशुक उवाच ।</span><br>
संसाराडम्बरमिदं कथमभ्युत्थितं गुरो ।<br>
कथं प्रशममायाति यथावत्कथयाशु मे ॥ २.१.२९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
गुरो (O teacher), इदं संसार-आडम्बरं कथम् अभ्युत्थितम् (how did this pageant of worldly existence arise)? कथं प्रशमम् आयाति (how does it come to rest)? यथावत् आशु मे कथय (tell me truly and quickly).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Śuka said: "Teacher, how did this pageant of worldly existence arise, and how does it come to rest? Tell me truly, and without delay."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The same question.** Word for word what he asked Vyāsa (2.1.14). He does not ask anything new; he asks to hear the same truth from a teacher he will accept.
- **गुरो.** He now addresses Janaka as his guru. That single word marks the end of "I knew this already."

## Verse 2.1.30 { #2-1-30 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">विश्वामित्र उवाच ।</span><br>
जनकेनेति पृष्टेन शुकस्य कथितं तदा ।<br>
तदेव यत्पुरा प्रोक्तं तस्य पित्रा महात्मना ॥ २.१.३० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तदा (then) इति पृष्टेन जनकेन (by Janaka, thus asked) शुकस्य (to Śuka) तत् एव कथितम् (that very same [teaching] was told) यत् पुरा तस्य महात्मना पित्रा प्रोक्तम् (which had been taught before by his great-souled father).
</p>

**Translation:** Viśvāmitra said: "So asked, Janaka told Śuka exactly what his great-souled father had taught him before."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Nothing new.** Janaka adds nothing to Vyāsa's teaching. What changed was not the teaching but the hearer: his readiness to receive it.
- **One truth, many teachers.** The Ṛg Veda: <span class="parallel">एकं सद्विप्रा बहुधा वदन्ति</span> — *truth is one; the wise speak of it in many ways* (1.164.46). Here it is spoken the same way twice, and only the second time lands.

## Verse 2.1.31 { #2-1-31 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीशुक उवाच ।</span><br>
स्वयमेव मया पूर्वमेतज्ज्ञातं विवेकतः ।<br>
एतदेव च पृष्टेन पित्रा मे समुदाहृतम् ॥ २.१.३१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मया पूर्वं स्वयम् एव (by myself, before) विवेकतः एतत् ज्ञातम् (this was known through discernment); पृष्टेन मे पित्रा च (and by my father, when asked) एतत् एव समुदाहृतम् (this very thing was explained).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Śuka said: "This I had already understood by myself, through discernment; and when I asked my father, he told me just the same."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Three witnesses.** Own insight, father's teaching, and now Janaka's. The tradition calls this agreement of experience, scripture and teacher एकवाक्यता — Vasiṣṭha will name it at 2.13.11.

## Verse 2.1.32 { #2-1-32 }

<p class="shloka">
भवताप्येष एवार्थः कथितो वाग्विदां वर ।<br>
एष एव च वाक्यार्थः शास्त्रेषु परिदृश्यते ॥ २.१.३२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वाग्विदां वर (O best of speakers), भवता अपि (by you too) एषः एव अर्थः कथितः (this very meaning has been told); शास्त्रेषु च (and in the scriptures) एषः एव वाक्य-अर्थः परिदृश्यते (this very sense is seen).
</p>

**Translation:** "You too, best of speakers, have told me the same meaning; and the same sense is found in the scriptures."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Scripture as the fourth witness.** Śāstra confirms what insight, father and teacher say. When all agree, doubt has nowhere to stand.

## Verse 2.1.33 { #2-1-33 }

<p class="shloka">
यथायं स्वविकल्पोत्थः स्वविकल्पपरिक्षयात् ।<br>
क्षीयते दग्धसंसारो निःसार इति निश्चयः ॥ २.१.३३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (that) अयं (this [saṃsāra]) स्व-विकल्प-उत्थः (arising from one's own mental construction) स्व-विकल्प-परिक्षयात् क्षीयते (wanes when one's own mental construction wanes); दग्ध-संसारः निःसारः (saṃsāra, once burnt, is without substance) इति निश्चयः (this is the certainty).
</p>

**Translation:** "That this saṃsāra rises from one's own mental constructions and fades as they fade; that once it is burnt away it is seen to have no substance — that is the certain conclusion."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The whole teaching in one verse.** Saṃsāra comes from विकल्प (mental construction) and ends with it. This is the thesis the Yoga Vasiṣṭha will spend five more books proving.
- **Mind as the source.** The Maitrī Upaniṣad: <span class="parallel">मन एव मनुष्याणां कारणं बन्धमोक्षयोः</span> — *the mind alone is the cause of bondage and liberation* (6.34). The Buddha's Dhammapada opens: *manopubbaṅgamā dhammā* — *mind precedes all things* (1).

## Verse 2.1.34 { #2-1-34 }

<p class="shloka">
तत्किमेतन्महाबाहो सत्यं ब्रूहि ममाचलम् ।<br>
त्वत्तो विश्रान्तिमाप्नोमि चेतसा भ्रमता जगत् ॥ २.१.३४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
महाबाहो (O mighty-armed one), तत् एतत् किम् (what then is this)? मम अचलं सत्यं ब्रूहि (tell me the unshakeable truth); जगत् भ्रमता चेतसा (with my mind that has wandered the world) त्वत्तः विश्रान्तिम् आप्नोमि (I [seek to] find rest from you).
</p>

**Translation:** "Mighty-armed one, what then is this? Tell me the truth that cannot be shaken. My mind has wandered the whole world; from you I would find rest."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Seeking the seal.** Śuka asks not for information but for confirmation — the "unshakeable truth" that will end his wandering. This is exactly the rest Viśvāmitra said Rāma needs (2.1.4).
- **भ्रमता चेतसा.** A mind that has "wandered the world" — both outwardly from Meru to Videha and inwardly through every idea. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">चञ्चलं हि मनः कृष्ण</span> — *the mind is restless, O Kṛṣṇa* (6.34).

## Verse 2.1.35 { #2-1-35 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">जनक उवाच ।</span><br>
नातः परतरः कश्चिन्निश्चयोऽस्त्यपरो मुने ।<br>
स्वयमेव त्वया ज्ञातं गुरुतश्च पुनः श्रुतम् ॥ २.१.३५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मुने (O sage), अतः परतरः अपरः कश्चित् निश्चयः न अस्ति (there is no other conclusion higher than this); त्वया स्वयम् एव ज्ञातम् (you have known it yourself) गुरुतः च पुनः श्रुतम् (and heard it again from your teacher).
</p>

**Translation:** Janaka said: "Sage, there is no conclusion higher than this. You have known it yourself, and heard it again from your teacher."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The seal.** Janaka's role is to say: *this is it, there is nothing beyond.* Not more teaching, but the confirmation that ends the search.
- **Own knowledge plus teacher.** Both are needed. Insight without confirmation wavers; teaching without insight stays words.

## Verse 2.1.36 { #2-1-36 }

<p class="shloka">
अविच्छिन्नचिदात्मैकः पुमानस्तीह नेतरत् ।<br>
स्वसंकल्पवशाद्बद्धो निःसंकल्पश्च मुच्यते ॥ २.१.३६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह (here) अविच्छिन्न-चित्-आत्मा एकः पुमान् अस्ति (there is only the one Person, the Self of unbroken consciousness), न इतरत् (nothing else); [सः] स्व-संकल्प-वशात् बद्धः (bound by the force of his own imagining) निःसंकल्पः च मुच्यते (and freed when free of it).
</p>

**Translation:** "There is only the one Person, the Self of unbroken consciousness; nothing else exists. It is bound through the power of its own imagining, and freed when imagining ceases."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **One Self, self-bound, self-freed.** No outside jailer, no outside saviour. Bondage is self-made by संकल्प (intention, imagining), and freedom is its absence.
- **The Gītā:** <span class="parallel">उद्धरेदात्मनात्मानं नात्मानमवसादयेत् । आत्मैव ह्यात्मनो बन्धुरात्मैव रिपुरात्मनः</span> — *lift yourself by yourself; do not let yourself sink. The self alone is one's friend, and the self alone one's enemy* (6.5).
- **Mahāvīra** said the same: *purisā, tumam eva tumaṃ mittaṃ, kiṃ bahiyā mittam icchasi* — *O man, you are your own friend; why seek a friend outside?* (Ācārāṅga Sūtra 1.3.3).

## Verse 2.1.37 { #2-1-37 }

<p class="shloka">
तेन त्वया स्फुटं ज्ञातं ज्ञेयं यस्य महात्मनः ।<br>
भोगेभ्यो विरतिर्जाता दृश्यात्प्राक्सकलादिह ॥ २.१.३७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तेन (therefore) यस्य महात्मनः (of the great Self) ज्ञेयं (what is to be known) त्वया स्फुटं ज्ञातम् (has been clearly known by you); इह (here) सकलात् दृश्यात् प्राक् (even before [taking up] the whole visible world) भोगेभ्यः विरतिः जाता (dispassion from pleasures has arisen [in you]).
</p>

**Translation:** "So you have clearly understood what is to be known about the great Self; and here, dispassion for pleasures has arisen in you before the visible world could take hold."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Proof by dispassion.** Janaka judges Śuka's knowledge by its fruit: he does not want pleasures. Knowing and not wanting are two sides of one state. Vasiṣṭha will make this the test of knowledge (2.2.3).

## Verse 2.1.38 { #2-1-38 }

<p class="shloka">
तव बाल महावीर मतिर्विरतिमागता ।<br>
भोगेभ्यो दीर्घरोगेभ्यः किमन्यच्छ्रोतुमिच्छसि ॥ २.१.३८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
बाल (O child) महावीर (O great hero), तव मतिः (your mind) दीर्घ-रोगेभ्यः भोगेभ्यः (from pleasures, which are long diseases) विरतिम् आगता (has reached dispassion); अन्यत् किं श्रोतुम् इच्छसि (what else do you wish to hear)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Child, great hero, your mind has turned away from pleasures, those lingering diseases. What more do you want to hear?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Child and hero.** Śuka is young in years, a hero in victory over the senses. The real battle is inward. The Dhammapada: *yo sahassaṃ sahassena saṅgāme mānuse jine, ekañca jeyyamattānaṃ sa ve saṅgāmajuttamo* — *greater than one who conquers a thousand thousand in battle is one who conquers himself* (103).
- **Pleasures as long diseases.** A pleasure seems brief, but the craving it leaves is chronic. Compare Rāma's own words on craving (1.17).

## Verse 2.1.39 { #2-1-39 }

<p class="shloka">
न तथा पूर्णता जाता सर्वज्ञानमहानिधेः ।<br>
तिष्ठतस्तपसि स्फारे पितुस्तव यथा तव ॥ २.१.३९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्व-ज्ञान-महानिधेः (of the great treasure-house of all knowledge) स्फारे तपसि तिष्ठतः (who is established in vast austerity) तव पितुः (in your father) तथा पूर्णता न जाता (fullness has not arisen as much) यथा तव (as in you).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even your father — a vast treasury of knowledge, devoted to great austerity — has not reached the fullness that you have."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The student surpasses.** A strong statement, and it prepares Rāma's question in 2.3.3. Fullness (पूर्णता) is not measured by learning or austerity but by the absence of wanting.
- **Knowledge and fullness differ.** Vyāsa knows all scriptures; Śuka wants nothing. The Chāndogya's Nārada: <span class="parallel">सोऽहं भगवो मन्त्रविदेवास्मि नात्मवित्</span> — *sir, I know the texts, but not the Self* (7.1.3).

## Verse 2.1.40 { #2-1-40 }

<p class="shloka">
व्यासादधिक एवाहं व्यासशिष्योऽसि तत्सुतः ।<br>
भोगेच्छातानवेनेह मत्तोऽप्यत्यधिको भवान् ॥ २.१.४० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अहं व्यासात् अधिकः एव (I am indeed greater than Vyāsa); त्वं व्यास-शिष्यः तत्-सुतः असि (you are Vyāsa's disciple and his son); इह भोग-इच्छा-तानवेन (here, by the thinness of your desire for pleasure) भवान् मत्तः अपि अत्यधिकः (you are even greater than I).
</p>

**Translation:** "I may stand higher than Vyāsa, and you are his disciple and his son; yet in the slightness of your desire for pleasure, you stand higher even than me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Greatness measured by desire.** Janaka ranks himself, Vyāsa and Śuka not by learning or office but by how little they want. Freedom is measured by the thinning of desire — exactly how Vasiṣṭha will define liberation (2.2.5).
- **Śāntideva** prays to be content with little: the less one needs, the less one is bound.

## Verse 2.1.41 { #2-1-41 }

<p class="shloka">
प्राप्तं प्राप्तव्यमखिलं भवता पूर्णचेतसा ।<br>
न दृश्ये पतसि ब्रह्मन्मुक्तस्त्वं भ्रान्तिमुत्सृज ॥ २.१.४१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O brahmin), पूर्ण-चेतसा भवता (by you, of full mind) प्राप्तव्यम् अखिलं प्राप्तम् (all that is to be attained has been attained); [त्वं] दृश्ये न पतसि (you do not fall into the visible); त्वं मुक्तः (you are free); भ्रान्तिम् उत्सृज (give up the delusion).
</p>

**Translation:** "Brahmin, your mind is full; you have attained all that is to be attained. You no longer fall into the visible world. You are free — let go of your doubt."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **"You are free."** The decisive word. Śuka's only remaining delusion was the thought that he was not yet free. The teacher's last act is to remove the idea that something more is needed.
- **भ्रान्तिमुत्सृज.** As Kṛṣṇa says at the end: <span class="parallel">मा शुचः</span> — *do not grieve* (Gītā 18.66).

## Verse 2.1.42 { #2-1-42 }

<p class="shloka">
अनुशिष्टः स इत्येवं जनकेन महात्मना ।<br>
अतिष्ठत्स शुकस्तूष्णीं स्वच्छे परमवस्तुनि ॥ २.१.४२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
महात्मना जनकेन (by the great-souled Janaka) इति एवम् अनुशिष्टः सः शुकः (Śuka, so instructed) तूष्णीं (silently) स्वच्छे परम-वस्तुनि अतिष्ठत् (rested in the clear supreme reality).
</p>

**Translation:** "So instructed by the great-souled Janaka, Śuka rested in silence in the clear, supreme reality."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **From knowing to resting.** The question of 2.1.5 is answered: the knowledge Śuka already had became rest once the last doubt was removed.
- **तूष्णीम् — silence.** No reply, no thanks in words. The end of inquiry is silence, not because nothing is understood, but because nothing remains to ask.

## Verse 2.1.43 { #2-1-43 }

<p class="shloka">
वीतशोकभयायासो निरीहश्छिन्नसंशयः ।<br>
जगाम शिखरं मेरोः समाध्यर्थमनिन्दितम् ॥ २.१.४३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सः] वीत-शोक-भय-आयासः (free of grief, fear and strain) निरीहः (without striving) छिन्न-संशयः (his doubts cut) अनिन्दितं मेरोः शिखरं (to the faultless peak of Meru) समाधि-अर्थं जगाम (went for the sake of samādhi).
</p>

**Translation:** "Free from grief, fear and strain, wanting nothing, his doubts cut away, he went to the pure summit of Meru to dwell in samādhi."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **छिन्नसंशय.** The Muṇḍaka: <span class="parallel">भिद्यते हृदयग्रन्थिश्छिद्यन्ते सर्वसंशयाः</span> — *the knot of the heart is broken, all doubts are cut* (2.2.8). The very word the Upaniṣad uses.

## Verse 2.1.44 { #2-1-44 }

<p class="shloka">
तत्र वर्षसहस्राणि निर्विकल्पसमाधिना ।<br>
दश स्थित्वा शशामासावात्मन्यस्नेहदीपवत् ॥ २.१.४४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
असौ (he) तत्र (there) निर्विकल्प-समाधिना (in samādhi free of mental constructions) दश वर्ष-सहस्राणि स्थित्वा (having remained ten thousand years) आत्मनि शशाम (became quiet in the Self), अ-स्नेह-दीपवत् (like a lamp without oil).
</p>

**Translation:** "There he stayed for ten thousand years in samādhi free of all mental forms, and came to rest in the Self like a lamp whose oil is spent."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **निर्विकल्प समाधि.** Absorption without any mental construction (विकल्प) — the opposite of the स्वविकल्प that creates saṃsāra (2.1.33).
- **A lamp without oil.** Not violently put out; it goes quiet when the fuel ends. This is the literal sense of *nirvāṇa*. The Buddha's disciples said of the liberated: *nibbanti dhīrā yathāyaṃ padīpo* — *the wise go out like this lamp* (Ratana Sutta).

## Verse 2.1.45 { #2-1-45 }

<p class="shloka">
व्यपगतकलनाकलङ्कशुद्धः स्वयममलात्मनि पावने पदेऽसौ ।<br>
सलिलकण इवाम्बुधौ महात्मा विगलितवासनमेकतां जगाम ॥ २.१.४५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
असौ महात्मा (that great soul) व्यपगत-कलना-कलङ्क-शुद्धः (pure, the stain of mental construction gone) विगलित-वासनम् (his latent tendencies dissolved) स्वयम् (by himself) अमल-आत्मनि पावने पदे (in the pure, sacred state of the stainless Self) एकतां जगाम (became one), अम्बुधौ सलिल-कणः इव (as a drop of water in the ocean).
</p>

**Translation:** "Purified, the stain of mental activity gone, every latent tendency dissolved, that great soul became one with the stainless, sacred state of the Self — as a drop of water becomes one with the ocean."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The drop and the ocean.** The Muṇḍaka: <span class="parallel">यथा नद्यः स्यन्दमानाः समुद्रेऽस्तं गच्छन्ति नामरूपे विहाय</span> — *as rivers flowing into the ocean disappear, leaving name and form* (3.2.8).
- **Rāma's road ahead.** Viśvāmitra has shown Rāma his own path: insight, a teacher's confirmation, and rest. Vasiṣṭha will be Rāma's Janaka.
