# Sarga 8

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · Fate refuted by reason (26 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 8 at a glance"
    A logical refutation of fate as an independent power.

    1. Fate has no form, action or movement; it is a figure of speech hardened into belief, like a snake seen in a rope (2.8.1–2.8.4).
    2. No fatalist walks into fire; teaching would be pointless; only corpses are motionless; the formless cannot push the formed (2.8.5–2.8.15).
    (Verse 6 is missing in the source.)
    3. The brave and the wise wait for no fate; predictions do not override action (2.8.16–2.8.19).
    4. Examples: Viśvāmitra became a brahmin, sages gained powers, gods and demons won empires — all by effort (2.8.20–2.8.26).

## Verse 2.8.1 { #2-8-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
नाकृतिर्न च कर्माणि न स्पन्दो न पराक्रमः ।<br>
तन्मिथ्याज्ञानवद्रूढं दैवं नाम किमुच्यते ॥ २.८.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[यस्य] न आकृतिः (which has no form) न च कर्माणि (nor actions) न स्पन्दः (nor movement) न पराक्रमः (nor strength) — मिथ्या-ज्ञानवत् रूढं (established like a false notion) तत् दैवं नाम किम् उच्यते (what is this thing called fate)?
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "It has no form, no actions, no movement, no strength; it has taken root like any false notion. What, then, is this thing called 'fate'?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **An argument begins.** Sarga 8 is a logical refutation. First: what kind of thing could fate be? It has none of the marks of anything that acts.
- **The method.** Vasiṣṭha argues; he does not simply assert. The Kena: knowledge must be examined. He will defend reasoning itself at 2.18.2–3.

## Verse 2.8.2 { #2-8-2 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वकर्मफलसंप्राप्ताविदमित्थमितीति याः ।<br>
गिरस्ता दैवनाम्नैताः प्रसिद्धिं समुपागताः ॥ २.८.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
स्व-कर्म-फल-संप्राप्तौ (when the fruit of one's own action arrives) "इदम् इत्थम्" इति इति याः गिरः (the words "this is how it is") — एताः ताः दैव-नाम्ना प्रसिद्धिं समुपागताः (these have become known by the name "fate").
</p>

**Translation:** "When the fruit of one's own actions arrives, one says 'so this is how it is'; it is such words that have come to be known as 'fate'."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Fate as a manner of speaking.** "That's how it is" — a phrase, nothing more. Language has hardened into a belief.

## Verse 2.8.3 { #2-8-3 }

<p class="shloka">
तत्रैव मूढमतिभिर्दैवमस्तीति निश्चयः ।<br>
आत्तो दुरवबोधेन रज्ज्वामिव भुजंगमः ॥ २.८.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र एव (just there) मूढ-मतिभिः (by the dull-minded) "दैवम् अस्ति" इति निश्चयः आत्तः (the conviction "fate exists" has been taken up) दुरवबोधेन (through poor understanding), रज्ज्वाम् इव भुजंगमः (like a snake [seen] in a rope).
</p>

**Translation:** "From this, the dull-minded have formed the conviction that 'fate exists', through poor understanding, just as one sees a snake in a rope."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The rope and the snake.** The most famous illustration in Vedānta. In poor light a rope is taken for a snake; the fear is real, the snake is not. Fate, says Vasiṣṭha, is such a snake: a misreading of one's own karma.

## Verse 2.8.4 { #2-8-4 }

<p class="shloka">
ह्यस्तनी दुष्क्रियाभ्येति शोभां सत्क्रियया यथा ।<br>
अद्यैवं प्राक्तनी तस्माद्यत्नात्सत्कार्यवान्भवेत् ॥ २.८.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (as) ह्यस्तनी दुष्क्रिया (yesterday's wrong deed) सत्-क्रियया शोभाम् अभ्येति (becomes fair by a good deed), अद्य एवं प्राक्तनी (so today the past [does]); तस्मात् यत्नात् सत्-कार्यवान् भवेत् (therefore one should with effort do good).
</p>

**Translation:** "As yesterday's wrong is made fair by today's good deed, so with the whole past; so make an effort, and do good."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The refrain once more** (2.5.12, 2.6.5, 2.6.37). Every argument returns to this hope: the past is redeemable.

## Verse 2.8.5 { #2-8-5 }

<p class="shloka">
मूढानुमानसंसिद्धं दैवं यस्यास्ति दुर्मतेः ।<br>
दैवाद्दाहोऽस्ति नैवेति गन्तव्यं तेन पावके ॥ २.८.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यस्य दुर्मतेः (for the misguided one for whom) मूढ-अनुमान-संसिद्धं दैवम् अस्ति (fate, established by foolish inference, exists), तेन (he) "दैवात् दाहः न एव अस्ति" इति ("I will not burn unless it is fated") पावके गन्तव्यम् (should walk into the fire).
</p>

**Translation:** "Let the misguided one who believes in a fate established by foolish inference walk into a fire, telling himself, 'Unless it is fated, I shall not burn.'"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A reductio.** No fatalist actually lives by fatalism. He steps back from fire, eats when hungry and locks his door. His actions refute his belief.

## Verse 2.8.7 { #2-8-7 }

<p class="shloka">
किंवा शास्त्रोपदेशेन मूकोऽयं पुरुषः किल ।<br>
संचार्यते तु दैवेन किं कस्येहोपदिश्यते ॥ २.८.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
किं वा (or why) शास्त्र-उपदेशेन (teaching scripture)? अयं पुरुषः मूकः किल (this person may as well be mute); [सः] दैवेन तु संचार्यते (he is moved by fate); इह कस्य किम् उपदिश्यते (what is taught here, and to whom)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Or why teach scripture at all? A person might as well be mute, if fate moves him; what is taught, and to whom?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Fatalism makes teaching pointless.** If fate decides everything, the Vedas, the Gītā and this very teaching would be meaningless. Scripture presupposes that we can choose.
- **The Buddha** argued the same against fatalists: if all is predetermined, there is no point in effort or in teaching the path (Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.61).

## Verse 2.8.8 { #2-8-8 }

<p class="shloka">
न च निस्पन्दता लोके दृष्टेह शवतां विना ।<br>
स्पन्दाच्च फलसंप्राप्तिस्तस्माद्दैवं निरर्थकम् ॥ २.८.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इह लोके (in this world) शवतां विना (except in a corpse) निस्पन्दता न च दृष्टा (no motionlessness is seen); स्पन्दात् च फल-संप्राप्तिः (and from movement comes the fruit); तस्मात् दैवं निरर्थकम् (so fate is meaningless).
</p>

**Translation:** "Nowhere in the world is complete stillness seen, except in a corpse; and results come from movement. So 'fate' is meaningless."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Life is movement.** Only the dead are inactive. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">न हि कश्चित्क्षणमपि जातु तिष्ठत्यकर्मकृत्</span> — *no one remains for even a moment without acting* (3.5).

## Verse 2.8.9 { #2-8-9 }

<p class="shloka">
न चामूर्तेन दैवेन मूर्तस्य सहकर्तृता ।<br>
पुंसः संदृश्यते काचित्तस्माद्दैवं निरर्थकम् ॥ २.८.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
न च (nor) अमूर्तेन दैवेन (with formless fate) मूर्तस्य पुंसः (of an embodied person) काचित् सह-कर्तृता संदृश्यते (is any shared agency seen); तस्मात् दैवं निरर्थकम् (so fate is meaningless).
</p>

**Translation:** "Nor is any joint action between a formless fate and an embodied person ever seen. So 'fate' is meaningless."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **How could the formless push the formed?** If fate has no body, how does it move a body? The argument is philosophical: interaction needs some common ground.

## Verse 2.8.10 { #2-8-10 }

<p class="shloka">
मिथोऽङ्गानि समासाद्य द्वयोरेकैककर्तृता ।<br>
हस्तादीनां हतत्वे ह न दैवेन क्वचित्कृतम् ॥ २.८.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अङ्गानि मिथः समासाद्य (when limbs come together) द्वयोः एक-एक-कर्तृता (each of the two has its own agency); हस्त-आदीनां हतत्वे (when the hands and the like are injured) ह दैवेन क्वचित् न कृतम् (it is never done by fate).
</p>

**Translation:** "When two limbs work together, each acts on its own part; and when hands and other limbs are injured, it is never fate that did it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Visible causes.** An injury has a visible cause — a fall, a blow. One need not invent a hidden one.

## Verse 2.8.11 { #2-8-11 }

<p class="shloka">
मनोबुद्धिवदप्येतद्दैवं नेहानुभूयते ।<br>
आगोपालं कृतप्रज्ञैस्तेन दैवमसत्सदा ॥ २.८.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एतत् दैवं (this fate) मनः-बुद्धिवत् अपि (unlike even mind and intellect) इह न अनुभूयते (is not experienced here) आ-गोपालं (by anyone, down to a cowherd) कृत-प्रज्ञैः (or by the learned); तेन दैवं सदा असत् (so fate is always unreal).
</p>

**Translation:** "Mind and intellect are experienced directly; fate is not — not by the learned, not by a cowherd. So fate is always unreal."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Mind is experienced; fate is not.** We cannot see the mind, but we know it from within. Fate has neither outer nor inner evidence.

## Verse 2.8.12 { #2-8-12 }

<p class="shloka">
पृथक्चेद्बुद्धिरन्योऽर्थः सैव चेत्कान्यता तयोः ।<br>
कल्पनायां प्रमाणं चेत्पौरुषं किं न कल्प्यते ॥ २.८.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
बुद्धिः पृथक् चेत् (if [fate] is separate from intellect) [तदा] अन्यः अर्थः (it is a different thing); सा एव चेत् (if it is the same) तयोः का अन्यता (what difference is there)? कल्पनायां प्रमाणं चेत् (if there is evidence for such an assumption) पौरुषं किं न कल्प्यते (why not assume effort instead)?
</p>

**Translation:** "If fate is different from one's intellect, it is some other thing; if it is the same, what is the difference? And if mere assumption counts as evidence, why not assume effort instead?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A dilemma.** Either fate is one's own mind (then it is just one's mind at work) or something else (then show it). And if guessing is allowed, guess effort — at least effort is visible.
- **The economy of explanation.** Do not add unseen causes where seen ones suffice.

## Verse 2.8.13 { #2-8-13 }

<p class="shloka">
नामूर्तेस्तेन सङ्गोऽस्ति नभसेव वपुष्मतः ।<br>
मूर्तं च दृश्यते लग्नं तस्माद्दैवं न विद्यते ॥ २.८.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तेन (with it) अमूर्तेः सङ्गः न अस्ति (the formless has no contact), नभसा इव वपुष्मतः (as the sky has none with a body); मूर्तं च लग्नं दृश्यते (only what has form is seen to make contact); तस्मात् दैवं न विद्यते (so fate does not exist).
</p>

**Translation:** "The formless has no contact with anything, any more than the sky touches a body; only what has form is seen to make contact. So fate does not exist."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Space touches nothing.** Space contains everything but pushes nothing. A formless fate could not act as a cause. Note the later teaching: consciousness too is formless like space — not a cause pushing things, but the light in which all appears.

## Verse 2.8.14 { #2-8-14 }

<p class="shloka">
विनियोक्तृ भूतानामस्त्यन्यच्चेज्जगत्त्रये ।<br>
शेरते भूतवृन्दानि दैवं सर्वं करिष्यति ॥ २.८.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
जगत्-त्रये (in the three worlds) भूतानाम् अन्यत् विनियोक्तृ अस्ति चेत् (if there is some other director of beings), अथ (then) भूत-वृन्दानि शेरते (all beings may lie down to sleep): दैवं सर्वं करिष्यति (fate will do everything).
</p>

**Translation:** "If something else in the three worlds directed all beings, then let all beings lie down and sleep — fate will do everything."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Why bother?** If an outside director does everything, all beings could simply rest. No one acts that way; so no one truly believes it.
- **Mahāvīra** taught that the soul is its own agent (*kartā*) and enjoyer (*bhoktā*); there is no external director of its karma.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints विनियोक्रथ; corrected here to विनियोक्तृ ("director").

## Verse 2.8.15 { #2-8-15 }

<p class="shloka">
दैवेन त्वभियुक्तोऽहं तत्करोमीदृशं स्थितम् ।<br>
समाश्वासनवागेषा न दैवं परमार्थतः ॥ २.८.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
"अहं तु दैवेन अभियुक्तः (I am employed by fate) तत् ईदृशं स्थितं करोमि" (so I do what is set for me thus) — एषा समाश्वासन-वाक् (this is a statement of consolation); दैवं परमार्थतः न (fate does not exist in truth).
</p>

**Translation:** "'Fate has set me to this, so I do what is laid down' — this is only a saying for consolation. In truth there is no fate."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Consolation again** (2.7.15). Vasiṣṭha is gentle with the sorrowful but firm about the truth: what consoles is not therefore real.

## Verse 2.8.16 { #2-8-16 }

<p class="shloka">
मूढैः प्रकल्पितं दैवं तत्परास्ते क्षयं गताः ।<br>
प्राज्ञास्तु पौरुषार्थेन पदमुत्तमतां गताः ॥ २.८.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मूढैः दैवं प्रकल्पितम् (fate is imagined by the deluded); तत्-पराः ते क्षयं गताः (those devoted to it go to ruin); प्राज्ञाः तु (but the wise) पौरुष-अर्थेन (through effort) उत्तमतां पदं गताः (reached the highest state).
</p>

**Translation:** "Fate is an invention of the deluded, and those who rely on it are ruined; the wise reach the highest state through effort."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Two outcomes, two attitudes.** Ruin and the highest state: the difference lies in one's stance towards one's own power.

## Verse 2.8.17 { #2-8-17 }

<p class="shloka">
ये शूरा ये च विक्रान्ता ये प्राज्ञा ये च पण्डिताः ।<br>
तैस्तैः किमिव लोकेऽस्मिन्वद दैवं प्रतीक्ष्यते ॥ २.८.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये शूराः (those who are brave) ये च विक्रान्ताः (and those who are bold) ये प्राज्ञाः (those who are wise) ये च पण्डिताः (and those who are learned) — तैः तैः (by them) अस्मिन् लोके (in this world) किम् इव दैवं प्रतीक्ष्यते (what fate is waited for)? वद (say)!
</p>

**Translation:** "The brave, the bold, the wise, the learned — tell me, what 'fate' do any of them wait for in this world?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Look at the great.** No hero, sage or scholar waits on fate. Vasiṣṭha invites Rāma to look at the models he admires.

## Verse 2.8.18 { #2-8-18 }

<p class="shloka">
कालविद्भिर्विनिर्णीता यस्यातिचिरजीविता ।<br>
स चेज्जीवति संछिन्नशिरास्तद्दैवमुत्तमम् ॥ २.८.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
काल-विद्भिः (by astrologers) यस्य अति-चिर-जीविता विनिर्णीता (whose very long life has been predicted), सः चेत् संछिन्न-शिराः जीवति (if he lives with his head cut off), तत् दैवम् उत्तमम् (then fate would be supreme).
</p>

**Translation:** "If a man whose long life the astrologers had foretold were to go on living after his head was cut off, then fate would indeed be supreme."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Predictions and their limits.** A forecast does not override cause and effect. Vasiṣṭha does not ridicule astrology as such, but denies that any prediction outranks action.
- **Practical lesson.** No one should neglect his health, study or duty because a horoscope promises a good outcome.

## Verse 2.8.19 { #2-8-19 }

<p class="shloka">
कालविद्भिर्विनिर्णीतं पाण्डित्यं यस्य राघव ।<br>
अनध्यापित एवासौ तज्ज्ञश्चेद्दैवमुत्तमम् ॥ २.८.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राघव (O Rāghava), यस्य पाण्डित्यं काल-विद्भिः विनिर्णीतम् (whose learning has been foretold by astrologers), असौ अन्-अध्यापितः एव (if he, without being taught) तत्-ज्ञः चेत् (were to become learned), तत् दैवम् उत्तमम् (then fate would be supreme).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāghava, if one whose learning had been foretold by the astrologers became learned without ever being taught, then fate would be supreme."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Learning needs study.** Even a gifted child must study. The prediction can only come true through effort.

## Verse 2.8.20 { #2-8-20 }

<p class="shloka">
विश्वामित्रेण मुनिना दैवमुत्सृज्य दूरतः ।<br>
पौरुषेणैव संप्राप्तं ब्राह्मण्यं राम नान्यथा ॥ २.८.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राम (O Rāma), मुनिना विश्वामित्रेण (by the sage Viśvāmitra) दैवं दूरतः उत्सृज्य (setting fate far aside) पौरुषेण एव (by effort alone) ब्राह्मण्यं संप्राप्तम् (brahminhood was attained), न अन्यथा (not otherwise).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāma, the sage Viśvāmitra, setting fate far aside, attained the status of a brahmin by effort alone, and in no other way."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A living example in the hall.** Viśvāmitra was born a king (kṣatriya), and by fierce austerity became a brahmarṣi — something considered impossible. He sits beside Vasiṣṭha now. Vasiṣṭha, once his great rival, honours his effort publicly.
- **Status earned, not inherited.** The Buddha: *na jaccā brāhmaṇo hoti … kammunā brāhmaṇo hoti* — *not by birth is one a brahmin; by action is one a brahmin* (Sutta Nipāta 650, Vāseṭṭha Sutta).

## Verse 2.8.21 { #2-8-21 }

<p class="shloka">
अस्माभिरपरै राम पुरुषैर्मुनितां गतैः ।<br>
पौरुषेणैव संप्राप्ता चिरं गगनगामिता ॥ २.८.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राम (O Rāma), अस्माभिः (by us) अपरैः मुनितां गतैः पुरुषैः (and by other men who became sages) पौरुषेण एव (by effort alone) चिरं गगन-गामिता संप्राप्ता (the power to move through the sky was long ago attained).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāma, we and other men who became sages gained the power to travel through the sky by effort alone, long ago."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Powers from practice.** The yogic powers (सिद्धि) too are fruits of discipline. Patañjali lists them in the third chapter of the Yoga Sūtra, always as results of practice — and warns that they are obstacles to the final goal (3.37).

## Verse 2.8.22 { #2-8-22 }

<p class="shloka">
उत्साद्य देवसंघातं चक्रुस्त्रिभुवनोदरे ।<br>
पौरुषेणैव यत्नेन साम्राज्यं दानवेश्वराः ॥ २.८.२२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दानव-ईश्वराः (the lords of the Dānavas) देव-संघातं उत्साद्य (having overthrown the host of gods) पौरुषेण एव यत्नेन (by effort alone) त्रि-भुवन-उदरे साम्राज्यं चक्रुः (established empire over the three worlds).
</p>

**Translation:** "The demon lords drove out the whole company of the gods and ruled an empire over the three worlds — through their own effort and nothing else."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Even demons.** Effort works for the wicked too. That is why it must be guided by scripture (2.4.19).

## Verse 2.8.23 { #2-8-23 }

<p class="shloka">
आलूनशीर्णमाभोगि जगदाजह्रुरोजसा ।<br>
पौरुषेणैव यत्नेन दानवेभ्यः सुरेश्वराः ॥ २.८.२३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सुर-ईश्वराः (the lords of the gods) पौरुषेण एव यत्नेन (by effort alone) ओजसा (with vigour) आलून-शीर्णम् आभोगि जगत् (the vast world, cut down and shattered) दानवेभ्यः आजह्रुः (took back from the demons).
</p>

**Translation:** "And the lords of the gods, by effort alone and with vigour, took back from the demons the wide world they had laid waste."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Recovery.** The gods too were defeated, and recovered by effort. Defeat is not final for those who keep striving.

## Verse 2.8.24 { #2-8-24 }

<p class="shloka">
राम पौरुषयुक्त्या च सलिलं धार्यतेऽनया ।<br>
चिरं करण्डके चारु न दैवं तत्र कारणम् ॥ २.८.२४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राम (O Rāma), अनया पौरुष-युक्त्या च (and by this skill of effort) सलिलं करण्डके (water in a basket) चिरं चारु धार्यते (is held well for a long time); तत्र दैवं न कारणम् (fate is not the cause of that).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāma, by skilful effort even water can be held in a basket for a long time; fate is not the cause of that."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Water in a basket.** Seemingly impossible — yet with skill (a woven basket sealed with pitch) it is done. Effort joined to skill (युक्ति) achieves what seems impossible.

## Verse 2.8.25 { #2-8-25 }

<p class="shloka">
भरणादानसंरम्भविभ्रमश्रमभूमिषु ।<br>
शक्तता दृश्यते राम न दैवस्यौषधेरिव ॥ २.८.२५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राम (O Rāma), भरण-आदान-संरम्भ-विभ्रम-श्रम-भूमिषु (in the fields of supporting, acquiring, striving, moving and labour) शक्तता दृश्यते (capability is seen); न दैवस्य (not of fate), औषधेः इव (as [it is of] a medicine).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāma, in supporting, acquiring, striving, moving and labouring, it is capability that is seen at work — as the power of a medicine is seen — not fate."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Like a medicine.** A medicine's power is shown by its effect. Effort is the same: its power is visible in results.

## Verse 2.8.26 { #2-8-26 }

<p class="shloka">
सकलकारणकार्यविवर्जितं निजविकल्पवशादुपकल्पितम् ।<br>
त्वमनपेक्ष्य हि दैवमसन्मयं श्रय शुभाशय पौरुषमुत्तमम् ॥ २.८.२६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
शुभ-आशय (O good-hearted one), त्वं (you) सकल-कारण-कार्य-विवर्जितं (devoid of all cause and effect) निज-विकल्प-वशात् उपकल्पितम् (fashioned by one's own imagining) असत्-मयं दैवं हि अनपेक्ष्य (disregarding unreal fate) उत्तमं पौरुषं श्रय (take refuge in the highest effort).
</p>

**Translation:** "Good-hearted one, disregard that unreal fate, which has no part in cause and effect and is only made up by one's own imagining, and take refuge in the highest effort."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The closing verse** echoes 2.6.39 almost word for word. Each sarga on effort ends in the same call: let go of fate, take up effort.
