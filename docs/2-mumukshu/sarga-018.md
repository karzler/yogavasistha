# Sarga 18

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · How to study, and how to read illustrations (70 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 18 at a glance"
    How to study the teaching, and how to read its illustrations.

    1. Through the teaching that all is mind, awakening surely comes. Accept what is reasonable even from a child; reject the unreasonable even from Brahmā (2.18.1–2.18.5).
    2. The fruits of study: refined speech and manners, thinning faults, a mind clear as an autumn lake, unshaken by fear (2.18.6–2.18.31). (Verse 16 is missing in the source.)
    3. The text is easy, poetic and testable; studied again and again, it brings rare wisdom. The illusion of "I and world" stills of itself, like a dream recognised (2.18.32–2.18.47). (Verse 46 is missing.)
    4. Illustrations: they make the unknown known through the known, and apply in one respect only. Do not quibble over them, and do not let sophistry destroy understanding (2.18.48–2.18.70). (Verse 52 is missing.)

## Verse 2.18.1 { #2-18-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
अस्यां वा चित्तमात्रायां प्रबोधः संप्रवर्तते ।<br>
बीजादिव सतो व्युप्तादवश्यंभावि सत्फलम् ॥ २.१८.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्यां वा चित्त-मात्रायां (in this [teaching] that the world is mind alone) प्रबोधः संप्रवर्तते (awakening arises), सतः व्युप्तात् बीजात् इव (as from a good seed that has been sown) अवश्यं-भावि सत्-फलम् (good fruit is sure to come).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "Through this teaching, that all is mind, awakening is sure to come, as good fruit surely comes from a good seed once it is sown."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **चित्तमात्र — mind only.** A term shared with the Buddhist Yogācāra school (*cittamātra*). The Yoga Vasiṣṭha teaches that the world is mind, and that mind is consciousness — the Vedāntic step beyond.
- **Sarga 18** turns to method: how to study the text, and the role of reason and of examples.

## Verse 2.18.2 { #2-18-2 }

<p class="shloka">
अपि पौरुषमादेयं शास्त्रं चेद्युक्तिबोधकम् ।<br>
अन्यत्त्वार्षमपि त्याज्यं भाव्यं न्याय्यैकसेविना ॥ २.१८.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पौरुषम् अपि शास्त्रं (even a treatise by a human author) युक्ति-बोधकं चेत् (if it teaches with reason) आदेयम् (should be accepted); अन्यत् तु (but any other) आर्षम् अपि (even if from a seer) त्याज्यम् (should be set aside); न्याय्य-एक-सेविना भाव्यम् (one should be devoted to reason alone).
</p>

**Translation:** "Even a work by a human author should be accepted if it teaches through reason; any other should be set aside, even if it comes from a seer. Be devoted to what is reasonable."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A remarkable verse.** Authority is not enough; reason is the test. This is among the most famous statements of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha.
- **The Buddha's advice to the Kālāmas:** do not accept a teaching merely because of tradition, scripture, or the teacher's standing; when you know for yourselves that it is wholesome and leads to welfare, then accept it (Aṅguttara Nikāya 3.65).
- **Not against scripture.** Vasiṣṭha constantly upholds scripture; here he says that scripture's authority rests on its truth, which reason confirms.

## Verse 2.18.3 { #2-18-3 }

<p class="shloka">
युक्तियुक्तमुपादेयं वचनं बालकादपि ।<br>
अन्यत्तृणमिव त्याज्यमप्युक्तं पद्मजन्मना ॥ २.१८.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
युक्ति-युक्तं वचनं (a statement in accord with reason) बालकात् अपि उपादेयम् (should be accepted even from a child); अन्यत् (any other) पद्म-जन्मना उक्तम् अपि (even if spoken by the lotus-born Brahmā) तृणम् इव त्याज्यम् (should be cast aside like straw).
</p>

**Translation:** "A reasonable statement should be accepted even from a child; anything else should be thrown away like straw, even if Brahmā himself said it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Even from a child.** Truth does not depend on who says it. Even Brahmā, the source of this very teaching (2.10), is not to be followed against reason. Vasiṣṭha applies the rule to his own lineage.
- **Jaina anekāntavāda** similarly asks one to weigh every claim from its standpoint, rather than accepting or rejecting it by authority.

## Verse 2.18.4 { #2-18-4 }

<p class="shloka">
योऽस्मत्तातस्य कूपोऽयमिति कौपं पिबत्यपः ।<br>
त्यक्त्वा गाङ्गं पुरस्थं तं को नाशास्त्यतिरागिणम् ॥ २.१८.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यः (whoever) "अयम् अस्मत्-तातस्य कूपः" इति ("this is my father's well") पुरः-स्थं गाङ्गं त्यक्त्वा (leaving the Gaṅgā water right before him) कौपम् अपः पिबति (drinks well-water), तम् अति-रागिणं (that over-attached person) कः न आशास्ति (who would not admonish)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Who would not scold a man so attached that he drinks from a well because 'it is my father's well', leaving the Gaṅgā flowing right in front of him?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **My father's well.** Attachment to one's own tradition merely because it is one's own. The Gītā uses a well too: <span class="parallel">यावानर्थ उदपाने सर्वतः संप्लुतोदके</span> — *as much use as a well has when water floods everywhere* (2.46).

## Verse 2.18.5 { #2-18-5 }

<p class="shloka">
यथोषसि प्रवृत्तायामालोकोऽवश्यमेष्यति ।<br>
अस्यां वा चित्तमात्रायां सुविवेकस्तथैष्यति ॥ २.१८.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (as) उषसि प्रवृत्तायाम् (when dawn has come) आलोकः अवश्यम् एष्यति (light will surely come), [तथा] अस्यां चित्त-मात्रायां (in this teaching of mind-only) सु-विवेकः एष्यति (sound discernment will come).
</p>

**Translation:** "As light is sure to follow dawn, so through this teaching that all is mind, sound discernment will come."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dawn and day.** Understanding grows gradually but surely.

## Verse 2.18.6 { #2-18-6 }

<p class="shloka">
श्रुतायां प्राज्ञवदनाद्बुद्ध्वान्तं स्वयमेव च ।<br>
शनैःशनैर्विचारेण बुद्धौ संस्कार आगते ॥ २.१८.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
प्राज्ञ-वदनात् श्रुतायां (when it has been heard from the mouth of the wise) स्वयम् एव च अन्तं बुद्ध्वा (and understood to the end by oneself) शनैः शनैः विचारेण (by gradual inquiry) बुद्धौ संस्कारे आगते (when an impression has formed in the mind) —
</p>

**Translation:** "When it has been heard from the wise, understood fully by oneself, and impressed on the mind little by little through inquiry —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hearing, understanding, deepening.** The classical three: श्रवण (hearing), मनन (reflection), निदिध्यासन (deep absorption).

## Verse 2.18.7 { #2-18-7 }

<p class="shloka">
पूर्वं तावदुदेत्यन्तर्भृशं संस्कृतवाक्यता ।<br>
शुद्धयुक्ता लतेवोच्चैर्या सभास्थानभूषणम् ॥ २.१८.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पूर्वं तावत् (first of all) अन्तः (within) भृशं संस्कृत-वाक्यता उदेति (a refined way of speaking arises) शुद्ध-युक्ता (pure and fitting) लता इव उच्चैः (rising like a creeper) या सभा-स्थान-भूषणम् (which adorns every assembly).
</p>

**Translation:** "— first there arises a refined and fitting way of speaking, growing tall like a creeper, an ornament in any assembly."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Study refines speech.** The earliest visible fruit.

## Verse 2.18.8 { #2-18-8 }

<p class="shloka">
परा नागरतोदेति महत्त्वगुणशालिनी ।<br>
सा यया स्नेहमायान्ति राजानो अमरा अपि ॥ २.१८.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[ततः] परा नागरता उदेति (then a high refinement of manner arises) महत्त्व-गुण-शालिनी (rich in the quality of greatness), सा यया (by which) राजानः अमराः अपि स्नेहम् आयान्ति (kings and even gods become fond).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then a fine grace of manner appears, full of nobility, which wins the affection of kings and even of gods."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Courtesy.** नागरता — urbanity, the grace of a cultured person. Inner refinement shows outwardly.

## Verse 2.18.9 { #2-18-9 }

<p class="shloka">
पूर्वापरज्ञः सर्वत्र नरो भवति बुद्धिमान् ।<br>
पदार्थानां यथा दीपहस्तो निशि सुलोचनः ॥ २.१८.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नरः (a person) पूर्व-अपर-ज्ञः (knowing what comes before and after) सर्वत्र बुद्धिमान् भवति (becomes wise in all things), पदार्थानां (in regard to things) यथा निशि दीप-हस्तः सु-लोचनः (as one with good eyes and a lamp in hand at night).
</p>

**Translation:** "Knowing what comes before and after, a person becomes wise in everything, like one with good eyes carrying a lamp at night."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Seeing consequences.** Wisdom is foresight.

## Verse 2.18.10 { #2-18-10 }

<p class="shloka">
लोभमोहादयो दोषास्तानवं यान्त्यलं शनैः ।<br>
धियो दिशः समासन्नशरदो मिहिका यथा ॥ २.१८.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
लोभ-मोह-आदयः दोषाः (faults like greed and delusion) शनैः अलं तानवं यान्ति (gradually grow very thin), धियः दिशः (from the regions of the mind) समासन्न-शरदः मिहिकाः यथा (as fog with the coming of autumn).
</p>

**Translation:** "Faults like greed and delusion slowly wear thin from the horizons of the mind, as fog lifts when autumn approaches."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Thinning, not vanishing at once.** Compare the definition of liberation as the thinning of tendencies (2.2.5).

## Verse 2.18.11 { #2-18-11 }

<p class="shloka">
केवलं समवेक्ष्यन्ते विवेकाध्यासनं धियः ।<br>
न किंचन फलं धत्ते स्वाभ्यासेन विना क्रिया ॥ २.१८.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
धियः (minds) केवलं विवेक-अध्यासनं समवेक्ष्यन्ते (look only to dwelling in discernment); स्व-अभ्यासेन विना क्रिया (action without one's own practice) किंचन फलं न धत्ते (bears no fruit at all).
</p>

**Translation:** "The mind turns only towards dwelling in discernment. Without one's own practice, no action bears any fruit."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **One's own practice.** No one can practise for another. Mahāvīra: *appā kattā vikattā ya* — *the self is the doer and the undoer* (Uttarādhyayana 20.37).

## Verse 2.18.12 { #2-18-12 }

<p class="shloka">
मनः प्रसादमायाति शरदीव महत्सरः ।<br>
परं साम्यमुपादत्ते निर्मन्दर इवार्णवः ॥ २.१८.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मनः प्रसादम् आयाति (the mind becomes clear) शरदि महत् सरः इव (like a great lake in autumn); परं साम्यम् उपादत्ते (it takes on perfect evenness) निर्मन्दरः अर्णवः इव (like the ocean when the Mandara mountain no longer churns it).
</p>

**Translation:** "The mind becomes clear like a great lake in autumn, and perfectly even, like the ocean once Mount Mandara has stopped churning it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The churning stops.** In the Purāṇic story the gods churned the ocean with Mount Mandara. When the churning ends, the ocean settles. So with the mind when craving stops.

## Verse 2.18.13 { #2-18-13 }

<p class="shloka">
निरस्तकालिमारत्नशिखेवास्ततमःपटा ।<br>
प्रति ज्वलत्यलं प्रज्ञा पदार्थप्रविभागिनी ॥ २.१८.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पदार्थ-प्रविभागिनी प्रज्ञा (insight that discerns things clearly) निरस्त-कालिमा रत्न-शिखा इव (like the flame of a gem whose dark film has been removed) अस्त-तमः-पटा (its veil of darkness gone) अलं प्रतिज्वलति (shines brightly).
</p>

**Translation:** "Insight that sees things clearly shines brightly, like the flame of a gem from which the dark film has been wiped and the veil of darkness removed."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The gem's own light.** Insight is not added; it is uncovered.

## Verse 2.18.14 { #2-18-14 }

<p class="shloka">
दैन्यदारिद्र्यदोषाढ्या दृष्टयो दर्शितान्तराः ।<br>
न निकृन्तन्ति मर्माणि ससंनाहमिवेषवः ॥ २.१८.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दैन्य-दारिद्र्य-दोष-आढ्याः (full of the faults of misery and poverty) दर्शित-अन्तराः दृष्टयः (situations whose inner nature has been seen) मर्माणि न निकृन्तन्ति (do not cut to the quick), स-संनाहम् इषवः इव (as arrows [do not pierce] one in armour).
</p>

**Translation:** "Hard circumstances of misery and poverty, once their inner nature has been seen, do not cut to the quick, as arrows do not pierce one who wears armour."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Understanding as armour.** Compare the armour of calm (2.13.67).

## Verse 2.18.15 { #2-18-15 }

<p class="shloka">
हृदयं नावलुम्पन्ति भीमाः संसृतिभीतयः ।<br>
पुरःस्थितमपि प्राज्ञं महोपलमिवेषवः ॥ २.१८.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भीमाः संसृति-भीतयः (the terrible fears of saṃsāra) पुरः-स्थितम् अपि प्राज्ञं (the wise, even when they stand before him) हृदयं न अवलुम्पन्ति (do not tear his heart), महा-उपलम् इषवः इव (as arrows [do not tear] a great rock).
</p>

**Translation:** "The terrible fears of saṃsāra, even when they stand before him, do not tear the heart of the wise, as arrows cannot tear a great rock."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rock and arrows.** The Dhammapada's rock, unmoved by wind (81).

## Verse 2.18.17 { #2-18-17 }

<p class="shloka">
सर्वदा सर्वभावेषु संशान्तिरुपजायते ।<br>
यामिन्यामिव शान्तायां प्रजालोक उपागते ॥ २.१८.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सर्वदा (always) सर्व-भावेषु (in all conditions) संशान्तिः उपजायते (deep peace arises), यामिन्याम् इव शान्तायां (as in a still night) प्रजा-लोके उपागते [निद्राम्] (when all people have gone to rest).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The quiet of night.** A peace one can feel physically.

## Verse 2.18.18 { #2-18-18 }

<p class="shloka">
समुद्रस्येव गाम्भीर्यं धैर्यं मेरोरिव स्थितम् ।<br>
अन्तः शीतलता चेन्दोरिवोदेति विचारिणः ॥ २.१८.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विचारिणः (in the one who inquires) समुद्रस्य इव गाम्भीर्यम् (depth like the ocean's) मेरोः इव स्थितं धैर्यम् (steadiness like Meru's) इन्दोः इव अन्तः शीतलता च (and inner coolness like the moon's) उदेति (arise).

**Translation:** "In the one who inquires there arise the depth of the ocean, the steadiness of Mount Meru and the inner coolness of the moon."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Three qualities.** Depth, steadiness, coolness — ocean, mountain, moon.

## Verse 2.18.19 { #2-18-19 }

<p class="shloka">
सा जीवन्मुक्तता तस्य शनैः परिणतिं गता ।<br>
शान्ताशेषविशेषस्य भवत्यविषयो गिराम् ॥ २.१८.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तस्य (his) सा जीवन्मुक्तता (that liberation in life) शनैः परिणतिं गता (gradually having matured) शान्त-अशेष-विशेषस्य (for one in whom all distinctions are stilled) गिराम् अविषयः भवति (passes beyond the reach of words).
</p>

**Translation:** "His liberation in life gradually ripens, and for one in whom all distinctions are stilled, it passes beyond the reach of words."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Beyond words.** The Taittirīya: <span class="parallel">यतो वाचो निवर्तन्ते अप्राप्य मनसा सह</span> — *from which words turn back, with the mind, not reaching it* (2.4.1).

## Verse 2.18.21 { #2-18-21 }

<p class="shloka">
हृद्याकाशे विवेकार्के शमालोकिनि निर्मले ।<br>
अनर्थसार्थकर्तारो नोद्यन्ति किल केतवः ॥ २.१८.२१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
निर्मले हृत्-आकाशे (in the clear sky of the heart) शम-आलोकिनि विवेक-अर्के (when the sun of discernment, whose light is calm, [shines]) अनर्थ-सार्थ-कर्तारः केतवः (the comets that bring hosts of misfortune) न उद्यन्ति किल (do not rise).

**Pointers for Reflection:**

## Verse 2.18.22 { #2-18-22 }

<p class="shloka">
शाम्यन्ति शुद्धिमायान्ति सौम्यास्तिष्ठन्ति सून्नते ।<br>
अचञ्चले जलेऽतृष्णाः शरदीवाभ्रमालिकाः ॥ २.१८.२२ ॥

अङ्ग (dear one), एक-देश-समर्थत्वात् (by being apt in one respect) उपमानं (an illustration) उपमेय-अवबोधनं करोति (makes the thing illustrated understood), यथा दीपः अर्थ-प्रभया (as a lamp [reveals] by the light on objects).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear one, an illustration makes its subject understood by being apt in one respect, as a lamp shows things by its light alone."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The lamp image completed.**

## Verse 2.18.67 { #2-18-67 }

<p class="shloka">
दृष्टान्तस्यांशमात्रेण बोध्यबोधोदये सति ।<br>
उपादेयतया ग्राह्यो महावाक्यार्थनिश्चयः ॥ २.१८.६७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
दृष्टान्तस्य अंश-मात्रेण (by only a part of the illustration) बोध्य-बोध-उदये सति (when understanding of what is to be understood arises), महा-वाक्य-अर्थ-निश्चयः (the certainty of the meaning of the great sayings) उपादेयतया ग्राह्यः (should be taken up as to be embraced).
</p>

**Translation:** "When understanding has dawned through even one part of an illustration, take hold of the certainty of the great sayings' meaning and embrace it."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **महावाक्य — the great sayings.** The Upaniṣadic statements of identity: <span class="parallel">तत्त्वमसि</span> — *you are That* (Chāndogya 6.8.7); <span class="parallel">अहं ब्रह्मास्मि</span> — *I am Brahman* (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 1.4.10). Illustrations serve these.

## Verse 2.18.68 { #2-18-68 }

<p class="shloka">
न कुतार्किकतामेत्य नाशनीया प्रबुद्धता ।<br>
अनुभूत्यपलापान्तैरपवित्रैर्विकल्पितैः ॥ २.१८.६८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कुतार्किकताम् एत्य (by resorting to bad reasoning) अनुभूति-अपलाप-अन्तैः (ending in denial of experience) अपवित्रैः विकल्पितैः (with impure fabrications) प्रबुद्धता न नाशनीया (awakened understanding should not be destroyed).
</p>

**Translation:** "Do not destroy awakened understanding by resorting to bad logic and impure fabrications that end by denying experience itself."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reason, yes; sophistry, no.** Vasiṣṭha praised reason (v. 2–3); here he warns against its abuse. The Kaṭha: <span class="parallel">नैषा तर्केण मतिरापनेया</span> — *this understanding is not to be reached, or destroyed, by mere argument* (1.2.9).

## Verse 2.18.69 { #2-18-69 }

<p class="shloka">
विचारणादनुभवकारिवैरिणोऽपि वाङ्मयं त्वनुगतमस्मदादिषु ।<br>
स्त्रियोक्तमप्यपपरमार्थवैदिकं वचो वचःप्रलपनमेव नागमः ॥ २.१८.६९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विचारणात् (through inquiry) अनुभव-कारि वाङ्मयं (words that bring experience) वैरिणः अपि (even from an enemy) अस्मत्-आदिषु अनुगतं तु (are accepted by us and others); अप-परमार्थ-वैदिकं वचः (but words without the highest truth) [केनचित्] उक्तम् अपि (even if spoken [by anyone]) वचः-प्रलपनम् एव (are mere babble) न आगमः (not scripture).
</p>

**Translation:** "Words that, through inquiry, bring real experience are accepted by us and others even from an enemy; words that lack the highest truth, whoever speaks them, are mere chatter, not scripture."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Truth defines scripture.** What makes a text authoritative is that it leads to experience of the truth.
- **Reading note.** One word in the second line is corrupt in the online text; the contrast — truth-bearing words versus empty words, whatever their source — is clear from the context.

## Verse 2.18.70 { #2-18-70 }

<p class="shloka">
अस्माकमस्ति मतिरङ्ग तयेति सर्वशास्त्रैकवाक्यकरणं फलितं यतो यः ।<br>
प्रातीतिकार्थमपशास्त्रनिजाङ्गपुष्टात्संवेदनादितरदस्ति ततः प्रमाणम् ॥ २.१८.७० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अङ्ग (dear one), अस्माकं मतिः अस्ति (our view is) तया इति (by this) सर्व-शास्त्र-एक-वाक्य-करणं फलितम् (the harmony of all scriptures is achieved); यतः (because) यः प्रातीतिक-अर्थम् अप-शास्त्र-निज-अङ्ग-पुष्टात् संवेदनात् इतरत् अस्ति (that which is other than mere appearance, supported only by unscriptural fancies) ततः प्रमाणम् (that is the valid authority).
</p>

**Translation:** "Dear one, this is our view: by this method all scriptures are brought into harmony. What has authority is that which goes beyond mere appearances propped up by fancies outside scripture."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Harmony of all scriptures.** Vasiṣṭha's method makes the scriptures speak with one voice — the एकवाक्यता of 2.13.11. This harmonising spirit is one of the marks of the Yoga Vasiṣṭha, noted on the Home page.
