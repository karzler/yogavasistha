# Sarga 17

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · The six books, and the freed one (50 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 17 at a glance"
    The plan of the whole work, and the one who completes it.

    1. The fit student is great; this text, *The Means to Liberation*, has 32,000 verses and gives nirvāṇa when understood, as a lamp gives light (2.17.1–2.17.9).
    2. Its six books: Dispassion (1,500 verses), The Conduct of the Seeker (1,000), Origination (7,000), Existence (3,000), Stilling (5,000) and Nirvāṇa (the rest) (2.17.10–2.17.41).
    3. The world as Book 3 shows it: never born, yet seeming to arise — a dream-city, a mirage, the blue of the sky, a painted fire (2.17.14–2.17.28).
    4. For the one in whom delusion is stilled, the world remains only as a fading picture (2.17.34–2.17.40).
    5. The completed sage: consciousness itself, at rest; the world rests on the tip of his hair; his heart is vaster than all the gods (2.17.42–2.17.50).

## Verse 2.17.1 { #2-17-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
एवमन्तर्विवेको यः स महानिह राघव ।<br>
योग्यो ज्ञानगिरः श्रोतुं राजेव नयभारतीम् ॥ २.१७.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राघव (O Rāghava), इह (here) यः एवम् अन्तः-विवेकः (one who has such inner discernment) सः महान् (he is great) ज्ञान-गिरः श्रोतुं योग्यः (and fit to hear words of knowledge), राजा इव नय-भारतीम् (as a king [is fit to hear] words of statecraft).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "Rāghava, whoever has discernment of this inward kind is truly great; he is ready for the words of wisdom, as a king is ready for advice on statecraft."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Fitness.** Sargas 11–16 described the fit student. Now Vasiṣṭha describes what he will teach.

## Verse 2.17.2 { #2-17-2 }

<p class="shloka">
अवदातोऽवदातस्य विचारस्य महाशयः ।<br>
जडसङ्गोज्झितो योग्यः शरदिन्दोर्यथा नभः ॥ २.१७.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अवदातः (pure) महा-आशयः (large-hearted) जड-सङ्ग-उज्झितः (free from attachment to the dull) अवदातस्य विचारस्य योग्यः (is fit for pure inquiry), यथा नभः शरत्-इन्दोः (as the sky for the autumn moon).
</p>

**Translation:** "One who is pure, large-hearted and free from dull attachments is fit for pure inquiry, as the clear sky is fit for the autumn moon."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Clear sky, bright moon.** The moon is always bright; only a clear sky shows it fully.

## Verse 2.17.3 { #2-17-3 }

<p class="shloka">
त्वमेतया खण्डितया गुणलक्ष्म्या समाश्रितः ।<br>
मनोमोहहरं वाक्यं वक्ष्यमाणमिदं श्रृणु ॥ २.१७.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
त्वम् (you) एतया अखण्डितया गुण-लक्ष्म्या समाश्रितः (endowed with this unbroken wealth of virtues) इदं मनो-मोह-हरं वक्ष्यमाणं वाक्यं शृणु (hear these words, which will remove the mind's delusion).
</p>

**Translation:** "You possess this wealth of virtues unbroken; so listen to the words I shall now speak, which remove the delusion of the mind."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Reading note.** The online text reads खण्डितया ("broken"), which contradicts the praise; read as अखण्डितया ("unbroken"), with the negative hidden by sandhi after एतया.

## Verse 2.17.4 { #2-17-4 }

<p class="shloka">
पुण्यकल्पद्रुमो यस्य फलभारानतः स्थितः ।<br>
मुक्तये जायते जन्तोस्तस्येदं श्रोतुमुद्यमः ॥ २.१७.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यस्य (whose) पुण्य-कल्प-द्रुमः (wishing tree of merit) फल-भार-आनतः स्थितः (stands bowed with the weight of its fruit), तस्य जन्तोः (for that being) मुक्तये (for liberation) इदं श्रोतुम् उद्यमः जायते (the eagerness to hear this arises).
</p>

**Translation:** "Only in one whose wishing tree of merit stands bowed under the weight of its fruit does the eagerness to hear this, for the sake of freedom, arise."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Eagerness is earned.** The wish to hear the teaching is itself a sign of past merit (2.11.2).

## Verse 2.17.5 { #2-17-5 }

<p class="shloka">
पावनानामुदाराणां परबोधैकदायिनाम् ।<br>
वचसां भाजनं भूत्यै भव्यो भवति नाधमः ॥ २.१७.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पावनानाम् उदाराणां पर-बोध-एक-दायिनां वचसां (of words that are purifying, noble, and give the highest awakening) भाजनं (a vessel) भूत्यै (for one's good) भव्यः भवति (a noble person becomes), न अधमः (not a base one).
</p>

**Translation:** "Only a noble person, not a base one, becomes the vessel of words that are purifying, generous and give the highest awakening."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The vessel again** (2.2.20–21).

## Verse 2.17.6 { #2-17-6 }

<p class="shloka">
मोक्षोपायाभिधानेयं संहिता सारसंमिता ।<br>
त्रिंशद्द्वे च सहस्राणि ज्ञाता निर्वाणदायिनी ॥ २.१७.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
इयं मोक्ष-उपाय-अभिधाना संहिता (this compendium named The Means to Liberation) सार-संमिता (measured out from essence) त्रिंशत् द्वे च सहस्राणि (thirty-two thousand [verses]) ज्ञाता निर्वाण-दायिनी (when understood, gives nirvāṇa).
</p>

**Translation:** "This compendium, called *The Means to Liberation*, distilled from the essence, has thirty-two thousand verses; when it is understood, it gives nirvāṇa."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The text names itself and its size.** मोक्षोपाय — *The Means to Liberation* — and 32,000 verses (त्रिंशद् द्वे च सहस्राणि: thirty and two thousand). This is the source of the traditional count mentioned on the Home page.
- **When understood.** Not when read, but when understood (ज्ञाता).

## Verse 2.17.7 { #2-17-7 }

<p class="shloka">
दीपे यथा विनिद्रस्य ज्वलिते संप्रवर्तते ।<br>
आलोकोऽनिच्छतोऽप्येवं निर्वाणमनया भवेत् ॥ २.१७.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (as) दीपे ज्वलिते (when a lamp is lit) विनिद्रस्य (for one who is awake) अनिच्छतः अपि (even if unwilling) आलोकः संप्रवर्तते (light appears), एवम् (so) अनया (by this) निर्वाणं भवेत् (nirvāṇa will come).
</p>

**Translation:** "Just as, when a lamp is lit, one who is awake sees light even without wishing to, so nirvāṇa comes through this text."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Light works by itself.** One who is awake and near a lamp cannot help seeing. Knowledge, rightly received, frees as naturally.

## Verse 2.17.8 { #2-17-8 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वयं ज्ञाता श्रुता वापि भ्रान्तिशान्त्यैकसौख्यदा ।<br>
आप्रेक्ष्य वर्णिता सद्यो यथा स्वर्गतरङ्गिणी ॥ २.१७.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[इयं] स्वयं ज्ञाता (known by oneself) श्रुता वा अपि (or heard) भ्रान्ति-शान्ति-एक-सौख्य-दा (gives the one happiness of the stilling of delusion); आप्रेक्ष्य वर्णिता (when understood and explained) सद्यः (at once) [पवित्रयति] यथा स्वर्ग-तरङ्गिणी (like the heavenly river).
</p>

**Translation:** "Whether read oneself or heard from another, it gives the unique happiness of delusion stilled; once grasped and explained, it purifies at once, like the heavenly Gaṅgā."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Read or heard.** Both work. Many in India first met the Yoga Vasiṣṭha through recitation and discourse.

## Verse 2.17.9 { #2-17-9 }

<p class="shloka">
यथा रज्ज्वामहिभ्रान्तिर्विनश्यत्यवलोकनात् ।<br>
तथैतत्प्रेक्षणाच्छान्तिमेति संसारदुःखिता ॥ २.१७.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा (as) रज्ज्वाम् अहि-भ्रान्तिः (the illusion of a snake in a rope) अवलोकनात् विनश्यति (vanishes on looking), तथा (so) एतत्-प्रेक्षणात् (by examining this) संसार-दुःखिता शान्तिम् एति (the sorrow of saṃsāra comes to rest).
</p>

**Translation:** "As the illusion of a snake in a rope vanishes when one looks closely, so the sorrow of saṃsāra comes to rest by examining this teaching."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rope and snake again** (2.8.3). Nothing has to be destroyed; only seen.

## Verse 2.17.10 { #2-17-10 }

<p class="shloka">
युक्तियुक्तार्थवाक्यानि कल्पितानि पृथक्पृथक् ।<br>
दृष्टान्तसारसूक्तानि चास्यां प्रकरणानि षट् ॥ २.१७.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्याम् (in this) षट् प्रकरणानि (six books) युक्ति-युक्त-अर्थ-वाक्यानि (with statements full of reasoning) दृष्टान्त-सार-सूक्तानि च (and sayings whose essence is illustration) पृथक् पृथक् कल्पितानि (are set out separately).
</p>

**Translation:** "In it, six books are set out one by one, made of reasoned statements and sayings whose substance is illustration."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The plan of the work.** Verses 10–41 give the Yoga Vasiṣṭha's own table of contents: six prakaraṇas with their verse counts. Compare the table of the six books on this site's Home page.
- **Reason and story.** The two tools of the work: युक्ति (argument) and दृष्टान्त (illustration).

## Verse 2.17.11 { #2-17-11 }

<p class="shloka">
वैराग्याख्यं प्रकरणं प्रथमं परिकीर्तितम् ।<br>
विरागो वर्धते येन सेकेनेव मरौ तरुः ॥ २.१७.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
प्रथमं प्रकरणं (the first book) वैराग्य-आख्यं परिकीर्तितम् (is called Dispassion); येन (by which) विरागः वर्धते (dispassion grows), मरौ सेकेन तरुः इव (as a tree in the desert by watering).
</p>

**Translation:** "The first book is called *Dispassion*; by it dispassion grows, as a tree in the desert grows when it is watered."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Book 1, Vairāgya.** Rāma's despair — now completed on this site (1,146 verses in the surviving text).

## Verse 2.17.12 { #2-17-12 }

<p class="shloka">
अनुबन्धेन सहितं दिष्टतत्त्वनिरूपणम् ।<br>
सार्धं सहस्रं ग्रन्थस्य यस्मिन्हृदि विचारिते ।<br>
प्रकाशाच्छुद्धतोदेति मणाविव सुमार्जिते ॥ २.१७.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनुबन्धेन सहितं (with its connections) दिष्ट-तत्त्व-निरूपणम् (it sets out the true nature of what is given) ग्रन्थस्य सार्धं सहस्रम् (one and a half thousand verses) — यस्मिन् हृदि विचारिते (when it is reflected on in the heart) प्रकाशात् शुद्धता उदेति (purity arises from its light), मणौ सु-मार्जिते इव (as in a well-polished gem).
</p>

**Translation:** "With all that connects to it, it shows the true nature of what life brings; it has one and a half thousand verses. When it is reflected on in the heart, purity dawns through its light, as in a well-polished gem."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **1,500 verses.** The surviving Vairāgya Prakaraṇa is a little shorter; texts often vary from the number they announce.

## Verse 2.17.13 { #2-17-13 }

<p class="shloka">
मुमुक्षुव्यवहाराख्यं ततः प्रकरणं कृतम् ।<br>
सहस्रमात्रं ग्रन्थस्य युक्तिग्रन्थेन सुन्दरम् ॥ २.१७.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः (then) मुमुक्षु-व्यवहार-आख्यं प्रकरणं कृतम् (the book called The Conduct of the Seeker was made) — ग्रन्थस्य सहस्र-मात्रम् (only a thousand verses) युक्ति-ग्रन्थेन सुन्दरम् (lovely with its reasoned exposition).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then comes the book called *The Conduct of the Seeker*, only a thousand verses, made beautiful by its reasoning."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Book 2 describes itself.** The present book. Its full name is मुमुक्षुव्यवहार-प्रकरण, "the conduct of one who longs for freedom".

## Verse 2.17.14 { #2-17-14 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वभावो हि मुमुक्षूणां नराणां यत्र वर्ण्यते ।<br>
अथोत्पत्तिप्रकरणं दृष्टान्ताख्यायिकामयम् ॥ २.१७.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यत्र हि (in which) मुमुक्षूणां नराणां स्वभावः वर्ण्यते (the nature of people longing for freedom is described); अथ (next) उत्पत्ति-प्रकरणं (the book of Origination) दृष्टान्त-आख्यायिका-मयम् (full of illustrations and stories) —

## Verse 2.17.15 { #2-17-15 }

<p class="shloka">
सप्तग्रन्थसहस्राणि विज्ञानप्रतिपादकम् ।<br>
जागती द्रष्टृदृश्यश्रीरहंत्वमितिरूपिणी ॥ २.१७.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सप्त-ग्रन्थ-सहस्राणि (seven thousand verses) विज्ञान-प्रतिपादकम् (setting out true knowledge); [यत्र] जागती द्रष्टृ-दृश्य-श्रीः (the world's splendour of seer and seen) अहं-त्वम्-इति-रूपिणी (in the form of 'I' and 'you') —

**Translation:** "— seven thousand verses setting out true knowledge, in which the world's display of seer and seen, in the form of 'I' and 'you' —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

## Verse 2.17.16 { #2-17-16 }

<p class="shloka">
अनुत्पन्नैवोत्थितेव यत्रेति परिवर्ण्यते ।<br>
यस्मिन्श्रुते जगदिदं श्रोतान्तर्बुध्यतेऽखिलम् ॥ २.१७.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनुत्पन्ना एव उत्थिता इव (though never born, seems to have arisen) इति यत्र परिवर्ण्यते (is described); यस्मिन् श्रुते (on hearing which) श्रोता (the hearer) इदम् अखिलं जगत् अन्तः बुध्यते (understands this whole world within).
</p>

**Translation:** "— is shown to have seemingly arisen though it was never born. Hearing it, the listener comes to understand the whole world as within."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Never born.** The doctrine of *ajātivāda*, non-origination. Gauḍapāda: <span class="parallel">न कश्चिज्जायते जीवः संभवोऽस्य न विद्यते</span> — *no being is ever born; there is no origination for it* (Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 3.48).

## Verse 2.17.17 { #2-17-17 }

<p class="shloka">
सास्मद्युष्मत्सविस्तारं सलोकाकाशपर्वतम् ।<br>
पिण्डग्रहविनिर्मुक्तं निर्भित्तिकमपर्वतम् ॥ २.१७.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
सा (that [world]) अस्मत्-युष्मत्-स-विस्तारं (with all its 'we' and 'you') स-लोक-आकाश-पर्वतम् (with worlds, sky and mountains) पिण्ड-ग्रह-विनिर्मुक्तं (free of solid substance) निर्भित्तिकम् (without a supporting wall) अपर्वतम् (with no real mountains) —
</p>

**Translation:** "That world, with all its 'we' and 'you', its realms, sky and mountains, is free of solid substance, has no wall to stand on and no real mountains —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Verses 17–27** form one long chain of similes describing the world as Book 3 will present it.

## Verse 2.17.18 { #2-17-18 }

<p class="shloka">
पृथ्व्यादिभूतरहितं संकल्प इव पत्तनम् ।<br>
स्वप्नोपलम्भभावाभं मनोराज्यवदाततम् ॥ २.१७.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पृथ्वी-आदि-भूत-रहितं (without earth and the other elements) संकल्पे पत्तनम् इव (like a city in the imagination) स्वप्न-उपलम्भ-भाव-आभं (like things seen in a dream) मनो-राज्यवत् आततम् (spread out like a daydream kingdom) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— without earth or any element, like a city in the imagination, like what is seen in a dream, spread out like a kingdom in a daydream —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Dream and daydream.** Gauḍapāda: <span class="parallel">स्वप्नमाये यथा दृष्टे गन्धर्वनगरं यथा । तथा विश्वमिदं दृष्टं वेदान्तेषु विचक्षणैः</span> — *as a dream, as magic, as a city of the gandharvas — so the wise of Vedānta see this world* (Māṇḍūkya Kārikā 2.31).

## Verse 2.17.19 { #2-17-19 }

<p class="shloka">
गन्धर्वनगरप्रख्यमर्थशून्योपलम्भनात् ।<br>
द्विचन्द्रविभ्रमाभासं मृगतृष्णाम्बुवर्तनम् ॥ २.१७.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
गन्धर्व-नगर-प्रख्यम् (like a city of the gandharvas) अर्थ-शून्य-उपलम्भनात् (being perceived without real substance) द्वि-चन्द्र-विभ्रम-आभासं (like the illusion of two moons) मृगतृष्णा-अम्बु-वर्तनम् (behaving like mirage-water) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— like a city in the clouds, perceived though empty of substance, like the illusion of a double moon, like water in a mirage —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The double moon.** Pressing on one eye makes the moon seem two. A classic example of error in perception.

## Verse 2.17.20 { #2-17-20 }

<p class="shloka">
नौयानलोलशैलाभं सत्यलाभविवर्जितम् ।<br>
चित्तभ्रमपिशाचाभं निर्बीजमपि भासुरम् ॥ २.१७.२० ॥

- **The arithmetic.** 1,500 + 1,000 + 7,000 + 3,000 + 5,000 = 17,500; the rest of the 32,000 — about 14,500 verses — belongs to the Nirvāṇa Prakaraṇa.
- **शान्तकल्पन.** Nirvāṇa as the stilling of कल्पना, mental fabrication — the same word the Buddha's tradition uses (*papañca-vūpasama*).

## Verse 2.17.42 { #2-17-42 }

<p class="shloka">
अचेत्यचित्प्रकाशात्मा विज्ञानात्मा निरामयः ।<br>
परमाकाशकोशाच्छः शान्तसर्वभवभ्रमः ॥ २.१७.४२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[सः ज्ञानी] अचेत्य-चित्-प्रकाश-आत्मा (whose nature is the light of consciousness that is not an object) विज्ञान-आत्मा (whose nature is knowledge) निरामयः (free of affliction) परम-आकाश-कोश-अच्छः (clear as the sheath of the supreme space) शान्त-सर्व-भव-भ्रमः (with all illusion of becoming stilled) —
</p>

**Translation:** "The knower then is the light of a consciousness that is never an object; his nature is knowing; he is free of affliction, clear as the supreme space, with every illusion of becoming stilled —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Verses 42–49** describe the one who has completed the work. Consciousness that is never an object: <span class="parallel">येन सर्वमिदं विजानाति तं केन विजानीयात्</span> — *by what could one know that by which all is known?* (Bṛhadāraṇyaka 2.4.14).

## Verse 2.17.43 { #2-17-43 }

<p class="shloka">
निर्वापितजगद्यात्रः कृतकर्तव्यसुस्थितः ।<br>
समस्तजनतारम्भवज्रस्तम्भो नभोनिभः ॥ २.१७.४३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
निर्वापित-जगत्-यात्रः (for whom the world's journey has come to rest) कृत-कर्तव्य-सु-स्थितः (well established, having done what was to be done) समस्त-जनता-आरम्भ-वज्र-स्तम्भः (an adamantine pillar amid all people's undertakings) नभो-निभः (like the sky) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— for him the journey of the world has come to rest; he stands firm, having done what was to be done, an adamantine pillar among all the busy undertakings of the people, like the sky —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **कृतकृत्य.** "One who has done what was to be done" — the classical term for the free. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">एतद्बुद्ध्वा बुद्धिमान्स्यात्कृतकृत्यश्च भारत</span> (15.20). The Buddha's formula: *kataṃ karaṇīyaṃ* — *done is what was to be done*.

## Verse 2.17.44 { #2-17-44 }

<p class="shloka">
विनिगीर्णयथासंख्यजगज्जालातितृप्तिमान् ।<br>
आकाशीभूतनिःशेषरूपालोकमनस्कृतिः ॥ २.१७.४४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
विनिगीर्ण-यथा-संख्य-जगत्-जाल-अति-तृप्तिमान् (fully satisfied, as if having swallowed the whole net of worlds) आकाशी-भूत-निःशेष-रूप-आलोक-मनः-कृतिः (in whom all forms, perceptions and mental workings have become like space) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— fully satisfied, as though he had swallowed the whole net of worlds; in him every form, every perception, every working of mind has become like space —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Everything becomes space.** Not destroyed, but seen through: transparent.

## Verse 2.17.45 { #2-17-45 }

<p class="shloka">
कार्यकारणकर्तृत्वहेयादेयदृशोज्झितः ।<br>
सदेह इव निर्देहः ससंसारोऽप्यसंसृतिः ॥ २.१७.४५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कार्य-कारण-कर्तृत्व-हेय-आदेय-दृशा उज्झितः (free from the view of effect and cause, doership, and what to reject or accept) स-देहः इव निर्देहः (bodiless though seeming embodied) स-संसारः अपि असंसृतिः (free of saṃsāra though seeming in it) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— free of every notion of cause and effect, of doership, of things to reject or accept; as if embodied yet bodiless, as if in saṃsāra yet free of it —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **As if.** The free one looks like everyone else (2.18.31). The difference is invisible.

## Verse 2.17.46 { #2-17-46 }

<p class="shloka">
चिन्मयो घनपाषाणजठरापीवरोपमः ।<br>
चिदादित्यस्तपँल्लोकानन्धकारोदरोपमः ॥ २.१७.४६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चित्-मयः (made of consciousness) घन-पाषाण-जठर-आपीवर-उपमः (solid like the inside of a great rock) चित्-आदित्यः (a sun of consciousness) लोकान् तपन् (lighting the worlds) अन्धकार-उदर-उपमः (yet like the inside of darkness) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— made of consciousness, yet solid like the heart of a great rock; a sun of consciousness lighting the worlds, yet like the depths of darkness —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Paradox.** Full of light yet to the world like darkness — because it is not an object anyone can see. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">या निशा सर्वभूतानां तस्यां जागर्ति संयमी</span> (2.69).
- **Reading note.** The end of the second line is corrupt in the online text (अन्धकारोपरोपमम्); read as अन्धकारोदरोपमः, "like the inside of darkness", matching the next verse.

## Verse 2.17.47 { #2-17-47 }

<p class="shloka">
परप्रकाशरूपोऽपि परमान्ध्यमिवागतः ।<br>
रुद्धसंसृतिदुर्लीलः प्रक्षीणाशाविषूचिकः ॥ २.१७.४७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पर-प्रकाश-रूपः अपि (though of the nature of supreme light) परम-आन्ध्यम् इव आगतः (as if gone into utter darkness) रुद्ध-संसृति-दुः-लीलः (the cruel play of saṃsāra halted) प्रक्षीण-आशा-विषूचिकः (the cholera of desire wholly gone) —
</p>

**Translation:** "— though he is supreme light, he seems to have gone into utter darkness; the cruel play of saṃsāra is halted in him, and the sickness of desire is gone —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Hidden light.** The sage often looks ordinary, even dull, to the world.

## Verse 2.17.48 { #2-17-48 }

<p class="shloka">
नष्टाहंकारवेतालो देहवानकलेवरः ।<br>
कस्मिंश्चिद्रोमकोट्यग्रे तस्येयमवतिष्ठते ।<br>
जगल्लक्ष्मीर्महामेरोः पुष्पे क्वचिदिवालिनी ॥ २.१७.४८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
नष्ट-अहंकार-वेतालः (the ghost of ego destroyed) देहवान् अकलेवरः (embodied yet bodiless) — तस्य (of him) कस्मिंश्चित् रोम-कोटि-अग्रे (on the tip of a single hair) इयं जगत्-लक्ष्मीः अवतिष्ठते (this whole splendour of the world rests), महा-मेरोः क्वचित् पुष्पे अलिनी इव (as a bee on some flower of mighty Meru).
</p>

**Translation:** "— the phantom of ego gone, he has a body and yet no body. On the point of a single hair of his, the glory of the whole world sits, like a bee on some blossom of mighty Meru."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The world on a hair.** The vastness of the free mind dwarfs the world. The Avataṃsaka Sūtra too speaks of whole world-systems in a single hair-tip.

## Verse 2.17.49 { #2-17-49 }

<p class="shloka">
परमाणौ परमाणौ चिदाकाशः स्वकोटरे ।<br>
जगल्लक्ष्मीसहस्राणि धत्ते कृत्वाथ पश्यति ॥ २.१७.४९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
चित्-आकाशः (the space of consciousness) परमाणौ परमाणौ (in every single atom) स्व-कोटरे (in its own hollow) जगत्-लक्ष्मी-सहस्राणि (thousands of world-splendours) कृत्वा धत्ते (creates and holds) अथ पश्यति (and then beholds).
</p>

**Translation:** "The space of consciousness, in every single atom, in its own hollow, creates and holds thousands of worlds, and then beholds them."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Worlds in atoms.** Consciousness is the space in which all worlds appear, at every scale. The Kaṭha: <span class="parallel">अणोरणीयान्महतो महीयान्</span> — *smaller than the small, greater than the great* (1.2.20).

## Verse 2.17.50 { #2-17-50 }

<p class="shloka">
विततता हृदयस्य महामतेर्हरिहराब्जजलक्षशतैरपि ।<br>
तुलनमेति न मुक्तिमतो यतः प्रविततास्ति निरुत्तमवस्तुनः ॥ २.१७.५० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
महा-मतेः मुक्तिमतः (of the great-minded liberated one) हृदयस्य विततता (the vastness of the heart) हरि-हर-अब्ज-ज-लक्ष-शतैः अपि (even with hundreds of thousands of Viṣṇus, Śivas and Brahmās) तुलनं न एति (cannot be compared), यतः (because) निरुत्तम-वस्तुनः प्रवितता अस्ति (it is the expanse of the unsurpassed reality).
</p>

**Translation:** "The vastness of the liberated sage's heart cannot be matched even by hundreds of thousands of Viṣṇus, Śivas and Brahmās, for it is the very expanse of the unsurpassed reality."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The heart as the infinite.** The Chāndogya: <span class="parallel">यावान्वा अयमाकाशस्तावानेषोऽन्तर्हृदय आकाशः । उभे अस्मिन्द्यावापृथिवी अन्तरेव समाहिते</span> — *as vast as this space is the space within the heart; both heaven and earth are contained in it* (8.1.3).
- **Reading note.** The online text prints हरिहराञ्जज; corrected here to हरिहराब्जज ("Hari, Hara and the lotus-born").
