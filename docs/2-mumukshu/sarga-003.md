# Sarga 3

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · Endless worlds, and Vyāsa's freedom (36 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 3 at a glance"
    Rāma asks why Vyāsa is still embodied while his son Śuka is released. Vasiṣṭha answers with a vision of endless worlds.

    1. Rāma's doubt (2.3.1–2.3.3).
    2. Worlds past, present and future are countless; at death each being sees a new world arise in its own mind (2.3.4–2.3.17).
    3. This is ignorance, endless in its forms; beings and sages recur in cycles, alike or different (2.3.18–2.3.25).
    4. This Vyāsa is the tenth appearance of himself, and already liberated while living (2.3.26–2.3.31).
    5. Māyā has no end, but the knower stands unveiled, at peace (2.3.32–2.3.36).

## Verse 2.3.1 { #2-3-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
पूर्वमुक्तं भगवता यज्ज्ञानं पद्मजन्मना ।<br>
सर्गादौ लोकशान्त्यर्थं तदिदं कथयाम्यहम् ॥ २.३.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अहं (I) तत् इदं ज्ञानं कथयामि (now tell this knowledge) यत् भगवता पद्म-जन्मना (which by the blessed lotus-born [Brahmā]) पूर्वं सर्ग-आदौ (long ago, at the beginning of creation) लोक-शान्ति-अर्थम् उक्तम् (was spoken for the peace of the world).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "I shall now tell the knowledge that the blessed lotus-born Brahmā spoke at the beginning of creation, for the peace of the world."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **For the peace of the world.** लोकशान्त्यर्थम् — the teaching was given from compassion, not for display. Śāntideva's whole work turns on the same wish: *may all beings be at peace*.
- **At the beginning.** The knowledge is as old as creation. It is not a late philosophy but the original medicine for the original disease.

## Verse 2.3.2 { #2-3-2 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
कथयिष्यसि विस्तीर्णा भगवन्मोक्षसंहिताम् ।<br>
इमं तावत्क्षणं जातं संशयं मे निवारय ॥ २.३.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
भगवन् (O blessed one), [त्वं] विस्तीर्णां मोक्ष-संहिताम् (the extensive compendium on liberation) कथयिष्यसि (will tell); तावत् (first) क्षणं जातं (just arisen) इमं मे संशयं निवारय (remove this doubt of mine).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Blessed one, you will tell me the full treatise on liberation. But first, clear away a doubt that has just arisen in me."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Doubt cleared at once.** Rāma does not store his doubts for later. A good student asks as soon as a doubt arises — before it hardens into a barrier.
- **मोक्षसंहिता.** The Yoga Vasiṣṭha's own name for itself is *Mokṣopāya*, "the means to liberation" (2.17.6).

## Verse 2.3.3 { #2-3-3 }

<p class="shloka">
पिता शुकस्य सर्वज्ञो गुरुर्व्यासो महामतिः ।<br>
विदेहमुक्तो न कथं कथं मुक्तः सुतोऽस्य सः ॥ २.३.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
शुकस्य पिता (Śuka's father) सर्वज्ञः महामतिः गुरुः व्यासः (the all-knowing, great-minded teacher Vyāsa) कथं विदेह-मुक्तः न (how is he not liberated from the body)? अस्य सः सुतः कथं मुक्तः (how is that son of his liberated)?
</p>

**Translation:** "Vyāsa, Śuka's father, the all-knowing, great-minded teacher, is still in his body, not released from it. How then is his son released, and not he?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A sharp question.** If Śuka merged like a drop in the ocean (2.1.45), and Janaka said Śuka surpassed Vyāsa (2.1.39), is Vyāsa, sitting here in the hall, not free? Rāma thinks clearly and asks directly.
- **Embodied and disembodied freedom.** Behind the question is a real issue: is liberation only at death (विदेहमुक्ति), or can one be free while living (जीवन्मुक्ति)? Vasiṣṭha answers in Sarga 4.

## Verse 2.3.4 { #2-3-4 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
परमार्कप्रकाशान्तस्त्रिजगत्त्रसरेणवः ।<br>
उत्पत्योत्पत्य लीना ये न संख्यामुपयान्ति ते ॥ २.३.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ये (those) त्रि-जगत्-त्रसरेणवः (specks of dust that are the three worlds) परम-अर्क-प्रकाश-अन्तः (within the light of the supreme sun) उत्पत्य उत्पत्य लीनाः (have arisen again and again and dissolved), ते संख्यां न उपयान्ति (they cannot be counted).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "In the light of the supreme sun, the three worlds are like specks of dust in a sunbeam. They have risen and vanished again and again, beyond all counting."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Specks in a sunbeam.** त्रसरेणु — the dust that dances in a sunbeam through a window. Whole universes are like that in the light of consciousness. Vasiṣṭha answers Rāma's question about one sage by widening the frame to countless worlds.
- **The Gītā's cosmic scale:** <span class="parallel">अव्यक्ताद्व्यक्तयः सर्वाः प्रभवन्त्यहरागमे</span> — *at the coming of Brahmā's day, all manifest things arise from the unmanifest* (8.18).

## Verse 2.3.5 { #2-3-5 }

<p class="shloka">
वर्तमानाश्च याः सन्ति त्रैलोक्यगणकोटयः ।<br>
शक्यन्ते ताश्च संख्यातुं नैव काश्चन केनचित् ॥ २.३.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
वर्तमानाः च याः त्रैलोक्य-गण-कोटयः सन्ति (and the millions of groups of three-world systems that exist now) ताः च (those too) केनचित् (by anyone) काश्चन (any of them) न एव संख्यातुं शक्यन्ते (can be counted).
</p>

**Translation:** "And the millions of world-systems that exist at present — not even these can be counted by anyone."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Countless now.** Not only in the past: the present too holds innumerable worlds. Buddhist cosmology speaks likewise of world-systems as many as the grains of sand in the Ganges.

## Verse 2.3.6 { #2-3-6 }

<p class="shloka">
भविष्यन्ति पराम्भोधौ जन्तुसर्गतरङ्गकाः ।<br>
तांश्च वै परिसंख्यातुं सा कथैव न विद्यते ॥ २.३.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
परा-अम्भोधौ (in the supreme ocean) [ये] जन्तु-सर्ग-तरङ्गकाः भविष्यन्ति (the little waves of creations of beings that will come) तान् च परिसंख्यातुं (and to count them) सा कथा एव न विद्यते (there is no question at all).
</p>

**Translation:** "And as for the waves of creation that will yet rise in the supreme ocean, there can be no talk of counting them."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Past, present, future** — all beyond number. The ocean is one; the waves are endless. Against this background, one sage's state is a small matter — but Vasiṣṭha will show it is also no contradiction.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints जन्तसर्ग; corrected here to जन्तुसर्ग.

## Verse 2.3.7 { #2-3-7 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
या भूता या भविष्यन्त्यो जगत्सर्गपरम्पराः ।<br>
तासां विचारणा युक्ता वर्तमानास्तु का इव ॥ २.३.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
याः भूताः (those that were) याः भविष्यन्त्यः (those that will be) जगत्-सर्ग-परम्पराः (successions of world-creations) तासां विचारणा युक्ता (thinking about them is fitting); वर्तमानाः तु काः इव (but what of the present ones)?
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "It is fine to reflect on past and future chains of creation. But what of those that exist now?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rāma keeps to the point.** The cosmic vision is grand, but Rāma wants to understand the present. Vasiṣṭha's next verses explain how present worlds arise — from each being's own mind.

## Verse 2.3.8 { #2-3-8 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
तिर्यक्पुरुषदेवादेर्यो नाम स विनश्यति ।<br>
यस्मिन्नेव प्रदेशेऽसौ तदैवेदं प्रपश्यति ॥ २.३.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तिर्यक्-पुरुष-देव-आदेः (among animals, humans, gods and others) यः नाम विनश्यति (whoever dies), असौ (he) यस्मिन् एव प्रदेशे (in that very place) तदा एव (at that very moment) इदं प्रपश्यति (sees this [a world]).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "Whoever dies — animal, human, god or any other — at that very place and moment begins to see a world."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Death opens a world.** At death the being does not go anywhere; right where it is, a new world appears to it. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">यं यं वापि स्मरन्भावं त्यजत्यन्ते कलेवरम् । तं तमेवैति कौन्तेय</span> — *whatever state one remembers on leaving the body, to that one goes* (8.6).
- **Each a private world.** This is how the present worlds are countless: every dying being sees its own.

## Verse 2.3.9 { #2-3-9 }

<p class="shloka">
आतिवाहिकनाम्नान्तः स्वहृद्येव जगत्त्रयम् ।<br>
व्योम्नि चित्तशरीरेण व्योमात्मानुभवत्यजः ॥ २.३.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अजः व्योमात्मा (the unborn one, whose nature is space) व्योम्नि (in space) चित्त-शरीरेण (with a body made of mind) आतिवाहिक-नाम्ना (called the ātivāhika, the subtle travelling body) अन्तः स्व-हृदि एव (within, in its own heart) जगत्-त्रयम् अनुभवति (experiences the three worlds).
</p>

**Translation:** "The unborn one, whose nature is space, experiences the three worlds within its own heart, in space, through a body made of mind — the subtle body called ātivāhika."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **आतिवाहिक.** The "carrying-across" body, the subtle body of mind that moves from life to life. The gross body is left behind; the mind-body goes on imagining worlds.
- **The Bṛhadāraṇyaka** describes the dreamer making his own world: <span class="parallel">स्वयं विहत्य स्वयं निर्माय स्वेन भासा स्वेन ज्योतिषा प्रस्वपिति</span> — *he himself destroys, himself creates, and dreams by his own light* (4.3.9).

## Verse 2.3.10 { #2-3-10 }

<p class="shloka">
एवं मृता म्रियन्ते च मरिष्यन्ति च कोटयः ।<br>
भूतानां यां जगन्त्याशामुदितानि पृथक्पृथक् ॥ २.३.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
एवं (thus) भूतानां कोटयः (millions of beings) मृताः (have died) म्रियन्ते च (are dying) मरिष्यन्ति च (and will die); [तेषां] यां आशाम् (according to their longing) जगन्ति पृथक् पृथक् उदितानि (worlds arise for each separately).
</p>

**Translation:** "So millions of beings have died, are dying and will die; and according to each one's longing, a world arises for each separately."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Longing shapes the world.** आशा — desire, hope, expectation — is the seed of the next world. The Bṛhadāraṇyaka: <span class="parallel">स यथाकामो भवति तत्क्रतुर्भवति</span> — *as is one's desire, so is one's resolve* (4.4.5).

## Verse 2.3.11 { #2-3-11 }

<p class="shloka">
संकल्पनिर्माणमिव मनोराज्यविलासवत् ।<br>
इन्द्रजालामाल इव कथार्थप्रतिभासवत् ॥ २.३.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[तानि जगन्ति] संकल्प-निर्माणम् इव (like something built by imagination) मनो-राज्य-विलासवत् (like the play of a daydream kingdom) इन्द्रजाल-मालः इव (like a string of conjuring tricks) कथा-अर्थ-प्रतिभासवत् (like the scenes that appear when one hears a story).
</p>

**Translation:** "These worlds are like things built by imagination, like the play of a kingdom in a daydream, like a string of conjuring tricks, like the scenes that form as one listens to a story."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The Yoga Vasiṣṭha's images.** Here begins a stream of similes that will run through the whole work. Each picks out one feature: vividness without substance.
- **A story's scenes.** When we hear a story, a whole world appears to us — real while it lasts, nowhere afterwards. This is the text's own method: its stories create worlds to show how worlds are created.

## Verse 2.3.12 { #2-3-12 }

<p class="shloka">
दुर्वातभूकम्प इव त्रस्तबालपिशाचवत् ।<br>
मुक्तालीवामले व्योम्नि नौस्पन्दतरुयानवत् ॥ २.३.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
[तानि] दुर्वात-भूकम्पः इव (like an earthquake felt in a storm) त्रस्त-बाल-पिशाचवत् (like a ghost seen by a frightened child) अमले व्योम्नि मुक्ता-आली इव (like a string of pearls in the clear sky) नौ-स्पन्द-तरु-यानवत् (like trees moving as seen from a moving boat).
</p>

**Translation:** "They are like the shaking of the ground one feels in a gale, like the ghost a frightened child sees, like a string of pearls seen in a clear sky, like the trees that seem to move when one sails past in a boat."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Errors of perception.** Each image is a real experience with a false object: the ground seems to shake, the ghost seems present, specks seem like pearls, the shore seems to move. The experience is real; its content is not.
- **The moving shore.** Classic Indian example of superimposing one's own motion on the world. Śaṅkara uses it too.

## Verse 2.3.13 { #2-3-13 }

<p class="shloka">
स्वप्नसंवित्तिपुरवत्स्मृतिजातखपुष्पवत् ।<br>
जगत्संसरणं स्वान्तर्मृतोऽनुभवति स्वयम् ॥ २.३.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मृतः (the one who has died) स्व-अन्तः (within himself) स्वयं (by himself) स्वप्न-संवित्ति-पुरवत् (like a city known in a dream) स्मृति-जात-ख-पुष्पवत् (like a sky-flower born of memory) जगत्-संसरणम् अनुभवति (experiences the wandering through the world).
</p>

**Translation:** "The one who has died experiences, within himself and by himself, the passage through a world — like a city seen in a dream, or a flower in the sky conjured by memory."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **खपुष्प — the sky-flower.** A standard example in Indian logic of something wholly unreal. Yet it can be imagined vividly. So with the after-death world.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints स्वप्नसंवित्तिषुरवत्; corrected here to स्वप्नसंवित्तिपुरवत् ("like a city known in a dream").

## Verse 2.3.14 { #2-3-14 }

<p class="shloka">
तत्रातिपरिणामेन तदेव घनतां गतम् ।<br>
इहलोकोऽयमित्येव जीवाकाशे विजृम्भते ॥ २.३.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्र (there) अति-परिणामेन (through long ripening) तत् एव (that very thing) घनतां गतम् (becomes solid); "अयम् इह-लोकः" इति एव (as "this is the world here") जीव-आकाशे विजृम्भते (it unfolds in the space of the individual).
</p>

**Translation:** "There, by long maturing, that same imagining grows dense, and unfolds in the inner space of the individual as 'this world, here'."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **How imagination becomes solid.** Repeated and unquestioned, a mental picture hardens into "the world". This is the process the whole text will unravel: घनता, solidification, and its reversal.
- **The Buddha** said: *mano-pubbaṅgamā dhammā … manomayā* — *things are led by mind, made of mind* (Dhammapada 1).

## Verse 2.3.15 { #2-3-15 }

<p class="shloka">
पुनस्तत्रैव जातो वामरणाद्यनुभूतिमान् ।<br>
परं लोकं कल्पयति मृतस्तत्र तथा पुनः ॥ २.३.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुनः तत्र एव जातः वा (again, being born there) मरण-आदि-अनुभूतिमान् (experiencing death and the rest) मृतः (having died) तत्र तथा पुनः (there, likewise, again) परं लोकं कल्पयति (imagines another world).
</p>

**Translation:** "Born again there and going through death and the rest, he dies and once more imagines another world — and so on."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Worlds within worlds, endlessly.** Each death opens a new world, in which another death opens another. This is saṃsāra seen from inside.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints a corrupt word (जग्नेद्वा); read here as जातो वा, "or, being born", which fits the sense.

## Verse 2.3.16 { #2-3-16 }

<p class="shloka">
तदन्तरन्ये पुरुषास्तेषामन्तस्तथेतरे ।<br>
संसार इति भान्तीमे कदलीदलपीठवत् ॥ २.३.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तत्-अन्तः (within that [world]) अन्ये पुरुषाः (other persons), तेषाम् अन्तः तथा इतरे (and within theirs, still others) — इमे संसारः इति भान्ति (these appear as saṃsāra), कदली-दल-पीठवत् (like the layers of a plantain stem).
</p>

**Translation:** "Within that world are other persons, and within theirs still others. All these appear as saṃsāra, like the layers of a plantain stem, one inside another."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The plantain stem.** Peel one layer and another appears, and another — and in the centre, nothing. A perfect image of worlds within minds within worlds, with no solid core.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints तेवामन्त; corrected here to तेषामन्त.

## Verse 2.3.17 { #2-3-17 }

<p class="shloka">
न पृथ्व्यादिमहाभूतगणा न च जगत्क्रमाः ।<br>
मृतानां सन्ति तत्रापि तथाप्येषां जगद्भ्रमाः ॥ २.३.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मृतानां (for the dead) तत्र अपि (even there) पृथ्वी-आदि-महाभूत-गणाः न (there are no great elements such as earth) न च जगत्-क्रमाः (nor the orders of a world); तथा अपि (yet) एषां जगत्-भ्रमाः [सन्ति] (they have the illusion of a world).
</p>

**Translation:** "For the dead there is no earth or other element, nor any order of a world; and yet they have the illusion of one."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Worlds without matter.** The after-death world has no elements, yet it is fully experienced. So experience does not need matter; it needs only mind. The question is then whether this waking world is any different — a question the Utpatti Prakaraṇa will press.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints पृम्ब्यादि … जगद्भमाः; corrected here to पृथ्व्यादि … जगद्भ्रमाः.

## Verse 2.3.18 { #2-3-18 }

<p class="shloka">
अविद्यैव ह्यनन्तेयं नानाप्रसरशालिनी ।<br>
जडानां सरिदादीर्घा तरत्सर्गतरङ्गिणी ॥ २.३.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">

</p>

**Translation:** "This is ignorance itself — endless, spreading in countless ways. For the unawakened it is a long river, rolling with waves of creation."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **अविद्या.** Not "lack of information" but the basic misreading of reality. It is endless in its forms, though not in its existence — it ends with knowledge.
- **The river.** One cannot count its waves, but one can cross it. Vasiṣṭha's interest is not in the waves but in the crossing.
