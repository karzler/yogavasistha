# Sarga 11

*Mumukṣu Prakaraṇa · The royal knowledge, and the four gatekeepers (73 verses)*

!!! info "Sarga 11 at a glance"
    Why Brahmā sent the teaching, and who is fit to receive it.

    1. As the ages declined, Brahmā sent Vasiṣṭha and other sages; first works and laws were given, then knowledge was taught to kings — the royal knowledge (2.11.1–2.11.19).
    2. Rāma's dispassion is causeless and sāttvika, born of his own discernment, by grace; he is soft ground for the seed (2.11.20–2.11.32).
    3. Only knowledge crosses saṃsāra. Choose a reliable teacher, ask well, and take his words in like cloth takes dye (2.11.33–2.11.56).
    4. The four gatekeepers of liberation: calm, inquiry, contentment, and the company of the good. Master one, and the others follow (2.11.57–2.11.73).

## Verse 2.11.1 { #2-11-1 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
एतत्ते कथितं सर्वं ज्ञानावतरणं भुवि ।<br>
मया स्वमीहितं चैव कमलोद्भवचेष्टितम् ॥ २.११.१ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मया (by me) ते (to you) एतत् सर्वं (all this) भुवि ज्ञान-अवतरणं (the descent of knowledge to earth) स्वम् ईहितं च एव (and my own story) कमल-उद्भव-चेष्टितम् (and the doings of the lotus-born) कथितम् (has been told).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "I have told you all this: how knowledge came down to earth, my own story, and the doings of the lotus-born Brahmā."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The lineage is complete.** Vasiṣṭha has shown where his knowledge comes from. Now he turns to the student's readiness to receive it.

## Verse 2.11.2 { #2-11-2 }

<p class="shloka">
तदिदं परमं ज्ञानं श्रोतुमद्य तवानघ ।<br>
भृशमुत्कण्ठितं चेतो महतः सुकृतोदयात् ॥ २.११.२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अनघ (O sinless one), अद्य (today) तव चेतः (your mind) महतः सुकृत-उदयात् (because of the rising of great merit) इदं परमं ज्ञानं श्रोतुं (to hear this supreme knowledge) भृशम् उत्कण्ठितम् (is intensely eager).
</p>

**Translation:** "Sinless one, today, because great merit has ripened, your mind is intensely eager to hear this supreme knowledge."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Eagerness as merit's fruit.** The longing to know is itself a sign of past good. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">बहूनां जन्मनामन्ते ज्ञानवान्मां प्रपद्यते</span> — *at the end of many births the wise one comes to Me* (7.19).

## Verse 2.11.3 { #2-11-3 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीराम उवाच ।</span><br>
कथं ब्रह्मन्भगवतो लोके ज्ञानावतारणे ।<br>
सर्गादनन्तरं बुद्धिः प्रवृत्ता परमेष्ठिनः ॥ २.११.३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ब्रह्मन् (O brahmin), सर्गात् अनन्तरं (after creation) भगवतः परमेष्ठिनः बुद्धिः (the mind of the blessed Brahmā) लोके ज्ञान-अवतारणे (to bringing knowledge down into the world) कथं प्रवृत्ता (how did it turn)?
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Rāma said: "Brahmin, how did the mind of the blessed Brahmā turn, after creation, to bringing knowledge down into the world?"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Why after creation?** Rāma asks for the motive in more detail. Vasiṣṭha will place it in the decline of the ages.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints ब्रह्मन्मगवतो; corrected here to ब्रह्मन्भगवतो.

## Verse 2.11.4 { #2-11-4 }

<p class="shloka">
<span class="speaker">श्रीवसिष्ठ उवाच ।</span><br>
परमे ब्रह्मणि ब्रह्मा स्वभाववशतः स्वयम् ।<br>
जातः स्पन्दमयो नित्यमूर्मिरम्बुनिधाविव ॥ २.११.४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
परमे ब्रह्मणि (in the supreme Brahman) ब्रह्मा स्वयं (Brahmā himself) स्वभाव-वशतः (by the force of its own nature) नित्यं स्पन्द-मयः जातः (arose, made of movement), अम्बु-निधौ ऊर्मिः इव (as a wave in the ocean).
</p>

**Translation:** Śrī Vasiṣṭha said: "Brahmā himself arose in the supreme Brahman by its own nature, made of movement, like a wave in the ocean."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Again the wave.** Brahmā is a movement of Brahman, not a separate being. Creator and creation are waves of one ocean.

## Verse 2.11.5 { #2-11-5 }

<p class="shloka">
दृष्ट्वैवमातुरं सर्गं सर्गस्य सकलां गतिम् ।<br>
भूतभव्यभविष्यस्थां ददर्श परमेश्वरः ॥ २.११.५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
परमेश्वरः (the supreme lord) एवम् आतुरं सर्गं (creation thus in distress) [च] सर्गस्य भूत-भव्य-भविष्य-स्थां सकलां गतिम् (and the whole course of creation — past, present and future) दृष्ट्वा ददर्श (looked and saw).
</p>

**Translation:** "The supreme lord looked and saw creation in distress, and its whole course in past, present and future."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Seeing the whole.** The creator sees the arc of time. What he sees moves him to act.
- **Reading note.** The online text prints सर्ग … दश; corrected here to सर्गं … ददर्श.

## Verse 2.11.6 { #2-11-6 }

<p class="shloka">
सक्रियाक्रमकालस्य कृतादेः क्षय आगते ।<br>
मोहमालोच्य लोकानां कारुण्यमगमत्प्रभुः ॥ २.११.६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
कृत-आदेः (beginning with the Kṛta age) स-क्रिया-क्रम-कालस्य (the age with its order of works) क्षये आगते (when its decline came) लोकानां मोहम् आलोच्य (seeing the delusion of the people) प्रभुः कारुण्यम् अगमत् (the Lord was moved to compassion).
</p>

**Translation:** "When the Kṛta age, with its ordered way of life, came to its decline, the Lord saw the delusion of the people and was moved to compassion."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **The decline of the ages.** Kṛta (Satya) is the golden age. As ages decline, order fades and teaching becomes necessary. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">स कालेनेह महता योगो नष्टः परन्तप</span> — *in the long course of time this yoga was lost* (4.2).

## Verse 2.11.7 { #2-11-7 }

<p class="shloka">
ततो मामीश्वरः सृष्ट्वा ज्ञानेनायोज्य चासकृत् ।<br>
विससर्ज महीपीठं लोकस्याज्ञानशान्तये ॥ २.११.७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः (then) ईश्वरः (the Lord) मां सृष्ट्वा (creating me) ज्ञानेन च असकृत् आयोज्य (and endowing me again and again with knowledge) लोकस्य अज्ञान-शान्तये (to still the ignorance of the world) मही-पीठं विससर्ज (sent me to the earth).
</p>

**Translation:** "So the Lord created me, filled me again and again with knowledge, and sent me down to the earth to still the world's ignorance."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **A mission.** Vasiṣṭha's presence on earth has a purpose: to end ignorance. A teacher is a sent one.

## Verse 2.11.8 { #2-11-8 }

<p class="shloka">
यथाहं प्रहितस्तेन तथान्ये च महर्षयः ।<br>
सनत्कुमारप्रमुखा नारदाद्याश्च भूरिशः ॥ २.११.८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
यथा अहं तेन प्रहितः (as I was sent by him) तथा अन्ये च महर्षयः (so were other great sages) सनत्कुमार-प्रमुखाः (led by Sanatkumāra) नारद-आद्याः च (Nārada and others) भूरिशः (many).
</p>

**Translation:** "As I was sent, so were many other great sages — Sanatkumāra, Nārada and others."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Sanatkumāra and Nārada.** In the Chāndogya Upaniṣad (Book 7), Sanatkumāra teaches Nārada the knowledge of the Self. Teachers of the same lineage.

## Verse 2.11.9 { #2-11-9 }

<p class="shloka">
क्रियाक्रमेण पुण्येन तथा ज्ञानक्रमेण च ।<br>
मनोमोहामयोन्नद्धमुद्धर्तुं लोकमीरिताः ॥ २.११.९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मनो-मोह-आमय-उन्नद्धं लोकम् (the world, bound by the disease of the mind's delusion) पुण्येन क्रिया-क्रमेण (by the holy path of works) तथा ज्ञान-क्रमेण च (and by the path of knowledge) उद्धर्तुम् ईरिताः (they were sent to lift up).
</p>

**Translation:** "They were sent to lift up the world, bound by the sickness of delusion, through the holy path of works and the path of knowledge."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Two paths.** Works for those who need them, knowledge for those ready. The Gītā: <span class="parallel">लोकेऽस्मिन्द्विविधा निष्ठा पुरा प्रोक्ता मयानघ । ज्ञानयोगेन साङ्ख्यानां कर्मयोगेन योगिनाम्</span> — *in this world a twofold path was taught of old: knowledge for the contemplative, works for the active* (3.3).

## Verse 2.11.10 { #2-11-10 }

<p class="shloka">
महर्षिभिस्ततस्तैस्तैः क्षीणे कृतयुगे पुरा ।<br>
क्रमात्क्रियाक्रमे शुद्धे पृथिव्या तनुतां गते ॥ २.११.१० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
पुरा (long ago) कृत-युगे क्षीणे (when the Kṛta age had passed) क्रमात् (gradually) पृथिव्याः शुद्धे क्रिया-क्रमे (the pure order of works on earth) तनुतां गते (having grown thin) — ततः तैः तैः महर्षिभिः (then, by those great sages) —
</p>

**Translation:** "Long ago, when the Kṛta age had passed and the pure order of works on earth had gradually grown thin, those great sages —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Decline is gradual.** No sudden fall; a slow thinning. So too in a single life: neglect works slowly.

## Verse 2.11.11 { #2-11-11 }

<p class="shloka">
क्रियाक्रमविधानार्थं मर्यादानियमाय च ।<br>
पृथग्देशविभागेन भूपालाः परिकल्पिताः ॥ २.११.११ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
क्रिया-क्रम-विधान-अर्थं (to establish the order of works) मर्यादा-नियमाय च (and to regulate proper bounds) पृथक्-देश-विभागेन (by dividing the land into separate regions) भूपालाः परिकल्पिताः (kings were set up).
</p>

**Translation:** "— to establish the order of works and to keep society within its bounds, divided the land into regions and set up kings."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kingship as service.** Kings exist to uphold order (मर्यादा). Rāma, the future king, hears that his office was founded by sages for the people's good.

## Verse 2.11.12 { #2-11-12 }

<p class="shloka">
बहूनि स्मृतिशास्त्राणि यज्ञशास्त्राणि चावनौ ।<br>
धर्मकामार्थसिद्ध्यर्थं कल्पितान्युचितान्यथ ॥ २.११.१२ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (then) अवनौ (on earth) बहूनि उचितानि स्मृति-शास्त्राणि (many fitting texts of law) यज्ञ-शास्त्राणि च (and texts on sacrifice) धर्म-काम-अर्थ-सिद्धि-अर्थं कल्पितानि (were composed for the attainment of dharma, pleasure and wealth).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then many fitting books of law and of sacrifice were composed on earth, for the attainment of dharma, pleasure and prosperity."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Three aims, then a fourth.** Dharma, artha, kāma are the worldly aims. Mokṣa, the fourth, needs something more — knowledge.

## Verse 2.11.13 { #2-11-13 }

<p class="shloka">
कालचक्रे वहत्यस्मिंस्ततो विगलिते क्रमे ।<br>
प्रत्यहं भोजनपरे जने शाल्यर्जनोन्मुखे ॥ २.११.१३ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अस्मिन् काल-चक्रे वहति (as this wheel of time turned) ततः क्रमे विगलिते (and the order fell apart) जने प्रत्यहं भोजन-परे (with people every day intent on food) शालि-अर्जन-उन्मुखे (concerned only with getting rice) —
</p>

**Translation:** "As the wheel of time turned on and that order fell apart, people became intent day by day on food, concerned only with earning their rice —"

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **When life shrinks to survival.** A society busy only with getting and eating has lost its higher aims. The Buddha's term for such living is *āmisa*, the material, as opposed to *dhamma*.

## Verse 2.11.14 { #2-11-14 }

<p class="shloka">
द्वन्द्वानि संप्रवृत्तानि विषयार्थे महीभुजाम् ।<br>
दण्ड्यतां संप्रयातानि भूतानि भूवि भूरिशः ॥ २.११.१४ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
मही-भुजां (among kings) विषय-अर्थे (for territory) द्वन्द्वानि संप्रवृत्तानि (conflicts arose); भुवि (on earth) भूरिशः भूतानि (many beings) दण्ड्यतां संप्रयातानि (came to deserve punishment).
</p>

**Translation:** "— kings began to quarrel over territory, and many beings on earth fell into wrongdoing that called for punishment."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Conflict over land.** Greed at the top, disorder below. The pattern of every declining age.

## Verse 2.11.15 { #2-11-15 }

<p class="shloka">
ततो युद्धं विना भूपा महीं पालयितुं क्षमाः ।<br>
न समर्थास्तदा याताः प्रजाभिः सह दैन्यताम् ॥ २.११.१५ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः (then) भूपाः (kings) युद्धं विना (without war) महीं पालयितुं क्षमाः समर्थाः न (were not able to protect the earth); तदा प्रजाभिः सह दैन्यतां याताः (then they fell into misery along with their people).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then kings could no longer protect the earth without war, and they fell into misery together with their people."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Kings too suffer.** Power does not exempt from misery. This is the background from which Rāma himself has recoiled (1.12–1.31).
- **Reading note.** The online text prints युद्ध … मही … समथोः; corrected here to युद्धं … महीं … समर्थाः.

## Verse 2.11.16 { #2-11-16 }

<p class="shloka">
तेषां दैन्यापनोदार्थं सम्यग्दृष्टिक्रमाय च ।<br>
ततोऽस्मदादिभिः प्रोक्ता महत्यो ज्ञानदृष्टयः ॥ २.११.१६ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
ततः (then) तेषां दैन्य-अपनोद-अर्थं (to remove their misery) सम्यक्-दृष्टि-क्रमाय च (and for right understanding) अस्मत्-आदिभिः (by us and others) महत्यः ज्ञान-दृष्टयः प्रोक्ताः (great insights of knowledge were taught).
</p>

**Translation:** "Then, to remove their misery and give them right understanding, we and others taught great insights of knowledge."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Knowledge for kings.** The teaching is offered not to escape kingship but to make kings wise. सम्यग्दृष्टि — "right view" — is also the first limb of the Buddha's eightfold path.

## Verse 2.11.17 { #2-11-17 }

<p class="shloka">
अध्यात्मविद्या तेनेयं पूर्वं राजसु वर्णिता ।<br>
तदनु प्रसृता लोके राजविद्येत्युदाहृता ॥ २.११.१७ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
तेन (so) इयम् अध्यात्म-विद्या (this knowledge of the Self) पूर्वं राजसु वर्णिता (was first taught among kings); तत्-अनु (afterwards) लोके प्रसृता (it spread in the world) "राज-विद्या" इति उदाहृता (and was called "the royal knowledge").
</p>

**Translation:** "So this knowledge of the Self was first taught among kings; later it spread through the world and came to be called the royal knowledge."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Royal knowledge.** The Gītā: <span class="parallel">राजविद्या राजगुह्यं पवित्रमिदमुत्तमम्</span> — *this is the royal knowledge, the royal secret, the supreme purifier* (9.2). And: <span class="parallel">एवं परम्पराप्राप्तमिमं राजर्षयो विदुः</span> — *handed down in succession, the royal sages knew it* (4.2). In the Upaniṣads, kings like Janaka, Ajātaśatru and Pravāhaṇa teach brahmins.

## Verse 2.11.18 { #2-11-18 }

<p class="shloka">
राजविद्या राजगुह्यमध्यात्मज्ञानमुत्तमम् ।<br>
ज्ञात्वा राघव राजानः परां निर्दुःखतां गताः ॥ २.११.१८ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
राघव (O Rāghava), राजानः (kings) राज-विद्यां राज-गुह्यम् उत्तमम् अध्यात्म-ज्ञानं ज्ञात्वा (knowing the royal knowledge, the royal secret, the supreme knowledge of the Self) परां निर्दुःखतां गताः (reached the highest freedom from sorrow).
</p>

**Translation:** "Rāghava, by knowing this royal knowledge, the royal secret, the supreme knowledge of the Self, kings reached the highest freedom from sorrow."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Freedom for those who rule.** Kings need not abandon their thrones to be free. The verse speaks directly to Rāma's situation.

## Verse 2.11.19 { #2-11-19 }

<p class="shloka">
अथ राजस्वतीतेषु बहुष्वमलकीर्तिषु ।<br>
अस्माद्दशरथाद्राम जातोऽद्य त्वमिहावनौ ॥ २.११.१९ ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
अथ (now) राम (O Rāma), बहुषु अमल-कीर्तिषु राजसु अतीतेषु (after many kings of spotless fame have passed) अस्मात् दशरथात् (from this Daśaratha) अद्य त्वम् इह अवनौ जातः (you have now been born here on earth).
</p>

**Translation:** "And now, Rāma, after many kings of spotless fame have passed, you have been born on earth to Daśaratha here."

**Pointers for Reflection:**

- **Rāma in the line.** He stands at the end of a long line of royal sages. The knowledge is his inheritance.

## Verse 2.11.20 { #2-11-20 }

<p class="shloka">
तव चातिप्रसन्नेऽस्मिञ्जातं मनसि पावनम् ।<br>
निर्निमित्तमिदं चारु वैराग्यमरिमर्दन ॥ २.११.२० ॥
</p>

<p class="anvaya">
